## Core

The ritual that protects Core covers the greater part of the fertile plain inside the ring of the Spear
Mountains. A mild climate with short, snow-heavy winters has made rich farmland of it, crossed by
meandering rivers on their way to the fish-heavy Silver Lake. In the middle of the lake lies an island,
the Isle of Spirits. The island is the only wild country in Core, and people avoid it while the priests
maintain that it is a blasphemous place. In the north-east is the district's only mountain country,
around the mining town of SteinBrëk, and the capital Geistburg is built on top of an imposing mesa. Core
is led by a tangle of organisations and power-holders with unclear borders between them. The Church is
strongest and the mages' academy second. The emperor and his court hold the formal power but are weak in
their ability to exercise it. The Guild, which represents craftsmen and farmers, has great power and is
often in conflict with the others over land. All of them have their own courts of law. The Church of the
Eternal Spirit stirs things up further. The poor are oppressed, but one grouping, the Weasels, runs a
low-tempo resistance against those above them. The satellite cities are mainly under the Church's
control, and the conflict with the Weasels is stronger still there.

## Fiord

The Fiord District has a ritual border formed by six lighthouses around the western fiord and the islands
off it. The climate is wet, and a damp, treeless heath covers most of the ground inside the coastal
cliffs, which in many places fall in high, steep drops to the deep fiord. It is a clan society where
families hold together and try to increase the family's power at the cost of others. The legal system
consists of a council, annual or specially convened, with representatives from the twelve families. Each
family also has an area in which it may hand down punishment itself, but matters that touch other families
must go up to the council for decision. Blood feud is an accepted principle of law. The poor are divided
up under the clans and have no common voice. The priesthood puts the family first.

## Ruby

Sun and salt easterly winds have shaped Ruby, which with its agreeable climate was a prosperous holiday
destination before the invasion. Soft hills of vineyards lie around a shallow sea bay with sandbars and
tides. The former glory of the three small cities Damza, Tanto and Serf is reflected in cobbles laid in
mosaic patterns, white marble palaces and flaking gold ornament.

The Ruby District is divided into three regions with a city each, and every region has a seat on the
council. The district is a quiet place and conflict and violent crime are rare. The district's economy is
good and the middle class is larger than the working class; even the workers have better conditions than
in other districts. Money matters most to those who govern, and any law that reduces income disappears
quickly.

## Iron

Centred on two great oases in the Hyena Desert, where underground water cisterns reach the surface, lies
the Iron District. In the north and the east the ritual also protects parts of the mineral-rich Mouse
Mountains, a forerunner to the Spear Mountains. The minerals are what gave Iron its name, but it is a
great deal more than iron that is mined here. The climate is dry and hot with ice-cold nights.

The Iron District is a theocracy: the Grey rule everything centrally and have handed local power to
Emirs. The law is invisible and arbitrary, and people disappear without trace. Since those who rule are
unknown, there is no possibility of holding them to account. The thrall system fills the mines with
workers who die young, but in Sammel you see nothing of the poverty.

## Furnace

Before the invasion Furnace lay in a tidy conifer forest broken up with cultivation. Steam trains ran on
rails between the various cities and there was always at least one airship visible in the sky. The mostly
windless Everfog coast softened the northern climate. After the Horde invaded the district in the year
879, forests and cities have been laid waste to a grey bogland; burnt, abandoned tree trunks from the time
before are a reminder that the area was once a forest. The Furnace District was organised around engineer
groups, with the help of the Rune Temple for the magical parts in their constructions. Furnace is gone,
but many of the groups that survived the ending of the ritual have carried on in other districts and
enclaves, often with the odd mage among them. They have their own language and are often self-governing
units. Their skills are sought after, which has led religious groups and authorities to turn a blind eye
to how well they square with local law.

## Amber

On the east coast the land rises sharply out of the sea. Just as in Ruby the tides are strong, but since
the sea immediately off the coast is deep and cold there are no sandbars as in Ruby, only more or less
bare rock down to the water. Damp conifer forests dominate the landscape, in many places covered in mosses
and lichens. Deep fissure lakes, steep wooded hills and the occasional swamp complete the country.
Scattered across the wilderness lie small settlements, forts and villages. Amber is without leadership;
people take the law into their own hands, and local mayors, those forest barons who still hold a name, and
local warlords try to hold and extend their possessions. Karmat is neutral and is used by everyone to
train troopers, in exchange for a tenth of them being sold by lot to the Iron District. Unlike the other
districts, Amber has no tunnel connection: all traffic must go in silence over Horde-controlled ground.

## Hyrlock

Hyrlock rises as a granite plateau out of the restless Kelp Sea. Only a stair hewn out of the rock leads up
from the plain harbour to the top. The top is barren and fruitless and every resource but fish has to be
shipped in through Horde-controlled sea. Hyrlock is a matriarchy; women hold all the important roles. The
society is a caste society.

## Kelp Bay

A small harbour which is the starting point for expeditions to Hyrlock. Kelp Bay is a small harbour, but
the extensive freight traffic has meant that every square centimetre is put to use. The high cliffs that
lie inside the protected area have been hollowed out into caves for warehouses, shops and bars. The houses
along the minimal strip of beach have been built layer on layer and several stand five storeys high. The
mood in the harbour is stressed, raw and almost always in the shadow of the cliffs. The harbour is
governed by a captain who is voted in each year and may not hold the office for more than one year. The
three freight companies usually take turns appointing the captain, and the other two companies abstain
from their votes when it is not their turn.

## Solemn

In the wild Troubled Sea to the south-east lies an island shaped much like Hyrlock: Solemn. The granite is
exchanged for limestone and the climate is warm, but the plateau form is the same, and just as in Hyrlock
it is the women who rule. The island belongs to those commonly called witches, an isolated group who
worship the Primal Mother and seldom leave their island. The island itself is well-ordered and
self-sufficient. It is said that the island has the best contact with the other Sides, and the
architecture of the capital Sonebra is more elvish than human.

## Regensburg

Geistburg's military power was gathered in Regensburg, and then came the invasion. Nowadays they are
separated and tied together only by a cave system with hundreds of tunnels and halls. In the middle there
is a small community around The Deep, an underground lake with boat traffic and the towns To and From on
the lake's shores. Regensburg itself is not much more than an enormous fort wedged into a good strategic
position on a cliff overlooking Spear Pass, a pass through the Spear Mountains that used to be well
travelled. The climate is cold and windy with short hot summers. A Marshal governs the area, and with the
right of inheritance the city holds, his son is expected to take over when he dies. A woman cannot become
Marshal. Regensburg has made an art of cavalry, and noblemen from Core and other districts make their way
here for training, equipment and the horses that are trained in the enclave.

## Wolf Peak

The monastery of Wolf Peak is set into the side of the volcano of the same name. Easily defended stairs
lead up along the volcano's sides, and the active volcano's plumes of smoke protect against air attack
from the Horde. Wolf Peak has always protected itself without the help of blood rituals and human
sacrifice. The priesthood governs the monastery, and people of the wrong faith are not usually allowed to
stay in the enclave. Everything is shared citizen to citizen, so there are no shops or bars in the area;
with the outside world they conduct barter trade. Gunpowder and firearms against the provisions that those
in Wolf Peak cannot produce themselves. Fire is holy in Wolf Peak, the ground itself is warm from the
volcano's heat, and beacons burn constantly along the length of the monastery.

## Echterhof

A city permanently under a cloud of smoke, but with a sunny and pleasant climate in the cultivated areas
around the city. The smoke is burnt in the five towers that crown the five Strigoi palaces. Echterhof's
Strigoi princes. The smoke lets the vampires move about the city on most days, unless there is an unusual
combination of strong wind and sunshine. The palaces are stately marble colossi on several levels, all
with well-guarded crypts beneath them for the occasions when the Strigoi need to get back to their
coffins. The fertile surrounding land, and the fact that the terror of the Horde exceeds the terror of
vampires, means people make their way here, get a plot of land to farm and at least live for a while. The
Strigoi princes govern jointly and behave like any other despotic high-noble families, apart from drinking
blood, and they balance their withdrawals of citizens so as not to make the enclave unattractive to
potential guests and food. The Strigoi made an agreement with the Horde, which respects their area as
protected. Deep in a crypt under Echterhof sleeps The First. The First, who is a minor god, is the one
with the final word if the princes do not agree, or if their aims diverge from The First's. The princes'
families contain more Strigoi, and human or half-human henchmen.

## Velderon

The only place on Firma where you are guaranteed to run into elves. Velderon is a chalk-white palace among
the ruins of the elves' capital on Firma from before Athor defeated them. Velderon is an embassy and has
no resources or facilities beyond that other than a small troop force and a few mages who maintain the
protective rituals around the embassy to keep humans and Hordes away. A permanent gate to the Second Side
is built in the heart of the embassy, and the staff come and go through it, together with a smaller flow
of provisions to maintain the ambassador's and his staff's way of life. The ruins are seen as a holy place
by the elves, but are visited now and then by adventurers who, despite the danger, try to find artefacts
from the time of the elves there.

## Saag et Haar

Athor defeated most things, but he did not manage to subdue the necromancer kings in Seetch. The little
piece that remains of the realm is Saag et Haar. The enclave lies along the south coast, protected by the
blood mages' rituals. A little way in from the coast lies the city of the same name, near the outflow of
the now and then dried-up Lizard River. The land is half desert, hot and inhospitable, and sandstorms from
the Hyena Desert are common, with a small piece of arable land in the western part. The mining operation
is all the greater, and slaves and zombies are worn down in the hunt for silver, gold and precious stones
in the low mountains of the enclave. Fahlas, the necromancer king, has governed the land for 400 years. He
has cheated ageing and become a minor god. Beneath him the army and the necromancers share power. Fahlas
rules with an iron hand, constantly paranoid that someone will take his place.

## The Triple Enclave

North of Iron and halfway to Kelp Bay lies The Triple Enclave. High up lie the three castles Farsight,
Marke and Highfall, which respectively focus on religion, drugs and the military. The winters are cold,
but otherwise it is far enough south to have a pleasant temperature, despite its altitude.

## Desert Stream

The inhabitants of Desert Stream, an isolated group in an oasis that did not have the protection of Iron's
protective ritual, managed at the last second to attract a group of blood mages from Hyrlock. In exchange
they gave away power in the oasis to the blood mages, who now live as a couple of families set apart from
the rest. They speak the local language with an accent and do not understand the customs, but as long as
they protect the oasis the villagers gladly sacrifice their children and relatives and let the blood mages
govern the enclave. The alternative is worse. The isolation of the governing group of blood mages has
continued down the years; they keep to themselves and only take in local members in order to keep the
bloodline strong. They have even stopped speaking the local language, Gastve, and engage interpreters for
their communication with the others.

# Regions

## Mountains

### North Wall

North Wall is a north-westerly spur of the Spear Mountains. It is mineral-rich, and a number of abandoned
mines run along the young, sharp-peaked range.

### South Wall

South Wall is a free-standing mountain range in the south-west. Like North Wall it consists of high, sharp
mountains, but it has never been prospected for minerals and has no trace of mining.

### Spear Mountain

The central range that encloses the plain with Core lies near the cradle of civilisation, and its inner
mountainsides are strewn with the ruins of cities, mining communities and villages. Only Steinbrek remains
nowadays.

## Deserts

### Hyena Desert

Hyena Desert is the only large desert on Firma. It is a southern sand desert that was previously
controlled entirely by the Emirs in Iron. It is an inhospitable place, dry and hot with cold nights.

### Lost Lake

Lost Lake is a dried-out great salt lake east of Hyena Desert. It was once a living lake, but the Horde
dried it out and placed its Hive Chguuth in the middle of it.

## Seas

There is only one continuous sea around Firma, but because the sea varies so much around the continent's
coasts, it is separated into different seas.

### Kelp Sea

Kelp Sea is a fast-flowing and stormy sea, named after the giant kelp that forms long kelp rafts which
show how the sea currents run.

### The Breaks

In the south-west lies The Breaks. The sea here constantly produces waves of ten metres or more, which
shaped the steep drops of South Wall's coast.

### Athor's Bay

Athor's Bay, the calm southern bay, is named after the place where Athor's body was brought ashore on its
way to Geistburg, where he was buried. Before the invasion there were idyllic fishing villages along the
bay.

### Troubled Sea

The turbulent Troubled Sea was the death of many a sailor before the invasion. Solemn, which does not lie
so far from the mainland, was long undiscovered because of the difficulty of succeeding with exploratory
voyages here.

### The Tidals

A long stretch of calm sea with large tidal movements, from Amber down to the coastal regions of Ruby.

### Everfog

A mostly windless and fog-filled north-easterly body of sea.

### Windy Waters

The northernmost sea is violent and dangerous, like Troubled Sea.

## Forests

### The Wild

The easterly conifer forest is the largest on Firma. It takes in Amber and stretches westwards towards the
eastern side of the Spear Mountains and down towards Echterhof. It is a wild landscape of fierce rapids,
bogs, lakes and rocky knolls, which make it a hard-going natural labyrinth. In the southernmost parts of
The Wild the conifer forest is broken up by deciduous trees.

### Moss Forest

Moss Forest is a forest of enormous oaks with an undergrowth of tree ferns and thorn thickets. Before the
invasion humans avoided the forest, and after the invasion the Horde avoids it. Minor gods such as The Cat
God and Mother Longhand are associated with the forest, which is said to lie close to the Other Side.

## Islands

### Spray Islands

Spray Islands are a group of wind-beaten rocks and islands in the north that have never been inhabited.
They are said to be the home of The Winter Lady when she is not walking down into the northern lands with
frost and snow.

## Wetlands

### Troll Bogs

The whole of the middle tongue of land in the north is covered by a mire. As the name suggests it is
connected to trolls, and the Troll King is said to have ruled Firma from a cave system under the mire
before both elves and humans arrived.

### Thornmoor

The easternmost northern tongue of land is a little drier and is covered by a heath landscape with the
occasional lake and copse.

## Hives

### Xtchaarg

Xtchaarg lies west of Core and can be seen from the western part of the district. Like a gigantic anthill
pierced by winding tunnels, it reminds those living in Core of what lies outside the *warden.* Xtchaarg is
as large as a small mountain, and the Horde allows humans to visit if they are judged to be important. In
the middle of the hive lies Summer Village, a bizarre human enclave sparsely lit by a hole that leads out
of the hive. Summer Village comes across as a parody of an idyllic village from before the invasion,
created to let humans live in the middle of the Horde. The houses inside the village have white corner
boards and gardens with mushrooms instead of flowers, and the neat paths of shavings and woodchips are
lined with bioluminescent street lamps.

### Chguuth

More than twice as large as Xtchaarg, placed in the middle of Lost Lake's brutal heat haze, lies the
principal Hive on Firma. Its presence spreads a sour smell for several miles around it. Just like the
other hives it most resembles an anthill — if an anthill could be 800 metres high. Chguuth is the seat of
the queen of the Horde, The Queen, and enormous Skaah anchor at its top when they are not patrolling
Firma.

## Cities

### Salt Hill

Salt Hill is the northernmost city still standing on Firma. It was built to handle the lucrative whale
hunt, developed into a protection against northern pirates, and is notable for being an extension built on
top of elven ruins. The elves' massive stone walls have protected the inhabitants of Salt Hill from cold
and winds for generations.

### Black Harbour

The capital of Fiord is built in timber, timber that has darkened and given the city its name. The city is
a fish-smelling confusion of alleys and black houses, and even the manor houses here look shabby.

### Sammel

Sammel is a city built up of walled quarters, Rote. The step pyramid, the Ziggurat, thrones over the city
and is the seat of the Emir, who is in turn a puppet of the Grey. All around the city lies the oasis, with
canals drawn through the Rote.

### Lempak

Lempak is the religious centre of Iron and houses a large necropolis in the form of pyramids, crypts and
sarcophagus parks for former rulers.

### Gradenbosch

Gradenbosch is an unwalled city which, like most other cities in Core, is built up around agriculture. The
governing baron of the city protects his power with the help of the Academy.

### Academy

The mages of Core have their chief seat in the academy, governed by a mage with the title Magus. Academy
is a tall tower with settlements around it.

### Hirmer

Hirmer lies midway between Cathedral and Academy and is torn between the two powers. The conflicts have
left room for the Weasels to grow strong here, and the city is a city of whispers and hidden agendas.

### Erleng

Three noble families rule Erleng from their castles, and when a baron dies the power passes to the next
family according to a schedule that has held for centuries. Erleng is the largest breeder of horses in
Core, horses that are often sent to Regensburg for training.

### Geistburg

The city on the Mesa, Geistburg, has scattered building at the foot of the mesa and a tangle of marble
buildings on the top. Four cable cars and two stairs hewn into the rock connect the top with the bottom.
Upper Geistburg is rich, depraved and corrupt, with an emperor who exercises his imperial power in
Geistburg and no further than that. Intrigues are long and complex in upper Geistburg, with
representatives from the Church, the mages, the nobility and embassies from the other districts and
enclaves. Lower Geistburg supplies the upper. Its sprawling building mixes slum areas with middle class and
markets, along with the occasional castle belonging to the lesser nobility.

### Cathedral

The religious centre of Core lies in Cathedral, which despite its name has two cathedrals. The grandest
belongs to The Church of the Eternal God and the slightly less grand to The Church of the Eternal Spirit.
Around the cathedrals is a city and two parallel sets of religious warriors and monks in large forts and
monasteries, one for each church. The two religions live side by side, and conflicts sometimes arise
between them. The Church of the Eternal God has the advantage in numbers, but The Church of the Eternal
Spirit has a feared sect of assassins, the Unseen, which commands respect.

### Fynch

Fynch is an agricultural city in the south, best known for keeping a monastery for The Flagellants, the
most extreme sect in The Church of the Eternal God. Their influence has meant that harsh religious laws
have been introduced in the city, which has a large religious court to handle all the death sentences.

### Steinbrëk

The only mining operation in Core is centred on Steinbrëk, where mine shafts created over centuries bore
their way into the Spear Mountains. The people of Steinbrëk have kept themselves apart from the rest of
Core for generations, and you notice less of the conflicts and intrigues here. There is a quarter in the
city centred on an engineering guild of refugees from Furnace.

### Weder

Weder is a ruined city nowadays. It used to be the capital of Furnace. It was a wonder of architecture and
extended both upwards and downwards, with a dozen or so levels below ground.

### Farsee

Farsee lies to the north in Amber and is a larger city governed by a noble family. It is beset by local
warlords and forest barons, but its position on a hill by the coast makes it hard to take.

### Karmat

Karmat is the capital of Amber. It is a capital on the slide, and a city that has no power against the
forest barons, warlords and local mayors of lawless Amber. To survive, a smelting works has been built up
in the city, producing iron for weapons and armour, along with a military academy. The city itself is
governed by a council which, with bribes, corruption and cunning, tries to get money to flow into the city
without ending up in conflict with anyone. The idea that worked was the Tithe. Karmat receives young
people from all over Amber, equips them and trains them as warriors. At the end of their training a lot is
drawn, and a tenth of those trained are put together into squadrons and auctioned off to one of the
companies in the city, for onward sale to the rest of Firma via Iron. The constant stream of trained
soldiers keeps the conflicts going in Amber, and Karmat takes in gold to survive.

### Lenden

Lenden is a southern lawless city in Amber. It is constantly caught up in conflicts and its mayors do not
usually live a long time.

### Serf

Serf is the most fashionable city in Ruby. The city keeps both an opera house and a theatre filled with
gilded detail, and the seafront promenade is furnished with pavilions at regular intervals.

### Tanto

Tanto is perhaps the dullest city in Ruby and has therefore been given the council to house. Tanto is a
city of bureaucrats and trade, and although the marble palaces spread out, it does not have the same
flair as the others.

### Damza

Damza lies on a muddy slope and has become the centre of spas and beach life. The food in Damza is said to
be of the highest class on Firma, and rich tourists make their way here to take mud baths, eat and drink.

## The Underworld

The underworld consists of five main parts: man-made tunnels leading between the districts; natural cave
systems created over the millennia; abandoned elven ruins from the elves' underground activities; refuges
for the monsters that have been hunted down from Firma by the Horde; and the old underworld and the
successors to the Queen of the Dark.

## Tunnels

### The West Road

The West Road is a broad, high tunnel with few connections to other systems, running from Black Harbour in
Fiord to Erleng in Core. Fish transport dominates the trade. Three towns have been built up along the road
to serve travellers from Fiord to Core: Underswamp, Drowntown and The Pillars. The towns are damp, lively
places with bars, storage space for goods and inns. Outside the towns there are also a handful of smaller
inns for food and sleep.

### The South Road

The South Road runs the whole way from Tanto in Ruby to Fynch in Core. The journey from Core to the
underground city of Sol is served by a luxurious train, the Velvet Line, if you do not want to go on foot
or by horse. From Sol to Ruby the tunnels are unsuitable for a train and are served instead by
stagecoaches, if someone does not want to make the stretch under their own power. The tunnels are mainly
safe, with the exception of a couple of areas where adjoining tunnels have connections. At those places
there are military postings that can escort travellers for payment. Sol is a den of sin, a gaudy, lavishly
appointed pleasure city inside an extensive cave chamber.

### The East Road

The East Road goes from Sammel in Iron to Gradenbosch in Core. East Road is an unsafe tunnel; millennia of
mining have perforated parts of the tunnel like a Swiss cheese. Travellers on the train The Night Express
rarely meet with anything, but ordinary travellers disappear constantly into the dark. There are four
smaller forts along The East Road. They function as fortified inns and storage places.

### The North-East Road

The North-East Road tied Weder in Furnace together with Erleng in Core, and the creation of the tunnel was
a technological wonder, with twin railway lines and three smaller towns from Furnace to Core: Goldenburg
(a trading town where freight was assembled and could be bought by visitors), Strema (a town which,
besides acting as a resting place for travellers, also ran mining of its own) and Meredi (the town nearest
Core, which used the great chamber it lay in to farm mushrooms). The North-East Road was closed when
Furnace fell. In Erleng the entrance is bricked up with a vault door fitted with protective rituals.

### Coast Road

Coast Road is a narrow walking tunnel that leads between Kelp Bay and Farsight in Triple Enclave. The
tunnel is no larger than will let two people walk side by side in it. Caravans of porters delivering loads
to and from Kelp Bay often cause queues in the tunnel. An inn blasted out midway along the tunnel, The
Lost Inn, tends to gather the strangest of people in the one place.

### Steel Road

Steel Road leads from HighFall in The Triple Enclave to Gradenbosch in Core. It is a tunnel a little wider
than Coast Road; it can handle two riders abreast. Steel Road is safe against monsters, and there are few
side tunnels, even if Steel Road itself forks in places and later reunites. Since the tunnel is also how
the drug *dreamwort* is delivered into Core, there are risks of running into criminals here. There are no
cities in Steel Road, but there are a number of overnight places, simple inns with beer and beds.

### The Cracked Road

The Cracked Road leads from Regensburg to an opening between Erleng and Steinbrëk in Core. At the tunnel's
opening in Core there is a fort that is always ready for surprises. The Cracked Road is not a safe tunnel,
but it is an important one, since the military traffic between Core and Regensburg is heavy. The tunnels
are mostly natural cave systems in the limestone that dominates the mountains south-east of Core. Unknown
tunnels join along the whole length of the way, tunnels that may lead to the Queen of the Dark's domains,
to monster caves, or to areas that have collapsed and let the Horde in. A good many of the openings have
been sealed, but in limestone things are seldom sealed for ever. A large open chamber houses the
underground lake The Deep, which is connected to three underground rivers. The Deep has to be crossed by
boat, and as a result two towns have been built up on its shore: From, which is nearest Core, and To,
which is nearest Regensburg. The towns are walled, with a harbour each that lets rowers take ships between
the two. There are no inns outside the towns, so travellers have to pitch tents in one of the many
chambers along the way.

## The Third Side

From Saag et Haar a road runs through a portal across the Third Side to Sammel in Iron, where a
corresponding portal stands. For every journey a human sacrifice has to be made to the demons on the Third
Side. The road is a dusty road through desert with its borders marked by poles topped with skulls. As long
as the travellers do not go outside the markings and have made their sacrifice they go safely, but
sandstorms are common and it happens that people get lost and disappear.
