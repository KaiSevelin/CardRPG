# Player's Handbook

Everything you need to play a character. Nothing here is for the GM, and nothing here explains why
a rule is the way it is. You never need that at the table.

**The short version.** You have a deck of cards that is both your abilities and your stamina. Each
turn you play two of them. When you attack, you draw one card of luck. When you run out of cards,
you are exhausted, not because you were hurt, but because you have been fighting.

---

## The world

**The year is 1034, and humanity lives behind walls it pays for in blood.**

### The Sides

Firma is one of several worlds laid over the same ground. The mountains and lakes of one stand in the
same places on the next; what differs is who lives there and what they have done to it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **The First Side** | Firma. The normal world, and the one you are standing on. |
| **The Second Side** | Where the elves went. The same geography, smeared with glamour that makes everything look more beautiful than it is. |
| **The Third Side** | Where the demons went after Athor's armies beat them. They like to destroy things, so it is in worse condition: lava dug up out of the ground, jagged black mountains raised where there were none. |
| **The Fourth Side** | The land of the dead. The accounts contradict each other so completely that nobody can say whether it is a paradise, a hell or a shadow of here. Only the dead are let in. |
| **A Fifth** | Rumoured, along with a Sixth and a Seventh. Nobody has proved any of them. |

### How it came to this

Humans took the continent slowly. The elves withdrew to the Second Side and the demons to the Third.
Monsters and nature spirits were driven into remote places, and the trolls, the dragons and the minor
gods went down into their long sleep.

The old reckoning starts at the year 0, when the warrior-king **Athor** pushed his empire to the coast
in every direction. It held for a hundred years and then cracked, and for five hundred years after
that the pieces fought over where the lines between them ran. Regions drifted apart. The assassins
founded their state in the oasis of the Hyena Desert, the blood mages established themselves on the
island of Hyrlock, and the Strigoi princes organised to grow their undead power. In the old year 674
the counting started again at 0.

**Airships came out of the sky in that first year, and nobody has ever learned from where.** Another
world, or a land across the sea. What came out of them is called the **Horde**: insect-like, of one
shared mind, specialised into roles the way ants are. Everyone in it always knew what everyone else
was doing. Humanity was divided and did not.

The war has lasted a thousand years. Humans won battles and lost cities, and the direction never
changed. The Strigoi princes made their own pact with the Horde and kept their neutrality.

**In year 87, with everything nearly lost, humanity made a pact with the blood mages of Hyrlock.** Six
great territories, the **districts**, were ringed with a protective ritual powered by thousands of
human sacrifices. The isolated **enclaves** were each given blood mages of their own to renew smaller
barriers every year. The Horde had the world, but what was inside the rings was safe.

What followed was attrition. The Horde strangles the roads between the enclaves and hunts the caravans
that leave the barriers; humanity raids the **hives**. Tunnels were dug so that one safe place could
reach another, and the network grew until parts of it were forgotten. Monsters took sections of it,
landslides let the Horde into others, dissidents built their own communities out in the dark, and here
and there the digging broke into elven ruins older than anyone's records.

**The sacrifices were never popular.** In 879 a mob in the Fifth District beat its blood mages to
death. A month later the Horde came through the gap, and the district fell.

That is the arithmetic you live in. Five districts and some twenty enclaves, a thousand years of it,
and rumour this year says the Horde is close to getting through a ring that still holds.

### The five districts that are left

**The First.** Inland, on the plain inside the Spear Mountains. It protects **Geistburg**, Athor's old
capital, eight satellite cities and the farmland between them, and the realm is still called Denaria.
Geistburg is a melting pot of complicated politics, a dozen corrupt noble houses and a powerless man
who is still titled emperor. The mages' academy is here and so is the Church of the Eternal God, which
means hypocrisy and religious persecution walk together. The city stands on five mesas with the rich
on top and everyone else in the valleys between, and cable cars run from height to height.

**The Second.** The west coast and its islands, with six enormous lighthouses marking where the ring
runs. The capital is **Svarthamn**, Black Harbour, and the region used to belong to the Salt Barons,
an association of fishing magnates, smugglers and pirates. The old faith of the Five is strong here and
the Church of the Eternal God is considered blasphemy. It lands most of the continent's fish. The
**West Road**, a broad tunnel with underground cities along it, ties it to the First.

**The Third.** Rolling hills in the south-east, warm, famous for wine and fruit, where the rich once
built winter palaces. The coast feeds it fish and the farmland the rest, but wine is what it is known
for. The **South Road** runs to the First District, and the underground city of **Sol** is where the
rich of the First go to enjoy themselves, with a reputation that everything is permitted there.
Religion and politics are relaxed. A voted council governs from **Tanto**, at the centre of five
coastal cities, none of which dominates the others.

**The Fourth.** The northern half of the Hyena Desert and the mineral-rich Mouse Mountains above it.
**Sammel**, in the oasis, is the only large city and nearly matches Geistburg. The Grey and his
siblings are worshipped here. Emirs rule, and they have cut Sammel into sealed quarters called
**Rote**; the old assassin state holds the central one, around the Ziggurat, the tallest building in
the region. Its embassies sell services under the table in every district but the Sixth. The **East
Road** connects it to the First and is the least safe of the roads, because the mining lets monsters
and the Horde break in constantly. The **Night Express** runs it anyway, hauling freight and
passengers through tunnels black with its own coal smoke.

**The Sixth.** The far east, the second largest district and the one that was never entirely tamed.
Dragons turn up. Trolls live in the forests and the mountains. Dense wet conifer forest covers most of
it, broken by lakes, bogs and hills. Timber was the export until the Fifth fell and took the routes
with it, and now the capital **Karmat** is in decline and mercenaries are the only thing it sells: a
squadron of hard soldiers fights its way through the wilderness to the Fourth District once a year.
The forest barons are broken but not gone, every town runs itself, and the mayor's chair changes hands
bloodily and often. The war god **Vax** has the most followers, and nobody minds what you believe.

**The Fifth is gone.** It was the centre of smithing and manufacture, with an alchemical academy and a
centre of magical research called the Rune Temple in the labyrinth city of **Weder**, once the third
largest city on the continent. Technology and magic mattered there and religion did not. The Fifth
built the Fourth's trains, the Second's sea defences and the First's cable cars. Its refugees founded
engineering guilds in every surviving district and kept their language, **Valgar**, and their lack of
religion, which still causes trouble in the First District.

### The enclaves

Some twenty of them hold out on small territories with the Horde all around. These are the ones
everybody has heard of.

**Hyrlock.** The island of the blood mages, feared or revered depending on who you ask, a week's
sailing from the abandoned ports west of the First District. A caste society under the city of
**Manderklippan**: mages at the top, then administrators and merchants, then workers, and beneath them
the thralls, who are bred to be spent in sacrifice. Power radiates from the **Red Tower**, fifty metres
of it at the cliff's summit. The faith is of the blood goddess **Parfebe**. Every other district and
enclave pays Hyrlock an annual tribute in goods and thralls to keep the wards up, and everyone calls
it the **blood tax**.

**Tångviken.** Kelp Cove was a nothing port before year 0 and is now the only one left on the west
coast, which makes it the single way in to Hyrlock and the blood tax. Three shipping companies run the
voyages in an unstable cartel, and the pay for surviving one draws adventurers. Fog moves through
cobbled alleys lit blue for bars, red for brothels and green for business best not described. The
smell is rotting bodies, spilt beer and cheap perfume, under the fermenting kelp.

**Solemn.** A white limestone island south of the Third District, home of the witches, a sisterhood
with a magic they keep to themselves. They follow the **Primal Mother**, a fertility goddess, and they
are one of the few enclaves that needs no blood magic: their own ward holds, and nobody outside knows
how. It is guessed to have something to do with how thin the island's borders are to the Second and
Third Sides. Their city **Sonebra** sits walled at the top, all towers and spires.

**Regensburg.** The militaristic realm north-east of the First District, and the most important inland
enclave. Three concentric walls ten metres high divide the city into rings, each ring subdivided again,
all of it designed to make an invasion take as long as possible. A Marshal of the Realm sits at the top
of a strict hierarchy. It hires its cavalry out to other districts in exchange for the supplies it
cannot grow. The **Cracked Road** reaches the First District through cave-riddled limestone and is not
safe; halfway along it a large underground lake has two harbour villages on it, named From and To.

**Vargtoppen.** Wolf Peak, a religious centre of the faith of **the Fire**, on a dormant volcano north
of the Sixth District, never once taken. The roads to the summit are easy to hold and the mountain
feeds itself inside and out. It also has the best gunpowder and firearms on the continent, because the
ingredients are all in the mountain: bat guano that never runs out, sulphur from the volcanism, and
charcoal from fast trees that love the soil. The priesthood has its own language, **Perga**, its own
dress, and tattoos over every inch. They are rarely seen outside.

**Echterhof.** A large neutral city in the middle of Horde territory, ruled by Strigoi princes. Strigoi
live off humans, so a large human population lives there caught between the vampires and the Horde. A
human mayor is appointed to keep order and choose who is eaten, usually criminals, and when criminals
run short it is done by lot, by grudge or by whim. The city welcomes visitors, because the more people
there are the less likely you are the one chosen. The **Guest Law** guarantees that a guest will not be
killed, and guarantees that a guest who breaks any of its many clauses will be. It also offers land and
the prospect of a title, to keep people coming. They worship the First Vampire.

**Velderon.** The elves' embassy on Firma, east of the Second District: an ancient elven ruin with one
restored corner. The elves are neutral and live on the Second Side; their own magic keeps the Horde
out, and the embassy exists to watch the Horde and make sure it never starts looking upward. You do
not go in without an invitation.

**Saag et Haar.** What is left of Seetch, the realm of the necromancer kings, south of the Hyena
Desert. The greatest of those kings, **Fahlas**, still rules it after four hundred years and has become
a minor god. It depends on blood mages now like everywhere else, though raising the dead keeps the
number of sacrifices lower. The government is a tyranny, the religion is the death goddess **Aecht**,
and the tongue is **Veche**. A trade road runs to Sammel through the Third Side: portals at both ends
and a demon-permitted road between them, paid for in human sacrifice. The city buys slaves in Sammel
and pays in silver, gold and gemstones out of its own mines.

**The Triple Enclave.** Three frontier towns north of the Fourth District, each walled around a
castle and a duke, sharing one group of blood mages and one safe tunnel, the **Steel Road**.
**Farsight** is on a steppe and the Church of the Eternal God holds it absolutely, its duchess also a
cardinal, religion behind every law. **Bogtown** sits on dry ground in a swamp and exports
**dreamwort**, whose dried seed is a powerful drug; its duke is powerless and the **Dream Guild**, a
criminal organisation with branches in every district, holds the real power. They are rumoured to have
a safe-passage treaty with the Horde. **Highfall** is built into a mountain with a waterfall coming
over its walls, and tries to run things the old way: it lends the Dream Guild troops, and takes money
and sacrificial slaves from the Fourth District in exchange.

### The hives

Mostly they look like gigantic anthills of earth and organic matter, winding inside into tunnels and
chambers for larvae and for each of the Horde's roles. **Twelve are known.**

**Xtchaargh** is the only hive that will receive human envoys. It has a human quarter in the middle of
its tunnels, with ordinary houses and squares, called the **Summer Village**, where humans who serve
the Horde live and train spies to pull enclaves and districts apart. It is said that a delegation from
the Fifth District went there shortly before its blood mages were killed.

**Chguuth** is the main hive, in the south-east, twice the height of any other and reinforced with the
chitin of the dead. The enormous flying **Skaah** that were mistaken for airships all set out from
here. The Queen sits here.

**The underworld** is not empty either. Demons, minor gods and monsters pushed off the surface have
strongholds down there. The Queen of the Dark and the Troll King have always been there and do not
care for visitors. Other stretches are infested with the Horde.

### What people believe

**The Church of the Eternal God.** *The Eternal God is the only true god, and the emperor his only true
representative on Firma.* Three tenets: **purity** of soul and body, which means avoiding strong
feeling and poisons and keeping yourself trained, and holds that all other gods are impure;
**obedience**, because what the god, the emperor and his priests say is law and questioning it is
blasphemy; and **solidarity**, helping your brothers and sisters in faith and never charging them for
it. Its chief saint is Athor, who spread it across Firma, alongside lesser saints like the dragonslayer
Vesper, the healer Selma and the martyr Bullius, local saints, and every emperor who has ever lived.
Inside it: **the Black Circle**, a religious secret police; **the Holy Shield**, its order of knights;
**the White Brothers** and **the Black Sisters**, its largest orders of monks and nuns; and **the
Flagellants**, the most extreme and frequently violent.

**The Church of the Eternal Spirit.** A sister church holding that the Eternal God's spirit drifts
through all the air of Firma. Same tenets, same saints, plus its founder **Vegnar**, who was Athor's
brother. The two churches are rivals who tolerate each other, with accusations of blasphemy surfacing
now and then. Inside it: **God's Spirit** as its secret police, **the Holy Sword** as its knights,
**the Devoted** as its monks and nuns, and **the Unseen**, an order of assassins.

**The Five.** The largest religion before Athor, known all along the coasts. **Sessyfyn** the winged
fish, goddess of fishing and seafaring. **Valtor** the two-pointed spear, her brother and husband, god
of strength and battle. **Fagar** the mask of fish scales, their child, the trickster. **Vulpet** the
shark, daughter of Fagar and Sessyfyn, driven out for being so ugly. **Brendan** the conch horn, son of
Fagar and Valtor, who stands for the beauty that can come out of ugly things. Its **Mummers** walk
about in animal masks singing and pressing drink on people; its **Berserkers** work themselves up with
drums and dancing before a battle.

**The Grey.** Three siblings and three books. **The Grey** embodies assassination, and the Grey Book is
on avoiding discovery and killing quietly. **The Purple** brews poison, and the Purple Book is recipes.
**The Yellow** stands for lies, deceit and betrayal, and the Yellow Book is misinformation and
disguise. The three sects match the three books.

**Local gods.** **Vax**, war, in the Sixth District. **Parfebe**, blood, on Hyrlock. **The Primal
Mother**, fertility, on Solemn. **The Fire**, fire, at Vargtoppen. **The First**, vampirism, in
Echterhof. **Aecht**, death, in Saag et Haar.

**The minor gods, who demonstrably exist.** Perhaps a hundred powerful nature spirits and beings,
forced into remote places and underground when Athor took the continent. They grant no miracles, and
they are what the school of Spiritualism is built on. Among the known: the Winter Lady, the Queen of
the Dark, the Troll King, Baal Fagor, the Land Jelly, the Cat God, Fahlas, Mother Longhand. Cults
gather round them: **the Silent** follow the Winter Lady, **the Soot Club** try to summon Baal Fagor,
and **the Dancers** use drugs to speak with the Land Jelly.

### What a character can become

Your Way is the direction you grow in, and each one ends in specialists the world has names for.

**Way of Mind** runs to ritual magic, priest magic, and the schools of direct magic: blood,
necromancy, elemental, poison, glamour, strength, asceticism, shadow, spiritualism and household
magic. It also covers the trades that are not magic at all: brewers, smiths, jewellers and orators.

**Way of Strength** runs to the sword, the two-hander, the polearm, the dagger and the empty hand; to
formation fighting, crossbows, artillery and muskets; to protection, blunt weapons, gladiators,
berserkers, cavalry and sappers.

**Way of Shade** runs to thieves, assassins, spies, trapmakers and fences; to bards, politicians,
archers, pistoliers and duellists; to acrobats and half-mages.

**Way of Faith** runs to the churches and the gods above, and swearing to one closes the others.

## 1. What you have

Six stacks of cards, a seventh if you cast, and one number. Everything you own is in one of them.

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Action deck** | 12–20 cards. What your character can *do* in a fight, and how long they can keep doing it. |
| **Skill deck** | About 15 cards. What your character can *attempt* outside a fight. |
| **Fate deck** | 20 cards. Your luck, and nobody else's. |
| **Items** | Everything you own. Only **equipped** items can be used. |
| **Features** | Permanent traits: *Strong*, *Tough*, *Good Aim*. |
| **Conditions** | What is currently on you, each with a number counting down. |
| **Spells** | Your book, if you have one. The count is what you have **prepared**. |
| **Health** | At 0 you are down. |

### Decks you draw from, and decks you keep

Every one of those stacks is a deck of cards, printed the same size, held the same way. What tells
them apart is what happens to the deck.

- **Decks you draw from.** Action, skill and fate. These are shuffled, drawn from, played and
  discarded, and you do not get to choose what comes up.
- **Decks you keep.** Items, features, conditions, and your spellbook. Nothing in them is shuffled
  and nothing is drawn. You open the stack and look through it, because a stack is simply how you
  hold a lot of cards at a table.

A card you keep can still be **spent**. A one-use item is gone once you use it. What it never does
is go into a deck you draw from.

Watch for the two that cross over. **Wounds** and **Taint** are not kept. They are shuffled into the
decks you draw from, and that is the whole of what makes them frightening.

## 2. Your action cards

```
┌───────────────────────────┐
│ HEAVY SWING               │
│ ▲ Primary:   Strike +1    │
│ ▼ Secondary: Move 1       │
│ ⚡ Reaction:  Parry        │
└───────────────────────────┘
```

- **Primary**, the strong half. Your main action.
- **Secondary**, the weak half. Your second action.
- **Reaction**, what the card does on somebody else's turn.

**You can never use both halves of the same card.** A turn is one card's primary and a *different*
card's secondary.

Any card whose reaction cannot fire is still a **Brace: reduce incoming damage by 1**. That is
deliberately too little to be worth spending a good card on. Its purpose is that no card in your
hand is ever completely dead, and it matters exactly once, the hit that would have killed you, by
one.

### The six verbs

| Verb | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Strike** ⚔ | Attack with an equipped weapon. |
| **Cast** ✦ | Cast a prepared spell. |
| **Defend** 🛡 | Gain Block. |
| **Use** | Use an equipped item. |
| **Move** 👣 | Move that many squares. |
| **Ready** ⇄ | Equip or swap one item. |

## 3. A round

1. Everyone draws one fate card for initiative, once per fight. **Highest acts first** (Miss is
   lowest, Critical highest), and ties are redrawn until the order settles. The order then holds for
   the whole fight.
2. The GM draws one intent card per kind of enemy, face down.
3. Each character takes a turn in order.

### Your turn

- Refill your hand to **5 cards**.
- Play **one card for its primary** and **a different card for its secondary**, in either order.
- Both go to your discard pile.
- The rest of your hand stays in front of you, ready to react.

Two attacks in one turn are legal if they come from two different cards, a Strike primary plus a
card that has Strike on its secondary. Those cards are rare, and they are earned by levelling.

---

## 4. Reactions

**Reactions resolve by themselves. You are not asked.** When a trigger happens, the best reaction
your hand can offer fires, that card goes to your discard, and play continues.

There are two triggers:

| Trigger | What fires on it |
|---|---|
| **You are hit** | Parry, Block, Brace, Use |
| **An enemy ends its move next to you** | Sidestep, Counter |

A reaction fires when the hit is **serious**, when it would deal a Wound (5 or more past armour) or
drop you to 0. A scratch is a scratch, and a hand spent on scratches is a hand you do not have when
the real blow lands.

Three things are always true:

- **A reaction never draws fate.** A Parry is your weapon's Defend value, a Brace is 1, a Counter is
  your weapon's Strike value. Flat, every time.
- **A reaction's damage is not itself a trigger.** A Counter hits, and nothing answers it.
- **Reacting is never free.** Every card you react with is one more card off your deck at the next
  refill, and that brings exhaustion closer.

### Turning a card sideways

**A card turned sideways will not react.** That is the whole of the decision, and you take it in
advance, at leisure, not in the half-second after the dice would have landed in another game.

## 5. Resolving an action

Always in this order:

1. **Base value**, the item's line for the verb. A Longsword's Strike is 3.
2. **Card modifier**, the bonus printed on the action card. *Strike +1.*
3. **Features**, permanent modifiers. *Strong: +1 on Strike with Heavy items.*
4. **Conditions**, temporary modifiers on you or on the target.
5. **Fate**, draw one card from your fate deck and apply it.
6. **Their defences**, subtract **armour**, then **Block**. Never below 0.

**Fate is drawn for damage and healing only.** Move, Ready and Defend never draw fate.

**One draw per action, not per target.** An attack that hits several creatures draws once and
applies the same answer to all of them.

### Bonuses do not stack from the same kind of source

Every card that grants a numeric bonus belongs to one of seven groups: **Body, Training, Tool,
Make, Power, Station, Wonder.** One action takes **at most one bonus from each group**. Muscle and a
tool stack; two kinds of muscle do not.

Whatever supplies the *base* value is exempt, so a weapon and a feature can never lock each other
out.

## 6. The fate deck

Twenty cards, yours alone.

| Card | Count | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Miss** | 1 | The action does nothing. Shuffle the deck afterwards. |
| −2 | 1 | |
| −1 | 4 | |
| +0 | 8 | |
| +1 | 4 | |
| +2 | 1 | |
| **Critical** | 1 | On an attack, draw from the critical deck. On anything else, double the value. Shuffle afterwards. |

**Advantage** means draw two and take the better; **disadvantage**, draw two and take the worse. Two
sources of advantage still give one.

**Your deck thins as you use it.** A fate deck that has already spent its good cards is a different
deck from a fresh one, and you can see that coming. It reshuffles on a Miss or a Critical.

### The critical deck

A Critical on an attack draws one card from the critical deck: twelve cards, shared by the whole
table, read out face up.

| Face | Copies | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Through and Through** | 4 | Double the damage. |
| **Find the Gap** | 2 | **Exposed**, their armour counts 0 until the end of your next turn. Not this hit, and not doubled. |
| **Off the Feet** | 2 | **Off-Balance**, their Block counts 0 until the end of your next turn, and it is an opening. Not this hit, and not doubled. |
| **Driven Down** | 1 | **Halted**, their next turn does not happen. Not doubled. |
| **Stove In** | 1 | Their armour is one point worse until it is repaired. Permanent. Not doubled. |
| **Turn the Edge** | 1 | Their weapon strikes for one less until it is repaired. Permanent. Not doubled. |
| **Both** | 1 | Draw two others and take both. |

A Critical always gets **at least one point** through, however well armoured the target is.

## 7. Armour, Block and getting past them

- **Armour** is worn. It subtracts from every hit.
- **Block** is earned by a Defend, and subtracts after armour. How long it lasts depends on how you
  played it:

| Played as | Block lasts |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Against **every** attack until the start of your next turn |
| **Secondary** | Against the **next** attack only |
| **Reaction** | Against the attack that triggered it |

Blocks from different sources do not stack. **Use the highest**, and that source's duration is the
one that applies, so a Defend secondary played behind a Defend primary never shortens the stance you
already had.

Some attacks go **through** armour (*ignores N armour*, or all of it) and some go **round** Block.
They are different things and a card says which: a bodkin finds a gap in plate, and a shield raised
in answer does not care.

**Damaged gear.** Corrode takes points off armour; Blunt takes points off what a weapon strikes for.
Both are permanent until repaired, both stop at 0, and **neither destroys anything**. Armour at 0
is still armour and still repairable. Repair is an action: a **Lore check** with the right tools. A
Critical puts back 2, a Success 1, a Partial nothing and the time is gone.

## 8. Conditions

A condition is a token with a number, its duration. It remembers who created it, and it runs out at
the **end of that creature's next turn**. So a condition you inflict lasts through your own next
turn, and you can use it.

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Dazed** | You can't play a secondary. |
| **Slowed** | All Move values halved, rounding down. |
| **Stunned** | You can't play reactions. |
| **Halted** | Your turn does not happen. |
| **Prone** | Melee Strikes against you have advantage. Standing up costs Move 1. |
| **Grappled** | You can't Move. |
| **Silenced** | You can't Cast. |
| **Disarmed** | You can't Ready, or Strike with the disarmed item. |
| **Heckled** | You draw fate with disadvantage. |
| **Exposed** | Your armour counts 0. Block is untouched. |
| **Off-Balance** | Your Block counts 0, and it is an opening. |
| **Burning N** | N damage at the start of your turn, past armour and Block. Cancels with Wet and Frozen. |
| **Frozen N** | Your Move values are reduced by N. Cancels with Burning. |
| **Poisoned N** | You are Dazed, and take N damage at the start of your turn. N may be 0. |
| **Invisible** | Enemies can't target you. Ends if you Strike or Cast at one. |
| **Compelled** | You must spend your primary attacking whoever compelled you. |
| **Burdened / Overloaded** | See *Carrying*. |
| **Unconscious** | At 0 Health. No turns, no reactions, Prone. |

**Shake Off** spends a condition's duration rather than waiting for it. **Openings** (Wet,
Off-Balance, Marked and Judged) do nothing by themselves; they exist for other cards to key off, and
they cannot be shaken off.

## 9. Checks, outside a fight

Draw a hand of **4** skill cards. Each shows one **approach** and a value of 1–3.

| Approach | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Might** ✊ | Climbing, breaking, lifting, intimidating, enduring |
| **Agility** 🌀 | Balance, acrobatics, quick hands, riding, dodging traps |
| **Shadow** 🌑 | Stealth, deception, disguise, pickpocketing, lockpicking |
| **Wits** 👁 | Noticing, searching, reading people, tracking, survival |
| **Lore** 📜 | Knowledge, arcana, religion, history, healing |
| **Presence** 👑 | Persuading, leading, performing, bargaining, calming |

Every deck contains all six, so anyone can try anything. Your Way and background decide which ones
you hold more of, and better.

**To make a check:** the GM sets a difficulty. You play a card of the approach you are using, add
your bonuses, and draw fate.

| Difficulty | Number |
|---|---|
| Easy | 3 |
| Normal | 5 |
| Hard | 7 |
| Heroic | 9 |

| Result | When | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | Critical drawn | You do it, and something extra goes your way. |
| **Success** | Total ≥ difficulty | You do it. |
| **Partial** | 1–4 below | You do it, **but** it costs something. |
| **Fail** | 5 or more below, or a Miss | You don't, **and** the situation changes. |

A Critical comes off the card, so a Critical that still falls short is a success with a bonus, not a
near miss. A Miss is a Fail however high the total was.

**Tools** help the approach they name. An **untrained** user gets at most +1 from a tool however good
it is; training is what unlocks the rest, and training comes from levelling.

## 10. Running out

Your action deck is your stamina. You do not run out of spells. You run out of deck.

- **Exhausted**: if you cannot refill to at least **2 cards** because your draw pile is empty, you
  are Exhausted. You may play only **one** card a turn, and you cannot react.
- **Catch Breath**: spend your whole turn, no cards played, to shuffle your discard and move **2
  cards at random** back into your draw pile. You need 2 in the discard or you cannot do it, and you
  do not choose which.

A breath buys you exactly one turn that is not Exhausted. It is a way to keep playing, never a way
to get back on top. The discard always comes back, so what a breath costs is the turn.

### Wounds

**You gain 1 Wound when** you take **5 or more damage from a single hit** (after armour), or when you
drop to **0 Health**.

A Wound is a card. It goes into your action discard and comes back around like any other. When you
draw one it takes a slot in your hand, cannot be played, not even for Brace, and is discarded at
the end of your turn. Wounds stay between fights.

Removing one: a long rest takes off 1; a **Lore check (Normal 5)** with a Healer's Kit takes off 1,
once per patient per day; some magic says so.

### 0 Health

At 0 you are **Unconscious**: you fall Prone, take no turns, cannot react, and gain a Wound. You are
dying until somebody treats you.

## 11. Rests

Both need a safe place.

| | Short rest | Long rest |
|---|---|---|
| **Takes** | About an hour | A night |
| **Limit** | Costs a rest token | Once a day |
| **Action deck** | Spent cards return | Spent cards return |
| **Skill deck** | Discard and Spent shuffle back in | Same |
| **Rest tokens** | | Regain all |
| **Health** | Heal 1 | Heal 5 |
| **Wounds** | | Remove 1 |

You start with **one rest token**. More come from levelling.

**After a fight**, shuffle your hand and discard back into your action deck. **Spent cards stay
out.** Several fights without rest wear you down.

## 12. Carrying and equipping

**Only equipped items can be used.** A **Ready** buys one equip or one swap; because an item only
comes off by being replaced, those are the same transaction.

Your carrying limit is **40 kg**, counting everything.

| Load | | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Up to the limit | **Unburdened** | no penalty |
| 41–60 kg | **Burdened** | Slowed, and disadvantage on Agility and Shadow checks |
| 61–80 kg | **Overloaded** | Burdened, **and 2 random cards are Spent** |
| 81 kg + | | You cannot pick it up. Make two trips. |

Neither state has a duration, so they last until you drop the weight, and dropping things is never
an action.

## 13. Levelling up

**The GM says when.** There is no experience to count.

A level-up is a step on one shared graph. Your **frontier** is every node that a node you already
own points at, and you take one of them. Taking it spends the level and puts that node's own
successors on your frontier.

**The node is the reward.** Nothing is offered and nothing is passed over. Taking the node *is*
taking the reward. What a node gives is one of:

| Reward | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Action card** | A card added to your deck, the main currency of growing |
| **Training** | A tool type at its full bonus instead of +1, and the tool is issued with it |
| **Item** | A card, handed over |

A **specialization node** gives you a **title** as well as its reward, what people call you, and
the Tactic that comes with it. You only ever have your deepest title's Tactic.

The decision is **a direction, not a menu**. What you give up is the other directions you could have
gone this level, and a branch you walk past is usually still there next time.

**Except when it is not.** Some nodes belong to an exclusive group: taking one **permanently**
disables every other node in that group, and everything reachable only through them. Swearing to a
god is the clearest case. You will be told before it happens.
