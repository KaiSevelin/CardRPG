# Player's Handbook

Everything you need to play a character. Nothing here is for the GM, and nothing here explains why
a rule is the way it is. You never need that at the table.

**The short version.** You have a deck of cards that is your abilities, and a **stamina** that is
how many of them you can play. Each turn you play two. When you attack, you draw one card of luck.
Your deck comes round again for nothing; what runs out is the stamina, so you tire not because you
were hurt, but because you have been fighting.

---

## 1. The world

**The year is 1034, and humanity lives behind walls it pays for in blood.**

### The Sides

Firma is one of several worlds laid over the same ground. The mountains and lakes of one stand in the
same places on the next; what differs is who lives there and what they have done to it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **The First Side** | Firma. The normal world, and the one you are standing on. |
| **The Second Side** | Where the elves went. The same geography, smeared with glamour that makes everything look more beautiful than it is. |
| **The Third Side** | Where the demons went after Athor's armies beat them. They like to destroy things, so it is in worse condition: lava dug up out of the ground, jagged black mountains raised where there were none. |
| **The Fourth Side** | The land of the dead. The accounts contradict each other so completely that nobody can say whether it is a paradise, a hell or a shadow of here. Only the dead are let in. |
| **A Fifth** | Rumoured, along with a Sixth and a Seventh. Nobody has proved any of them. |

### How it came to this

Humans took the continent slowly. The elves withdrew to the Second Side and the demons to the Third.
Monsters and nature spirits were driven into remote places, and the trolls, the dragons and the minor
gods went down into their long sleep.

The old reckoning starts at the year 0, when the warrior-king **Athor** pushed his empire to the coast
in every direction. It held for a hundred years and then cracked, and for five hundred years after
that the pieces fought over where the lines between them ran. Regions drifted apart. The assassins
founded their state in the oasis of the Hyena Desert, the blood mages established themselves on the
island of Hyrlock, and the Strigoi princes organised to grow their undead power. In the old year 674
the counting started again at 0.

**Airships came out of the sky in that first year, and nobody has ever learned from where.** Another
world, or a land across the sea. What came out of them is called the **Horde**: insect-like, of one
shared mind, specialised into roles the way ants are. Everyone in it always knew what everyone else
was doing. Humanity was divided and did not.

The war has lasted a thousand years. Humans won battles and lost cities, and the direction never
changed. The Strigoi princes made their own pact with the Horde and kept their neutrality.

**In year 87, with everything nearly lost, humanity made a pact with the blood mages of Hyrlock.** Six
great territories, the **districts**, were ringed with a protective ritual powered by thousands of
human sacrifices. The isolated **enclaves** were each given blood mages of their own to renew smaller
barriers every year. The Horde had the world, but what was inside the rings was safe.

What followed was attrition. The Horde strangles the roads between the enclaves and hunts the caravans
that leave the barriers; humanity raids the **hives**. Tunnels were dug so that one safe place could
reach another, and the network grew until parts of it were forgotten. Monsters took sections of it,
landslides let the Horde into others, dissidents built their own communities out in the dark, and here
and there the digging broke into elven ruins older than anyone's records.

**The sacrifices were never popular.** In 879 a mob in the Furnace District beat its blood mages to
death. A month later the Horde came through the gap, and the district fell.

That is the arithmetic you live in. Five districts and some twenty enclaves, a thousand years of it,
and rumour this year says the Horde is close to getting through a ring that still holds.

### The shape of it

<!-- map: Firma Annotated -->

One continent, and you can hold the whole of it in your head.

**The middle is a plain**, ringed by the **Spear Mountains**, and the oldest of the safe places sits
inside them. **West** is coast and islands, cold and wet, and that is where the fishing is.
**South-east** is rolling hills, warm, and that is where the wine is. **South** is the **Hyena
Desert**, with the mineral-rich **Mouse Mountains** standing over its northern edge. **East**, and
furthest from everything, is forest: dense wet conifer, lakes and bogs, with trolls in it and
dragons over it. The **north-east** was the workshop of the continent until it fell, and the roads
that ran through it are the ones nobody uses any more.

**It is three layers, and you will travel in all of them.**

- **Inside the rings.** Five districts and some twenty enclaves. Safe, because the blood magic says
  so, and paid for every year in people.
- **Outside them.** Everything else, which is the Horde's. The roads between safe places are
  strangled and the caravans that leave are hunted, so open country is crossed only by those who
  have decided the crossing is worth it.
- **Underneath.** The tunnels, dug so that one safe place could reach another, with underground
  cities and harbour villages strung along the named roads. Parts have been forgotten, parts have
  been taken by monsters, landslides have let the Horde into others, and here and there the digging
  broke into elven ruins. Below even that is the underworld, which has its own occupants and does
  not want visitors.

**So distance is not measured in miles.** It is measured in whether there is a road, whether the
road is still open, and what it costs to go anyway. Two towns a week apart by tunnel can be
neighbours, and two a day apart overland can be unreachable.

### The five districts that are left

**The Core.** Inland, on the fertile damp plain inside the Spear Mountains. It protects **Geistburg**,
Athor's old capital, five satellite cities and the farmland between them, and the realm is still called
Denaria. Geistburg is a melting pot of complicated politics, a dozen corrupt noble houses and a
powerless man who is still titled emperor. The mages' academy and the cathedral of the Church of the
Eternal God stand apart out on the plain, each grown into a small town of its own around its magic or
its religion, which is how hypocrisy and religious persecution come to walk together. The capital is
built on one huge mesa, with the rich and noble inside Geistburg itself, slums over the rest of the
rock, and ordinary people out in the satellite cities. Cable cars connect the mesa to the plain below.

**The Fiord.** The westernmost of the three great fiords of the northern coast, where the sea cuts
deep inland, with a few islands lying close enough in to belong to the district and six enormous
lighthouses marking where the ring runs. The capital is **Black Harbour**, which shares the district
with a handful of fishing villages and one fortified northern town, and the region used to belong to
the Salt Barons, an association of fishing magnates, smugglers and pirates. The old faith of the Five is strong here and
the Church of the Eternal God is considered blasphemy. It lands most of the continent's fish. The
**West Road**, a broad tunnel with underground cities along it, ties it to the Core.

**The Ruby.** Rolling hills in the south-east, warm, famous for wine and fruit, where the rich once
built winter palaces. The coast feeds it fish and the farmland the rest, but wine is what it is known
for. The **South Road** runs to the Core District, and the underground city of **Sol** is where the
rich of the Core and the Ruby go to enjoy themselves, with a reputation that everything is permitted
there. Religion and politics are relaxed. A voted council governs from **Tanto**, at the centre of
three coastal cities, none of which dominates the others.

**The Iron.** The northern half of the Hyena Desert and the mineral-rich Mouse Mountains above it.
**Sammel**, in the northern oasis, is the only large city and nearly matches Geistburg; out at the
western oasis stands a holy town, thinly peopled, and the rest of the desert is desolate but for a
couple of caravan camps where there is water. **The Grey**
and his siblings are worshipped here. Emirs rule, and they have cut Sammel into sealed quarters called
**Rote**; the old assassin state holds the central one, around the Ziggurat, the tallest building in
the region. Its embassies sell services under the table in every district but the Amber. The **East
Road** connects it to the Core and is the least safe of the roads, because the mining lets monsters
and the Horde break in constantly. The **Night Express** runs it anyway, hauling freight and
passengers through tunnels black with its own coal smoke.

**The Amber.** The far east, the second largest district and the one that was never entirely
tamed. Dragons turn up. Trolls live in the forests and the mountains. Dense wet conifer forest
covers most of it, broken by lakes, bogs and hills. Timber was the export until the Furnace fell and took the routes
with it, and now the inland capital **Karmat**, two small coastal towns and a scatter of villages are
all there is of it. Karmat is in decline and mercenaries are the only thing it sells: a
squadron of hard soldiers fights its way through the wilderness to the Iron District once a year.
The forest barons are broken but not gone, every town runs itself, and the mayor's chair changes hands
bloodily and often. The war god **Vax** has the most followers, and nobody minds what you believe.

**The Furnace is gone.** It lay in the north-east, and was the centre of smithing and manufacture,
with an alchemical academy and a centre of magical research called the Rune Temple in the labyrinth
city of **Weder**, once the third largest city on the continent. Technology and magic mattered there and
religion did not. The Furnace built the Iron's trains, the Fiord's sea defences and the Core's cable
cars. Its damp pine forest has gone to barren bog since the Horde came in, and Weder is ruins. Its refugees founded
engineering guilds in every surviving district and kept their language, **Valgar**, and their lack of
religion, which still causes trouble in the Core District.

### The enclaves

Some twenty of them hold out on small territories with the Horde all around. These are the ones
everybody has heard of.

**Hyrlock.** The island of the blood mages, feared or revered depending on who you ask, a week's
sailing from the abandoned ports west of the Core District. A caste society under the city of
**Mander Cliff**: mages at the top, then administrators and merchants, then workers, and beneath them
the thralls, who are bred to be spent in sacrifice. Power radiates from the **Red Tower**, fifty metres
of it at the cliff's summit. The faith is of the blood goddess **Parfebe**. Every other district and
enclave pays Hyrlock an annual tribute in goods and thralls to keep the wards up, and everyone calls
it the **blood tax**.

**Kelp Bay.** It was a nothing port before year 0 and is now the only one left on the west
coast, which makes it the single way in to Hyrlock and the blood tax. Three shipping companies run the
voyages in an unstable cartel, and the pay for surviving one draws adventurers. Fog moves through
cobbled alleys lit blue for bars, red for brothels and green for business best not described. The
smell is rotting bodies, spilt beer and cheap perfume, under the fermenting kelp.

**Solemn.** A white limestone island south-east of the Ruby District, home of the witches, a sisterhood
with a magic they keep to themselves. They follow the **Primal Mother**, a fertility goddess, and they
are one of the few enclaves that needs no blood magic: their own ward holds, and nobody outside knows
how. It is guessed to have something to do with how thin the island's borders are to the Second and
Third Sides. Their city **Sonebra** sits walled at the top, all towers and spires.

**Regensburg.** The militaristic realm in a valley of the Spear Mountains, north-east of the Core
District, and the most important inland enclave. Three concentric walls ten metres high divide the city into rings, each ring subdivided again,
all of it designed to make an invasion take as long as possible. A Marshal of the Realm sits at the top
of a strict hierarchy. It hires its cavalry out to other districts in exchange for the supplies it
cannot grow. The **Cracked Road** reaches the Core District through cave-riddled limestone and is not
safe; halfway along it a large underground lake has two harbour villages on it, named From and To.

**Wolf Peak.** A religious centre of the faith of **the Fire**, on a dormant volcano north
of the Amber District, never once taken. The roads to the summit are easy to hold and the mountain
feeds itself inside and out. It also has the best gunpowder and firearms on the continent, because the
ingredients are all in the mountain: bat guano that never runs out, sulphur from the volcanism, and
charcoal from fast trees that love the soil. The priesthood has its own language, **Perga**, its own
dress, and tattoos over every inch. They are rarely seen outside.

**Echterhof.** A large neutral city in the middle of Horde territory, ruled by Strigoi princes, with
farmland around it the Horde has left alone. Strigoi
live off humans, so a large human population lives there caught between the vampires and the Horde. A
human mayor is appointed to keep order and choose who is eaten, usually criminals, and when criminals
run short it is done by lot, by grudge or by whim. The city welcomes visitors, because the more people
there are the less likely you are the one chosen. The **Guest Law** guarantees that a guest will not be
killed, and guarantees that a guest who breaks any of its many clauses will be. It also offers land and
the prospect of a title, to keep people coming. They worship **The First**, the first vampire.

**Velderon.** The elves' embassy on Firma, east of the Fiord District: an ancient elven ruin with one
restored corner. The elves are neutral and live on the Second Side; their own magic keeps the Horde
out, and the embassy exists to watch the Horde and make sure it never starts looking upward. You do
not go in without an invitation.

**Saag et Haar.** What is left of Seetch, the realm of the necromancer kings, south of the Hyena
Desert. The greatest of those kings, **Fahlas**, still rules it after four hundred years and has become
a minor god. It depends on blood mages now like everywhere else, though raising the dead keeps the
number of sacrifices lower. The government is a tyranny, the religion is the death goddess **Aecht**,
and the tongue is **Veche**. A trade road runs to Sammel through the Third Side: portals at both ends
and a demon-permitted road between them, paid for in human sacrifice. The city buys slaves in Sammel
and pays in silver, gold and gemstones out of its own mines.

**The Triple Enclave.** Three frontier towns north of the Iron District, on the western slopes of
the Spear Mountains and all of them in mountainous country, each walled around a castle and a duke,
sharing one group of blood mages and one safe tunnel, the **Steel Road**.
**Farsight** stands on the open upper slopes and the Church of the Eternal God holds it absolutely, its duchess also a
cardinal, religion behind every law. **Marke** sits above the high bogs of its valley and exports
**dreamwort**, whose dried seed is a powerful drug; its duke is powerless and the **Dream Guild**, a
criminal organisation with branches in every district, holds the real power. They are rumoured to have
a safe-passage treaty with the Horde. **Highfall** is built into a mountain with a waterfall coming
over its walls, and tries to run things the old way: it lends the Dream Guild troops, and takes money
and sacrificial slaves from the Iron District in exchange.

**Desert Stream.** The third oasis of the Hyena Desert, outside the Iron District's ward and holding
a small one of its own that covers the water and nothing else. The isolation has told on the people
there and their reputation is poor, but the oasis feeds everyone who reaches it.

### The hives

Mostly they look like gigantic anthills of earth and organic matter, winding inside into tunnels and
chambers for larvae and for each of the Horde's roles. **Twelve are known.**

**Xtchaargh** is the only hive that will receive human envoys. It has a human quarter in the middle of
its tunnels, with ordinary houses and squares, called the **Summer Village**, where humans who serve
the Horde live and train spies to pull enclaves and districts apart. It is said that a delegation from
the Furnace District went there shortly before its blood mages were killed.

**Chguuth** is the main hive, in the south-east, twice the height of any other and reinforced with the
chitin of the dead. The enormous flying **Skaah** that were mistaken for airships all set out from
here. The Queen sits here.

**The underworld** is not empty either. Demons, minor gods and monsters pushed off the surface have
strongholds down there. **The Queen of the Dark** and **the Troll King** have always been there
and do not care for visitors. Other stretches are infested with the Horde.

### What people believe

**The Church of the Eternal God.** *The Eternal God is the only true god, and the emperor his only true
representative on Firma.* Three tenets: **purity** of soul and body, which means avoiding strong
feeling and poisons and keeping yourself trained, and holds that all other gods are impure;
**obedience**, because what the god, the emperor and his priests say is law and questioning it is
blasphemy; and **solidarity**, helping your brothers and sisters in faith and never charging them for
it. Its chief saint is Athor, who spread it across Firma, alongside lesser saints like the dragonslayer
Vesper, the healer Selma and the martyr Bullius, local saints, and every emperor who has ever lived.
Inside it: **the Black Circle**, a religious secret police; **the Holy Shield**, its order of knights;
**the White Brothers** and **the Black Sisters**, its largest orders of monks and nuns; and **the
Flagellants**, the most extreme and frequently violent.

**The Church of the Eternal Spirit.** A sister church holding that the Eternal God's spirit drifts
through all the air of Firma. Same tenets, same saints, plus its founder **Vegnar**, who was Athor's
brother. The two churches are rivals who tolerate each other, with accusations of blasphemy surfacing
now and then. Inside it: **God's Spirit** as its secret police, **the Holy Sword** as its knights,
**the Devoted** as its monks and nuns, and **the Unseen**, an order of assassins.

**The Five.** The largest religion before Athor, known all along the coasts. **Sessyfyn** the winged
fish, goddess of fishing and seafaring. **Valtor** the two-pointed spear, her brother and husband, god
of strength and battle. **Fagar** the mask of fish scales, their child, the trickster. **Vulpet** the
shark, daughter of **Fagar** and **Sessyfyn**, driven out for being so ugly. **Brendan** the conch
horn, son of **Fagar** and **Valtor**, who stands for the beauty that can come out of ugly things.
Its **Mummers** walk about in animal masks singing and pressing drink on people; its **Berserkers**
work themselves up with drums and dancing before a battle.

**The Grey.** Three siblings and three books. **The Grey** embodies assassination, and the Grey Book is
on avoiding discovery and killing quietly. **The Purple** brews poison, and the Purple Book is recipes.
**The Yellow** stands for lies, deceit and betrayal, and the Yellow Book is misinformation and
disguise. The three sects match the three books.

**Local gods.** **Vax**, war, in the Amber District. **Parfebe**, blood, on Hyrlock. **The Primal
Mother**, fertility, on Solemn. **The Fire**, fire, at Wolf Peak. **The First**, vampirism, in
Echterhof. **Aecht**, death, in Saag et Haar.

**The minor gods, who demonstrably exist.** Perhaps a hundred powerful nature spirits and beings,
forced into remote places and underground when Athor took the continent. They grant no miracles, and
they are what the school of Spiritualism is built on. Among the known: **the Winter Lady**,
**the Queen of the Dark**, **the Troll King**, **Baal Fagor**, **the Land Jelly**, **the Cat God**,
**Fahlas** and **Mother Longhand**. Cults gather round them: **the Silent** follow **the Winter
Lady**, **the Soot Club** try to summon **Baal Fagor**, and **the Dancers** use drugs to speak with
**the Land Jelly**.

### What a character can become

Your Way is the direction you grow in, and each one ends in specialists the world has names for.

**Way of Mind** runs to ritual magic and the schools of direct magic: blood, necromancy, elemental,
poison, glamour, asceticism, shadow, spiritualism, household, ward and divination. It also covers the
trades that are not magic at all: brewers, smiths, jewellers and orators.

**Way of Strength** runs to the sword, the two-hander, the polearm, the dagger and the empty hand; to
formation fighting, crossbows, artillery and muskets; to protection, blunt weapons, gladiators,
berserkers, cavalry and sappers.

**Way of Shade** runs to thieves, assassins, spies, trapmakers and fences; to bards, politicians,
archers, pistoliers and duellists; to acrobats and half-mages.

**Way of Faith** runs to the churches and the gods above, and swearing to one closes the others.
Everything a priest does is here: the orders, the saints and the miracles of whichever god you swore
to.

## 2. What you have

Six stacks of cards, a seventh if you cast, and one number. Everything you own is in one of them.

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Action deck** | 12–20 cards. What your character can *do* in a fight, and how long they can keep doing it. |
| **Skill deck** | About 15 cards. What your character can *attempt* outside a fight. |
| **Fate deck** | 20 cards. Your luck, and nobody else's. |
| **Items** | Everything you own. Only **equipped** items can be used. |
| **Features** | Permanent traits: *Strong*, *Tough*, *Good Aim*. |
| **Conditions** | What is currently on you, each with a number counting down. |
| **Spells** | Your book, if you have one. The count is what you have **prepared**. |
| **Health** | At 0 you are down. |

### Decks you draw from, and decks you keep

Every one of those stacks is a deck of cards, printed the same size, held the same way. What tells
them apart is what happens to the deck.

- **Decks you draw from.** Action, skill and fate. These are shuffled, drawn from, played and
  discarded, and you do not get to choose what comes up.
- **Decks you keep.** Items, features, conditions, and your spellbook. Nothing in them is shuffled
  and nothing is drawn. You open the stack and look through it, because a stack is simply how you
  hold a lot of cards at a table.

A card you keep can still be **spent**. A one-use item is gone once you use it. What it never does
is go into a deck you draw from.

Watch for the two that cross over. **Wounds** and **Taint** are not kept. They are shuffled into the
decks you draw from, and that is the whole of what makes them frightening.

on it is a bonus you add to a card. Every one of them is a count of something you can point at on
the table, which is why the game has no character sheet worth the name.

Two of them move constantly, and the fight is about both: your Health, and how much of your action

## 4. Your action cards

A card carries three lines, and *Heavy Swing* has all three:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Strike +1 |
| **Secondary** | Move 1 |
| **Reaction** | Parry |

- **Primary**, the strong half. Your main action.
- **Secondary**, the weak half. Your second action.
- **Reaction**, what the card does on somebody else's turn.

**You can never use both halves of the same card.** A turn is one card's primary and a *different*
card's secondary.

Any card whose reaction cannot fire is still a **Brace: reduce incoming damage by 1**. That is
deliberately too little to be worth spending a good card on. Its purpose is that no card in your
hand is ever completely dead, and it matters exactly once, the hit that would have killed you, by
one.

### The six verbs

| Verb | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Strike** ⚔ | Attack with an equipped weapon. |
| **Cast** ✦ | Cast a prepared spell. |
| **Defend** 🛡 | Gain Block. |
| **Use** 🧪 | Use an equipped item. |
| **Move** 👣 | Move that many squares. |
| **Ready** ⇄ | Equip or swap one item. |

## 5. A round

1. Everyone draws one fate card for initiative, once per fight. **Highest acts first** (Miss is
   lowest, Critical highest), and ties are redrawn until the order settles. The order then holds for
   the whole fight.
2. The GM draws one intent card per kind of enemy, face down.
3. Each character takes a turn in order.

### Your turn

- Refill your hand to **5 cards**.
- Play **one card for its primary** and **a different card for its secondary**, in either order.
- Both go to your discard pile.
- The rest of your hand stays in front of you, ready to react.

Two attacks in one turn are legal if they come from two different cards, a Strike primary plus a
card that has Strike on its secondary. Those cards are rare, and they are earned by levelling.

## 6. Reactions

**Reactions resolve by themselves. You are not asked.** When a trigger happens, the best reaction
your hand can offer fires, that card goes to your discard, and play continues.

A reaction fires when the hit is **serious**, when it would deal a Wound (5 or more past armour)
or drop you to 0. A scratch is a scratch, and a hand spent on scratches is a hand you do not have
when the real blow lands.

### Every reaction, and what it does

Five things can trigger one. Being hit is the common case and the only one with a fallback: a card
whose own reaction cannot fire is still a Brace. The other four are an enemy arriving next to you,
an **ally** being hit, a spell being cast, and a check failing, and the last three belong to the
casting Ways, because they are printed on a prepared spell rather than on a card in your hand.

| **Hold to the Bargain** | a check fails | A check you had just failed is intervened in, once, by something that owes you. | a prepared spell |

### Facing, and the ground you watch

**Which way you are pointing matters for exactly one thing, and this is it.** Turn your figure and
you are watching the ground in front of it: your weapon's own range, through a narrow arc of
**sixty degrees**, thirty either side of the way you face. On the board that is **one square at one
pace, three from two paces out, and five from four**, so it is a lane rather than a quadrant.

A held shot answers the first enemy to **walk into** that lane. Not one standing in it already, and
not one shuffling about inside it: the lane is about the step that crosses into it, the same way an
arrival is about the step that brings somebody next to you.

**The weapon decides what arrives**, which is why one card covers a thrown knife and a musket. A
knife watches three squares and lands for 2; a longbow watches ten and lands for 3; a musket
watches twelve and lands for 6 through two points of armour. The card only chooses the moment.

**A wall stops it.** The lane is ground you can see, so a pillar, a corner or a closed door is
the end of it, and a window is not. This is the only rule in the game that works the wall out for
you, and it is because a held shot is the only attack that aims itself.

So a held shot answers somebody **stepping out of cover**, which is most of what you will use it
for. Walking into the open is walking into the lane.

**Only one shot answers a step.** The first watcher in initiative order looses, spends their card,
and that is the step dealt with. Four archers watching the same gap do not all fire into it.

Nothing else in the game asks which way you are pointing. A Parry answers a blow from anywhere, a
Sidestep is away rather than backward, and reach is a circle. If nobody at your table is holding a
shot, facing never comes up.

**A reaction spell has no fallback.** It is prepared or it is not, and once it has answered
something this fight it is spent. A card that cannot answer is still worth a Brace; a spell that
cannot answer simply does not.

**Stunned takes all of them, Brace included.** So does being Unconscious, and so does being
Exhausted. *You cannot play reactions* has no exception in it.

Three things are always true:

- **A reaction never draws fate.** A Parry is your weapon's Defend value, a Brace is 1, a Counter is
  your weapon's Strike value. Flat, every time.
- **A reaction's damage is not itself a trigger.** A Counter hits, and nothing answers it.
- **Reacting is never free.** Every card you react with is one more card off your deck at the next
  refill, and that brings exhaustion closer.

### Turning a card sideways

**A card turned sideways will not react.** That is the whole of the decision, and you take it in
advance, at leisure, not in the half-second after the dice would have landed in another game.

**When your turn is over, the rest of your hand turns sideways on its own.** A reaction costs you a
point of stamina, and the game reacts for you off whatever is still upright, so a full hand left
upright is a promise to spend four or five of the cards you can still play. If you mean to Parry,
turn that card back upright before you end your turn. There is a button that does the whole hand at
once, either way.

Cards you draw at the start of a round arrive upright. What goes sideways is the set you already
chose not to play.

## 7. Resolving an action

Always in this order:

1. **Base value**, the item's line for the verb. A Longsword's Strike is 3.
2. **Card modifier**, the bonus printed on the action card. *Strike +1.*
3. **Features**, permanent modifiers. *Strong: +1 on Strike with Heavy items.*
4. **Conditions**, temporary modifiers on you or on the target.
5. **Fate**, draw one card from your fate deck and apply it.
6. **Their defences**, subtract **armour**, then **Block**. Never below 0.

**Fate is drawn for damage and healing only.** Move, Ready and Defend never draw fate.

**One draw per action, not per target.** An attack that hits several creatures draws once and
applies the same answer to all of them.

### Bonuses do not stack from the same kind of source

Every card that grants a numeric bonus belongs to one of seven groups: **Body, Training, Tool,
Make, Power, Station, Wonder.** One action takes **at most one bonus from each group**. Muscle and a
tool stack; two kinds of muscle do not.

Whatever supplies the *base* value is exempt, so a weapon and a feature can never lock each other
out.

## 8. The fate deck

Twenty cards, yours alone.

| Card | Count | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Miss** | 1 | The action does nothing. Shuffle the deck afterwards. |
| −2 | 1 | |
| −1 | 4 | |
| +0 | 8 | |
| +1 | 4 | |
| +2 | 1 | |
| **Critical** | 1 | On an attack, draw from the critical deck. On anything else, double the value. Shuffle afterwards. |

**Advantage** means draw two and take the better; **disadvantage**, draw two and take the worse. Two
sources of advantage still give one.

**Your deck thins as you use it.** A fate deck that has already spent its good cards is a different
deck from a fresh one, and you can see that coming. It reshuffles on a Miss or a Critical.

### The critical deck

A Critical on an attack draws one card from the critical deck: twelve cards, shared by the whole
table, read out face up.

| Face | Copies | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Through and Through** | 4 | Double the damage. |
| **Find the Gap** | 2 | **Exposed**, their armour counts 0 until the end of your next turn. Not this hit, and not doubled. |
| **Off the Feet** | 2 | **Off-Balance**, their Block counts 0 until the end of your next turn, and it is an opening. Not this hit, and not doubled. |
| **Driven Down** | 1 | **Halted**, their next turn does not happen. Not doubled. |
| **Stove In** | 1 | Their armour is one point worse until it is repaired. Permanent. Not doubled. |
| **Turn the Edge** | 1 | Their weapon strikes for one less until it is repaired. Permanent. Not doubled. |
| **Both** | 1 | Draw two others and take both. |

A Critical always gets **at least one point** through, however well armoured the target is.

## 9. Armour, Block and getting past them

- **Armour** is worn. It subtracts from every hit.
- **Block** is earned by a Defend, and subtracts after armour. How long it lasts depends on how you
  played it:

| Played as | Block lasts |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Against **every** attack until the start of your next turn |
| **Secondary** | Against the **next** attack only |
| **Reaction** | Against the attack that triggered it |

Blocks from different sources do not stack. **Use the highest**, and that source's duration is the
one that applies, so a Defend secondary played behind a Defend primary never shortens the stance you
already had.

Some attacks go **through** armour (*ignores N armour*, or all of it) and some go **round** Block.
They are different things and a card says which: a bodkin finds a gap in plate, and a shield raised
in answer does not care.

**Damaged gear.** Corrode takes points off armour; Blunt takes points off what a weapon strikes for.
Both are permanent until repaired, both stop at 0, and **neither destroys anything**. Armour at 0
is still armour and still repairable. Repair is an action: a **Lore check** with the right tools. A
Critical puts back 2, a Success 1, a Partial nothing and the time is gone.

## 10. Conditions

A condition is a token with a number, its duration. It remembers who created it, and it runs out at
the **end of that creature's next turn**. So a condition you inflict lasts through your own next
turn, and you can use it.

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Dazed** | You can't play a secondary. |
| **Slowed** | All Move values halved, rounding down. |
| **Stunned** | You can't play reactions. |
| **Halted** | Your turn does not happen. |
| **Prone** | Melee Strikes against you have advantage. Standing up costs Move 1. |
| **Grappled** | You can't Move. |
| **Silenced** | You can't Cast. |
| **Disarmed** | You can't Ready, or Strike with the disarmed item. |
| **Heckled** | You draw fate with disadvantage. |
| **Exposed** | Your armour counts 0. Block is untouched. |
| **Off-Balance** | Your Block counts 0, and it is an opening. |
| **Burning N** | N damage at the start of your turn, past armour and Block. Cancels with Wet and Frozen. |
| **Frozen N** | Your Move values are reduced by N. Cancels with Burning. |
| **Poisoned N** | You are Dazed, and take N damage at the start of your turn. N may be 0. |
| **Invisible** | Enemies can't target you. Ends if you Strike or Cast at one. |
| **Compelled** | You must spend your primary attacking whoever compelled you. |
| **Burdened / Overloaded** | See *Carrying*. |
| **Unconscious** | At 0 Health. No turns, no reactions, Prone. |

**Shake Off** spends a condition's duration rather than waiting for it. **Openings** (Wet,
Off-Balance, Marked and Judged) do nothing by themselves; they exist for other cards to key off, and
they cannot be shaken off.

### Putting two together

Most conditions are worth more beside another one than they are alone, and the clock is what makes
that possible. A condition you inflict runs out at the end of **your** next turn, so it is still
sitting there through everybody else's. One of you opens and another one finishes.

**The four openings are the whole combo system.** They do nothing at all by themselves. They sit on
a target waiting for a card that reads them, and that card is usually not the card that set them.

| Set this | And these want it |
|---|---|
| **Off-Balance** | **Hammerblow** strikes at **+2**. **Sweep the Leg** goes from Normal to **Heroic** to save against, and **Overbear** from Normal to **Hard**. Goblins know it too: **Worry It** ignores armour against an off-balance target |
| **Marked** | **Between the Plates** ignores armour, so it goes through plate. **Take the Shot** draws with **advantage**. **Pin the Shadow** goes from Normal to **Hard** |
| **Wet** | **Lightning** hits for **2** more and goes from Hard to **Heroic**. **Frost** goes from Normal to **Heroic** |
| **Judged** | Faith reads it on its own miracles. **The Weight of It** puts a judged enemy's armour at **0 against everyone**, and **Grey Judgement** makes them act last this round |

Three things worth knowing beyond the openings.

**Armour and Block come off separately, and both can go.** Exposed puts armour at 0, Off-Balance
puts Block at 0. A figure carrying both has nothing between the fate card and their Health.

**Take their answer away first.** Stunned means no reactions, so no Parry and no Block however many
cards they are holding. Exposed and Stunned together is armour gone and nothing to be done about
it. Halted is the whole turn, and Grappled on a Prone figure keeps them down, because standing up
costs Move 1 and Grappled means you cannot Move at all.

**Some of them cancel, so the order matters.** Burning cancels with Wet and with Frozen. Wet is the
setup for a Frost or a Lightning, and it is also a bucket of water: drop Burning on a wet target and
you have spent both for nothing. The ones that slow you do stack, in a fixed order: the injury
first, which never takes you below one square, then halved if something halves it, then the
condition's own reduction, which can take you to nothing.

## 11. How a fight goes

Every chapter up to here answers one question. This is the order those questions come up in, so
you have the shape of a fight before you watch one.

**Before anybody moves.** Everyone draws once for initiative and that order holds for the whole
fight. The GM draws a face-down intent for each kind of enemy, so you know something is coming
without knowing what. *A round* owns this.

**Your turn is two halves of two different cards.** Refill your hand, then play one card for its
primary and another for its secondary. You can never use both halves of one card, and that single
restriction is what the game is built on: every card you spend attacking is a card you are not
holding to defend with. *Your action cards* and *A round* own this.

**Whatever is left in your hand is your defence.** On somebody else's turn a card in your hand can
fire its reaction, and a card whose reaction does not fit can still Brace. Nothing you hold is
dead. *Reactions* owns this.

**An attack is a card, a draw and two subtractions.** Play the card, draw one fate card for luck,
add them together, then take off their armour and then their Block. *Resolving an action*, *The
fate deck* and *Armour, Block and getting past them* own those steps between them.

**You never run out of magic. You run out of stamina.** Stamina is how many cards you can play in
a fight, whatever your deck holds; your deck itself cycles for free. Wounds shuffle into it and clog
your hand without costing stamina, because you never play them. Health is how you fall over;
stamina is how you tire. *Running out* owns this.

**Conditions are the fight's memory.** Each is a word with a number counting down, and most of
what a card does beyond damage is to put one on somebody. *Conditions* owns this.

Nothing above is a rule. Every number lives in the chapter named beside it, and what follows is
two rounds of all of it at once, with the arithmetic shown.

## 12. Two rounds, played out

Everything above, running. One character, two enemies, and every number taken from the cards and
stat blocks in this book rather than invented for the example.

**The Warrior** is a starting character: **12 Health**, a **Longsword** (Strike 3, Defend 1), a
**Wooden Shield** (Defend 2), **Leather Armor** (Armor 1) and a **Healing Potion** on the belt.
One feature, **Strong**: +1 damage on Strike with Heavy items, and the Longsword is Heavy. The
action deck is 16 cards and the hand is 5.

**A Goblin** stands next to him: 5 Health, no armour, speed 3, a spear that Strikes for 3. **A
Goblin Archer** is six squares back down the tunnel: 5 Health, no armour, a shortbow that Strikes
for 3 out to range 6.

**Initiative.** Everyone draws one fate card, once for the whole fight. The Archer draws **+2**,
the Goblin **+1**, the Warrior **−1**. The order is **Archer, Goblin, Warrior**, and it holds for
the rest of the fight. Acting last is a bad draw and it is going to cost him.

**Two intent cards, face down, one per kind of enemy.** The Goblin and the Archer are different
kinds, so that is two cards. If there were four goblins they would still share one.

### Round one

The intents are **Close In** for the goblin kind and **Loose** for the archer kind. Turns run in
the initiative order drawn above, which is why the Archer goes first and the Warrior goes last.

**1. The Archer** plays *Loose*: Strike at the weapon's range. The Warrior is exactly six squares
away and the bow reaches six. Shortbow **3**, fate **+0**, total **3**. The Warrior's armour takes
1, so **2 damage**. Warrior at **10**.

Nothing in the Warrior's hand is asked about it. A reaction fires only on a **serious** hit, and 2
is neither a Wound nor enough to drop him.

**2. The Goblin** plays *Close In*: move your speed, then Strike. It is already adjacent, so it
moves nothing. Spear **3**, fate **+1**, total **4**. Armour takes 1, so **3 damage**. Warrior at
**7**. Still not serious.

**3. The Warrior** refills to five and holds *Strike, Strike, Heavy Swing, Shield Up, Advance*.

- **Primary: Heavy Swing** at the Goblin. The order from *Resolving an action*: base **3** for the Longsword,
  **+1** for the card, **+1** for Strong, which is **5** before luck. Fate **−1** gives **4**.
  The goblin has no armour and no Block, so **4 damage**: it drops to **1 Health**. Heavy Swing
  also ignores 2 armour, which buys nothing here, because a goblin has none to ignore.
- **Secondary: Strike**, for its Move 1, one step, keeping the goblin in reach.

He wanted the shield up as well, and he cannot have it. *Shield Up* carries Defend on its
**primary**, and the primary is spent on Heavy Swing. **You never get both halves of one card**,
and two cards whose useful halves are both primaries compete for the same slot. That is most of
what a turn is.

Heavy Swing and the one Strike go to the discard. **Three cards stay in front of him**, ready to
react: *Strike, Shield Up, Advance*. Eleven are left in the draw pile.

### Round two

**Nobody draws for initiative again.** It is drawn once for the whole fight, so the order is the
same one as before: Archer, Goblin, Warrior. The numbers below are that order repeating, not a
new one. What is new each round is the intents, and this round they are **Find the Joint** for
the goblin and **Loose** again for the archer.

**1. The Archer** looses again: **3**, fate **−1**, total **2**. Armour takes 1, so **1 damage**.
Warrior at **6**.

**2. The Goblin** plays *Find the Joint*: Strike where the harness is buckled rather than where
it is thickest, **ignoring 2 armour**. The Warrior's armour is 1, so all of it counts for nothing.
Spear **3**, fate **+2**, total **5**, and no armour stands in the way: **5 incoming**.

**That is a Wound**, and a Wound is serious, so the hand is asked. The best answer in it is
**Shield Up** as a **Block**: the Wooden Shield's Defend is 2, and one more for being a shield, so
it takes **3** off. **2 damage** gets through, and because 2 is under the Wound line, **the Wound
never happens**. Shield Up goes to the discard. Warrior at **4**.

That is the whole of the reaction rule in one blow. Nothing answered the 1s, 2s and 3s, because a
reaction fires only when it **changes the outcome**: when it stops a Wound, or stops you being
dropped. A hand spent on scratches is a hand you do not have for this.

**3. The Warrior** refills to five: *Strike, Advance, Lunge, Quick Hands, Second Wind*.

- **Primary: Strike** at the Goblin, on 1 Health. Base **3** plus **1** for Strong is **4**, and
  the fate card is a **Critical**. So he draws from the critical deck and turns up **Through and
  Through**: double the damage, **8**. The goblin is down. The fate deck shuffles, because a
  Critical always does.
- **Secondary: Second Wind**, for its **Use**, the Healing Potion. Use **4**, fate **+0**,
  **4 Health back**, and the potion is spent doing it. Warrior at **8**.

*Second Wind* is printed **Spent** on its primary and he played the secondary, so it is only
discarded. A cost on one half is not a cost on the other.

**Where the fight stands.** The Warrior is on **8 of 12** with a goblin down, the Archer untouched
six squares away and still shooting, three cards in hand, five in the discard and eight to draw.
He is winning and he has spent a quarter of his deck to do it, which is the arithmetic the whole
game is about: you do not run out of spells, you run out of deck.

## 13. Checks, outside a fight

Draw a hand of **4** skill cards. Each shows one **approach** and a value of 1–3.

| Approach | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Might** ✊ | Climbing, breaking, lifting, intimidating, enduring |
| **Agility** 🌀 | Balance, acrobatics, quick hands, riding, dodging traps |
| **Shadow** 🌑 | Stealth, deception, disguise, pickpocketing, lockpicking |
| **Wits** 👁 | Noticing, searching, reading people, tracking, survival |
| **Lore** 📜 | Knowledge, arcana, religion, history, healing |
| **Presence** 👑 | Persuading, leading, performing, bargaining, calming |

Every deck contains all six, so anyone can try anything. Your Way and background decide which ones
you hold more of, and better.

**To make a check:** the GM sets a difficulty. You play a card of the approach you are using, add
your bonuses, and draw fate.

| Difficulty | Number |
|---|---|
| Easy | 3 |
| Normal | 5 |
| Hard | 7 |
| Heroic | 9 |

| Result | When | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | Critical drawn | You do it, and something extra goes your way. |
| **Success** | Total ≥ difficulty | You do it. |
| **Partial** | 1–4 below | You do it, **but** it costs something. |
| **Fail** | 5 or more below, or a Miss | You don't, **and** the situation changes. |

A Critical comes off the card, so a Critical that still falls short is a success with a bonus, not a
near miss. A Miss is a Fail however high the total was.

**Tools** help the approach they name. An **untrained** user gets at most +1 from a tool however good
it is; training is what unlocks the rest, and training comes from levelling.

### Challenges

A big situation is not one check. Getting into a manor, chasing a thief over rooftops, crossing a
mountain, talking a duke round: each is a **challenge**, and the whole party plays into two tracks.

- **Progress**, how much you need to achieve.
- **Pressure**, how much can go wrong before it fails or turns ugly.

Each round, every character takes **one** action: a check, a help, or anything else that fits.

| Result | Progress | Pressure |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | +2 | – |
| **Success** | +1 | – |
| **Partial** | +1 | +1 |
| **Fail** | – | +1, and a twist |

**You can watch both of them.** While a challenge is running, two bars sit on your tray: *Progress
3/6* filling from one side and *Pressure 1/4* from the other. You always know how close you are to
either ending, and that is the point of showing them, because every turn of a challenge is a
decision about whether to push or play safe, and you cannot take that decision blind.

**You win the moment the Progress bar fills.** You have done the thing, and whatever Pressure is
left unspent is how cleanly you did it. A manor entered on one point of Pressure is a quiet
burglary; the same manor entered on three is one the household will be talking about for a month.

**You lose if the Pressure bar fills first.** The alarm goes, the thief is gone, the duke walks out.
That is meant to open a new situation rather than close the road, and very often the new situation
is a fight.

**If both fill on the same check, you win.** You got there, and the cost arrives with you.

Neither bar stops at full, so a last check can push one well past the end, and the GM says what the
overshoot looks like here.

The GM may say a step **needs** a particular approach. That is when the right card in the right hand
matters, and when the party has to plan as a group rather than as four people taking turns.

**Catch Breath in a challenge.** Instead of a check you may take a breath, which costs the party
**Pressure +1**, because a breath is time the other side also gets: the guard changes shift, the
duke grows impatient. Then shuffle your skill discard back into your skill deck, set **1** card of
your choice aside as Spent until your next rest, and draw up to hand size.

### Twists

**A Partial costs you a twist. A Fail is one.**

A twist is a card the GM draws that names the *kind* of trouble rather than the trouble: the card
says *Gear* and the GM says a lockpick snapped off in the lock. Most of them are a question for the
GM to answer that way. **Time** adds a second point of Pressure, and **Opening** is the one that
gives you something instead of taking it.

A twist is **on top of** the Pressure a Fail already costs, never instead of it.

You will usually not see the card. It reaches you as something that has happened, and a ritual draws
from a deck of its own, because a ritual goes wrong in ways nothing else does.

## 14. Running out

**Your stamina is how many cards you can play in a fight.** It is a number on your sheet and it
has nothing to do with how big your deck is. You do not run out of spells, and you do not run out of
deck, because your deck cycles for nothing. You run out of stamina.

Every card you play costs 1: as a primary, as a secondary, or as a reaction, and whether it goes to
your discard or to your Spent pile. Nothing you did not play costs anything.

- **The cycle is free.** When your draw pile runs out and you still have stamina, shuffle your
  discard and it is your draw pile again.
- **Exhausted**: at **1** stamina you may play only **one** card a turn, and you cannot react.
- **At 0** you discard the rest of your draw pile, and the only thing you may do is catch your
  breath.
- **Catch Breath**: spend your whole turn, playing nothing, and regain **2 stamina**, never above
  your maximum.

Two stamina is one full turn, so a breath gives up this turn to buy a normal one next round. There
is no limit on how often you may do it. Running out is therefore a change of pace and not an
ending: you keep fighting, at half the rate, for as long as the fight lasts.

Breathing before you have run out is simply wasted, because the recovery stops at your maximum.

### Wounds

**You gain 1 Wound when** you take **5 or more damage from a single hit** (after armour), or when you
drop to **0 Health**.

A Wound is a card. It goes into your action discard and comes back around like any other. When you
draw one it takes a slot in your hand, cannot be played, not even for Brace, and is discarded at
the end of your turn. Wounds stay between fights.

Removing one: a long rest takes off 1; a **Lore check (Normal 5)** with a Healer's Kit takes off 1,
once per patient per day; some magic says so.

### 0 Health

At 0 you are **Unconscious**: you fall Prone, take no turns, cannot react, and gain a Wound. You are
dying until somebody treats you.

## 16. Carrying and equipping

**Only equipped items can be used.** A **Ready** buys one equip or one swap; because an item only
comes off by being replaced, those are the same transaction.

Your carrying limit is **40 kg**, counting everything.

| Load | | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Up to the limit | **Unburdened** | no penalty |
| 41–60 kg | **Burdened** | Slowed, and disadvantage on Agility and Shadow checks |
| 61–80 kg | **Overloaded** | Burdened, **and 2 random cards are Spent** |
| 81 kg + | | You cannot pick it up. Make two trips. |

## 17. Levelling up

**The GM says when.** There is no experience to count.

A level-up is a step on one shared graph. Your **frontier** is every node that a node you already
own points at, and you take one of them. Taking it spends the level and puts that node's own
successors on your frontier.

**The node is the reward.** Nothing is offered and nothing is passed over. Taking the node *is*
taking the reward. What a node gives is one of:

| Reward | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Action card** | A card added to your deck, the main currency of growing |
| **Training** | A tool type at its full bonus instead of +1, and the tool is issued with it |
| **Item** | A card, handed over |

A **specialization node** gives you a **title** as well as its reward, what people call you, and
the Tactic that comes with it. You only ever have your deepest title's Tactic.

The decision is **a direction, not a menu**. What you give up is the other directions you could have
gone this level, and a branch you walk past is usually still there next time.

**Except when it is not.** Some nodes belong to an exclusive group: taking one **permanently**
disables every other node in that group, and everything reachable only through them. Swearing to a
god is the clearest case. You will be told before it happens.

## 18. The four Ways

A Way is the direction your character grows in. It is not a class you are locked into: it is which
part of the graph you are walking, and the four do genuinely different things rather than the same
thing with different numbers.

### Way of Strength

**Strength breaks.** Everything a fighter does uses verbs everybody has, so a Way built on them
cannot be identified by its numbers. It is identified by rules nobody else is allowed: your Blocks
stack where everyone else takes only the highest, every ally beside you gains Block when you swing,
your Parry also strikes the one enemy you named. Its answer to armour is to hit through it, and its
characteristic card takes two points off armour by hitting harder.

**Strong at** standing in the way. It has the most Health and the heaviest armour, it is the only
Way whose answer to a defended enemy is to go through the defence rather than around it, and several
of its Signatures protect the people next to you rather than you. In a straight fight it is the Way
that is hardest to remove.

**Weak at** everything that is not a straight fight. Its skill deck is thin outside Might, and the
armour it wants is paid for in weight: a fighter in plate with a shield and a pack is one bad
decision away from **Burdened**, which is Slowed plus disadvantage on exactly the Agility and Shadow
checks it was already worst at. Nothing it does is quiet, and nothing it does is a surprise.

Its specialists are the sword, the two-hander, the polearm, the dagger and the empty hand; formation
fighting, crossbows, artillery and muskets; protection, blunt weapons, gladiators, berserkers,
cavalry and sappers.

### Way of Shade

**Shade bypasses.** Where Strength hits through armour, Shade goes around it, and its strikes are
deliberately the weakest in the game so that not needing to hit hard is the point. It works on
**openings**, states like Off-Balance or Marked that do nothing by themselves and everything to the
card that was waiting for one. It sees what is coming, it is worth most against a target that does
not know you are there, and one of its cards takes a card out of an enemy's deck and keeps it.

**Strong at** arriving before the problem does. It has the best Shadow and Agility in the game, it
sees what is coming where other Ways guess, and against a target that is unaware or carrying an
opening it does damage no other Way can match in one blow. Armour and shields barely slow it down,
because it was never trying to get through them.

**Weak at** a fair fight. Its strikes are **mod 0 and minus 1** where Strength's run to plus 2, so a
Shade who has arranged nothing is a worse fighter than the Warrior standing beside them, and that is
deliberate. It is lightly armoured, it is spending cards to set up the hit rather than to survive
one, and an enemy that cannot be surprised or unbalanced takes away most of what it is for.

Its specialists are thieves, assassins, spies, trapmakers and fences; bards, politicians, archers,
pistoliers and duellists; acrobats and half-mages.

### Way of Mind

**Mind studies.** Its spells are **formulas**: learned once, paid for once in your own substance, and
then yours. A first Way of Mind node grants **2 spell slots**, and a prepared spell occupies one
until you prepare something else.

**Strong at** answers nobody else has. It puts conditions on people, changes the ground they are
standing on, hits several enemies at once, reaches further than a weapon does, raises the dead and
wards against what is coming. It is the most adaptable Way between fights, because what it can do
tomorrow is whatever it prepares tonight.

**Weak at** doing it twice. Spell slots are few, a first node grants **two**, and a prepared spell
holds one until you prepare something else, so the decision of what you can do is made before you
know what you will meet. Several schools charge Health for the privilege. And **Silenced** switches
the entire Way off, because casting needs speech.

Its eleven schools are blood magic, spiritualism, necromancy, elemental magic, poison, glamour,
asceticism, shadow magic, household magic, ward magic and divination, with ritual magic for the
things that take days rather than a turn. It also covers the trades that are not magic at all:
brewers, smiths, jewellers and orators.

**No part of it is priestly.** A school is studied out of a book, and that is the whole difference
between the two casting Ways: what Mind learns, Faith is given. Every god, every order and every
miracle belongs to Way of Faith, including the priests who are only lightly magical.

### Way of Faith

**Faith asks.** Its spells are **miracles**: granted rather than learned, and paid for every single
time you ask. A first Way of Faith node also grants 2 spell slots.

**Everything priestly is here**, and nowhere else. A priest's list is their god's list, and there
are twelve: **the Eternal God** and **the Eternal Spirit**; **Sessyfyn**, **Valtor**, **Fagar**,
**Vulpet** and **Brendan** of the Five; **the Grey**, **the Purple** and **the Yellow**; and **Vax**
and **the First**. A school of magic is something a scholar studies; a miracle is something a god
hands over, and no amount of study substitutes for having sworn.

Faith is built out of twelve gods, and this is the one Way where a choice is final. **Swearing
closes every rival creed permanently**, along with everything reachable only through it, and what
counts as a creed is the thing to get right: the Five are one creed, the three of **the Grey** are
one, and the four remaining gods each stand alone. So swearing to **Sessyfyn** leaves the other four
of the Five open to you, and shuts the other seven gods for good.

In exchange you carry that god's **Obedience**, which applies to everything they ever grant. Unlike
a Tactic, which is only ever your deepest title's, you carry **every** Obedience you have sworn to.
An order is sworn separately and carries a rule of the same kind, so a priest in an order pays that
as well. Six is the most anybody can carry: all five of the Five, and one order.

**Strong at** holding a party together. Its miracles heal, protect and lift what has been put on
somebody, and because a miracle is granted rather than learned it does not have to be studied first.
Its gods give standing benefits that are simply true of you from the day you swear.

**Weak at** flexibility, in every direction at once. A miracle is paid for **every single time** you
ask, not once when you learn it. Its slots are as few as Mind's. **Silenced** shuts it off the same
way. And the Obedience you took on is permanent: it is a restriction on how you may behave for the
rest of the character's life, and you carry every one you have sworn, while the doors you closed
swearing the first oath never reopen.

**So read these before you swear.** An Obedience is not a price you pay at a moment, it is a way you
have to live, and it is the whole of what makes one priest a different character from another rather
than a different spell list. Four of the gods also give something back that nobody else can do.
