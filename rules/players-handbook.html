Player's Handbook

Everything you need to play a character. Nothing here is for the GM, and nothing here explains why a rule is the way it is. You never need that at the table.

The short version. You have a deck of cards that is both your abilities and your stamina. Each turn you play two of them. When you attack, you draw one card of luck. When you run out of cards, you are exhausted, not because you were hurt, but because you have been fighting.

The world

The year is 1034, and humanity lives behind walls it pays for in blood.

The Sides

Firma is one of several worlds laid over the same ground. The mountains and lakes of one stand in the same places on the next; what differs is who lives there and what they have done to it.

The First SideFirma. The normal world, and the one you are standing on.
The Second SideWhere the elves went. The same geography, smeared with glamour that makes everything look more beautiful than it is.
The Third SideWhere the demons went after Athor's armies beat them. They like to destroy things, so it is in worse condition: lava dug up out of the ground, jagged black mountains raised where there were none.
The Fourth SideThe land of the dead. The accounts contradict each other so completely that nobody can say whether it is a paradise, a hell or a shadow of here. Only the dead are let in.
A FifthRumoured, along with a Sixth and a Seventh. Nobody has proved any of them.

How it came to this

Humans took the continent slowly. The elves withdrew to the Second Side and the demons to the Third. Monsters and nature spirits were driven into remote places, and the trolls, the dragons and the minor gods went down into their long sleep.

The old reckoning starts at the year 0, when the warrior-king Athor pushed his empire to the coast in every direction. It held for a hundred years and then cracked, and for five hundred years after that the pieces fought over where the lines between them ran. Regions drifted apart. The assassins founded their state in the oasis of the Hyena Desert, the blood mages established themselves on the island of Hyrlock, and the Strigoi princes organised to grow their undead power. In the old year 674 the counting started again at 0.

Airships came out of the sky in that first year, and nobody has ever learned from where. Another world, or a land across the sea. What came out of them is called the Horde: insect-like, of one shared mind, specialised into roles the way ants are. Everyone in it always knew what everyone else was doing. Humanity was divided and did not.

The war has lasted a thousand years. Humans won battles and lost cities, and the direction never changed. The Strigoi princes made their own pact with the Horde and kept their neutrality.

In year 87, with everything nearly lost, humanity made a pact with the blood mages of Hyrlock. Six great territories, the districts, were ringed with a protective ritual powered by thousands of human sacrifices. The isolated enclaves were each given blood mages of their own to renew smaller barriers every year. The Horde had the world, but what was inside the rings was safe.

What followed was attrition. The Horde strangles the roads between the enclaves and hunts the caravans that leave the barriers; humanity raids the hives. Tunnels were dug so that one safe place could reach another, and the network grew until parts of it were forgotten. Monsters took sections of it, landslides let the Horde into others, dissidents built their own communities out in the dark, and here and there the digging broke into elven ruins older than anyone's records.

The sacrifices were never popular. In 879 a mob in the Fifth District beat its blood mages to death. A month later the Horde came through the gap, and the district fell.

That is the arithmetic you live in. Five districts and some twenty enclaves, a thousand years of it, and rumour this year says the Horde is close to getting through a ring that still holds.

The shape of it

One continent, and you can hold the whole of it in your head.

The middle is a plain, ringed by the Spear Mountains, and the oldest of the safe places sits inside them. West is coast and islands, cold and wet, and that is where the fishing is. South-east is rolling hills, warm, and that is where the wine is. South is the Hyena Desert, with the mineral-rich Mouse Mountains standing over its northern edge. East, and furthest from everything, is forest: dense wet conifer, lakes and bogs, with trolls in it and dragons over it. The north-east was the workshop of the continent until it fell, and the roads that ran through it are the ones nobody uses any more.

It is three layers, and you will travel in all of them.

  • Inside the rings. Five districts and some twenty enclaves. Safe, because the blood magic says so, and paid for every year in people.
  • Outside them. Everything else, which is the Horde's. The roads between safe places are strangled and the caravans that leave are hunted, so open country is crossed only by those who have decided the crossing is worth it.
  • Underneath. The tunnels, dug so that one safe place could reach another, with underground cities and harbour villages strung along the named roads. Parts have been forgotten, parts have been taken by monsters, landslides have let the Horde into others, and here and there the digging broke into elven ruins. Below even that is the underworld, which has its own occupants and does not want visitors.

So distance is not measured in miles. It is measured in whether there is a road, whether the road is still open, and what it costs to go anyway. Two towns a week apart by tunnel can be neighbours, and two a day apart overland can be unreachable.

The five districts that are left

The First. Inland, on the plain inside the Spear Mountains. It protects Geistburg, Athor's old capital, eight satellite cities and the farmland between them, and the realm is still called Denaria. Geistburg is a melting pot of complicated politics, a dozen corrupt noble houses and a powerless man who is still titled emperor. The mages' academy is here and so is the Church of the Eternal God, which means hypocrisy and religious persecution walk together. The city stands on five mesas with the rich on top and everyone else in the valleys between, and cable cars run from height to height.

The Second. The west coast and its islands, with six enormous lighthouses marking where the ring runs. The capital is Black Harbour, and the region used to belong to the Salt Barons, an association of fishing magnates, smugglers and pirates. The old faith of the Five is strong here and the Church of the Eternal God is considered blasphemy. It lands most of the continent's fish. The West Road, a broad tunnel with underground cities along it, ties it to the First.

The Third. Rolling hills in the south-east, warm, famous for wine and fruit, where the rich once built winter palaces. The coast feeds it fish and the farmland the rest, but wine is what it is known for. The South Road runs to the First District, and the underground city of Sol is where the rich of the First go to enjoy themselves, with a reputation that everything is permitted there. Religion and politics are relaxed. A voted council governs from Tanto, at the centre of five coastal cities, none of which dominates the others.

The Fourth. The northern half of the Hyena Desert and the mineral-rich Mouse Mountains above it. Sammel, in the oasis, is the only large city and nearly matches Geistburg. The Grey and his siblings are worshipped here. Emirs rule, and they have cut Sammel into sealed quarters called Rote; the old assassin state holds the central one, around the Ziggurat, the tallest building in the region. Its embassies sell services under the table in every district but the Sixth. The East Road connects it to the First and is the least safe of the roads, because the mining lets monsters and the Horde break in constantly. The Night Express runs it anyway, hauling freight and passengers through tunnels black with its own coal smoke.

The Sixth. The far east, the second largest district and the one that was never entirely tamed. Dragons turn up. Trolls live in the forests and the mountains. Dense wet conifer forest covers most of it, broken by lakes, bogs and hills. Timber was the export until the Fifth fell and took the routes with it, and now the capital Karmat is in decline and mercenaries are the only thing it sells: a squadron of hard soldiers fights its way through the wilderness to the Fourth District once a year. The forest barons are broken but not gone, every town runs itself, and the mayor's chair changes hands bloodily and often. The war god Vax has the most followers, and nobody minds what you believe.

The Fifth is gone. It was the centre of smithing and manufacture, with an alchemical academy and a centre of magical research called the Rune Temple in the labyrinth city of Weder, once the third largest city on the continent. Technology and magic mattered there and religion did not. The Fifth built the Fourth's trains, the Second's sea defences and the First's cable cars. Its refugees founded engineering guilds in every surviving district and kept their language, Valgar, and their lack of religion, which still causes trouble in the First District.

The enclaves

Some twenty of them hold out on small territories with the Horde all around. These are the ones everybody has heard of.

Hyrlock. The island of the blood mages, feared or revered depending on who you ask, a week's sailing from the abandoned ports west of the First District. A caste society under the city of Mander Cliff: mages at the top, then administrators and merchants, then workers, and beneath them the thralls, who are bred to be spent in sacrifice. Power radiates from the Red Tower, fifty metres of it at the cliff's summit. The faith is of the blood goddess Parfebe. Every other district and enclave pays Hyrlock an annual tribute in goods and thralls to keep the wards up, and everyone calls it the blood tax.

Kelp Bay. It was a nothing port before year 0 and is now the only one left on the west coast, which makes it the single way in to Hyrlock and the blood tax. Three shipping companies run the voyages in an unstable cartel, and the pay for surviving one draws adventurers. Fog moves through cobbled alleys lit blue for bars, red for brothels and green for business best not described. The smell is rotting bodies, spilt beer and cheap perfume, under the fermenting kelp.

Solemn. A white limestone island south of the Third District, home of the witches, a sisterhood with a magic they keep to themselves. They follow the Primal Mother, a fertility goddess, and they are one of the few enclaves that needs no blood magic: their own ward holds, and nobody outside knows how. It is guessed to have something to do with how thin the island's borders are to the Second and Third Sides. Their city Sonebra sits walled at the top, all towers and spires.

Regensburg. The militaristic realm north-east of the First District, and the most important inland enclave. Three concentric walls ten metres high divide the city into rings, each ring subdivided again, all of it designed to make an invasion take as long as possible. A Marshal of the Realm sits at the top of a strict hierarchy. It hires its cavalry out to other districts in exchange for the supplies it cannot grow. The Cracked Road reaches the First District through cave-riddled limestone and is not safe; halfway along it a large underground lake has two harbour villages on it, named From and To.

Wolf Peak. A religious centre of the faith of the Fire, on a dormant volcano north of the Sixth District, never once taken. The roads to the summit are easy to hold and the mountain feeds itself inside and out. It also has the best gunpowder and firearms on the continent, because the ingredients are all in the mountain: bat guano that never runs out, sulphur from the volcanism, and charcoal from fast trees that love the soil. The priesthood has its own language, Perga, its own dress, and tattoos over every inch. They are rarely seen outside.

Echterhof. A large neutral city in the middle of Horde territory, ruled by Strigoi princes. Strigoi live off humans, so a large human population lives there caught between the vampires and the Horde. A human mayor is appointed to keep order and choose who is eaten, usually criminals, and when criminals run short it is done by lot, by grudge or by whim. The city welcomes visitors, because the more people there are the less likely you are the one chosen. The Guest Law guarantees that a guest will not be killed, and guarantees that a guest who breaks any of its many clauses will be. It also offers land and the prospect of a title, to keep people coming. They worship The First, the first vampire.

Velderon. The elves' embassy on Firma, east of the Second District: an ancient elven ruin with one restored corner. The elves are neutral and live on the Second Side; their own magic keeps the Horde out, and the embassy exists to watch the Horde and make sure it never starts looking upward. You do not go in without an invitation.

Saag et Haar. What is left of Seetch, the realm of the necromancer kings, south of the Hyena Desert. The greatest of those kings, Fahlas, still rules it after four hundred years and has become a minor god. It depends on blood mages now like everywhere else, though raising the dead keeps the number of sacrifices lower. The government is a tyranny, the religion is the death goddess Aecht, and the tongue is Veche. A trade road runs to Sammel through the Third Side: portals at both ends and a demon-permitted road between them, paid for in human sacrifice. The city buys slaves in Sammel and pays in silver, gold and gemstones out of its own mines.

The Triple Enclave. Three frontier towns north of the Fourth District, each walled around a castle and a duke, sharing one group of blood mages and one safe tunnel, the Steel Road. Farsight is on a steppe and the Church of the Eternal God holds it absolutely, its duchess also a cardinal, religion behind every law. Bogtown sits on dry ground in a swamp and exports dreamwort, whose dried seed is a powerful drug; its duke is powerless and the Dream Guild, a criminal organisation with branches in every district, holds the real power. They are rumoured to have a safe-passage treaty with the Horde. Highfall is built into a mountain with a waterfall coming over its walls, and tries to run things the old way: it lends the Dream Guild troops, and takes money and sacrificial slaves from the Fourth District in exchange.

The hives

Mostly they look like gigantic anthills of earth and organic matter, winding inside into tunnels and chambers for larvae and for each of the Horde's roles. Twelve are known.

Xtchaargh is the only hive that will receive human envoys. It has a human quarter in the middle of its tunnels, with ordinary houses and squares, called the Summer Village, where humans who serve the Horde live and train spies to pull enclaves and districts apart. It is said that a delegation from the Fifth District went there shortly before its blood mages were killed.

Chguuth is the main hive, in the south-east, twice the height of any other and reinforced with the chitin of the dead. The enormous flying Skaah that were mistaken for airships all set out from here. The Queen sits here.

The underworld is not empty either. Demons, minor gods and monsters pushed off the surface have strongholds down there. The Queen of the Dark and the Troll King have always been there and do not care for visitors. Other stretches are infested with the Horde.

What people believe

The Church of the Eternal God. The Eternal God is the only true god, and the emperor his only true representative on Firma. Three tenets: purity of soul and body, which means avoiding strong feeling and poisons and keeping yourself trained, and holds that all other gods are impure; obedience, because what the god, the emperor and his priests say is law and questioning it is blasphemy; and solidarity, helping your brothers and sisters in faith and never charging them for it. Its chief saint is Athor, who spread it across Firma, alongside lesser saints like the dragonslayer Vesper, the healer Selma and the martyr Bullius, local saints, and every emperor who has ever lived. Inside it: the Black Circle, a religious secret police; the Holy Shield, its order of knights; the White Brothers and the Black Sisters, its largest orders of monks and nuns; and the Flagellants, the most extreme and frequently violent.

The Church of the Eternal Spirit. A sister church holding that the Eternal God's spirit drifts through all the air of Firma. Same tenets, same saints, plus its founder Vegnar, who was Athor's brother. The two churches are rivals who tolerate each other, with accusations of blasphemy surfacing now and then. Inside it: God's Spirit as its secret police, the Holy Sword as its knights, the Devoted as its monks and nuns, and the Unseen, an order of assassins.

The Five. The largest religion before Athor, known all along the coasts. Sessyfyn the winged fish, goddess of fishing and seafaring. Valtor the two-pointed spear, her brother and husband, god of strength and battle. Fagar the mask of fish scales, their child, the trickster. Vulpet the shark, daughter of Fagar and Sessyfyn, driven out for being so ugly. Brendan the conch horn, son of Fagar and Valtor, who stands for the beauty that can come out of ugly things. Its Mummers walk about in animal masks singing and pressing drink on people; its Berserkers work themselves up with drums and dancing before a battle.

The Grey. Three siblings and three books. The Grey embodies assassination, and the Grey Book is on avoiding discovery and killing quietly. The Purple brews poison, and the Purple Book is recipes. The Yellow stands for lies, deceit and betrayal, and the Yellow Book is misinformation and disguise. The three sects match the three books.

Local gods. Vax, war, in the Sixth District. Parfebe, blood, on Hyrlock. The Primal Mother, fertility, on Solemn. The Fire, fire, at Wolf Peak. The First, vampirism, in Echterhof. Aecht, death, in Saag et Haar.

The minor gods, who demonstrably exist. Perhaps a hundred powerful nature spirits and beings, forced into remote places and underground when Athor took the continent. They grant no miracles, and they are what the school of Spiritualism is built on. Among the known: the Winter Lady, the Queen of the Dark, the Troll King, Baal Fagor, the Land Jelly, the Cat God, Fahlas and Mother Longhand. Cults gather round them: the Silent follow the Winter Lady, the Soot Club try to summon Baal Fagor, and the Dancers use drugs to speak with the Land Jelly.

What a character can become

Your Way is the direction you grow in, and each one ends in specialists the world has names for.

Way of Mind runs to ritual magic and the schools of direct magic: blood, necromancy, elemental, poison, glamour, asceticism, shadow, spiritualism, household, ward and divination. It also covers the trades that are not magic at all: brewers, smiths, jewellers and orators.

Way of Strength runs to the sword, the two-hander, the polearm, the dagger and the empty hand; to formation fighting, crossbows, artillery and muskets; to protection, blunt weapons, gladiators, berserkers, cavalry and sappers.

Way of Shade runs to thieves, assassins, spies, trapmakers and fences; to bards, politicians, archers, pistoliers and duellists; to acrobats and half-mages.

Way of Faith runs to the churches and the gods above, and swearing to one closes the others. Everything a priest does is here: the orders, the saints and the miracles of whichever god you swore to.

Decks you draw from, and decks you keep

Every one of those stacks is a deck of cards, printed the same size, held the same way. What tells them apart is what happens to the deck.

  • Decks you draw from. Action, skill and fate. These are shuffled, drawn from, played and discarded, and you do not get to choose what comes up.
  • Decks you keep. Items, features, conditions, and your spellbook. Nothing in them is shuffled and nothing is drawn. You open the stack and look through it, because a stack is simply how you hold a lot of cards at a table.

A card you keep can still be spent. A one-use item is gone once you use it. What it never does is go into a deck you draw from.

Watch for the two that cross over. Wounds and Taint are not kept. They are shuffled into the decks you draw from, and that is the whole of what makes them frightening.

2. Your action cards

A card carries three lines, and Heavy Swing has all three:

PrimaryStrike +1
SecondaryMove 1
ReactionParry
  • Primary, the strong half. Your main action.
  • Secondary, the weak half. Your second action.
  • Reaction, what the card does on somebody else's turn.

You can never use both halves of the same card. A turn is one card's primary and a different card's secondary.

Any card whose reaction cannot fire is still a Brace: reduce incoming damage by 1. That is deliberately too little to be worth spending a good card on. Its purpose is that no card in your hand is ever completely dead, and it matters exactly once, the hit that would have killed you, by one.

3. A round

  1. Everyone draws one fate card for initiative, once per fight. Highest acts first (Miss is lowest, Critical highest), and ties are redrawn until the order settles. The order then holds for the whole fight.
  2. The GM draws one intent card per kind of enemy, face down.
  3. Each character takes a turn in order.

Your turn

  • Refill your hand to 5 cards.
  • Play one card for its primary and a different card for its secondary, in either order.
  • Both go to your discard pile.
  • The rest of your hand stays in front of you, ready to react.

Two attacks in one turn are legal if they come from two different cards, a Strike primary plus a card that has Strike on its secondary. Those cards are rare, and they are earned by levelling.

4. Reactions

Reactions resolve by themselves. You are not asked. When a trigger happens, the best reaction your hand can offer fires, that card goes to your discard, and play continues.

A reaction fires when the hit is serious, when it would deal a Wound (5 or more past armour) or drop you to 0. A scratch is a scratch, and a hand spent on scratches is a hand you do not have when the real blow lands.

Every reaction, and what it does

Five things can trigger one. Being hit is the common case and the only one with a fallback: a card whose own reaction cannot fire is still a Brace. The other four are an enemy arriving next to you, an ally being hit, a spell being cast, and a check failing, and the last three belong to the casting Ways, because they are printed on a prepared spell rather than on a card in your hand.

A reaction spell has no fallback. It is prepared or it is not, and once it has answered something this fight it is spent. A card that cannot answer is still worth a Brace; a spell that cannot answer simply does not.

Stunned takes all of them, Brace included. So does being Unconscious, and so does being Exhausted. You cannot play reactions has no exception in it.

Three things are always true:

  • A reaction never draws fate. A Parry is your weapon's Defend value, a Brace is 1, a Counter is your weapon's Strike value. Flat, every time.
  • A reaction's damage is not itself a trigger. A Counter hits, and nothing answers it.
  • Reacting is never free. Every card you react with is one more card off your deck at the next refill, and that brings exhaustion closer.

Turning a card sideways

A card turned sideways will not react. That is the whole of the decision, and you take it in advance, at leisure, not in the half-second after the dice would have landed in another game.

5. Resolving an action

Always in this order:

  1. Base value, the item's line for the verb. A Longsword's Strike is 3.
  2. Card modifier, the bonus printed on the action card. Strike +1.
  3. Features, permanent modifiers. Strong: +1 on Strike with Heavy items.
  4. Conditions, temporary modifiers on you or on the target.
  5. Fate, draw one card from your fate deck and apply it.
  6. Their defences, subtract armour, then Block. Never below 0.

Fate is drawn for damage and healing only. Move, Ready and Defend never draw fate.

One draw per action, not per target. An attack that hits several creatures draws once and applies the same answer to all of them.

Bonuses do not stack from the same kind of source

Every card that grants a numeric bonus belongs to one of seven groups: Body, Training, Tool, Make, Power, Station, Wonder. One action takes at most one bonus from each group. Muscle and a tool stack; two kinds of muscle do not.

Whatever supplies the base value is exempt, so a weapon and a feature can never lock each other out.

7. Armour, Block and getting past them

  • Armour is worn. It subtracts from every hit.
  • Block is earned by a Defend, and subtracts after armour. How long it lasts depends on how you played it:
Played asBlock lasts
PrimaryAgainst every attack until the start of your next turn
SecondaryAgainst the next attack only
ReactionAgainst the attack that triggered it

Blocks from different sources do not stack. Use the highest, and that source's duration is the one that applies, so a Defend secondary played behind a Defend primary never shortens the stance you already had.

Some attacks go through armour (ignores N armour, or all of it) and some go round Block. They are different things and a card says which: a bodkin finds a gap in plate, and a shield raised in answer does not care.

Damaged gear. Corrode takes points off armour; Blunt takes points off what a weapon strikes for. Both are permanent until repaired, both stop at 0, and neither destroys anything. Armour at 0 is still armour and still repairable. Repair is an action: a Lore check with the right tools. A Critical puts back 2, a Success 1, a Partial nothing and the time is gone.

Challenges

A big situation is not one check. Getting into a manor, chasing a thief over rooftops, crossing a mountain, talking a duke round: each is a challenge, and the whole party plays into two tracks.

  • Progress, how much you need to achieve.
  • Pressure, how much can go wrong before it fails or turns ugly.

You can watch both of them. While a challenge is running, two bars sit on your tray: Progress 3/6 filling from one side and Pressure 1/4 from the other. You always know how close you are to either ending, and that is the point of showing them, because every turn of a challenge is a decision about whether to push or play safe, and you cannot take that decision blind.

You win the moment the Progress bar fills. You have done the thing, and whatever Pressure is left unspent is how cleanly you did it. A manor entered on one point of Pressure is a quiet burglary; the same manor entered on three is one the household will be talking about for a month.

You lose if the Pressure bar fills first. The alarm goes, the thief is gone, the duke walks out. That is meant to open a new situation rather than close the road, and very often the new situation is a fight.

If both fill on the same check, you win. You got there, and the cost arrives with you.

Neither bar stops at full, so a last check can push one well past the end, and the GM says what the overshoot looks like here.

The GM may say a step needs a particular approach. That is when the right card in the right hand matters, and when the party has to plan as a group rather than as four people taking turns.

Catch Breath in a challenge. Instead of a check you may take a breath, which costs the party Pressure +1, because a breath is time the other side also gets: the guard changes shift, the duke grows impatient. Then shuffle your skill discard back into your skill deck, set 1 card of your choice aside as Spent until your next rest, and draw up to hand size.

Twists

A Partial costs you a twist. A Fail is one.

A twist is on top of the Pressure a Fail already costs, never instead of it.

10. Running out

Your action deck is your stamina. You do not run out of spells. You run out of deck.

  • Exhausted: if you cannot refill to at least 2 cards because your draw pile is empty, you are Exhausted. You may play only one card a turn, and you cannot react.
  • Catch Breath: spend your whole turn, no cards played, to shuffle your discard and move 2 cards at random back into your draw pile. You need 2 in the discard or you cannot do it, and you do not choose which.

A breath buys you exactly one turn that is not Exhausted. It is a way to keep playing, never a way to get back on top. The discard always comes back, so what a breath costs is the turn.

Wounds

You gain 1 Wound when you take 5 or more damage from a single hit (after armour), or when you drop to 0 Health.

A Wound is a card. It goes into your action discard and comes back around like any other. When you draw one it takes a slot in your hand, cannot be played, not even for Brace, and is discarded at the end of your turn. Wounds stay between fights.

Removing one: a long rest takes off 1; a Lore check (Normal 5) with a Healer's Kit takes off 1, once per patient per day; some magic says so.

0 Health

At 0 you are Unconscious: you fall Prone, take no turns, cannot react, and gain a Wound. You are dying until somebody treats you.

13. Levelling up

The GM says when. There is no experience to count.

A level-up is a step on one shared graph. Your frontier is every node that a node you already own points at, and you take one of them. Taking it spends the level and puts that node's own successors on your frontier.

The node is the reward. Nothing is offered and nothing is passed over. Taking the node is taking the reward. What a node gives is one of:

RewardWhat it does
Action cardA card added to your deck, the main currency of growing
TrainingA tool type at its full bonus instead of +1, and the tool is issued with it
ItemA card, handed over

A specialization node gives you a title as well as its reward, what people call you, and the Tactic that comes with it. You only ever have your deepest title's Tactic.

The decision is a direction, not a menu. What you give up is the other directions you could have gone this level, and a branch you walk past is usually still there next time.

Except when it is not. Some nodes belong to an exclusive group: taking one permanently disables every other node in that group, and everything reachable only through them. Swearing to a god is the clearest case. You will be told before it happens.

14. The four Ways

A Way is the direction your character grows in. It is not a class you are locked into: it is which part of the graph you are walking, and the four do genuinely different things rather than the same thing with different numbers.

Way of Strength

Strength breaks. Everything a fighter does uses verbs everybody has, so a Way built on them cannot be identified by its numbers. It is identified by rules nobody else is allowed: your Blocks stack where everyone else takes only the highest, every ally beside you gains Block when you swing, your Parry also strikes the one enemy you named. Its answer to armour is to hit through it, and its characteristic card takes two points off armour by hitting harder.

Strong at standing in the way. It has the most Health and the heaviest armour, it is the only Way whose answer to a defended enemy is to go through the defence rather than around it, and several of its Signatures protect the people next to you rather than you. In a straight fight it is the Way that is hardest to remove.

Weak at everything that is not a straight fight. Its skill deck is thin outside Might, and the armour it wants is paid for in weight: a fighter in plate with a shield and a pack is one bad decision away from Burdened, which is Slowed plus disadvantage on exactly the Agility and Shadow checks it was already worst at. Nothing it does is quiet, and nothing it does is a surprise.

Its specialists are the sword, the two-hander, the polearm, the dagger and the empty hand; formation fighting, crossbows, artillery and muskets; protection, blunt weapons, gladiators, berserkers, cavalry and sappers.

Way of Shade

Shade bypasses. Where Strength hits through armour, Shade goes around it, and its strikes are deliberately the weakest in the game so that not needing to hit hard is the point. It works on openings, states like Off-Balance or Marked that do nothing by themselves and everything to the card that was waiting for one. It sees what is coming, it is worth most against a target that does not know you are there, and one of its cards takes a card out of an enemy's deck and keeps it.

Strong at arriving before the problem does. It has the best Shadow and Agility in the game, it sees what is coming where other Ways guess, and against a target that is unaware or carrying an opening it does damage no other Way can match in one blow. Armour and shields barely slow it down, because it was never trying to get through them.

Weak at a fair fight. Its strikes are mod 0 and minus 1 where Strength's run to plus 2, so a Shade who has arranged nothing is a worse fighter than the Warrior standing beside them, and that is deliberate. It is lightly armoured, it is spending cards to set up the hit rather than to survive one, and an enemy that cannot be surprised or unbalanced takes away most of what it is for.

Its specialists are thieves, assassins, spies, trapmakers and fences; bards, politicians, archers, pistoliers and duellists; acrobats and half-mages.

Way of Mind

Mind studies. Its spells are formulas: learned once, paid for once in your own substance, and then yours. A first Way of Mind node grants 2 spell slots, and a prepared spell occupies one until you prepare something else.

Strong at answers nobody else has. It puts conditions on people, changes the ground they are standing on, hits several enemies at once, reaches further than a weapon does, raises the dead and wards against what is coming. It is the most adaptable Way between fights, because what it can do tomorrow is whatever it prepares tonight.

Weak at doing it twice. Spell slots are few, a first node grants two, and a prepared spell holds one until you prepare something else, so the decision of what you can do is made before you know what you will meet. Several schools charge Health for the privilege. And Silenced switches the entire Way off, because casting needs speech.

Its eleven schools are blood magic, spiritualism, necromancy, elemental magic, poison, glamour, asceticism, shadow magic, household magic, ward magic and divination, with ritual magic for the things that take days rather than a turn. It also covers the trades that are not magic at all: brewers, smiths, jewellers and orators.

No part of it is priestly. A school is studied out of a book, and that is the whole difference between the two casting Ways: what Mind learns, Faith is given. Every god, every order and every miracle belongs to Way of Faith, including the priests who are only lightly magical.

Way of Faith

Faith asks. Its spells are miracles: granted rather than learned, and paid for every single time you ask. A first Way of Faith node also grants 2 spell slots.

Everything priestly is here, and nowhere else. A priest's list is their god's list, and there are twelve: the Eternal God and the Eternal Spirit; Sessyfyn, Valtor, Fagar, Vulpet and Brendan of the Five; the Grey, the Purple and the Yellow; and Vax and the First. A school of magic is something a scholar studies; a miracle is something a god hands over, and no amount of study substitutes for having sworn.

Faith is built out of twelve gods, and this is the one Way where a choice is final. Swearing closes every rival creed permanently, along with everything reachable only through it, and what counts as a creed is the thing to get right: the Five are one creed, the three of the Grey are one, and the four remaining gods each stand alone. So swearing to Sessyfyn leaves the other four of the Five open to you, and shuts the other seven gods for good.

In exchange you carry that god's Obedience, which applies to everything they ever grant. Unlike a Tactic, which is only ever your deepest title's, you carry every Obedience you have sworn to. An order is sworn separately and carries a rule of the same kind, so a priest in an order pays that as well. Six is the most anybody can carry: all five of the Five, and one order.

Strong at holding a party together. Its miracles heal, protect and lift what has been put on somebody, and because a miracle is granted rather than learned it does not have to be studied first. Its gods give standing benefits that are simply true of you from the day you swear.

Weak at flexibility, in every direction at once. A miracle is paid for every single time you ask, not once when you learn it. Its slots are as few as Mind's. Silenced shuts it off the same way. And the Obedience you took on is permanent: it is a restriction on how you may behave for the rest of the character's life, and you carry every one you have sworn, while the doors you closed swearing the first oath never reopen.

So read these before you swear. An Obedience is not a price you pay at a moment, it is a way you have to live, and it is the whole of what makes one priest a different character from another rather than a different spell list. Four of the gods also give something back that nobody else can do.
