# Specializations (Draft 0.3)

Every major node in the graph across the **four Ways**, drawn from the world document
([../worldinfo/Firma.md](../worldinfo/Firma.md)) wherever it has one. The names are mostly the
world's; the **axis** and **cost** columns are the [leveling.md](leveling.md) §5a template applied to
them, and the handful of nodes marked ✱ are additions the audit below argued for.

Two columns carry different weight:

- **Pays in** for a **Way of Mind** school is *written in the world already*. Those are not proposals.
- **Leans on** is mostly a proposal, and the places where it is forced are marked.

## The find: the world already priced magic

Every magic school in Firma states what learning a formula costs, and nearly every one of those costs
is a rule this game already has or has already parked:

| School | The world's price | The rule it already is |
|---|---|---|
| **Necromancy** | each formula learned means **forgetting something else** | **Taint** of the mind, bound (core-rules §8) |
| **Poison** | preparing costs **skill cards set aside equal to the formula's strength** | **Strain**, on the skill deck (core-rules §8) |
| **Shadow** | a permanent **Wild card** in your deck per formula | **Overcast / wild magic** ([ideas.md](ideas.md)) |
| **Blood** | learning costs **damage equal to the strength**; 4–5 is permanent | Health, and **permanent injuries** ([ideas.md](ideas.md)) |
| **Tattoo (Styrka)** | each formula gives the GM a **secret** against you | **Secrets** (skill-rules §5) |
| **Asceticism** | you give up **body parts** | **permanent injuries** ([ideas.md](ideas.md)) |
| **Elemental** | money and materials | downtime (skill-rules §6) |
| **Glamour** | the elves do not teach for free | an **obligation** ([leveling.md](leveling.md) §4) |
| **Spiritualism** | a **pact with a minor god** per formula | an **obligation** |
| **Household** | negotiating with elf-kin | an **obligation** |
| **Divination** *(new)* | a **prophecy** the GM holds, one per formula | a **text obligation** ([leveling.md](leveling.md) §4) |
| **Ward** *(new)* | you may only learn a ward against magic **already used on you** | a **text obligation**, and a campaign |
| ~~Priest~~ | **vows or a pilgrimage**, whatever the school | moved — it is a whole Way now, see below |

> **Formula strength is 1–5**, and that is the spell-slot cost. A strength-3 formula takes three of
> your slots. "Some spells cost more than one slot" was already written in the world; it just was not
> written in the rules.

Four of those costs are the same shape — something owed to whoever taught you — and they are now one
mechanic: an **obligation** is a feature the character **cannot remove**. The GM writes it and the GM
holds it, and no prune, rest or level takes it off the sheet. One answer serving four nodes.

### Forgetting is a Taint card, not a prune

The world's price for a necromantic formula is *forgetting something else*. Read literally that is a
**prune** — delete a card — and reading it literally was a mistake, for four reasons:

1. **A prune is a reward everywhere else** ([leveling.md](leveling.md) §4): it is the only way to
   drop a dead card. A cost that uses the same verb as a benefit asks the player to be hurt by the
   thing they have been taught to want. If the player chooses the card, they bin their worst one and
   the price is nearly nothing; if the GM chooses, it can delete the card the character is built on.
   There is no setting in between.
2. **Deletion is invisible.** A card that is gone is never drawn, never seen, never felt. The table
   notices a necromancer's price exactly once — on the level-up — and then forgets it, which is a
   strange fate for a mechanic about forgetting.
3. **It pushes the wrong axis.** The Necromancer leans on **Preparation**. A forced prune is a
   **Deck shape** effect, so the cost pulls against the identity instead of expressing it.
4. **It only goes one way.** Nothing in the rules returns a pruned card, so the price compounds with
   no ceiling and no exit.

**Taint of the mind does all of it better, and it is already written.** Dark magic is the named
source for skill-deck Taint (core-rules §8), it has a face in the icon set, and it has a threshold
with teeth at 3. It is **dilution instead of deletion**: nothing is lost, the deck you think with
simply fills up with holes, and you meet one every few draws for the rest of the character's life.
That is what forgetting feels like from the inside, and it is felt at the table rather than recorded
on a sheet.

> **A formula puts one bound Taint (mind) card in your skill deck.** Bound means a cure and
> Cleansing will not lift it — *except* by taking the formula with it. Surrender what you learned and
> the hole it left closes.

The exception is the whole design. The price is reversible at exactly the right cost, so a
necromancer is never trapped by an early choice, and the trade is stated in one sentence: **you may
have the knowledge or the clear head, not both.**

Two consequences worth seeing before this is built:

- **At the third formula a necromancer is Afflicted** — max Health −3, plus whatever the affliction
  for this source turns out to be (core-rules §8 holds that list open). That is a real mark and it
  arrives on schedule, which is what the node wants. It is also a quarter of a 12-Health character,
  so the affliction's own effect should be flavour and standing, **not** another number: the −3 is
  already the number.
- **The price is per formula *learned*, not per slot prepared.** So a necromancer's library stays
  short while other schools hoard a book and choose on the morning. Breadth is the thing this school
  cannot have, which is a sharper constraint than any penalty — and it is the Preparation axis read
  from the opposite end.

### Ward Magic: the axis Mind did not have  **[PROPOSED]**

Removing Priest Magic left eleven schools and a hole. The histogram says which hole: **Way of Mind had
no Reactions node at all** — eleven schools and not one of them did anything on another creature's
turn. That is the same gap, found the same way, as the Support gap that produced the Divinator and the
Scout.

A mage who acts on someone else's turn is answering something, so:

| Part | |
|---|---|
| **Title** | **Warder** (*Bannmagi*) |
| **Stance** | You do not cast the first spell in a fight. You cast the last one. |
| **Leans on** | **Reactions** |
| **Pays in** | **Spent.** A ward is held in reserve and burns a card for the fight whether or not anything comes. |
| **Signature** | A card playable on another creature's turn that stops a ✦ Cast before it resolves. |
| **Gives up** | **Everything that is not magic.** Your wards answer spells and nothing else — against a warband with no caster you have paid for an empty hand. |
| **Pays to learn** | **You may only learn a ward against magic that has already been used on you.** Every formula in a warder's book is a scar. |

The learning price is the part worth keeping. It costs the rules nothing, it cannot be shortcut with
money, and it turns the school into a campaign: a warder's book is a record of what the party has
survived, and the GM fills it by using magic on them. It is also the sharpest *gives up* on the list
after the Divinator's — a build that is **useless in some fights** is a real risk, and it is flagged
as one: if Firma's encounters turn out to be mostly caster-free, this node needs widening to *any
incoming effect that draws fate*, which makes it a second Guardian and much duller.



**[OPEN]** The crafts and the two out-of-combat schools do not fit the six-axis template, because the
axes are all about a fight. They need either a second template or a different kind of node.

### Divination: the one deck nobody may touch  **[PROPOSED]**

A diviner peeks — but at *what*? Three of the four obvious answers are already spoken for:

| Peek at | Why not |
|---|---|
| **An enemy's intent card** | That is **Foresee**, and core-rules §3 is explicit: *a caster who can Foresee every round shouldn't exist.* Divination is already listed there as one of the things that may reveal an intent, so a whole school cannot be built on it. |
| **Your own action deck or hand** | That is **Deck shape**, and Tattoo Magic and Asceticism already hold it. |
| **Another player's hand** | Hands are private by design ([../foundry/hud-spec.md](../foundry/hud-spec.md)), and this is a table problem, not a mechanic. |
| **A fate deck** | ← unclaimed, and it is the only hidden randomiser in the game |

**The fate deck is the right target, and the rules have already built the fence around it.** The
mechanism is now a keyword in core-rules §6 — **Read N**, *look at the top N cards of one fate deck
and put them back in any order* — so cards and features reference it the way they reference *Foresee*,
and *Read 1* covers plain looking without a second keyword. Three things written for other reasons
make it work:

1. **Every character has their own 20-card fate deck**, and so does every NPC *type* (core-rules §6).
   So a fate deck is a thing that belongs to somebody, and reading somebody else's is a service.
2. **A Miss or a Critical reshuffles the deck.** This is the whole balance of the mechanic, for free:
   you can aim **one** good draw and never bank two, because the moment the good one is used the
   order you arranged is gone.
3. **The composition is known** — 1 Miss, 1 −2, 4 −1, 8 ±0, 4 +1, 1 +2, 1 Critical — so looking at
   three cards finds one of the three swing cards **about 40% of the time**. The other 60% is worth
   roughly +1 to somebody. Real, but not a guarantee.

#### The node

| Part | |
|---|---|
| **Title** | **Divinator** |
| **Stance** | You do not win the fight. You decide who does, and when. |
| **Leans on** | **Support** — the only axis Way of Mind had no node on, and the honest one: foresight nobody acts on is a feeling, not an effect. |
| **Pays in** | **Someone else's turn.** Everything you set up is cashed by another character, and only if they act before the deck reshuffles. |
| **Signature** | **The Reading** — ✦ Cast, a primary half: **Read 3** (core-rules §6) on any one fate deck — a companion's, or an enemy type's. |
| **Gives up** | **Your own fate.** No divination of yours may look at or reorder **your own** fate deck, ever. The seer is blind to themselves. |
| **Not** | not an intent-reader (that is Foresee), not a deck-shaper, not a damage school |
| **Pays to learn** | **A prophecy**, one per formula: the GM writes down one true thing about your future and keeps it. You may not act to prevent it. |

#### Why it holds up

**It passes the swap test** ([leveling.md](leveling.md) §5a.2) harder than anything else on the list.
Offer *The Reading* to a Blood mage, an Elementalist, a Shadow mage or a Necromancer and every one of
them turns it down, because the one deck it may never touch is their own. A card that cannot help the
caster is useless to eleven of the twelve schools and definitive for the twelfth.

**The subtraction is the identity**, which is what §5a.3 asks for. *Blind to themselves* is one clause,
needs no bookkeeping, is enforceable by eye, and is the oldest thing in the mythology of seers. It
also does the balancing: a diviner who could stack their own deck would be a damage school with a
tell, and the ban is what forces the node onto Support instead.

**The price is the mirror of Tattoo Magic.** Styrka gives the GM a **secret about your past**
(skill-rules §5); Spådom gives them a **prophecy about your future** and forbids you to dodge it. Same
shape, opposite direction, and it stays on §4's loosest rung — a sentence the table enforces, with
nothing to run.

**Its best turn is the enemy's deck.** Fate decks are per NPC *type*, so putting the Miss on top of
the goblin deck fails the next goblin to swing, whichever goblin that is. Core-rules §6 already notes
that precision scales with importance for Blessed and Cursed; divination reads the same scale from
the other end — worth most against a warband, exact against a boss.

#### Three mechanisms considered and not taken

| | Why not the Signature |
|---|---|
| **Read the top card** (look, don't touch) | Information with no commitment is advice. It also slows the table to a crawl, because everyone asks the diviner before acting. Fine as a cheap secondary half; too thin to name a school after. |
| **Avert** (top card of a fate deck to the bottom) | Strong and clean, but it is one denial with no texture, and it is nearly Cursed in reverse. **Better as a deeper node** in the branch. |
| **Hold a draw** (draw a fate card now, face down; the next character to draw fate uses yours) | Genuinely good, and very divination — you took a piece of fate out and spent it later. But it is a token on the table and a rules edge around who-draws-when. **Better as a deeper node**, where it reads as the school's mastery. |

All three are fate-deck shapes, so the branch has somewhere to go: *read* at the trunk, *order* at the
title, *avert* and *hold* beyond it.

> **[OPEN]** The Reading has not been simulated. It is a primary half whose value swings between
> nothing and one guaranteed enemy Miss, which is roughly a Defend — in range on paper, and exactly
> the kind of claim [../sim/](../sim/) exists to check.

## Way of Shade — restructured  **[PROPOSED]**

Firma's Shade chapter is twelve headings with nothing under them, so this is the cheapest of the
three Ways to change — and it needed it most. Three problems, in order of size:

**Three of the twelve do not fight.** Spy, Fence and Politician do nothing in a combat, and a major
node is a fighting identity. Shade therefore had **nine** fighting nodes against Strength's twelve
and Mind's twelve: the symmetry was on paper only. All three come back below as minor-node clusters,
which is what [leveling.md](leveling.md) §4 wants the nodes between titles to be.

**Two of the twelve are weapons.** Archer is a bow and Pistolier is a gun — the same error the
Strength list made eight times. One *ranged* node per Way is a playstyle; two is a weapon list. And
Pistolier's price, everything staked on one shot, **is Assassin's price**, which is why Pistolier kept
colliding with its neighbours in both Ways. It is absorbed.

**Bard had no price at all.** The Pays in column was blank — the tell for a node nobody has decided
anything about yet.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Thief** | Tjuvar | Control | A free hand and a light weapon | Shade |
| **Scout** ✱ | Spanare | **Support** | **Being alone** — ahead of the party, where nobody can reach you | Shade |
| **Duellist** | Duellister | Reactions | **Halves** — a secondary every round to keep your opponent named | Shade |
| **Assassin** | Lönnmördare | Tempo | **Spent** — the opening is a card gone for the whole fight | Thief |

### The shape

Twelve nodes is also the first **tree** written for any Way, because the audit's main lesson was that
uniqueness is a sibling property and a flat list cannot be checked at all:

```
  SHADE            (Position    / halves)
  ├─ THIEF         (Control     / a slot kept free)
  │  ├─ ASSASSIN   (Tempo       / Spent)
  │  ├─ CUTPURSE   (Deck shape  / deck size)
  │  └─ BURGLAR    (Preparation / weight)
  ├─ SCOUT         (Support     / being alone)
  │  ├─ ARCHER     (Position    / range)
  │  └─ TRAPMAKER  (Preparation / a turn spent early)
  └─ DUELLIST      (Reactions   / halves)
     ├─ ACROBAT    (Position    / weight)
     ├─ BARD       (Reactions   / someone else's turn)
     ├─ GAMBLER    (Deck shape  / the fate deck)
     └─ HALF-MAGE  (Preparation / spell slots)
```

**Every sibling set differs on both coordinates**, not merely on the cell, so this Way satisfies the
original strict form of the writing rule and not only the relaxed one:

| Siblings | Axes | Resources |
|---|---|---|
| Thief, Scout, Duellist | Control, Support, Reactions | a slot, being alone, halves |
| Assassin, Cutpurse, Burglar | Tempo, Deck shape, Preparation | Spent, deck size, weight |
| Archer, Trapmaker | Position, Preparation | range, a turn spent early |
| Acrobat, Bard, Gambler, Half-mage | Position, Reactions, Deck shape, Preparation | weight, someone else's turn, the fate deck, spell slots |

Two repeats are left in, deliberately, and both are instructive:

- **Duellist follows Shade on the same resource**, halves, and **Bard follows Duellist on the same
  axis**, Reactions. Neither is a fault: the cell rule governs *siblings*, and a child free to
  deepen its parent is what a branch is **for**. Bard is the Duellist's reactions pointed at somebody
  else.
- **Acrobat and Burglar both pay in weight**, in opposite directions — one carries nothing, one
  carries a kit. They sit in different branches and read as a contrast rather than a collision.

### The find: Way of Shade owes nobody

Every single Way of Mind school states a price to a *person* — an elven debt, a pact with a minor
god, vows, a secret the GM keeps. **Not one Shade node owes anything to anyone.** Their prices are
all mechanical, which makes the whole Way feel self-taught, and it leaves the obligation machinery
([leveling.md](leveling.md) §4) serving five Mind nodes and nothing else.

The world already has the creditor. The **Dream Guild** is *a criminal organisation with branches in
every district* ([../worldinfo/Firma.md](../worldinfo/Firma.md)), it moves dreamwort out of Bogtown,
it is said to hold a treaty with the Horde, and Highfall pays it in troops and slaves. Nobody learns
to pick a lock in that city without somebody knowing.

> **Proposed:** the Thief trunk carries a **Dream Guild obligation**, and it binds in text — §4's
> loosest rung. *They get a cut. They will ask for a favour, and the favour will be inconvenient.*

That one obligation gives the GM a standing hook into any Shade character, gives Fence and Cutpurse
something to be about, and costs the rules nothing to run.

### Spy, Fence and Politician

Demoted, not deleted — as clusters of **minor** nodes along the branches they belong to, carrying
skill cards, training and know-how:

| | Sits on the way to | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| **Spy** | Scout | know-how in courts and crowded cities; training in disguise; the Wits cards a watcher needs |
| **Fence** | Cutpurse | know-how in valuing and moving stolen goods; the contact who buys |
| **Politician** | Bard | know-how in a council chamber; the Presence cards for a room that has already decided |

This is the answer finding 5 wanted and it solves the other half of the problem: a trunk that was
three levels of filler waiting for a title now has three recognisable professions sitting on it.

### What changed

| | |
|---|---|
| **Added** | Scout (Support — Shade had none), Cutpurse, Burglar, Gambler |
| **Absorbed** | Pistolier → Assassin |
| **Demoted** | Spy, Fence, Politician → minor-node clusters |

## Way of Faith — 12 gods  **[PROPOSED]**

A fourth Way. Firma's **Religion** chapter is longer than any of its specialization chapters: six
bodies of worship, sixteen named gods, tenets, saints, holy books, orders of knights, secret police
and assassin sects. All of that was reaching the rules through **one node** called Priest Magic. It is
a Way.

### The gods, and what their miracles are

The world states a **Miracles** line for every god that grants any, and the first draft of this Way
read those as pointers into Way of Mind's schools — Valtor *is* Tattoo Magic, Sessyfyn *is* Elemental
Water, and so on. **That was too tight.** Now that priests have a Way of their own, each god's
miracles are **its own specialization**, written for that god. The world's Miracles line says what the
god's magic is *like*; it is a lineage note, not an equals sign.

| God | Religion | Miracles are like | Of the kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Eternal God** | Church of the Eternal God | Ascetic | denial, endurance, the clean body |
| **The Eternal Spirit** | Church of the Eternal Spirit | Air | breath, distance, the unseen |
| **Sessyfyn** | The Five | Water | the sea, weather, wisdom to the edge of madness |
| **Valtor** | The Five | Strength | fury, the drum, the body made a weapon |
| **Fagar** | The Five | Glamour | masks, seeming, the trick that works once |
| **Vulpet** | The Five | Necromancy | the dead, the outcast, the land beneath |
| **Brendan** | The Five | Earth | growth out of barren ground, slow and certain |
| **The Grey** | The Grey | Shadow | the unseen approach, the silent kill |
| **The Purple** | The Grey | Poison | the dose, the brew, the slow failure |
| **The Yellow** | The Grey | Glamour | the lie, the rumour, the false face |
| **Vax** | Vax of the Sixth District | Strength | war, plainly — the club, the shield, the line |
| **The First** | The First of Echterhof | Ether | blood, the long night, what does not die |

Two things this unlocks, both of them the point of the change:

- **Fagar and The Yellow may both be "Glamour" without colliding.** One is a trickster who deceived
  his own parents once and fathered gods by it; the other is a sect with a *book* of misinformation
  techniques and a network of rumour-spreaders. They share a flavour and nothing else, and under the
  old mapping only one of them could exist.
- **Valtor and Vax may both be "Strength".** One is the berserker's god of fury and drums; the other
  is war as an institution, in the Sixth District, with a spiked club. Again: same word, different
  god, different list.

**The old selection rule — one god per miracle — is retired.** It bought a tidy table and paid for it
by letting an arbitrary constraint pick the roster. The better reasons are the ones the roster now
follows: **The Grey is complete** at Grey, Purple and Yellow, matching its three holy books and three
sects, and **Vax is the cleric-fighter**, which the Way had no room for before. What was given up is
Parfebe's Blood and The Fire's Fire; both remain Way of Mind schools, and an add-on can promote either
god later by declaring its religion.

The cost of the loosening is honest and worth saying: **twelve gods now need twelve short miracle
lists** rather than twelve filters on existing schools. The world sizes that for us — priest magic is
*"a shorter list than a mage's"* — so a god grants a handful of miracles, not a school.

### Twelve gods, twelve prices

Way of Faith is the inverse of Way of Shade. Shade is **narrow and deep** — three titles off the trunk
and nine behind them. Faith is **wide and shallow**: twelve doors at the top, because in this Way *the
choice is the content*, and it is the only Way where the first step closes the others for good.

That breaks the strict writing rule, and it has to. **Twelve siblings cannot lean on seven axes
without repeating**; the pigeonhole principle says so before anyone writes a word. So Faith is
distinguished the other way round:

> **The gods do not differ in what they push. They differ in what they take.**
> Twelve gods, twelve different resources, so all twelve cells are distinct by the resource alone.

| God | Religion | Leans on | Pays in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Eternal God** | Eternal God | Control | **Deck size** — obedience: the faith names what may not be in your deck, and keeps it small |
| **The Eternal Spirit** | Eternal Spirit | Reactions | **Weight** — the spirit drifts through all the air, and carries nothing that anchors it |
| **Sessyfyn** | The Five | Support | **The fate deck** — the sea is not fair: a Miss *and* a Critical, permanently |
| **Valtor** | The Five | Tempo | **Strain** — his strength comes out of your hand, every time you ask |
| **Fagar** | The Five | Control | **Spent** — a trick works once |
| **Vulpet** | The Five | Preparation | **An ally** — she was driven out by everyone; nobody will stand beside you either |
| **Brendan** | The Five | Support | **A turn spent early** — beauty out of barren ground, and nothing he gives arrives quickly |
| **The Grey** | The Grey | Reactions | **Range** — close enough to touch, which is where the knife is |
| **The Purple** | The Grey | Control | **Taint** — you are the first to taste it |
| **The Yellow** | The Grey | Preparation | **Halves** — a lie is planted before anyone arrives, and kept up with a secondary every round |
| **Vax** | Vax | Position | **Health** — war is paid in blood and he counts yours first |
| **The First** | The First | Deck shape | **Spell slots** — the thinnest miracle and the dearest: few slots, and a deck narrow enough to hold them |

All seven axes are covered, no cell repeats anywhere in the four Ways, and two resources — *a slot
kept free* and *someone else's turn* — are deliberately left for the nodes **behind** each god.

**Control went from thin to the busiest axis.** It had 3 nodes across three Ways and now has 6, half
of them here, which answers the question the audit left open about whether Control was narrow or just
unexamined. It was unexamined: obedience, trickery and poison are all *doing something to someone
else's choices*, and a religion chapter is full of that.

### What the Way costs, as a Way

Every god grants its miracles on the same terms, and the world already wrote them: *vows or a
pilgrimage, whatever the school.* So **every node in Way of Faith carries an obligation** on top of
its own price — which is why *an obligation* is not listed as any single god's resource. It is the
entrance fee.

That is the real difference between this Way and Way of Mind, and it is worth stating plainly:

| | **Way of Mind** | **Way of Faith** |
|---|---|---|
| Who owns the magic | you do | the god does |
| When you pay | **once**, when you learn the formula | **continuously**, in how you behave |
| Paid in | your own substance — Health, Taint, secrets, body parts, money | obedience, and whatever the god's order asks this month |
| Who holds the price | the rules | the **GM** |
| If you stop paying | nothing; it is already spent | the miracles stop |

A mage who runs out of money studies less. A priest who breaks their vows has nothing.

### Exclusivity: the religion is the group

**The rule:** taking a god closes **every god of every other religion**, permanently. Gods of your
*own* religion stay open, so a pantheon's worshipper may come back for its other gods later.

> I am reading *"change between polytheistic religions are ok"* as movement **within** a polytheistic
> religion — a devotee of The Five may add Valtor and later Sessyfyn. Say the word if you meant that
> The Five and The Grey should also be open to each other.

Nothing new is needed for this and the earlier *jealous / tolerant* flag is withdrawn: **the religion
is simply the exclusive group**, nested inside the `faith` family. The behaviour a god gets falls out
of **how many gods its religion has** — which is content, not a rule.

| Religion | Gods | Taking one closes |
|---|---|---|
| **Church of the Eternal God** | 1 | every other god |
| **Church of the Eternal Spirit** | 1 | every other god, the Eternal God included |
| **The Five** | 5 | every god outside The Five |
| **The Grey** | 3 | every god outside The Grey |
| **Vax, of the Sixth District** | 1 | every other god |
| **The First, of Echterhof** | 1 | every other god |

The world supports it closely. The Church of the Eternal God's first tenet is *"All other gods are
impure"*; the Church of the Eternal Spirit holds the same tenets, so the sister churches exclude each
other — they *"accept one another tolerably"*, which is not the same as letting you be both, and that
is what a schism is. The local gods are *"a myriad of small religions"*, **plural**: each is its own
religion with one god in it, which is why swearing to Vax ends the question as finally as swearing to
the emperor's god does.

### A religion of one must be worth it

Four of the twelve gods — the Eternal God, the Eternal Spirit, Vax and The First — are alone in their
religion. Choosing one of them is the most restrictive choice in the game: **one door, and no second
god, ever.** So:

> **A religion's size is its price.** Serve a pantheon and you may add its other gods later, a level
> each, going **wider**. Serve a lone god and you never will — so a lone god's branch runs **deeper**,
> and its gift is one no pantheon god gets.

That is a rule rather than four exceptions, and it sets the content constraint: a lone god's branch
should run about as many nodes deeper as its rivals' pantheons have spare doors, or
[leveling.md](leveling.md) §7's depth-consistency goes out of true.

Each lone god's gift bends a rule the others obey:

| God | The gift | What it bends |
|---|---|---|
| **Vax** | **His miracles need no free hand.** The spiked club is the holy symbol. | core-rules §4's free-hand rule — the single thing standing between a caster and a shield. This is what makes the **cleric-fighter** possible, and nothing else in the game does it. |
| **The First** | **His miracles return what they take.** Health drained from a living target comes back to you. | the Health economy — the only renewable pool in the game, and the price of it is written on the node: a narrow deck and few slots |
| **The Eternal God** | **Solidarity.** His miracles cast on a brother or sister in faith cost one less — no Strain, no Spent. | the cost rules, straight out of the third tenet: *"Help your brothers and sisters, and ask no coin."* |
| **The Eternal Spirit** | **The spirit drifts through all the air.** His miracles ignore range and cover. | the Position rules — the one caster for whom distance is not a question |

Each is a **cost waiver** or a **rule exception**, shapes 4 and 10 of §5a.4 — the two that carry the
most identity for the least arithmetic. And each is worth a closed door, which is the test.

> **[OPEN]** Whether a pantheon's **second** god costs a full level. Five doors with four re-openable
> means a devotee of three of The Five has spent three levels on entrance fees; that may be exactly
> the trade, or the second and third may want to be cheaper.

> **[OPEN]** Each god's **Signature** and the nodes behind it. For the eight pantheon gods that is
> still unwritten; the four lone gods have their gift above, which is most of a Signature already.

> **[OPEN]** The **minor gods** grant no miracles, so there is no Faith node for them — they stay
> Spiritualism's business in Way of Mind. Whether a cult of the Winter Lady should nonetheless be
> reachable from Faith is open.

## Way of Strength — restructured  **[PROPOSED]**

### Why this Way felt the least special

The weapon-type problem was the easy half, and it was fixed: the world lists fifteen specializations
and eight of them are *a weapon type*, which the engine already handles through an item's Strike line,
so Sword, Two-handed, Polearm, Dagger, Blunt, Crossbow, Musket and Unarmed were one branch written
eight times. **Roles work where weapons do not** — a Knight and an Infantryman fight differently
whatever they are holding.

Twelve roles later it still read flatter than the other three Ways, and the audit says why. Four
reasons, and none of them is about the names:

1. **Every other Way owns a noun the engine treats specially.** Mind has spell slots and formulas,
   Shade has conditions and the fate deck, Faith has gods and miracles. Strength's nouns are **Strike,
   Defend and Move**, which every character in the game already has. So the natural reward for a
   Strength node is *more of what everyone does*, which is precisely the **+2 trap**
   [leveling.md](leveling.md) §5a.4 exists to prevent.
2. **The resource list was written from Mind and Shade.** It had no entry for a mount, for open
   ground, or for giving up your weapon — three of the most vivid things a fighter can pay. **Our
   vocabulary for this Way was thinner than the Way is**, so half its prices had to be written as
   prose, and prose cannot be checked. Three resources are added in §5a.1 for it.
3. **Strength had no tree.** All twelve were siblings, so the writing rule had to separate twelve
   nodes at once — which is what piled **four of them onto Reactions**.
4. **Strength had no Control node**, in the one Way where grabbing, tripping and shoving people is the
   obvious thing to do. Every other Way had one.

### The fix: one unclaimed lever each

Here is the thing the audit did not expect. **Strength is the Way with the most unclaimed mechanics**,
not the fewest — it only reads flat because nobody had claimed them. Every rule in this list already
exists in core-rules, was written for another reason, and belonged to no node:

| Role | The lever it owns outright |
|---|---|
| **Infantry** | **Adjacency** — the only build in the game that gets better for standing beside someone |
| **Guardian** | **Block**, and the Defend-primary that covers every attack until your next turn (§3) |
| **Ironclad** | **Armor and Overloaded** — carrying past the limit on purpose |
| **Artillerist** | **Blast against Aimed**, and cover (§11) |
| **Tactician** | **Other people's turns** |
| **Executioner** | **The Wound threshold** — 5 or more damage from a single hit (§8) |
| **Knight** | **The mount**, and reach: *Move, then Strike* |
| **Berserker** | **The deck burning down** |
| **Musketeer** | **The loaded state** — a turn spent before the fight, carried into it |
| **Gladiator** | **Being the target** |
| **Street Fighter** | **Two empty hands**, and what you do with them: grapple, trip, shove |
| **Bodyguard** | **Somebody else's position** |

> **A Strength role is special when it owns a rule, not a number.** A bigger Strike is the same build
> twelve times; the only build that can cross the Wound line, or the only one that is better for having
> a friend nearby, cannot be confused with anything.

The two best of these cost nothing to write:

- **Executioner owns the Wound threshold.** Core-rules §8 already says a hit of 5 or more causes a
  Wound — a card shuffled into the target's own deck that cannot be played. **Nothing in the game is
  built around crossing that line.** A role whose whole job is to cross it is instantly legible, and
  it is *why* he holds nothing back.
- **Ironclad owns armor.** Simulation already established that flat armor **cancels** small hits
  rather than reducing them — at Strike 2 against armor 1, half of every hit is gone before fate is
  drawn ([design-direction.md](design-direction.md)). A role built on *the many cannot hurt me* is
  mechanically real and already measured, and the price is written on the node: you carry the armoury,
  and **Overloaded** Spends 2 random cards for it.

### The twelve

| Node | Leans on | Pays in | Absorbs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Infantry** | Position | **An ally** — alone it is an ordinary fighter | Formation, Polearm |
| **Guardian** | Reactions | **A slot kept free** — a hand that will never cast | Protection, Shield |
| **Ironclad** ✱ | Deck shape | **Weight** — over the limit on purpose, and Overloaded for it | *(new)* |
| **Artillerist** | Position | **Ground** — immobile while it fires | Artillerists, Sappers |
| **Tactician** | Support | **Halves** — your secondaries go on their turn, not your damage | *(new)* |
| **Executioner** | Tempo | **Halves** — nothing held back: no Move, no Ready, both hands on the swing | Two-handed, Blunt |
| **Knight** | Position | **Gear you can lose** — a mount, and the room to use it | Cavalry, Sword |
| **Berserker** | Deck shape | **Spent** — the rage burns cards for the fight, and it cannot defend | Berserkers |
| **Musketeer** | Preparation | **Range** — nothing at all up close | Musketeers, Crossbow |
| **Gladiator** | Reactions | **Health** — paid to be the target, and duly hit | Gladiators |
| **Street Fighter** | Control | **A weapon's value** — none at all, and both hands always free | Unarmed |
| **Bodyguard** ✱ | Reactions | **Ground** — you stand where your ward stands | *(new)* |

✱ **Ironclad is new and Vanguard is gone.** Vanguard was *Tempo / first in, and takes what that
costs*, which is a sentence rather than a cost — "goes first" is initiative, which no node owns and
which the Tempo axis already covers. **Ironclad replaces it** because armor, weight and Overloaded
were three live rules with no node, and they make one build rather than three.

**Street Fighter moves from Reactions to Control**, which fixes two things with one change: it takes
Reactions down from four nodes to three, and it fills the Control gap. The move is also more honest
about what the role is — the point of two empty hands is not that you react, it is **what you can do
to someone with them.** Grapple, trip and shove are conditions, and conditions are Control.

**Dagger specialists** are not here: a knife fighter is Way of Shade, and the Thief and Assassin nodes
already carry it.

**Gladiator and Bodyguard are mirrors.** Both react; the difference is *whose* being hit triggers
them. A Gladiator is paid to be the target and a Bodyguard is paid never to be, so the two cannot
share a card between them.

**Tactician brings the seventh axis.** Helping an ally is not any of the six — it is not your tempo,
your position or your reactions, it is theirs. **Support** is what you spend your turn on when the
payoff lands on somebody else's. Infantry is next to it but is not it: Infantry wants an ally beside
it, Tactician makes the ally better.

### The shape

Strength's second tree, and the same rule holds as for Shade — **every sibling set differs on both
coordinates**, not merely on the cell:

```
  STRENGTH              (Position    / halves)
  ├─ INFANTRY           (Position    / an ally)
  │  ├─ GUARDIAN        (Reactions   / a slot kept free)
  │  ├─ IRONCLAD        (Deck shape  / weight)
  │  ├─ ARTILLERIST     (Position    / ground)
  │  └─ TACTICIAN       (Support     / halves)
  ├─ EXECUTIONER        (Tempo       / halves)
  │  ├─ KNIGHT          (Position    / gear you can lose)
  │  ├─ BERSERKER       (Deck shape  / Spent)
  │  └─ MUSKETEER       (Preparation / range)
  └─ GLADIATOR          (Reactions   / Health)
     ├─ STREET FIGHTER  (Control     / a weapon's value)
     └─ BODYGUARD       (Reactions   / ground)
```

| Siblings | Axes | Resources |
|---|---|---|
| Infantry, Executioner, Gladiator | Position, Tempo, Reactions | an ally, halves, Health |
| Guardian, Ironclad, Artillerist, Tactician | Reactions, Deck shape, Position, Support | a slot, weight, ground, halves |
| Knight, Berserker, Musketeer | Position, Deck shape, Preparation | gear, Spent, range |
| Street Fighter, Bodyguard | Control, Reactions | a weapon's value, ground |

The three trunk nodes are **how you stand, how you strike, and who you fight in front of** — the line,
the kill, the crowd. Each is the broadest version of its branch, which is what a trunk should be, and
the fan is uneven on purpose: four hang off the line because a line has more jobs in it than a duel
does.

Twelve nodes: three on Position, three on Reactions, two on Deck shape, and one each on Tempo,
Preparation, Control and Support. Reactions came down from four, Control came up from nothing, and
all seven axes are covered.

### Block and Brace, since Guardian's lever rests on them

Both are fully specified already — **Block** has its own subsection in core-rules §4 and **Brace** is
defined in §2 with its order of resolution in §3. Two earlier `[OPEN]` flags here claimed otherwise
and were simply wrong.

**Block is in good shape, and it is the right lever to hand a node.** The division it draws is the
useful one: **armor is what you wear and Block is what you do.** Armor is flat, permanent and
passive; Block comes from a verb, costs a half, and expires. That is exactly what keeps a Guardian
from being *a fighter with more armor*, which is the failure this Way was most at risk of.

The three durations are the good part, and they were settled by simulation rather than taste:

| Played as | Covers | |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | every attack until your next turn | worth **+3.3 points of win rate** in the hardest fight ([design-direction.md](design-direction.md)) |
| **Secondary** | the next attack | a one-off |
| **Reaction** | the attack that triggered it | a one-off, on somebody else's turn |

The cost decides the coverage, which is the cleanest shape a mechanic can have.

**Brace is correctly small, and the rules now say so.** One point is deliberately not worth a card, so
anyone holding a real reaction will never Brace — and that is the design, not a weakness. Its job is
that no card in a hand is ever completely dead, which is why nothing can block it. Raising it to 2
would put it in competition with Parry and Block and flatten the reaction choice.

> **Therefore: Brace is withdrawn as a candidate lever.** A node that Braces *harder* is a bigger
> number on a thing every character already has — the +2 trap exactly. Brace is universal by design,
> so it is the one mechanic in the game that **must not** become anybody's identity.

### Proposed: the Guardian's Signature

Block carries one universal restriction, and it is the only place in the defensive rules where an
exception would be felt immediately:

> **Blocks from different sources don't stack. If you have more than one, use the highest.**

So:

> **The Guardian's Blocks stack.** They are the one exception to highest-wins.

That is **Signature shape 9** — *a bonus group only you may stack in* ([leveling.md](leveling.md)
§5a.4) — and it earns the slot on every test. It is one sentence. It adds no number and no keyword. It
cannot be handed to a sibling, because Ironclad, Artillerist and Tactician would each rather have
almost anything else, so it passes the **swap test**. And it changes what a Guardian *does* with a
turn rather than how big their shield is: a shield's Defend and a card's Defend secondary become worth
holding at the same time, which is a decision nobody else at the table has.

It also has a natural ceiling, which is why it is safe to give away: Block is cleared at the start of
your turn, so stacking is bounded by how many defensive halves you can spend in one round, and every
one of them is a half not spent attacking. The Guardian pays in **a slot kept free** and now in tempo
as well, which is the shape the node always claimed.

> **[OPEN]** The world document still lists fifteen weapon types for this Way. It needs the same pass
> as Shade's chapter once these restructures are accepted.

2. **Spell-slot cost is already written**: formula strength 1–5.
3. **`Armborstspecialister` appeared twice** in the world document. Counted once.
4. **All four Ways are twelve nodes wide.** The world document's own tally was 15 Strength *weapon

   clusters of ordinary nodes along a branch rather than titles of their own — which also gives the

# Review: the axis is the aisle, not the item

Three devices are meant to keep these nodes apart: the **axis**, the **resource**, and the writing
rule in [leveling.md](leveling.md) §5a — *siblings lean on different axes and pay in different
resources*. Auditing the list above against that rule is the first time it has been tested, and it
found both a weakness in the device and two outright breaches.

## 1. The axis cannot carry it alone

Forty-seven of these nodes fight, across four Ways. Seven axes:

| Axis | At the audit | Now |
|---|---|---|
| **Position** | 9 | 8 |
| **Reactions** | 6 | 8 |
| **Preparation** | 4 | 8 |
| **Control** | 3 | 7 |
| **Deck shape** | 3 | 7 |
| **Support** | 1 | 5 |
| **Tempo** | 6 | 4 |
| | **32** | **47** |

Nine nodes "leaned on Position". Tell a player that and they have learned nearly nothing: it is the
aisle, not the item. **With 47 nodes and 7 axes the pigeonhole principle alone guarantees
collisions**, so no amount of care in writing the axis column will separate these builds. The axis
says which lever a build pulls; it was never going to say what the build *is*.

What the histogram turned out to be good for is finding **missing** nodes, which is not what it was
built to do. Every one of the five additions above was found this way:

| Gap the histogram showed | What filled it |
|---|---|
| **Support** had one node anywhere — the Tactician, in the Way where helping an ally is incidental | **Divinator** in Mind, **Scout** in Shade; Support is now one or two per Way |
| **Preparation** was defined as "spell slots", so it could exist in only one Way | widened to *anything decided before it matters*; Trapmaker, Musketeer and Burglar moved onto it |
| **Way of Mind had no Reactions node at all** — eleven schools, none that acted on another creature's turn | **Ward Magic** |
| **Control** sat at 3 and might have been narrow or merely unexamined | unexamined: Faith took it to 6 — obedience, trickery and poison are all interference in someone else's choices — and Strength to 7, because grappling people was missing from the Way that does it with its hands |
| **Way of Strength had no Control node** and four on Reactions, because it had no tree and all twelve were siblings | a tree, and **Street Fighter** moved to Control: what two empty hands are *for* is grabbing somebody |

The spread went from 9:1 at its worst to **8:4** — flat enough that no axis is now either a dumping
ground or an empty shelf, and four of the seven sit within one node of each other. That is the
histogram's whole job, and it is done; from here the work is Signatures, which no count can check.

One last thing it turned up, and the only one that was about the **rules** rather than the roster: the
resource list in §5a.1 was written out of Mind and Shade, so it had no word for a mount, for open
ground, or for giving up your weapon. Way of Strength's prices came out as prose because of it, and
prose cannot be sorted. **When a Way's costs keep arriving as sentences, suspect the vocabulary before
the Way.**

Two of the re-homings above came straight out of the count. **Preparation was defined as "spell
slots"**, which confined it to Way of Mind and left Position carrying every build that was really
about deciding early: a Trapmaker's whole identity is turns spent before anyone arrives, and a loaded
gun is a turn you already spent. Widening Preparation to *anything decided before it matters*
([leveling.md](leveling.md) §5a) moved two nodes off the overloaded axis and gave two Ways access to
one they could not previously reach.

## 2. The writing rule was already broken, twice

Normalising the **Pays in** column into a closed set of resources turns up two pairs that are the
same node written twice:

| | Axis | Pays in | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pistolier** (Shade) | Tempo | Reloading | ← identical |
| **Musketeer** (Strength) | Tempo | Reloading | ← identical |
| **Acrobat** (Shade) | Position | Carries nothing heavy | ← identical |
| **Skirmisher** (Strength) | Position | Carries nothing heavy | ← identical |

Both pairs were legal under the writing rule as written, because the rule only compares **siblings**
and these sit in different Ways. Both are nonetheless indefensible: a player choosing between Ways
reads the whole list, and finding the same build in two chapters tells them the Ways are decoration.
Skirmisher is deleted, Musketeer re-homed, and Pistolier — which caused both halves of the first
pair — absorbed into Assassin when Way of Shade was restructured.

The useful part is *how* they were found. They were not spotted by reading; they were spotted by
writing the two columns as a pair and sorting. **That is a check a machine can run**, and it is the
first device below.

## 3. Uniqueness is local, except when it is not

A node deep in a branch inherits everything above it. Assassin does not need to be distinguishable
from Artillerist — nobody will ever compare them, because reaching one means not having walked to the
other. It needs to be distinguishable from **Burglar and Cutpurse**, the nodes it is actually weighed
against on the level-up where it appears.

So uniqueness is a **sibling** property, and the demand it places on content is far smaller than
thirty-one-way distinctness. Two sets with teeth:

- **Hard rule — siblings.** Nodes sharing a predecessor must occupy different cells. Violation is a
  content bug and the graph validator should refuse it ([leveling.md](leveling.md) §8).
- **Warning — a whole Way.** A repeated cell inside one Way is allowed but suspicious; across Ways it
  is what the two pairs above were. Worth a warning, not a refusal.

## 4. Where the identity actually lives

If the axis is the aisle, the **Signature** is the item — and it is the one part of the six that has
not been written for a single node yet ([leveling.md](leveling.md) §5a). That is the real finding of
this review: **the uniqueness problem and the Signature gap are the same problem.** Everything else
on a major node is a label.

Four devices are now in §5a to make Signatures hard to confuse, in the order they cost to apply:

| Device | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The cell** | (axis, resource) as a pair, from closed lists. Mechanical, sortable, and it caught both breaches above. |
| **The swap test** | Offer the Signature to a sibling. If it would work there, it is not a signature. |
| **One thing you give up** | Every major node subtracts something in rules. Additions stack and blur; subtractions do not. |
| **Signature shapes** | Ten kinds of thing a Signature can be, two per Way. Stops a Way from being ten cards that all add +2. |

The third is the one with the most leverage and the least writing. The two most vivid nodes on this
whole list are **Berserker** (cannot defend) and **Street Fighter** (no weapon value at all), and
both are vivid for the same reason: you can describe them by what they have given up. Nobody confuses
a build that cannot do something.

## Open

  gods of Way of Faith, and the sub-group is the **religion** — which is all the theology the rules
  need, since a god's restrictiveness then falls out of how many gods its religion has
  ([leveling.md](leveling.md) §3). Each god grants its own short miracle list, exactly as the world
  says priest magic is *"a school according to their religion"*.
- **[OPEN]** Whether Spy, Fence, Politician and Bard stay as Shade **titles** or become clusters of
  minor nodes, the way the crafts probably should (finding 5). Spy and Politician in particular do
  nothing in a fight, and a major node is a fighting identity.
- **[OPEN]** Each node's **Signature** — the one card or feature it is named for. That is where the
  axis gets earned, and it is the next thing to write.
- **[OPEN]** Which specializations hang off which trunk node, and how deep each branch runs.
