# Player's Handbook

Everything you need to play a character. Nothing here is for the GM, and nothing here explains why
a rule is the way it is. You never need that at the table.

**The short version.** You have a deck of cards that is your abilities, and a **stamina** that is
how many of them you can play. Each turn you play two. When you attack, you draw one card of luck.
Your deck comes round again for nothing; what runs out is the stamina, so you tire not because you
were hurt, but because you have been fighting.

---

## 1. The world

**The year is 1034, and humanity lives behind walls it pays for in blood.**

### The Sides

Firma is one of several worlds laid over the same ground. The mountains and lakes of one stand in
the same places on the next; what differs is who lives there and what they have done to it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **The First Side** | Firma. The normal world, and the one you are standing on. |
| **The Second Side** | Where the elves went. The same geography, smeared with glamour that makes everything look more beautiful than it is. |
| **The Third Side** | Where the demons went after Athor's armies beat them. They like to destroy things, so it is in worse condition: lava dug up out of the ground, jagged black mountains raised where there were none. |
| **The Fourth Side** | The land of the dead. The accounts contradict each other so completely that nobody can say whether it is a paradise, a hell or a shadow of here. Only the dead are let in. |
| **A Fifth** | Rumoured, along with a Sixth and a Seventh. Nobody has proved any of them. |

**Two crossings are known to stand open permanently.** The elves keep a gate to the Second Side in
the heart of Velderon, and their staff come and go through it; nobody else goes through without an
invitation. The necromancers of Saag et Haar keep a portal of their own, matched by one in Sammel,
with a demon-permitted road between them: a dusty track through desert, its edges marked by poles
topped with skulls. Stay inside the markings and pay what the crossing costs, one human sacrifice
per journey, and you come out the other end. Sandstorms are common, and people who wander off the
road are not seen again.

### How it came to this

Humans took the continent slowly. The elves withdrew to the Second Side and the demons to the Third.
Monsters and nature spirits were driven into remote places, and the trolls, the dragons and the
minor gods went down into their long sleep.

The old reckoning starts at the year 0, when the warrior-king **Athor** pushed his empire to the
coast in every direction. It held for a hundred years and then cracked, and for five hundred years
after that the pieces fought over where the lines between them ran. Regions drifted apart. The
assassins founded their state in the oasis of the Hyena Desert, the blood mages established
themselves on the island of Hyrlock, and the Strigoi princes organised to grow their undead power.
In the old year 674 the counting started again at 0.

**Airships came out of the sky in that first year, and nobody has ever learned from where.** Another
world, or a land across the sea. What came out of them is called the **Horde**: insect-like, of one
shared mind, specialised into roles the way ants are. Everyone in it always knew what everyone else
was doing. Humanity was divided and did not.

The war has lasted a thousand years. Humans won battles and lost cities, and the direction never
changed. The Strigoi princes made their own pact with the Horde and kept their neutrality.

**In year 87, with everything nearly lost, humanity made a pact with the blood mages of Hyrlock.**
Six great territories, the **districts**, were ringed with a protective ritual powered by thousands
of human sacrifices. The isolated **enclaves** were each given blood mages of their own to renew
smaller barriers every year. The Horde had the world, but what was inside the rings was safe.

What followed was attrition. The Horde strangles the roads between the enclaves and hunts the
caravans that leave the barriers; humanity raids the **hives**. Tunnels were dug so that one safe
place could reach another, and the network grew until parts of it were forgotten. Monsters took
sections of it, landslides let the Horde into others, dissidents built their own communities out in
the dark, and here and there the digging broke into elven ruins older than anyone's records.

**The sacrifices were never popular.** In 879 a mob in the Furnace District beat its blood mages to
death. A month later the Horde came through the gap, and the district fell.

That is the arithmetic you live in. Five districts and some twenty enclaves, a thousand years of it,
and rumour this year says the Horde is close to getting through a ring that still holds.

### The shape of it


One continent, and you can hold the whole of it in your head.

**The middle is a plain**, ringed by the **Spear Mountains**, and the oldest of the safe places sits
inside them. **West** is coast and islands, cold and wet, and that is where the fishing is.
**South-east** is rolling hills, warm, and that is where the wine is. **South** is the **Hyena
Desert**, with the mineral-rich **Mouse Mountains** standing over its northern edge. **East**, and
furthest from everything, is forest: dense wet conifer, lakes and bogs, with trolls in it and
dragons over it. The **north-east** was the workshop of the continent until it fell, and the roads
that ran through it are the ones nobody uses any more.

**The mountains have names and so do the ranges nobody goes to.** The **Spear Mountains** are the
ring around the Core plain, and because they stand next to the cradle of civilisation their inner
slopes are strewn with the ruins of towns, mining camps and villages; Steinbrëk is the last of them
still lived in. **Spear Pass** cuts through them and used to be a busy road. The **Mouse Mountains**
are a forerunner of the Spears, low and mineral-rich, standing over the north edge of the desert.
**North Wall** is a north-westerly spur, young and sharp-peaked, with abandoned mines strung along
it. **South Wall** stands on its own in the south-west, the same high sharp rock, never prospected
and never mined.

**The dry country is in the south.** The **Hyena Desert** is the only real desert on the continent:
sand, hot days, cold nights, three oases and nothing else that holds water. East of it lies **Lost
Lake**, once a living salt lake, which the Horde dried out so it could plant its main hive in the
middle of the bed.

**One sea goes round the whole continent, and sailors name the stretches of it separately.** The
**Kelp Sea** off the west is fast and stormy, named for the giant kelp whose rafts show which way
the current runs. **The Breaks** in the south-west throws up ten-metre waves without stopping and
cut the cliffs of South Wall. **Athor's Bay** in the south is calm, named for the beach where
Athor's body was landed on its way to burial in Geistburg, and had idyllic fishing villages along it
before the invasion. **The Tidals** run calm with enormous tides from the Amber coast down to the
Ruby. The **Troubled Sea** in the south-east killed sailors routinely, which is why Solemn stayed
undiscovered so long despite being close in. **Everfog** in the north-east is windless and full of
fog. **Windy Waters** in the far north is as violent as the Troubled Sea.

**And the green and the wet.** **The Wild** is the great eastern conifer forest, taking in the whole
Amber District and running west to the Spears and south towards Echterhof: rapids, bogs, lakes and
rocky knolls in a natural labyrinth, with broadleaf trees mixed in at its southern end. **Moss
Forest** is something else: enormous oaks over tree ferns and thorn thickets, avoided by humans
before the invasion and avoided by the Horde since, and said to lie close to the other Sides, which
is why minor gods like **the Cat God** and **Mother Longhand** are named with it. The northern
tongues of land are wetland, with **Troll Bogs** in the middle, where **the Troll King** is said to have
ruled the continent from caves beneath the mire before either elves or humans arrived, and the drier
**Thornmoor** to the east, heath with the odd lake and copse. The **Spray Islands** in the north are
bare wind-beaten rock nobody has ever lived on, and are held to be where **the Winter Lady** stays
when she is not walking south with the frost.

**It is three layers, and you will travel in all of them.**

- **Inside the rings.** Five districts and some twenty enclaves. Safe, because the blood magic says
  so, and paid for every year in people.
- **Outside them.** Everything else, which is the Horde's. The roads between safe places are
  strangled and the caravans that leave are hunted, so open country is crossed only by those who
  have decided the crossing is worth it.
- **Underneath.** The tunnels, dug so that one safe place could reach another, with underground
  cities and harbour villages strung along the named roads. Parts have been forgotten, parts have
  been taken by monsters, landslides have let the Horde into others, and here and there the digging
  broke into elven ruins. Below even that is the underworld, which has its own occupants and does
  not want visitors.

**So distance is not measured in miles.** It is measured in whether there is a road, whether the
road is still open, and what it costs to go anyway. Two towns a week apart by tunnel can be
neighbours, and two a day apart overland can be unreachable.

### The five districts that are left

**The Core.** Inland, on the fertile damp plain inside the Spear Mountains. The climate is mild and
the winters are short and heavy with snow, and meandering rivers cross the farmland on their way to
the **Silver Lake**, which is thick with fish. There is an island in the middle of that lake, the
**Isle of Spirits**, and it is the only wild ground left inside the ring: ordinary people avoid it
and the priests will tell you it is a blasphemous place. The only mountain country in the district
is in the north-east around the mine town of **Steinbrëk**. The district protects **Geistburg**,
Athor's old capital, five satellite cities and the farmland between them, and the realm is still
called Denaria.

**Nobody is in charge of the Core, which is not the same as saying nobody rules it.** The Church is
the strongest power and the mages' academy the second. The emperor and his court hold the formal
power and not the ability to use it. The **Guild**, which speaks for craftsmen and farmers, is
strong enough to fight the others over land and frequently does. Every one of them keeps its own
courts, so which law you are under depends on who claims you. The Church of **the Eternal Spirit** makes
the tangle worse. The poor are held down hard, and one grouping, the **Weasels**, runs a slow,
patient resistance against everyone above them, strongest out in the satellite cities, which are
mostly the Church's ground.

**The capital is built on one huge mesa**, with the rich and noble inside Geistburg proper, slums
over the rest of the rock, and ordinary people out in the satellite cities. Four cable cars and two
stairs cut into the stone connect the top to the plain; upper Geistburg is rich, depraved and
corrupt, and its intrigues run for years between the Church, the mages, the noble houses and the
embassies of every other district and enclave. Lower Geistburg feeds the upper, and sprawls from
slum to market to the odd minor-noble castle. Out on the plain, the mages' academy and the cathedral
of the Church of the Eternal God stand apart, each grown into a small town of its own around its
magic or its religion, which is how hypocrisy and religious persecution come to walk together.
**Academy** is one tall tower with a settlement around it, run by a mage titled the **Magus**.
**Cathedral**, despite the name, has two of them, the grander for **the Eternal God** and the slightly
less grand for **the Eternal Spirit**, each with its own forts and monasteries of warrior-monks facing
the other across the same city. Of the satellites, **Gradenbosch** is unwalled and agricultural, and
its baron keeps his chair with the Academy's help; **Hirmer** sits midway between Cathedral and
Academy and is pulled apart by both, which has left it a city of whispers and hidden agendas where
the Weasels are strong; **Erleng** is ruled from three castles by three noble families who pass the
barony between them on a rota that has held for centuries, and breeds more horses than anywhere in
the Core, most of them sent on to Regensburg to be trained; **Fynch** farms, keeps the monastery of
the Flagellants, and has a religious court big enough to handle the volume of death sentences their
influence produces; and **Steinbrëk** holds the Core's only mines, shafts bored centuries deep into
the Spears, and has kept itself apart for so long that the politics of the plain barely reach it.
One quarter of Steinbrëk belongs to an engineering guild of Furnace refugees.

**The Fiord.** The westernmost of the three great fiords of the northern coast, where the sea cuts
deep inland, with a few islands lying close enough in to belong to the district and six enormous
lighthouses marking where the ring runs. It is a wet place, and inside the coastal cliffs, which in
places drop sheer and high into deep water, the ground is damp treeless heath, most of the way
across. The capital is **Black Harbour**, which shares the district with a handful of fishing
villages and one fortified northern town, and the region used to belong to the Salt Barons, an
association of fishing magnates, smugglers and pirates. Black Harbour is built of timber that has
weathered black, which is where the name comes from: a fish-stinking snarl of alleys and dark houses
in which even the manor houses look shabby. The old faith of the Five is strong here and the Church
of the Eternal God is considered blasphemy. It lands most of the continent's fish. The **West
Road**, a broad tunnel with underground cities along it, ties it to the Core.

**It is a clan society of twelve families**, each of them working to grow its own power at the
others' expense. The law is a council of family representatives that sits once a year, or sooner if
called. Inside its own ground a family may judge and punish as it likes; anything that touches
another family has to go up to the council. **Blood feud is an accepted principle of law**, not a
breakdown of it. The poor are parcelled out under the clans and have no common voice at all, and the
priests here put the family first, before the faith and before you.

**The Ruby.** Rolling hills in the south-east, warm, famous for wine and fruit, where the rich once
built winter palaces. Sun and salt easterly winds made it the holiday country of the continent
before the invasion, and the vineyards run down to a shallow bay of sandbars and big tides. The
coast feeds it fish and the farmland the rest, but wine is what it is known for. The **South Road**
runs to the Core District, and the underground city of **Sol** is where the rich of the Core and the
Ruby go to enjoy themselves, with a reputation that everything is permitted there.

**Religion and politics are relaxed, and money is not.** The district is three regions with one city
each, and each region holds one seat on the council. Violent crime is rare and conflict rarer. The
economy is good enough that the middle class outnumbers the working class, and even the workers live
better than they would anywhere else. What the governing care about is income, and a law that
reduces it does not stay law for long. The old glory still shows in mosaic cobbles, white marble
palaces and gold ornament that nobody has repaired for a century. **Tanto** sits at the centre and
is comfortably the dullest of the three, which is exactly why it was given the council to house: a
city of clerks and trade whose marble spreads wide and impresses nobody. **Serf** is the fashionable
one, with an opera house, a theatre gilded in every detail and a seafront promenade of pavilions.
**Damza** lies on a muddy shore and has made that into spas and beach life; the food there is held
to be the best on Firma, and rich visitors come to take mud baths, eat and drink.

**The Iron.** Two great oases of the Hyena Desert, where underground cisterns reach the surface, and
the mineral-rich Mouse Mountains above them, which the ring covers in the north and the east. Iron
gave the district its name and is the least of what comes out of the ground here. The days are dry
and hot and the nights are freezing. **Sammel**, in the northern oasis, is the only large city and
nearly matches Geistburg; out at the western oasis stands **Lempak**, a holy town, thinly peopled,
holding a necropolis of pyramids, crypts and whole parks of sarcophagi for rulers long dead. The
rest of the desert is desolate but for a couple of caravan camps where there is water. **The Grey**
and his siblings are worshipped here.

**It is a theocracy, and it does not tell you who runs it.** The Grey rule centrally and have handed
the local power down to Emirs, who are puppets and are not pretending otherwise. The law is
invisible and arbitrary; people disappear and leave no trace, and because the rulers are unnamed
there is nobody to hold to account for it. The **thrall system** keeps the mines full of workers who
die young, and none of that is visible in Sammel. The city is built as walled quarters called
**Rote**, each shut off from the next, with the oasis canals drawn through them; the old assassin
state holds the central one, around the **Ziggurat**, a step pyramid that is the tallest building
in the region and the seat of an emir who is their puppet. Its embassies sell services under the table in every district but the Amber. The **East Road**
connects it to the Core and is the least safe of the roads, because the mining lets monsters and the
Horde break in constantly. The **Night Express** runs it anyway, hauling freight and passengers
through tunnels black with its own coal smoke.

**The Amber.** The far east, the second largest district and the one that was never entirely tamed.
Dragons turn up. Trolls live in the forests and the mountains. The land comes up sharply out of the
sea; the tides are as strong as the Ruby's, but the water just offshore is deep and cold, so instead
of sandbars there is bare rock down to the waterline. Dense wet conifer forest covers most of it,
mossed and lichened over, broken by deep fissure lakes, steep wooded hills and the odd swamp, with
small settlements, forts and villages scattered through it. Timber was the export until the Furnace
fell and took the routes with it, and now the inland capital **Karmat**, two small coastal towns and
a scatter of villages are all there is of it.

**Nobody leads the Amber.** People take the law into their own hands, and the local mayors, those
forest barons who still have a name worth holding, and the local warlords all work to keep and
extend what they have. Every town runs itself and the mayor's chair changes hands bloodily and often, and
in **Lenden**, in the lawless south, the mayors tend not to last. **Farsee** in the north is the
exception that holds: a larger city under one noble family, beset constantly by warlords and forest
barons, and hard to take because it stands on a hill on the coast. Karmat is in decline and sells
the one thing it still can. It is neutral, so everyone uses it: it takes in young people from across
the district, equips and trains them as fighters at its military academy, and feeds its own smelter
to arm them. At the end of the training a lot is drawn, and **a tenth of each intake**, the
**Tithe**, is formed into squadrons and auctioned to the companies in the city for onward sale to
the rest of Firma through the Iron District. A squadron of hard soldiers fights its way through the
wilderness to the Iron once a year. The arrangement pays Karmat's bills, and the steady supply of
trained soldiers is also what keeps the Amber's wars running. The city council manages the rest with
bribes, corruption and cunning, and tries very hard not to be in conflict with anyone. **The Amber
is the one district with no tunnel of its own**, so everything that comes or goes crosses Horde
ground, quietly. The war god **Vax** has the most followers, and nobody minds what you believe.

**The Furnace is gone.** It lay in the north-east in tidy conifer forest broken up with farmland,
with the windless **Everfog** coast taking the edge off the northern weather. Steam trains ran on
rails between its cities and there was never a time when you could not see at least one airship in
the sky. It was the centre of smithing and manufacture, with an alchemical academy and a centre of
magical research called the Rune Temple in the labyrinth city of **Weder**, once the third largest
city on the continent and built a dozen levels down as well as up. Technology and magic mattered
there and religion did not. The district was organised around its engineer groups, with the Rune
Temple supplying the magical components they built into their work. The Furnace built the Iron's
trains, the Fiord's sea defences and the Core's cable cars.

**Then the ritual stopped, in 879.** Forest and cities were levelled into grey bog, and the burnt
abandoned trunks still standing in it are the only sign the ground was ever wooded. Weder is ruins,
and the **North-East Road** that served it, a twin-tracked technological marvel with three towns
strung along it, closed when the district fell; its Core end at Erleng is bricked up behind a
warded vault door. Many engineer groups got out, and have carried on in other districts and enclaves
as self-governing units, usually with a mage or two among them. They kept their language,
**Valgar**, and their lack of religion, which still causes trouble in the Core District, but their
skills are wanted badly enough that authorities and churches look the other way about how well a
guild squares with local law.

### The enclaves

Some twenty of them hold out on small territories with the Horde all around. These are the ones
everybody has heard of.

**Hyrlock.** The island of the blood mages, feared or revered depending on who you ask, a week's
sailing from the abandoned ports west of the Core District. It is a granite plateau standing out of
the restless Kelp Sea, and one stair hewn into the rock is the only way up from the plain little
harbour at its foot. The top is barren and grows nothing, so everything but fish is shipped in
across Horde-held water. It is a matriarchy in which women hold every role that matters, and a caste
society under the city of **Mander Cliff**: mages at the top, then administrators and merchants,
then workers, and beneath them the thralls, who are bred to be spent in sacrifice. Power radiates
from the **Red Tower**, fifty metres of it at the cliff's summit. The faith is of the blood goddess
**Parfebe**. Every other district and enclave pays Hyrlock an annual tribute in goods and thralls to
keep the wards up, and everyone calls it the **blood tax**.

**Kelp Bay.** It was a nothing port before year 0 and is now the only one left on the west coast,
which makes it the single way in to Hyrlock and the blood tax. The freight traffic has used up every
square inch of the place: the cliffs inside the ward have been hollowed out into caves for
warehouses, shops and bars, and the houses on the thin strip of beach have been built layer on layer
until several of them stand five storeys. It is stressed, raw, and almost always in the cliffs'
shadow. Three shipping companies run the voyages in an unstable cartel, and the pay for surviving
one draws adventurers. The harbour is governed by a captain voted in each year who may not hold the
office two years running; the three companies take it in turn to put a captain up, and the other two
abstain when it is not their turn. Fog moves through cobbled alleys lit blue for bars, red for
brothels and green for business best not described. The smell is rotting bodies, spilt beer and
cheap perfume, under the fermenting kelp. The **Coast Road** runs from here to Farsight, which is
the only way out that is not a ship.

**Solemn.** A white limestone island out in the wild Troubled Sea south-east of the Ruby District,
home of the witches, a sisterhood with a magic they keep to themselves. It has the same shape as
Hyrlock, a plateau rising out of the water, with limestone instead of granite and a warm climate
instead of a cold one, and like Hyrlock it is the women who rule. They follow the **Primal Mother**,
a fertility goddess, seldom leave the island, and run it as a well-ordered, self-sufficient place.
They are one of the few enclaves that needs no blood magic: their own ward holds, and nobody outside
knows how. It is guessed to have something to do with how thin the island's borders are to the
Second and Third Sides, and the island is said to have better contact with them than anywhere else.
Their city **Sonebra** sits walled at the top, all towers and spires, and its architecture is more
elvish than human.

**Regensburg.** The militaristic realm in the Spear Mountains, north-east of the Core District, and
the most important inland enclave. Geistburg's whole military power used to be gathered here; the
invasion cut the two apart, and now the only thing joining them is a cave system of hundreds of
tunnels and halls. Regensburg itself is not much more than an enormous fort wedged into a
strategically excellent position on a cliff that overlooks **Spear Pass**, a route through the
mountains that used to be busy. Three concentric walls ten metres high divide the city into rings,
each ring subdivided again, all of it designed to make an invasion take as long as possible. The
weather is cold and windy with short hot summers. A Marshal of the Realm sits at the top of a strict
hierarchy, and by the city's right of inheritance his son is expected to take the chair when he
dies; a woman cannot be Marshal. It hires its cavalry out to other districts in exchange for the
supplies it cannot grow, and it has made an art of the business: noblemen come from the Core and
further for the training, the equipment and the horses raised in the enclave. The **Cracked Road**
reaches the Core District through cave-riddled limestone and is not safe; halfway along it a large
underground lake, **The Deep**, has two harbour villages on it, named From and To.

**Wolf Peak.** A religious centre of the faith of **the Fire**, built into the side of a live
volcano north of the Amber District, never once taken. The stairs to the summit are easy to hold and
the volcano's plumes of smoke keep the Horde from coming at it out of the air. **It has never used
blood ritual or human sacrifice**, and has never needed to. The mountain feeds itself inside and
out. It also has the best gunpowder and firearms on the continent, because the ingredients are all
in the mountain: bat guano that never runs out, sulphur from the volcanism, and charcoal from fast
trees that love the soil. Powder and guns are what it barters outward for the few things it cannot
make. Inside, everything is shared citizen to citizen, so there is no shop and no bar anywhere in
the enclave. Fire is holy here: the ground is warm underfoot and beacons burn along the whole length
of the monastery, never out. The priesthood rules, people of the wrong faith are not usually allowed
to stay, and it has its own language, **Perga**, its own dress, and tattoos over every inch. They
are rarely seen outside.

**Echterhof.** A large neutral city in the middle of Horde territory, ruled by Strigoi princes, with
farmland around it the Horde has left alone, because the Strigoi have an agreement with the Horde, which
respects their ground as protected. The city sits permanently under a cloud of smoke, though the
farmland around it is sunny and pleasant; the smoke is burnt in the five towers that crown the five
Strigoi palaces, and it is what lets the vampires move about outdoors on most days. Only an unusual
combination of hard wind and strong sun clears it. The palaces are stately marble colossi built in
tiers, each over a well-guarded crypt for the days its prince has to get back to his coffin. Strigoi
live off humans, so a large human population lives there caught between the vampires and the Horde,
drawn by good farmland and by the fact that fear of the Horde outweighs fear of vampires. A human
mayor is appointed to keep order and choose who is eaten, usually criminals, and when criminals run
short it is done by lot, by grudge or by whim. The princes rule jointly and behave like any other
pack of despotic high nobility, bar the drinking of blood, and they are careful to keep their take
low enough that the enclave stays attractive to guests, which is to say to food. The city welcomes
visitors, because the more people there are the less likely you are the one chosen. The **Guest
Law** guarantees that a guest will not be killed, and guarantees that a guest who breaks any of its
many clauses will be. It also offers land and the prospect of a title, to keep people coming. Their
households hold further Strigoi and a quantity of human and half-human handlers. They worship **The
First**, the first vampire, who is a minor god, and who sleeps in a crypt deep under the city: when
the princes cannot agree, or when what they want drifts from what he wants, his is the last word.

**Velderon.** The elves' embassy on Firma, east of the Fiord District, and the one place on the
continent where you are guaranteed to meet an elf. It is a chalk-white palace standing in the ruins
of the elves' own capital here, from before Athor beat them: an ancient ruin with one restored
corner. The elves are neutral and live on the Second Side; their own magic keeps the Horde out,
along with everyone else, and a few mages maintain those wards. Beyond them there is a small
garrison and nothing: no resources, no facilities. A permanent gate to the Second Side stands in the
heart of the embassy, and the staff come and go through it, with a modest flow of supplies to keep
the ambassador in the manner he expects. The embassy exists to watch the Horde and make sure it
never starts looking upward. You do not go in without an invitation. The ruins around it are holy
ground to the elves, which does not stop adventurers trying them for artefacts from the elves' time,
danger and all.

**Saag et Haar.** What is left of Seetch, the realm of the necromancer kings. Athor beat nearly
everything and never managed to beat them. The enclave runs along the south coast below the Hyena
Desert, and the city of the same name stands a little inland, near the mouth of the **Lizard
River**, which dries up in some years. The land is half desert, hot and unwelcoming, with sandstorms
blowing in off the Hyena and one small band of arable ground in the west. The mining is the opposite
of small: slaves and zombies are worn through in the low hills after silver, gold and gemstones. The
greatest of those kings, **Fahlas**, still rules it after four hundred years and has become a minor
god: he cheated age and arrived at divinity. Below him the army and the necromancers split the
power between them, and he governs with an iron hand and permanent paranoia about who is coming for
his chair. It depends on blood mages now like everywhere else, though raising the dead keeps the
number of sacrifices lower. The government is a tyranny, the religion is the death goddess
**Aecht**, and the tongue is **Veche**. A trade road runs to Sammel through the Third Side: portals
at both ends and a demon-permitted road between them, paid for in human sacrifice. The city buys
slaves in Sammel and pays in silver, gold and gemstones out of its own mines.

**The Triple Enclave.** Three frontier towns north of the Iron District and halfway to Kelp Bay, on
the western slopes of the Spear Mountains and all of them in mountainous country, each walled around
a castle and a duke, sharing one group of blood mages and one safe tunnel, the **Steel Road**. They
sit high up, and the winters are cold, but it is far enough south that the rest of the year is
pleasant in spite of the altitude. Between them they come to religion, drugs and soldiers, one each.
**Farsight** stands on the open upper slopes and the Church of the Eternal God holds it absolutely,
its duchess also a cardinal, religion behind every law. **Marke** sits above the high bogs of its
valley and exports **dreamwort**, whose dried seed is a powerful drug; its duke is powerless and the
**Dream Guild**, a criminal organisation with branches in every district, holds the real power. They
are rumoured to have a safe-passage treaty with the Horde. **Highfall** is built into a mountain
with a waterfall coming over its walls, and tries to run things the old way: it lends the Dream
Guild troops, and takes money and sacrificial slaves from the Iron District in exchange.

**Desert Stream.** The third oasis of the Hyena Desert, outside the Iron District's ward and holding
a small one of its own that covers the water and nothing else. When the rings went up and the oasis
found itself outside them, its people managed at the last moment to attract a group of blood mages
down from Hyrlock, and bought them by handing over power in the oasis. The mages have held it ever
since, two or three families of them living entirely apart from everyone else. **They speak the
local tongue, Gastve, with an accent, and have stopped speaking it at all**, and they keep interpreters
for dealing with the people they rule, and they do not understand local custom and have not tried
to. They take in local members only where it keeps the bloodline strong. In exchange the ward holds,
so the villagers hand over their children and their relatives for the sacrifices without complaint,
the alternative being what it is. The isolation has told on everybody and the enclave's reputation
is poor, but the oasis feeds everyone who reaches it.

### The roads under the ground

**What is under the continent is five things at once**, and only the first of them was built on
purpose: the tunnels dug between the districts; natural cave systems that have been down there for
millennia; abandoned elven ruins left from whatever the elves were doing underground; the boltholes
of monsters the Horde hunted off the surface; and the old underworld proper, with the Queen of the
Dark's successors in it. A named road is a road somebody maintains. Everything it passes through is
one of the other four.

**The West Road**, Black Harbour to Erleng, is the good one: broad, high, with few side connections
to anything. Fish is most of what moves along it. Three towns have grown up to service the traffic:
**Underswamp**, **Drowntown** and **The Pillars**, damp, loud places of bars, bonded warehouses and
inns, with a handful of smaller inns on the stretches between them.

**The South Road** runs from Tanto in the Ruby to Fynch in the Core. From the Core as far as **Sol**
you can take the **Velvet Line**, which is a luxury train; from Sol onward to the Ruby the tunnel
will not take rail and you go by stagecoach, or on your own feet. It is mostly safe. The exceptions
are the two or three places where neighbouring tunnels join it, and at those there are military
posts that will escort you for a fee. Sol itself is a gaudy, lavishly-appointed pleasure city
filling one enormous cavern, and the rumour that everything is permitted there is not much of an
exaggeration.

**The East Road**, Sammel to Gradenbosch, is the least safe of the roads. Thousands of years of
mining have left whole stretches perforated like a cheese. The **Night Express** runs it and is
rarely troubled; ordinary travellers walking it disappear into the dark all the time. Four small
forts along the way serve as fortified inns and depots.

**The North-East Road** tied Weder to Erleng and was a wonder of engineering: twin rail lines, and
three towns between the Furnace and the Core. **Goldenburg** assembled freight and sold it to
passers-through, **Strema** rested travellers and ran mines of its own, and **Meredi**, nearest the
Core, farmed mushrooms across the floor of the great chamber it sat in. It closed when the Furnace
fell. The Erleng end is bricked up behind a vault door carrying protective rituals, and what is on
the other side of it is a matter of opinion.

**The Coast Road**, Kelp Bay to Farsight, is a walking tunnel and nothing more, two abreast at its
widest. Porter caravans hauling the blood tax and everything else jam it regularly. Midway along it
somebody blasted out **The Lost Inn**, which reliably has the strangest collection of people on the
continent under one roof.

**The Steel Road**, Highfall to Gradenbosch, is a little wider: two riders abreast. It is safe from
monsters, having few side tunnels, though the road itself forks here and there and comes back
together later. It is also the route **dreamwort** travels into the Core, so the risk on it is
criminal rather than monstrous. No towns, but a string of overnight stops with beer and a bed.

**The Cracked Road**, Regensburg to an opening between Erleng and Steinbrëk, is unsafe and important
at the same time, because the military traffic between the Core and Regensburg has to go somewhere.
There is a fort at the Core mouth that is permanently braced for surprises. Most of the route is
natural limestone cave, and unknown tunnels join it along its whole length, some running to
**the Queen of the Dark**'s country, some to monster dens, some to collapses that have let the Horde in. A
good many of those openings have been sealed. Limestone does not stay sealed. One great chamber
holds **The Deep**, an underground lake fed by three underground rivers, which has to be crossed by
boat; the walled harbour towns of **From** (Core side) and **To** (Regensburg side) exist to row
people over. There are no inns between them, so travellers pitch tents in the chambers along the
way.

### The hives

Mostly they look like gigantic anthills of earth and organic matter, winding inside into tunnels and
chambers for larvae and for each of the Horde's roles. **Twelve are known.**

**Xtchaargh** stands west of the Core and can be seen from the western edge of the district: a thing
the size of a small mountain, bored through with winding tunnels, sitting there to remind everyone
inside the ward what is outside it. It is the only hive that will receive human envoys, if it judges
them important enough to bother with. It has a human quarter in the middle of its tunnels, with
ordinary houses and squares, called the **Summer Village**, where humans who serve the Horde live
and train spies to pull enclaves and districts apart. One hole to the outside lights the whole of
it, barely. The village is a parody of a pre-invasion idyll built so that humans can live inside a
hive: white corner boards on the houses, gardens planted with mushrooms where the flowers would go,
tidy paths of woodchip and shavings, and bioluminescent lamps along them. It is said that a
delegation from the Furnace District went there shortly before its blood mages were killed.

**Chguuth** is the main hive, in the south-east, standing in the brutal heat haze at the middle of
the dried-out bed of Lost Lake, which the Horde dried out to put it there. It is more than twice
the size of Xtchaargh, eight hundred metres of anthill reinforced with the chitin of the dead, and
you can smell it sour on the air for miles out. The enormous flying **Skaah** that were mistaken for
airships all set out from here, and moor at its summit when they are not out over the continent. The
Queen sits here.

**The underworld** is not empty either. Demons, minor gods and monsters pushed off the surface have
strongholds down there. **The Queen of the Dark** and **the Troll King** have always been there and
do not care for visitors. Other stretches are infested with the Horde.

### What people believe

**The Church of the Eternal God.** *The Eternal God is the only true god, and the emperor his only
true representative on Firma.* Three tenets: **purity** of soul and body, which means avoiding
strong feeling and poisons and keeping yourself trained, and holds that all other gods are impure;
**obedience**, because what the god, the emperor and his priests say is law and questioning it is
blasphemy; and **solidarity**, helping your brothers and sisters in faith and never charging them
for it. Its chief saint is Athor, who spread it across Firma, alongside lesser saints like the
dragonslayer Vesper, the healer Selma and the martyr Bullius, local saints, and every emperor who
has ever lived. Inside it: **the Black Circle**, a religious secret police; **the Holy Shield**, its
order of knights; **the White Brothers** and **the Black Sisters**, its largest orders of monks and
nuns; and **the Flagellants**, the most extreme and frequently violent.

**The Church of the Eternal Spirit.** A sister church holding that the Eternal God's spirit drifts
through all the air of Firma. Same tenets, same saints, plus its founder **Vegnar**, who was Athor's
brother. The two churches are rivals who tolerate each other, with accusations of blasphemy
surfacing now and then. Inside it: **God's Spirit** as its secret police, **the Holy Sword** as its
knights, **the Devoted** as its monks and nuns, and **the Unseen**, an order of assassins.

**The Five.** The largest religion before Athor, known all along the coasts. **Sessyfyn** the winged
fish, goddess of fishing and seafaring. **Valtor** the two-pointed spear, her brother and husband,
god of strength and battle. **Fagar** the mask of fish scales, their child, the trickster.
**Vulpet** the shark, daughter of **Fagar** and **Sessyfyn**, driven out for being so ugly.
**Brendan** the conch horn, son of **Fagar** and **Valtor**, who stands for the beauty that can come
out of ugly things. Its **Mummers** walk about in animal masks singing and pressing drink on people;
its **Berserkers** work themselves up with drums and dancing before a battle.

**The Grey.** Three siblings and three books. **The Grey** embodies assassination, and the Grey Book
is on avoiding discovery and killing quietly. **The Purple** brews poison, and the Purple Book is
recipes. **The Yellow** stands for lies, deceit and betrayal, and the Yellow Book is misinformation
and disguise. The three sects match the three books.

**Local gods.** **Vax**, war, in the Amber District. **Parfebe**, blood, on Hyrlock. **The Primal
Mother**, fertility, on Solemn. **The Fire**, fire, at Wolf Peak. **The First**, vampirism, in
Echterhof. **Aecht**, death, in Saag et Haar.

**The minor gods, who demonstrably exist.** Perhaps a hundred powerful nature spirits and beings,
forced into remote places and underground when Athor took the continent. They grant no miracles, and
they are what the school of Spiritualism is built on. Among the known: **the Winter Lady**, **the
Queen of the Dark**, **the Troll King**, **Baal Fagor**, **the Land Jelly**, **the Cat God**,
**Fahlas** and **Mother Longhand**. Cults gather round them: **the Silent** follow **the Winter
Lady**, **the Soot Club** try to summon **Baal Fagor**, and **the Dancers** use drugs to speak with
**the Land Jelly**.

### What a character can become

Your Way is the direction you grow in, and each one ends in specialists the world has names for.
Every one of those names is in *Specializations*, with the standing rule it carries and what it pays
for it in.

**Way of Mind** runs to the schools of direct magic, each a vocabulary rather than a rank, to
ritual magic for what takes days rather than a turn, and to the trades: a Furnace engineer studies
as hard as a necromancer and casts nothing at all.

**Way of Strength** runs to the people who stand in the line, hold the door, and go through what is
in the way rather than around it.

**Way of Shade** runs to the people who arranged the fight before it started, and were not seen
arriving.

**Way of Faith** runs to the churches and the gods above, and swearing to one closes the others.
Everything a priest does is here: the orders, the saints and the miracles of whichever god you swore
to.

## 2. What you have

Six stacks of cards, a seventh if you cast, and one number. Everything you own is in one of them.

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Action deck** | 12–20 cards. What your character can *do* in a fight, and how long they can keep doing it. |
| **Skill deck** | About 15 cards. What your character can *attempt* outside a fight. |
| **Fate deck** | 20 cards. Your luck, and nobody else's. |
| **Items** | Everything you own. Only **equipped** items can be used. |
| **Features** | Permanent traits: *Strong*, *Tough*, *Good Aim*. |
| **Conditions** | What is currently on you, each with a number counting down. |
| **Spells** | Your book, if you have one. The count is what you have **prepared**. |
| **Health** | At 0 you are down. |

### Decks you draw from, and decks you keep

Every one of those stacks is a deck of cards, printed the same size, held the same way. What tells
them apart is what happens to the deck.

- **Decks you draw from.** Action, skill and fate. These are shuffled, drawn from, played and
  discarded, and you do not get to choose what comes up.
- **Decks you keep.** Items, features, conditions, and your spellbook. Nothing in them is shuffled
  and nothing is drawn. You open the stack and look through it, because a stack is simply how you
  hold a lot of cards at a table.

A card you keep can still be **spent**. A one-use item is gone once you use it. What it never does
is go into a deck you draw from.

Watch for the two that cross over. **Wounds** and **Taint** are not kept. They are shuffled into the
decks you draw from, and that is the whole of what makes them frightening.

## 4. Your action cards

A card carries three lines, and *Heavy Swing* has all three:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Strike +1 |
| **Secondary** | Move 1 |
| **Reaction** | Parry |

- **Primary**, the strong half. Your main action.
- **Secondary**, the weak half. Your second action.
- **Reaction**, what the card does on somebody else's turn.

**You can never use both halves of the same card.** A turn is one card's primary and a *different*
card's secondary.

Any card whose reaction cannot fire is still a **Brace: reduce incoming damage by 1**. That is
deliberately too little to be worth spending a good card on. Its purpose is that no card in your
hand is ever completely dead, and it matters exactly once, the hit that would have killed you, by
one.

### The six verbs

| Verb | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Strike** ⚔ | Attack with an equipped weapon. |
| **Cast** ✦ | Cast a prepared spell. |
| **Defend** 🛡 | Gain Block. |
| **Use** 🧪 | Use an equipped item. |
| **Move** 👣 | Move that many squares. |
| **Ready** ⇄ | Equip or swap one item. |

## 5. A round

1. Everyone draws one fate card for initiative, once per fight. **Highest acts first** (Miss is
   lowest, Critical highest), and ties are redrawn until the order settles. The order then holds for
   the whole fight.
2. The GM draws one intent card per kind of enemy, face down.
3. Each character takes a turn in order.

### Your turn

- Refill your hand to **5 cards**.
- Play **one card for its primary** and **a different card for its secondary**, in either order.
- Both go to your discard pile.
- The rest of your hand stays in front of you, ready to react.

Two attacks in one turn are legal if they come from two different cards, a Strike primary plus a
card that has Strike on its secondary. Those cards are rare, and they are earned by levelling.

---

## 6. Reactions

**Reactions resolve by themselves. You are not asked.** When a trigger happens, the best reaction
your hand can offer fires, that card goes to your discard, and play continues.

A reaction fires when the hit is **serious**, when it would deal a Wound (5 or more past armour)
or drop you to 0. A scratch is a scratch, and a hand spent on scratches is a hand you do not have
when the real blow lands.

### Every reaction, and what it does

Five things can trigger one. Being hit is the common case and the only one with a fallback: a card
whose own reaction cannot fire is still a Brace. The other four are an enemy arriving next to you,
an **ally** being hit, a spell being cast, and a check failing, and the last three belong to the
casting Ways, because they are printed on a prepared spell rather than on a card in your hand.


| Reaction | Fires when | What it does | Printed on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Overwatch** | an enemy walks into the ground you are watching | A held shot, loosed at the first enemy to walk into the ground you are watching: your weapon's own range, through a sixty degree arc on the way you are facing, and no further than you can see. The weapon decides what arrives, so a thrown knife and a musket are the same card and nothing like each other. | 3 cards |
| **Brace** | you are hit | Reduce the damage, barely. Any card whose own reaction cannot fire is still worth this much, which is deliberately too little to be worth spending a good card on. | any card that cannot answer otherwise |
| **Parry** | you are hit | Turn the blow with what you are already holding. Reduces the damage by your weapon's own Defend value, so a sword parries better than a knife. | 10 cards |
| **Block** | you are hit | The shield comes up. Reduces the damage by the shield's Defend value and one more for being a shield, which is the best answer to a hit in the game. | 6 cards |
| **Use** | you are hit | Something off your belt, drunk or broken open as the blow lands. Heals you by the item's Use value, and the item is spent doing it. | 4 cards |
| **Deflect** | you are hit | You move the attacker's arm, and one of their own takes the blow instead. It has to be a serious one, and it has to be somebody standing next to them. | a prepared spell |
| **Sidestep** | an enemy ends its move next to you | Step out of reach as they arrive, by the number the card prints. The attack that was walking toward you never arrives. | 12 cards |
| **Counter** | an enemy ends its move next to you | They walked onto your point. A free Strike at the enemy that just arrived, for your weapon's full value, and it is the only reaction in the game that deals damage. | 1 card |
| **Heckle** | an enemy ends its move next to you | A word, at the moment they close. The enemy that just arrived draws fate with disadvantage, so the attack it came to make is already spoiled. | 1 card |
| **Bodyguard** | an ally within reach is hit | The blow meant for your friend arrives at you instead. It is settled before any other answer, because stepping in front of somebody has to happen first. | 1 card |
| **Mend** | an ally within reach is hit | Help reaches an ally on the turn they were hit rather than on yours, which is most of what makes it worth a prepared slot. | a prepared spell |
| **Unmake** | a spell is cast | You have felt this spell before, and that is the only reason you can stop it. | a prepared spell |
| **Hold to the Bargain** | a check fails | A check you had just failed is intervened in, once, by something that owes you. | a prepared spell |


### Facing, and the ground you watch

**Which way you are pointing matters for exactly one thing, and this is it.** Turn your figure and
you are watching the ground in front of it: your weapon's own range, through a narrow arc of
**sixty degrees**, thirty either side of the way you face. On the board that is **one square at one
pace, three from two paces out, and five from four**, so it is a lane rather than a quadrant.

A held shot answers the first enemy to **walk into** that lane. Not one standing in it already, and
not one shuffling about inside it: the lane is about the step that crosses into it, the same way an
arrival is about the step that brings somebody next to you.

**The weapon decides what arrives**, which is why one card covers a thrown knife and a musket. A
knife watches three squares and lands for 2; a longbow watches ten and lands for 3; a musket
watches twelve and lands for 6 through two points of armour. The card only chooses the moment.

**A wall stops it.** The lane is ground you can see, so a pillar, a corner or a closed door is
the end of it, and a window is not. This is the only rule in the game that works the wall out for
you, and it is because a held shot is the only attack that aims itself.

So a held shot answers somebody **stepping out of cover**, which is most of what you will use it
for. Walking into the open is walking into the lane.

**Only one shot answers a step.** The first watcher in initiative order looses, spends their card,
and that is the step dealt with. Four archers watching the same gap do not all fire into it.

Nothing else in the game asks which way you are pointing. A Parry answers a blow from anywhere, a
Sidestep is away rather than backward, and reach is a circle. If nobody at your table is holding a
shot, facing never comes up.

**A reaction spell has no fallback.** It is prepared or it is not, and once it has answered
something this fight it is spent. A card that cannot answer is still worth a Brace; a spell that
cannot answer simply does not.

**Stunned takes all of them, Brace included.** So does being Unconscious, and so does being
Exhausted. *You cannot play reactions* has no exception in it.

Three things are always true:

- **A reaction never draws fate.** A Parry is your weapon's Defend value, a Brace is 1, a Counter is
  your weapon's Strike value. Flat, every time.
- **A reaction's damage is not itself a trigger.** A Counter hits, and nothing answers it.
- **Reacting is never free.** Every card you react with is one more card off your deck at the next
  refill, and that brings exhaustion closer.

### Turning a card sideways

**A card turned sideways will not react.** That is the whole of the decision, and you take it in
advance, at leisure, not in the half-second after the dice would have landed in another game.

**When your turn is over, the rest of your hand turns sideways on its own.** A reaction costs you a
point of stamina, and the game reacts for you off whatever is still upright, so a full hand left
upright is a promise to spend four or five of the cards you can still play. If you mean to Parry,
turn that card back upright before you end your turn. There is a button that does the whole hand at
once, either way.

**Your hand stands back up at the start of your round.** Everything you draw arrives upright, and
so does everything you kept. You decide again each round, from a clean hand, and nothing you held
last round is still holding itself this one.

## 7. Resolving an action

Always in this order:

1. **Base value**, the item's line for the verb. A Longsword's Strike is 3.
2. **Card modifier**, the bonus printed on the action card. *Strike +1.*
3. **Features**, permanent modifiers. *Strong: +1 on Strike with Heavy items.*
4. **Conditions**, temporary modifiers on you or on the target.
5. **Fate**, draw one card from your fate deck and apply it.
6. **Their defences**, subtract **armour**, then **Block**. Never below 0.

**Fate is drawn for damage and healing only.** Move, Ready and Defend never draw fate.

**One draw per action, not per target.** An attack that hits several creatures draws once and
applies the same answer to all of them.

### Bonuses do not stack from the same kind of source

Every card that grants a numeric bonus belongs to one of seven groups: **Body, Training, Tool,
Make, Power, Station, Wonder.** One action takes **at most one bonus from each group**. Muscle and a
tool stack; two kinds of muscle do not.

Whatever supplies the *base* value is exempt, so a weapon and a feature can never lock each other
out.

## 8. The fate deck

Twenty cards, yours alone.

| Card | Count | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Miss** | 1 | The action does nothing. Shuffle the deck afterwards. |
| −2 | 1 | |
| −1 | 4 | |
| +0 | 8 | |
| +1 | 4 | |
| +2 | 1 | |
| **Critical** | 1 | On an attack, draw from the critical deck. On anything else, double the value. Shuffle afterwards. |

**Advantage** means draw two and take the better; **disadvantage**, draw two and take the worse. Two
sources of advantage still give one.

**Your deck thins as you use it.** A fate deck that has already spent its good cards is a different
deck from a fresh one, and you can see that coming. It reshuffles on a Miss or a Critical.

### The critical deck

A Critical on an attack draws one card from the critical deck: twelve cards, shared by the whole
table, read out face up.

| Face | Copies | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Through and Through** | 4 | Double the damage. |
| **Find the Gap** | 2 | **Exposed**, their armour counts 0 until the end of your next turn. Not this hit, and not doubled. |
| **Off the Feet** | 2 | **Off-Balance**, their Block counts 0 until the end of your next turn, and it is an opening. Not this hit, and not doubled. |
| **Driven Down** | 1 | **Halted**, their next turn does not happen. Not doubled. |
| **Stove In** | 1 | Their armour is one point worse until it is repaired. Permanent. Not doubled. |
| **Turn the Edge** | 1 | Their weapon strikes for one less until it is repaired. Permanent. Not doubled. |
| **Both** | 1 | Draw two others and take both. |

A Critical always gets **at least one point** through, however well armoured the target is.

## 9. Armour, Block and getting past them

- **Armour** is worn. It subtracts from every hit.
- **Block** is earned by a Defend, and subtracts after armour. How long it lasts depends on how you
  played it:

| Played as | Block lasts |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Against **every** attack until the start of your next turn |
| **Secondary** | Against the **next** attack only |
| **Reaction** | Against the attack that triggered it |

Blocks from different sources do not stack. **Use the highest**, and that source's duration is the
one that applies, so a Defend secondary played behind a Defend primary never shortens the stance you
already had.

Some attacks go **through** armour (*ignores N armour*, or all of it) and some go **round** Block.
They are different things and a card says which: a bodkin finds a gap in plate, and a shield raised
in answer does not care.

**Damaged gear.** Corrode takes points off armour; Blunt takes points off what a weapon strikes for.
Both are permanent until repaired, both stop at 0, and **neither destroys anything**. Armour at 0
is still armour and still repairable. Repair is an action: a **Lore check** with the right tools. A
Critical puts back 2, a Success 1, a Partial nothing and the time is gone.

## 10. Conditions

A condition is a token with a number, its duration. It remembers who created it, and it runs out at
the **end of that creature's next turn**. So a condition you inflict lasts through your own next
turn, and you can use it.

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Dazed** | You can't play a secondary. |
| **Slowed** | All Move values halved, rounding down. |
| **Stunned** | You can't play reactions. |
| **Halted** | Your turn does not happen. |
| **Prone** | Melee Strikes against you have advantage. Standing up costs Move 1. |
| **Grappled** | You can't Move. |
| **Silenced** | You can't Cast. |
| **Disarmed** | You can't Ready, or Strike with the disarmed item. |
| **Heckled** | You draw fate with disadvantage. |
| **Exposed** | Your armour counts 0. Block is untouched. |
| **Off-Balance** | Your Block counts 0, and it is an opening. |
| **Burning N** | N damage at the start of your turn, past armour and Block. Cancels with Wet and Frozen. |
| **Frozen N** | Your Move values are reduced by N. Cancels with Burning. |
| **Poisoned N** | You are Dazed, and take N damage at the start of your turn. N may be 0. |
| **Invisible** | Enemies can't target you. Ends if you Strike or Cast at one. |
| **Compelled** | You must spend your primary attacking whoever compelled you. |
| **Burdened / Overloaded** | See *Carrying*. |
| **Unconscious** | At 0 Health. No turns, no reactions, Prone. |

**Shake Off** spends a condition's duration rather than waiting for it. **Openings** (Wet,
Off-Balance, Marked and Judged) do nothing by themselves; they exist for other cards to key off, and
they cannot be shaken off.

### Putting two together

Most conditions are worth more beside another one than they are alone, and the clock is what makes
that possible. A condition you inflict runs out at the end of **your** next turn, so it is still
sitting there through everybody else's. One of you opens and another one finishes.

**The four openings are the whole combo system.** They do nothing at all by themselves. They sit on
a target waiting for a card that reads them, and that card is usually not the card that set them.

| Set this | And these want it |
|---|---|
| **Off-Balance** | **Hammerblow** strikes at **+2**. **Sweep the Leg** goes from Normal to **Heroic** to save against, and **Overbear** from Normal to **Hard**. Goblins know it too: **Worry It** ignores armour against an off-balance target |
| **Marked** | **Between the Plates** ignores armour, so it goes through plate. **Take the Shot** draws with **advantage**. **Pin the Shadow** goes from Normal to **Hard** |
| **Wet** | **Lightning** hits for **2** more and goes from Hard to **Heroic**. **Frost** goes from Normal to **Heroic** |
| **Judged** | Faith reads it on its own miracles. **The Weight of It** puts a judged enemy's armour at **0 against everyone**, and **Grey Judgement** makes them act last this round |

Three things worth knowing beyond the openings.

**Armour and Block come off separately, and both can go.** Exposed puts armour at 0, Off-Balance
puts Block at 0. A figure carrying both has nothing between the fate card and their Health.

**Take their answer away first.** Stunned means no reactions, so no Parry and no Block however many
cards they are holding. Exposed and Stunned together is armour gone and nothing to be done about
it. Halted is the whole turn, and Grappled on a Prone figure keeps them down, because standing up
costs Move 1 and Grappled means you cannot Move at all.

**Some of them cancel, so the order matters.** Burning cancels with Wet and with Frozen. Wet is the
setup for a Frost or a Lightning, and it is also a bucket of water: drop Burning on a wet target and
you have spent both for nothing. The ones that slow you do stack, in a fixed order: the injury
first, which never takes you below one square, then halved if something halves it, then the
condition's own reduction, which can take you to nothing.

## 11. How a fight goes

Every chapter up to here answers one question. This is the order those questions come up in, so
you have the shape of a fight before you watch one.

**Before anybody moves.** Everyone draws once for initiative and that order holds for the whole
fight. The GM draws a face-down intent for each kind of enemy, so you know something is coming
without knowing what. *A round* owns this.

**Your turn is two halves of two different cards.** Refill your hand, then play one card for its
primary and another for its secondary. You can never use both halves of one card, and that single
restriction is what the game is built on: every card you spend attacking is a card you are not
holding to defend with. *Your action cards* and *A round* own this.

**Whatever is left in your hand is your defence.** On somebody else's turn a card in your hand can
fire its reaction, and a card whose reaction does not fit can still Brace. Nothing you hold is
dead. *Reactions* owns this.

**An attack is a card, a draw and two subtractions.** Play the card, draw one fate card for luck,
add them together, then take off their armour and then their Block. *Resolving an action*, *The
fate deck* and *Armour, Block and getting past them* own those steps between them.

**You never run out of magic. You run out of stamina.** Stamina is how many cards you can play in
a fight, whatever your deck holds; your deck itself cycles for free. Wounds shuffle into it and clog
your hand without costing stamina, because you never play them. Health is how you fall over;
stamina is how you tire. *Running out* owns this.

**Conditions are the fight's memory.** Each is a word with a number counting down, and most of
what a card does beyond damage is to put one on somebody. *Conditions* owns this.

Nothing above is a rule. Every number lives in the chapter named beside it, and what follows is
two rounds of all of it at once, with the arithmetic shown.

## 12. Two rounds, played out

Everything above, running. One character, two enemies, and every number taken from the cards and
stat blocks in this book rather than invented for the example.

**The Warrior** is a starting character: **12 Health**, a **Longsword** (Strike 3, Defend 1), a
**Wooden Shield** (Defend 2), **Leather Armor** (Armor 1) and a **Healing Potion** on the belt.
One feature, **Strong**: +1 damage on Strike with Heavy items, and the Longsword is Heavy. The
action deck is 16 cards and the hand is 5.

**A Goblin** stands next to him: 5 Health, no armour, speed 3, a spear that Strikes for 3. **A
Goblin Archer** is six squares back down the tunnel: 5 Health, no armour, a shortbow that Strikes
for 3 out to range 6.

**Initiative.** Everyone draws one fate card, once for the whole fight. The Archer draws **+2**,
the Goblin **+1**, the Warrior **−1**. The order is **Archer, Goblin, Warrior**, and it holds for
the rest of the fight. Acting last is a bad draw and it is going to cost him.

**Two intent cards, face down, one per kind of enemy.** The Goblin and the Archer are different
kinds, so that is two cards. If there were four goblins they would still share one.

### Round one

The intents are **Close In** for the goblin kind and **Loose** for the archer kind. Turns run in
the initiative order drawn above, which is why the Archer goes first and the Warrior goes last.

**1. The Archer** plays *Loose*: Strike at the weapon's range. The Warrior is exactly six squares
away and the bow reaches six. Shortbow **3**, fate **+0**, total **3**. The Warrior's armour takes
1, so **2 damage**. Warrior at **10**.

Nothing in the Warrior's hand is asked about it. A reaction fires only on a **serious** hit, and 2
is neither a Wound nor enough to drop him.

**2. The Goblin** plays *Close In*: move your speed, then Strike. It is already adjacent, so it
moves nothing. Spear **3**, fate **+1**, total **4**. Armour takes 1, so **3 damage**. Warrior at
**7**. Still not serious.

**3. The Warrior** refills to five and holds *Strike, Strike, Heavy Swing, Shield Up, Advance*.

- **Primary: Heavy Swing** at the Goblin. The order from *Resolving an action*: base **3** for the Longsword,
  **+1** for the card, **+1** for Strong, which is **5** before luck. Fate **−1** gives **4**.
  The goblin has no armour and no Block, so **4 damage**: it drops to **1 Health**. Heavy Swing
  also ignores 2 armour, which buys nothing here, because a goblin has none to ignore.
- **Secondary: Strike**, for its Move 1, one step, keeping the goblin in reach.

He wanted the shield up as well, and he cannot have it. *Shield Up* carries Defend on its
**primary**, and the primary is spent on Heavy Swing. **You never get both halves of one card**,
and two cards whose useful halves are both primaries compete for the same slot. That is most of
what a turn is.

Heavy Swing and the one Strike go to the discard. **Three cards stay in front of him**, ready to
react: *Strike, Shield Up, Advance*. Eleven are left in the draw pile.

### Round two

**Nobody draws for initiative again.** It is drawn once for the whole fight, so the order is the
same one as before: Archer, Goblin, Warrior. The numbers below are that order repeating, not a
new one. What is new each round is the intents, and this round they are **Find the Joint** for
the goblin and **Loose** again for the archer.

**1. The Archer** looses again: **3**, fate **−1**, total **2**. Armour takes 1, so **1 damage**.
Warrior at **6**.

**2. The Goblin** plays *Find the Joint*: Strike where the harness is buckled rather than where
it is thickest, **ignoring 2 armour**. The Warrior's armour is 1, so all of it counts for nothing.
Spear **3**, fate **+2**, total **5**, and no armour stands in the way: **5 incoming**.

**That is a Wound**, and a Wound is serious, so the hand is asked. The best answer in it is
**Shield Up** as a **Block**: the Wooden Shield's Defend is 2, and one more for being a shield, so
it takes **3** off. **2 damage** gets through, and because 2 is under the Wound line, **the Wound
never happens**. Shield Up goes to the discard. Warrior at **4**.

That is the whole of the reaction rule in one blow. Nothing answered the 1s, 2s and 3s, because a
reaction fires only when it **changes the outcome**: when it stops a Wound, or stops you being
dropped. A hand spent on scratches is a hand you do not have for this.

**3. The Warrior** refills to five: *Strike, Advance, Lunge, Quick Hands, Second Wind*.

- **Primary: Strike** at the Goblin, on 1 Health. Base **3** plus **1** for Strong is **4**, and
  the fate card is a **Critical**. So he draws from the critical deck and turns up **Through and
  Through**: double the damage, **8**. The goblin is down. The fate deck shuffles, because a
  Critical always does.
- **Secondary: Second Wind**, for its **Use**, the Healing Potion. Use **4**, fate **+0**,
  **4 Health back**, and the potion is spent doing it. Warrior at **8**.

*Second Wind* is printed **Spent** on its primary and he played the secondary, so it is only
discarded. A cost on one half is not a cost on the other.

**Where the fight stands.** The Warrior is on **8 of 12** with a goblin down, the Archer untouched
six squares away and still shooting, three cards in hand, five in the discard and eight to draw.
He is winning and he has spent a quarter of his deck to do it, which is the arithmetic the whole
game is about: you do not run out of spells, you run out of deck.

## 13. Checks, outside a fight

Most of what you do away from a fight is a **check**: one card out of your hand, a difficulty the
GM names, and a fate card to say how it went. This chapter walks through one from start to finish,
and then through everything you are allowed to add to it.

Only make a check when **failing would be interesting**. Walking across a room is not a check.
Walking across a room with a tray of glasses while the duke is watching might be.

### 13.1 The six approaches

A skill card names one **approach** and a value of **1 to 3**. The approach is *how* you are doing
the thing, not what the thing is: you can get over a wall with Might or with Agility, and the
difference is whether you went through it or over it.

| Approach | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Might** ✊ | Climbing, breaking, lifting, intimidating, enduring |
| **Agility** 🌀 | Balance, acrobatics, quick hands, riding, dodging traps |
| **Shadow** 🌑 | Stealth, deception, disguise, pickpocketing, lockpicking |
| **Wits** 👁 | Noticing, searching, reading people, tracking, survival |
| **Lore** 📜 | Knowledge, arcana, religion, history, healing |
| **Presence** 👑 | Persuading, leading, performing, bargaining, calming |

**Every deck holds all six**, so anyone can try anything. Nobody is locked out of talking or of
climbing; some people are simply better at one of them.

### 13.2 Your deck, your hand, and running dry

Your skill deck is a different deck from your action deck, and it works on a different clock.

- You start with **fifteen** cards, plus **two** your occupation dealt in: seventeen in all.
- Draw a hand of **4** skill cards at the start of each **scene**, back up to four if you have
  some already. A scene is the GM's call: a new place, a new conversation, time passing.
- A card you play goes to your **skill discard**, and the discard **does not shuffle back**. It
  comes back on a **rest** (§15).
- If your deck and hand are both empty you can still **improvise**: value **0**, with
  disadvantage.

That last point is the shape of the whole thing. Your skill deck is not a per-scene resource like
stamina. **It is a budget for the day**. Four checks into an afternoon you are holding your
leftovers, and the question stops being *can I do this* and becomes *is this worth a card*.

**A clogged deck is worse than a thin one.** Mind **Taint** and **Doubt** both live in this deck,
and this is the expensive place to put them: a played card comes back on a rest, so an intruder
here costs you across a session rather than within a fight.

**Your Way deals in more.** Walking into a Way adds a card, and each of its branches adds another,
so a character deep in a Way has more checks in a day and draws any one of them less often. See
*Skill cards* for every card in the game.

### 13.3 One check, start to finish

> **Mira** is a Street Urchin out of the Core. She carries **Lockpicks** (*Shadow +2, locks and
> mechanisms*) and is **trained in lockpicks**. Her friend Osric is holding a lantern.

**Step 1. The GM names the obstacle and a difficulty.** This half is the GM's and you cannot do
it for them. Without a call there is nothing to answer.

| Difficulty | Number | Who gets there reliably |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | 3 | Anyone with a decent card |
| Normal | 5 | Trained, or anyone with a good tool or help |
| Hard | 7 | Specialists with a tool or help |
| Heroic | 9 | Specialists pushing hard, with help |

> *"The counting-house door. Shadow, Normal 5."*

**Step 2. You play one card and say how.** The approach has to fit what you described. A card that
cannot answer the call is greyed out rather than hidden, so you can see why it will not go.

> Mira plays **Out of the Light**, a Shadow **1**. It is not a good card. It is the Shadow card she
> has.

**Step 3. Add what applies.** This is the part worth learning properly, and §13.4 does it one
piece at a time. For now: her picks are **+2**, Osric's lantern is **+1**.

> `1 + 2 + 1 =` **4**, against a difficulty of 5.

**Step 4. Claim anything you can argue for.** Mira has been picking locks since she was eight.

> *"This is lockpicks."* The GM agrees, so her card **counts as 2** and her total is **5**.

**Step 5. Draw fate, and double it.** A fate card is worth **twice** its value on a check. That is
the single biggest difference between a check and an attack: the deck swings a check around twice
as hard, which is why a point of bonus matters less here than it looks.

**Step 6. Compare.**

| Result | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | Critical drawn | You do it, and something extra goes your way. |
| **Success** | Total ≥ difficulty | You do it. |
| **Partial** | 1–4 below | You do it, **but** it costs something: a twist. |
| **Fail** | 5+ below, or a Miss | You don't, **and** the situation changes: a twist. |

A **Critical** comes off the card, so a Critical that still falls short is a success with a bonus
rather than a near miss. A **Miss** is a Fail however high your total was.

**Partial is the common result and it is not a failure.** You get the thing. The GM gets to say
what it cost. Most doors in this game open; the interesting question is what was on the other side
by the time you got through.

### 13.4 The five things you can add

In the order you should think about them.

**1. A tool**, if one helps *here*. Tools name a situation, not just an approach: Lockpicks are
`Shadow +2 (locks and mechanisms)`, and a Climbing Rope is `Might +2 (climbing)`: worth two points
going up a wall and **nothing at all** shoving a stuck door, which is also Might. Say which tool
you are using and what it is doing. Nothing is added for you.

**Untrained, a tool gives at most +1** however good it is. Training is what unlocks the rest, and
training comes from your background and from levelling.

**2. A training**, if one covers it. Say which one you are claiming. If the GM agrees, **the card
you play counts as at least 2**. You do not fumble the thing you have done ten thousand times.
It cannot lift a 2 or a 3, so it is worth most on exactly the hand you did not want. It does not
rescue an improvised check: having once been taught something is not a card in your hand.

**3. A push.** Play a **second card of the same approach** from your hand and add its value. This
is how a specialist reaches Hard and Heroic, and it costs you two cards out of a day's four.

**4. Help.** Another character plays **any** card and says how they help: **+1**, or **+2** if
their card is the same approach. One helper. **If the check fails, the helper shares the
consequences**: helping is not free and not risk-free.

**5. Advantage**, which is not a number. A good plan, a clever use of the ground, a background that
fits: the GM may give you advantage, and you may argue for it. Bad conditions give disadvantage the
same way.

> **Advantage never stacks, and it cancels.** Two sources of advantage are one advantage. One of
> each is neither: being **Heckled** while you have talked your way into a favour leaves you
> drawing normally. However many of each there are, you end up with advantage, disadvantage, or
> neither.

### 13.5 The card's edge, and why the 1 is sometimes the right card

Every skill card carries an **edge**: a line of text that happens whether or not the check
succeeds. The edge is not a bonus. It is the card's second half.

**The size of the number tells you how much to expect of the edge.** A **3** is nearly all number
and its edge is a detail that is occasionally decisive. A **1** is nearly all text, and the number
is the excuse to play it.

> *Get the forged writ onto his desk. Any approach, Normal 5.* Mira can play:
>
> - **Quick Hands**, Agility 2, +1 from Light Step = **3**
> - **Out of the Light**, Shadow 1 = **1**, *edge: nobody can say afterwards that it was you*
>
> She plays the 1. A Partial on Quick Hands is *the writ is on the desk and the clerk saw who put
> it there*. A Partial on Out of the Light is *the room noticed something and nobody can name her*.
> The number is worse and the failure is survivable.

Some edges are a **price**, not a gift. *The Loud Voice* says everyone nearby hears you and knows
it was you, and it says that whether or not you wanted it today.

### 13.6 What the numbers actually buy

Success, partial and fail, counted off all twenty fate cards.

| total − difficulty | advantage | normal | disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **−3** | 19 / 72 / 9 | 10 / 60 / 30 | 1 / 48 / 51 |
| **−2, −1** | 51 / 48 / 1 | **30 / 60 / 10** | 9 / 72 / 19 |
| **0, +1** | 91 / 9 / 0 | **70 / 25 / 5** | 49 / 41 / 10 |
| **+2, +3** | 99 / 1 / 0 | **90 / 5 / 5** | 81 / 9 / 10 |

Two things are worth taking from that table.

**Advantage is worth about as much as two points of bonus.** On the difficulty it takes you from 70
to 91. Arguing well is not flavour.

**The rows pair up.** A fate card is doubled on a check, so the swing is always even and the odds
move in steps of two. Hitting the difficulty exactly and beating it by one are the same check.
**One point is worth either nothing at all or a great deal**. Mira's claimed training above took
her from 30% to 70% because it crossed a step; on a night with no lantern the same claim would have
moved her total from 3 to 4 and her odds not at all.

### 13.7 Things you never check for

Some things you notice without trying: an ambush in the trees, a lie in a friendly sentence, a
tripwire. These cost no card and no draw.

**Your passive score in an approach is your best card of it, plus 2.** If the party's best Wits
passive is 6, the party simply notices a Normal 5: the GM does not call for anything, and you
never knew there was a check to make. The point of a passive is to **delete** checks, not to speed
them up.

### 13.8 Opposed checks

When somebody is actively working against you, a guard watching or a merchant haggling back, the
opposition is not a difficulty, it is a character. **You still play and draw; they never do.** The
GM sets their resistance on the same scale: 3 for someone not expecting trouble, 5 for someone
doing their job, 7 for someone good at it and alert.

### 13.9 When everybody has to get past it

Some obstacles are not one task with many hands on it: they are the same task, once each. Everyone
has to get over the wall, and the question is not whether the party manages it but who gives it
away.

**Anyone whose passive meets the difficulty is simply through**, with no card spent, which is the
second job a passive does. Everyone else makes a normal check, and the people already through can
help.

**What you get wrong is yours.** Your Partial costs you something and your Fail is your twist.
Nobody pays for your bad hand, and you do not pay for theirs.

The GM draws **at most two** twists however many of you came up short: the worst result gets its
own, and the rest of you share one. So a bad crossing costs the party two complications, not five.

### 13.10 Challenges

A big situation is not one check. Getting into a manor, chasing a thief over rooftops, crossing a
mountain, talking a duke round: each is a **challenge**, and the whole party plays into two tracks.

- **Progress**, how much you need to achieve.
- **Pressure**, how much can go wrong before it fails or turns ugly.

Each round, every character takes **one** action: a check, a help, or anything else that fits.

| Result | Progress | Pressure |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | +2 | – |
| **Success** | +1 | – |
| **Partial** | +1 | +1 |
| **Fail** | – | +1, and a twist |

**You can watch both of them.** While a challenge is running, two bars sit on your tray: *Progress
3/6* filling from one side and *Pressure 1/4* from the other. You always know how close you are to
either ending, and that is the point of showing them, because every turn of a challenge is a
decision about whether to push or play safe, and you cannot take that decision blind.

**You win the moment the Progress bar fills.** You have done the thing, and whatever Pressure is
left unspent is how cleanly you did it. A manor entered on one point of Pressure is a quiet
burglary; the same manor entered on three is one the household will be talking about for a month.

**You lose if the Pressure bar fills first.** The alarm goes, the thief is gone, the duke walks out.
That is meant to open a new situation rather than close the road, and very often the new situation
is a fight.

**If both fill on the same check, you win.** You got there, and the cost arrives with you.

Neither bar stops at full, so a last check can push one well past the end, and the GM says what the
overshoot looks like here.

The GM may say a step **needs** a particular approach. That is when the right card in the right hand
matters, and when the party has to plan as a group rather than as four people taking turns.

**Catch Breath in a challenge.** Instead of a check you may take a breath, which costs the party
**Pressure +1**, because a breath is time the other side also gets: the guard changes shift, the
duke grows impatient. Then shuffle your skill discard back into your skill deck, set **1** card of
your choice aside as Spent until your next rest, and draw up to hand size.

### 13.11 Twists

**A Partial costs you a twist. A Fail is one.**

A twist is a card the GM draws that names the *kind* of trouble rather than the trouble: the card
says *Gear* and the GM says a lockpick snapped off in the lock. Most of them are a question for the
GM to answer that way. **Time** adds a second point of Pressure, and **Opening** is the one that
gives you something instead of taking it.

A twist is **on top of** the Pressure a Fail already costs, never instead of it.

You will usually not see the card. It reaches you as something that has happened, and a ritual draws
from a deck of its own, because a ritual goes wrong in ways nothing else does.


### The twist deck

Thirty cards, drawn when a check outside a ritual asks for one.

| Twist | Copies | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Noise** | 4 | Attention. The opposition now knows something is wrong. |
| **Time** | 4 | The clock. Pressure +1 on top of the normal cost. |
| **Gear** | 4 | A tool. It breaks or is lost: no tool bonus until repaired or replaced. |
| **Strain** | 5 | Cards. Discard 1 skill card of the GM's choice from your hand. |
| **Hurt** | 3 | Health. Lose 2 Health, or gain a condition that fits. |
| **Arrival** | 4 | Someone new. A third party walks into the scene. |
| **Secret** | 2 | Information, in either direction. You learn something unwelcome, or they learn something about you. |
| **Opening** | 4 | Nothing, this one gives. The complication also creates an opportunity. |

That is **30** cards.

### The ritual twist deck

Twenty-four cards, and a ritual draws from these instead. Every one of them is the thing you are trying to lift answering back.

| Twist | Copies | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Backlash** | 4 | The working turns. The subject takes 2 damage, ignoring armor. |
| **It Answers** | 4 | Whatever did this knows the ritual is happening. The curser, the god, the rot itself, and it has time to arrive before you finish. |
| **Residue** | 3 | Magic leaves marks. 1 Taint into the subject's deck, in the flavour that fits. |
| **Deeper** | 3 | The injury speaks for itself: it lands as a condition that fits, for the rest of the ritual. A bad leg is Slowed, a shattered nerve is Stunned. |
| **Spoiled** | 3 | A component is consumed wrongly. No further check until it is replaced, and the GM says what replacing it takes. |
| **Not Alone** | 3 | The working reaches somebody else present. One ally takes a Doubt, or the condition the subject just took. |
| **The Long Way** | 3 | The recipe was wrong about the order. Pressure +1, and the next check needs a different approach than the one you have been using. |
| **It Sets** | 1 | The injury becomes **bound**: no ritual lifts it until the GM's own condition is met, the way an obligation's Taint is bound. The recipe is not spent. |

That is **24** cards.

## 14. Running out

**Your stamina is how many cards you can play in a fight.** It is a number on your sheet and it
has nothing to do with how big your deck is. You do not run out of spells, and you do not run out of
deck, because your deck cycles for nothing. You run out of stamina.

Every card you play costs 1: as a primary, as a secondary, or as a reaction, and whether it goes to
your discard or to your Spent pile. Nothing you did not play costs anything.

- **The cycle is free.** When your draw pile runs out and you still have stamina, shuffle your
  discard and it is your draw pile again.
- **Exhausted**: at **1** stamina you may play only **one** card a turn, and you cannot react.
- **At 0** you discard the rest of your draw pile, and the only thing you may do is catch your
  breath.
- **Catch Breath**: spend your whole turn, playing nothing, and regain **2 stamina**, never above
  your maximum.

Two stamina is one full turn, so a breath gives up this turn to buy a normal one next round. There
is no limit on how often you may do it. Running out is therefore a change of pace and not an
ending: you keep fighting, at half the rate, for as long as the fight lasts.

Breathing before you have run out is simply wasted, because the recovery stops at your maximum.

### Wounds

**You gain 1 Wound when** you take **5 or more damage from a single hit** (after armour), or when you
drop to **0 Health**.

A Wound is a card. It goes into your action discard and comes back around like any other. When you
draw one it takes a slot in your hand, cannot be played, not even for Brace, and is discarded at
the end of your turn. Wounds stay between fights.

Removing one: a long rest takes off 1; a **Lore check (Normal 5)** with a Healer's Kit takes off 1,
once per patient per day; some magic says so.

### 0 Health

At 0 you are **Unconscious**: you fall Prone, take no turns, cannot react, and gain a Wound. You are
dying until somebody treats you. **You come out of the initiative order** while you are down, and
healing puts you back into it at the place you drew. Your place is still yours: the order is drawn
once and holds for the whole fight.

## 15. Rests

Both need a safe place.

| | Short rest | Long rest |
|---|---|---|
| **Takes** | About an hour | A night |
| **Limit** | Costs a rest token | Once a day |
| **Action deck** | Spent cards return | Spent cards return |
| **Skill deck** | Discard and Spent shuffle back in | Same |
| **Rest tokens** | | Regain all |
| **Health** | Heal 1 | Heal 5 |
| **Wounds** | | Remove 1 |

You start with **one rest token**. More come from levelling.

**After a fight**, shuffle your hand and discard back into your action deck. **Spent cards stay
out.** Several fights without rest wear you down.

## 16. Carrying and equipping

**Only equipped items can be used.** A **Ready** buys one equip or one swap; because an item only
comes off by being replaced, those are the same transaction.

Your carrying limit is **40 kg**, counting everything.

| Load | | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Up to the limit | **Unburdened** | no penalty |
| 41–60 kg | **Burdened** | Slowed, and disadvantage on Agility and Shadow checks |
| 61–80 kg | **Overloaded** | Burdened, **and 2 random cards are Spent** |
| 81 kg + | | You cannot pick it up. Make two trips. |

Neither state has a duration, so they last until you drop the weight, and dropping things is never
an action.

## 17. Levelling up

**The GM says when.** There is no experience to count.

A level-up is a step on one shared graph. Your **frontier** is every node that a node you already
own points at, and you take one of them. Taking it spends the level and puts that node's own
successors on your frontier.

**The node is the reward.** Nothing is offered and nothing is passed over. Taking the node *is*
taking the reward. What a node gives is one of:

| Reward | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Action card** | A card added to your deck, the main currency of growing |
| **Training** | A tool type at its full bonus instead of +1, and the tool is issued with it |
| **Item** | A card, handed over |

A **specialization node** gives you a **title** as well as its reward, what people call you, and
the Tactic that comes with it. You only ever have your deepest title's Tactic.

The decision is **a direction, not a menu**. What you give up is the other directions you could have
gone this level, and a branch you walk past is usually still there next time.

**Except when it is not.** Some nodes belong to an exclusive group: taking one **permanently**
disables every other node in that group, and everything reachable only through them. Swearing to a
god is the clearest case. You will be told before it happens.

## 18. Where you are from, and what you did before

Two things are true of your character before the first level is spent, and neither of them costs
one: **where you are from**, and **what you did before you picked anything up**. You choose them in
that order, and then you choose a Way.

**Why an order rather than three open doors.** You were somewhere before you were something, and
you were something before you learned to fight. An **origin** is taken once and closes the other
fourteen. An **occupation** then follows only the origins it could plausibly have come out of, so
choosing the place is what makes the second list worth reading: a Miner comes out of mining country
and a Vintner out of wine country. A Way follows any occupation, and all four of them stay open for
the whole of a life.

### 18.1 Origin

about. It is deliberately not *Might checks*: see *Checks, outside a fight* for what advantage does,

## 19. The four Ways

A Way is the direction your character grows in. It is not a class you are locked into: it is which
part of the graph you are walking, and the four do genuinely different things rather than the same
thing with different numbers.

### Way of Strength

**Strength breaks.** Everything a fighter does uses verbs everybody has, so a Way built on them
cannot be identified by its numbers. It is identified by rules nobody else is allowed: your Blocks
stack where everyone else takes only the highest, every ally beside you gains Block when you swing,
your Parry also strikes the one enemy you named. Its answer to armour is to hit through it, and its
characteristic card takes two points off armour by hitting harder.

**Strong at** standing in the way. It has the most Health and the heaviest armour, it is the only
Way whose answer to a defended enemy is to go through the defence rather than around it, and several
of its Signatures protect the people next to you rather than you. In a straight fight it is the Way
that is hardest to remove.

**Weak at** everything that is not a straight fight. Its skill deck is thin outside Might, and the
armour it wants is paid for in weight: a fighter in plate with a shield and a pack is one bad
decision away from **Burdened**, which is Slowed plus disadvantage on exactly the Agility and Shadow
checks it was already worst at. Nothing it does is quiet, and nothing it does is a surprise.

Its titles are in *Specializations*, each with the Tactic that comes with it.

### Way of Shade

**Shade bypasses.** Where Strength hits through armour, Shade goes around it, and its strikes are
deliberately the weakest in the game so that not needing to hit hard is the point. It works on
**openings**, states like Off-Balance or Marked that do nothing by themselves and everything to the
card that was waiting for one. It sees what is coming, it is worth most against a target that does
not know you are there, and one of its cards takes a card out of an enemy's deck and keeps it.

**Strong at** arriving before the problem does. It has the best Shadow and Agility in the game, it
sees what is coming where other Ways guess, and against a target that is unaware or carrying an
opening it does damage no other Way can match in one blow. Armour and shields barely slow it down,
because it was never trying to get through them.

**Weak at** a fair fight. Its strikes are **mod 0 and minus 1** where Strength's run to plus 2, so a
Shade who has arranged nothing is a worse fighter than the Warrior standing beside them, and that is
deliberate. It is lightly armoured, it is spending cards to set up the hit rather than to survive
one, and an enemy that cannot be surprised or unbalanced takes away most of what it is for.

Its titles are in *Specializations*, with the Tactic each one carries.

### Way of Mind

**Mind studies.** Its spells are **formulas**: learned once, paid for once in your own substance, and
then yours. A first Way of Mind node grants **2 spell slots**, and a prepared spell occupies one
until you prepare something else.

**Strong at** answers nobody else has. It puts conditions on people, changes the ground they are
standing on, hits several enemies at once, reaches further than a weapon does, raises the dead and
wards against what is coming. It is the most adaptable Way between fights, because what it can do
tomorrow is whatever it prepares tonight.

**Weak at** doing it twice. Spell slots are few, a first node grants **two**, and a prepared spell
holds one until you prepare something else, so the decision of what you can do is made before you
know what you will meet. Several schools charge Health for the privilege. And **Silenced** switches
the entire Way off, because casting needs speech.

Its schools are in *Specializations*, and what to read there is the **Spends** column: every one of
them charges for a formula in something other than slots, and that price is the school. Ritual magic
is the part that takes days rather than a turn, and it draws from a twist deck of its own
(*Checks, outside a fight*).

**No part of it is priestly.** A school is studied out of a book, and that is the whole difference
between the two casting Ways: what Mind learns, Faith is given. Every god, every order and every
miracle belongs to Way of Faith, including the priests who are only lightly magical.

### Way of Faith

**Faith asks.** Its spells are **miracles**: granted rather than learned, and paid for every single
time you ask. A first Way of Faith node also grants 2 spell slots.

**Everything priestly is here**, and nowhere else. A priest's list is their god's list, and there
are twelve: **the Eternal God** and **the Eternal Spirit**; **Sessyfyn**, **Valtor**, **Fagar**,
**Vulpet** and **Brendan** of the Five; **the Grey**, **the Purple** and **the Yellow**; and **Vax**
and **the First**. A school of magic is something a scholar studies; a miracle is something a god
hands over, and no amount of study substitutes for having sworn.

Faith is built out of twelve gods, and this is the one Way where a choice is final. **Swearing
closes every rival creed permanently**, along with everything reachable only through it, and what
counts as a creed is the thing to get right: the Five are one creed, the three of **the Grey** are
one, and the four remaining gods each stand alone. So swearing to **Sessyfyn** leaves the other four
of the Five open to you, and shuts the other seven gods for good.

In exchange you carry that god's **Obedience**, which applies to everything they ever grant. Unlike
a Tactic, which is only ever your deepest title's, you carry **every** Obedience you have sworn to.
An order is sworn separately and carries a rule of the same kind, so a priest in an order pays that
as well. Six is the most anybody can carry: all five of the Five, and one order.

**Strong at** holding a party together. Its miracles heal, protect and lift what has been put on
somebody, and because a miracle is granted rather than learned it does not have to be studied first.
Its gods give standing benefits that are simply true of you from the day you swear.

**Weak at** flexibility, in every direction at once. A miracle is paid for **every single time** you
ask, not once when you learn it. Its slots are as few as Mind's. **Silenced** shuts it off the same
way. And the Obedience you took on is permanent: it is a restriction on how you may behave for the
rest of the character's life, and you carry every one you have sworn, while the doors you closed
swearing the first oath never reopen.

**So read these before you swear.** An Obedience is not a price you pay at a moment, it is a way you
have to live, and it is the whole of what makes one priest a different character from another rather
than a different spell list. Four of the gods also give something back that nobody else can do.


#### The Five

Swear to one of these and the other 4 stay open to you. Take two and you pay two obediences, take all of them and you pay all of them.

| God | Obedience |
|---|---|
| **Sessyfyn** | A permanent Miss AND a permanent Critical in your fate deck. Not one or the other. |
| **Valtor** | Every miracle costs Strain 1 on top of its slots. |
| **Fagar** | Each miracle may be cast once per fight. A trick works once. |
| **Vulpet** | You may not accept help: no ally may Heal you, Interpose for you, or Shake a condition off you. |
| **Brendan** | Nothing he gives arrives on the turn you ask for it: every miracle resolves at the start of your next turn at the earliest. |

#### The three of the Grey

Swear to one of these and the other 2 stay open to you. Take two and you pay two obediences, take all of them and you pay all of them.

| God | Obedience |
|---|---|
| **The Grey** | Adjacent only. Every miracle of The Grey reaches one square and no further. |
| **The Purple** | The first miracle you cast each fight gives you 1 Taint. You are the first to taste it, always. |
| **The Yellow** | A lie must be kept up: every miracle ends the moment you fail to spend a secondary on it. |

#### The gods who stand alone

One each, and swearing to any of them closes the other eleven gods for good. Each of the four gives something back that no other god does, which is the other half of the bargain.

| God | Obedience | What the god gives back |
|---|---|---|
| **The Eternal God** | Your action deck may hold no more than 12 cards, and the faith names what may not be in it. | Solidarity: his miracles cast on a brother or sister in faith cost one less -- no Strain, no Spent. |
| **The Eternal Spirit** | You must stay Unburdened, and wear no armour heavier than Leather. | The spirit drifts through all the air: his miracles ignore range and cover. |
| **Vax** | Every miracle costs at least 1 Health, and the card may ask more. | His miracles need no free hand: the spiked club is the holy symbol. |
| **The First** | Your spell slots are capped at 3, for ever. | His miracles return what they take: Health drained from a living target comes back to you, which is the only renewable pool in the game. |

#### The four orders

An order is sworn separately and carries a rule of its own, so a priest in an order pays that **and** their god's Obedience. You may belong to one order.

| Order | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Inquisitor** | You hold one thing about every ally that the others do not know, and the order may ask you for it. |
| **Shieldsworn** | You may not give ground: while an ally stands further forward than you, you may not move away from the enemy. |
| **Anchorite** | Three skill cards are set aside each morning and are not yours until the next. The cloister keeps them. |
| **Flagellant** | One Wild stays in your fate deck for good. An ecstasy is not a thing you put down. |

## 20. Specializations

A **specialization node** is a step on a Way that also makes you something. It hands over its
reward like any other node, and on top of that it gives you a **title**, which is what people call
you from there on, and a **Tactic**, which is a standing rule that nobody who has not walked that
far is allowed.

No character sees more than a handful of these, so this is a map rather than a reading list. Find
your Way, read down to where you are, and look one row further to see what the next step makes you.

Every one of them is written in the same five parts:

| Part | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Title** | what you are called from here on |
| **Leans on** | the one axis this build pushes |
| **Spends** | the resource it pays in, and therefore the thing it is bad at |
| **Signature** | the single card the build is named for |
| **Tactic** | a standing rule: always on, never a number |

**Signature and Tactic are a pair, and they divide cleanly.** The Signature is what the title lets
you **do**. It is a card, so it is in your hand about a third of the time. The Tactic is how the
title makes you **stand**. It is simply true, which means it is true on the turn you are deciding
where to go, and that is the only turn it would ever have mattered on.

**You only ever have your deepest title's Tactic.** Collecting titles does not collect Tactics, so a
character who has walked through four of these has four titles and one standing rule. The Way of
Faith is the exception you already know about: an **Obedience** is not a Tactic, and you carry every
one you have sworn (*The four Ways*).


### The axes

Every title leans on exactly one of these. An axis is not a new rule: it is which lever the build pulls on the rules you already have.

| Axis | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Control** | What you put on other people: conditions, and keeping them there. |
| **Deck shape** | The deck itself, how large it is allowed to be, and what you prune out of it. |
| **Position** | Where everybody is standing: Move, range, cover, and what one step is worth. |
| **Preparation** | Anything decided before it mattered: a spell slot, a set trap, a loaded gun. |
| **Reactions** | What you do on somebody else's turn, paid for out of your own hand. |
| **Support** | What you spend your turn on when the payoff lands on somebody else's. |
| **Tempo** | What a turn sets up and what you burn setting it up: Spent, Strain, getting Ready. |

### What the first step calls you

Walking into a Way is not yet a specialization, so these four carry no Signature and no Tactic. They are what you are called until you take one.

| Way | Title | Leans on | Spends |
|---|---|---|---|
| Way of Strength | **Warrior** | Position | weight |
| Way of Shade | **Shade** | Position | halves |
| Way of Mind | **Scholar** | Preparation | spell slots |
| Way of Faith | **Sworn** | Support | an obligation |

**Street Fighters** usually have no or little connection to armies. They often learned their art of fighting in order to survive a tough childhood.

**Shadow mages** find their power in dark places.

**Warders** are specialists in removing magic and in protecting areas.

**Household magicians** mostly focus on mundane magic, such as getting a good harvest or spoiling the neighbours' milk.

**Glamour mages** made a deal with the elves to be able to use a part of the elven magic.

**Tattoo magicians** brand themselves with prepared spells in the form of tattoos.

**Diviners** focus on predicting the future, or on finding answers about now by magic.

### Way of Faith: 16 titles

**The Eternal God's** servants see themselves as the true religion, and enforce that more than other religions do.

**The Eternal Spirit** shares the attitude of the Eternal God, being an offshoot, but has fewer members and less power. Sometimes the Eternal God and the Eternal Spirit work together, sometimes they kill each other.

**Vax** is a local god in Parfebe, and most followers have a connection to the city, even though cults pop up now and then in other places. Vax is a very macho religion.

**The First** worship the creator of all Strigloi. He is said to still be around, and is either described as senile or as terrifying.

**Sessyfyn**, **Valtor**, **Fagar**, **Vulpet** and **Brendan** are the **Five**, a small pantheon of gods whose servants work together and accept each other. It is mainly a coastal religion, and predates the Eternal God.

**The servants of the Grey, the Purple and the Yellow** also work together, but with less trust. It is a newer religion with origins among assassins and thieves.

**Inquisitors** are a religious role, church members working to remove sin as the church defines it.

**Shieldsworn** form the devout part of a church's army.

**Anchorites** ignore banal things in life in order to be fully one with their god. Some of them stay their whole life on a pillar, others just try to ignore people.

**Flagellants** drive themselves into ecstasy from the love of their religion. Some walk in processions whipping their bare backs, others dance for hours in groups.
