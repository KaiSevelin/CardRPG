# Simulation analysis – draft 0.2 (Warrior vs goblins)

3,000 simulated fights per scenario, run with `sim/combat_sim.py`.
Supersedes [analysis-2026-10-21.md](analysis-2026-10-21.md), which recorded draft 0.1.

## What changed in the rules since the last run

| Change | Where |
|---|---|
| Goblin weapons raised to **Strike 3**, and NPC gear is now tuned separately from player gear | core-rules §9, starter-cards *NPC gear* |
| A **Defend primary** now blocks every attack until your next turn (it used to be spent on the first hit) | core-rules §4, *Block* |
| Conditions tick at the start of the **creator's** turn | core-rules §7 (already in the last run) |
| Goblins carry **Pack Tactics + Cowardly** as their standard tactics, so both are on in the baseline | starter-cards |

The simulator's defaults now *are* the rules. Scenarios named "draft 0.1 …" keep an old rule
alive so the cost of each change stays measurable.

## Setup and assumptions

Unchanged from the last run except where noted:
- **Warrior:** 12 HP, Leather Armor (armor 1), Longsword (Strike 3), Wooden Shield (Defend 2), 1 Healing Potion, Strong feature, 16-card starter deck, hand size 5.
- **Goblins:** 5 HP, armor 0, Goblin Spear or Goblin Shortbow (**Strike 3**), 6-card goblin deck, Pack Tactics + Cowardly.
- **Map:** open 10×8 grid, goblins start 7 squares away.
- **Solo hero:** no party, so Taunt and Ready still do nothing.

### A bug in the simulator, not the rules

The last run warned that Cowardly looked "exaggerated by the test map". It was worse than that.
Goblins are seeded across the full height of an 8-row grid, so two of every three start **on** the
top or bottom edge — and a fleeing goblin escaped the moment it stood on any edge. A wounded goblin
standing next to the warrior was already at the exit.

Fleeing NPCs now have to be at the edge **and** at least 5 squares from the hero to get away.
That alone moved the baseline from 3.8 rounds back to 5.1. Every number below is post-fix.

## Findings

### 1. Cowardly is broken — a goblin with two tactics is weaker than one with none

This is the headline, and it's new.

| 4 goblins, Strike 3 | Win % | Rounds |
|---|---|---|
| No tactics (K) | 92.5 | 8.6 |
| **Pack Tactics only (L)** | **81.5** | 8.2 |
| Pack Tactics + Cowardly (C) | 97.1 | 6.5 |

Pack Tactics is a good keyword: it costs the hero 11 points of win rate. Cowardly then hands back
**15.6**, finishing above where the goblins started with no tactics at all. About 1.5 of 4 goblins
run before they can be killed, and they take their remaining attacks with them.

The intent — "the tactic gives players a weakness to exploit" — is right. The size is wrong. Right
now the keyword is a discount the GM pays without noticing, and stacking it on a monster that also
has a real tactic cancels the threat entirely.

**Cause:** Cowardly triggers per goblin, on its own Health. The first goblin to get hurt leaves
while its three healthy friends fight on, so the group is ground down one deserter at a time and
never reaches the dangerous part of the fight.

**Tested alternative — group morale:** the goblins hold while the pack is strong and break together
once half the group is down.

| | 4 spears | 2 spear + 2 bow | 5 spears |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cowardly as written (per goblin) | 97.1 | 91.2 | 83.2 |
| **Group morale** | **95.0** | **92.7** | **73.5** |

Group morale is better on every count that matters: it costs the hero more as the group grows (5
goblins go from 83% to 73.5%), it keeps the early fight dangerous, and the rout becomes a climax the
players earn rather than a slow leak. It's also better at the table — one dramatic moment where the
line breaks, instead of bookkeeping each goblin's nerve.

**Suggestion:** redefine the keyword.

> **Cowardly** — When half or more of its group is down, the survivors break and flee.

Even then Cowardly is a net discount (95.0 vs 92.5 with no tactics). That's acceptable *if* it's
priced that way: a Cowardly monster should be cheaper than its stats suggest, or the rout should
cost the players something — the fled goblins warn the next camp, carry off the loot, or come back
in the next fight with friends.

### 2. Goblin Strike 3 was the right call, but Cowardly is hiding most of it

| | Strike 2 | Strike 3 |
|---|---|---|
| 3 goblins | 100.0% (F) | 99.9% (A) |
| 4 goblins | 99.9% (G) | 97.1% (C) |

Compare the old run with Cowardly off: Pack goblins at Strike 2 won 2.3% of fights, at Strike 3 they
won 20.5% — the raise was worth about 18 points. Here it's worth 2.8. The damage change did exactly
what it was supposed to; the morale rule is eating it. Fix Cowardly (finding 1) and the raise pays
off as predicted.

**The "Strike at least 2 above armor" rule holds, and its corollary bites harder than expected:**

| Warrior armor | Win % (3 goblins) | HP left |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 98.7 | 9.8 |
| 1 | 99.9 | 10.7 |
| 2 | 100.0 | 11.1 |

At armor 2 against Strike 3, one point of damage gets through per hit and goblins are harmless
again. Armor 2 is a *full tier* of enemy switched off. Keep armor scarce, make heavy armor cost
something, and when heroes do get armor 2, retire the Strike 3 trash mobs rather than fielding more
of them.

### 3. Persistent Block is the right fix, but Shield Up is still a weak primary

| 4 goblins | Win % | Damage taken | Prevented by Shield Up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Draft 0.1: one-shot block (I) | 95.5 | 4.1 | 0.3 |
| **Draft 0.2: blocks every attack (C)** | **97.1** | **3.8** | **0.6** |

The change doubles the card's output and is worth 1.6 points of win rate. It is still a bad deal:
**0.6 damage prevented for a primary half**, against roughly 4.5 damage from a Strike primary. The
AI plays Shield Up as a primary 0.62 times per fight and almost always because it had nothing better.

The rule is now coherent — a defensive stance holds for a turn, which is what players expect — so
keep it. The remaining problem is the **number**, not the duration: Wooden Shield Defend 2 against
Strike 3 saves 2 damage from the one or two hits that actually land. Options for the next pass:

- raise shield Defend values (a real shield should be Defend 3);
- give Defend primaries a rider, so they do something besides absorb — Block adjacent allies too, or
  Block *and* counter-attack the first enemy that misses you;
- accept that Defend primaries are for specialists and put them behind a Way tree, where a
  shield-wall build can stack them.

### 4. Exhaustion: the 12-card deck works

Finding 3 of the last run suggested trying a smaller starter deck. It does what was wanted:

| 16-card deck | 12-card deck | |
|---|---|---|
| Deck dry in round 5.9 | **4.4** | 4 goblins |
| Catch Breath 0.51 | **0.89** | |
| Win 97.1% | 95.6% | |
| Win 91.2% | **83.7%** | 2 spear + 2 bow |

A 12-card deck costs 7.5 points of win rate in the hard fight and makes the hero catch their breath
almost once per fight. That's exhaustion becoming a real cost without dominating the easy fights.
Worth playtesting on paper before committing, since it also makes the deck less interesting to build.

When to Catch Breath still barely matters (T vs U: 96.0 vs 96.8), so leave it to the player.

### 5. Double strike is now worth half a round, not a full one

Removing Strike from Weapon Swap's secondary (N) costs 0.5 rounds, down from a full round in draft
0.1 — goblins die faster now, so there's less fight to shorten. It remains the strongest card in the
deck. The decision stands: these cards are **leveling rewards**, not starter cards. The starter deck
keeps its one copy until the leveling rules exist.

### 6. The heavy-hits rule still never fires

0.09 triggers per fight at Strike 3 with four goblins, up from 0.01. Armor 1 against Strike 3 means a
goblin needs a Critical to reach 5 damage. The rule is for ogres and bosses; it costs nothing to keep
and should be tested against a big monster, not a swarm.

### 7. Smaller observations

- **Who goes first still barely matters** (A vs B: 99.9 vs 99.7).
- **Strong (+1 damage)** is worth 0.8 rounds and 0.6 points of win rate (A vs M) — less than the 1.4
  rounds it was worth at Strike 2, because goblins now die to an unmodified Longsword often enough.
- **Dazed finally does something:** 0.23 applied and 0.19 suffered per fight in the baseline, up to
  1.13/0.61 in the archer fight. The condition-timing fix from the last run holds.
- **Archers are still the threat multiplier.** The bow fights are the only ones where the warrior
  ends below 8 HP, and cover disadvantage comes up 2.1 times per fight there.

## Limitations

Unchanged, and worth repeating:

- **Solo fights only.** No party, so Taunt, Ready, healing allies, Bodyguard and Commander are untested.
  Pack Tactics against a formation of four players is a different rule than against one hero.
- **Wounds and Taint aren't modelled.** The sim discards a card on a heavy hit instead of shuffling in a
  Wound card that clogs the hand. Lingering damage across several fights is untested.
- **Nothing out of combat.** The skill rules, challenges and the twist deck have no simulator at all,
  and their numbers (difficulty 3/5/7/9, Progress and Pressure tracks) are still first guesses.
- **Empty room.** No obstacles, so cover only comes from figures and the Sniper/Shield Wall keywords
  can't be tested.
- **The AI is decent, not optimal.** A human plays a little better.
