# Hand size as a levelling stat — 2026-11-11

[analysis-2026-11-01.md](analysis-2026-11-01.md) priced **a card of hand size at 6–10 points of win
rate**, called it the most expensive stat measured anywhere in this project, and wrote *remember
that before anything offers one*. [../rules/leveling.md](../rules/leveling.md) §4 has had no reward
row for it since, and the validator pins the absence as a decision rather than an oversight.

That figure measured **5 → 4**. Nobody had measured **5 → 6**, which is the direction a levelling
stat goes. This document measures the whole curve, finds the mechanism changes at the default, and
prices one node.

All runs: `combat_sim.py`, **3 seeds × 3000 fights** except §4, which is 3 × 2000. Encounters are
the four published ones. The Leather / shield 2 / hand 5 baseline is unchanged throughout.

---

## 1. The curve, hand 3 to hand 8

| hand | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3** | 90.4% | 67.5% | 37.3% | 10.1% |
| **4** | 96.3% | 81.6% | 52.6% | 20.8% |
| **5** *(default)* | 98.1% | 87.6% | 61.6% | 29.1% |
| **6** | 98.8% | 91.2% | 67.7% | 36.0% |
| **7** | 99.0% | 92.6% | 71.1% | 41.9% |
| **8** | 99.3% | 93.9% | 73.9% | 46.2% |

Standard deviation across seeds is 0.1 to 0.8 points everywhere.

| step | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 to 4 | +6.0 | +14.1 | +15.3 | +10.7 |
| 4 to 5 | +1.8 | +6.0 | +9.0 | +8.3 |
| **5 to 6** | **+0.6** | **+3.6** | **+6.2** | **+6.9** |
| 6 to 7 | +0.3 | +1.4 | +3.4 | +5.9 |
| 7 to 8 | +0.3 | +1.3 | +2.7 | +4.3 |

> **The reproduction check.** The 4 → 5 step is **+6.0 / +9.0 / +8.3** on the three encounters
> 2026-11-01 reported, against that document's **−6.1 / −9.9 / −8.2**. The simulator agrees with
> itself across four months of changes, which is what makes the rest of the table worth reading.

### Finding 1: it halves each step

6–10 points is the price of the card **at the default**, not the price of a card. The next one is
0.6–6.9 and the one after 0.3–5.9. The stat is far less dangerous going up than the published
figure makes it sound, and far more dangerous going down than anyone has written.

### Finding 2: the reaction reserve saturates at the default

Per fight, against 5 spears:

| hand | reactions fired | exhausted turns | breaths |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 2.86 | 0.23 | 2.11 |
| 4 | 4.02 | 0.19 | 2.70 |
| **5** | **4.50** | 0.17 | 2.88 |
| 6 | 4.61 | 0.17 | 2.96 |
| 7 | 4.68 | 0.16 | 2.94 |
| 8 | 4.63 | 0.15 | 2.91 |

> **Above the default, hand size stops being a reserve stat and becomes a selection stat.** The
> sixth card fires **0.11** more reactions than the fifth and wins 6.2 points more against 5
> spears. It is not buying another defence; it is buying a better pair to play. Exhaustion and
> breaths are flat across the same range, so it is not buying stamina either.
>
> This is the finding that decides where the reward belongs. **Selection scales with how varied a
> deck is, not with how large it is** — it is worth most to a character carrying situational cards
> and least to one who wants the same pair every turn. A deck-size reward and a hand-size reward
> are therefore not two sizes of the same thing, and should not go to the same Way.

### Finding 3: below the default it is a cliff

Hand 3 wins **10.1%** of the Deadly encounter against 29.1% at the default. A hand of 2, which the
turn structure makes the floor, is below that.

> **So hand 2 to 6 is not a stat range, it is four difficulty settings.** Across it the Deadly
> encounter runs roughly 10% to 36%. Lowering the default to open room underneath is the mistake
> §2 of 2026-11-01 already refused for stamina: it rewrites every published encounter rating.

## 2. What a point of Health is worth, so the card can be priced in a rung that exists

Health is weight **2** on [../rules/leveling.md](../rules/leveling.md) §5b's rung table and is
granted a point at a time by eleven nodes. Nothing in this directory had ever measured it, so there
was no exchange rate between the dearest stat in the game and the ordinary one it would sit beside.

| arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Health 12** *(baseline)* | 98.1% | 87.6% | 61.6% | 29.1% |
| Health 13 | 98.7% | 89.1% | 65.5% | 32.7% |
| Health 14 | 99.0% | 90.8% | 68.2% | 35.2% |
| Health 15 | 99.0% | 91.8% | 71.3% | 39.3% |
| Health 16 | 99.3% | 93.0% | 72.9% | 42.5% |
| Health 18 | 99.6% | 94.6% | 77.4% | 48.1% |

A point of Health, averaged over the six: **+0.25 / +1.17 / +2.63 / +3.17**. Close to linear, which
is worth knowing on its own — nothing else measured here is.

> **One card of hand size is worth about two and a half points of Health**, and the ratio holds
> across the whole difficulty range: **2.4 / 3.1 / 2.4 / 2.2**.
>
> That is a much better description of the problem than *the dearest stat in the game*. It is not
> off the rung table by an order of magnitude; it is off by about 2.5×. A reward 2.5 nodes deep is
> not unprintable, it is **a major node's reward rather than a minor one's** — which is where
> §5a.4's shape 8, *a deck or hand rule, draw one more*, already puts it.

## 3. Deck size is steeply asymmetric, and this corrects a proposal

| arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **16 cards** *(baseline)* | 98.1% | 87.6% | 61.6% | 29.1% |
| 14 cards, two cut at random | 95.6% | 80.2% | 49.8% | 20.4% |

**Removing two cards costs −2.5 / −7.4 / −11.8 / −8.7.** Against [the March's own
measurement](../rules/leveling.md) — *filling the extra slots with the deck's best card: +1.5 at
18, +3.3 at 20, +4.3 at 22* — the deck is worth two to four times as much at the bottom as at the
top.

> **Which kills the obvious trade.** *+1 hand size, −2 deck cap* was the first proposal, written
> because it mirrors *The March* exactly (`carry: 5, deckMax: 2`). It is wrong in both readings:
> as a **cap** it costs almost nothing, because it only forbids cards worth +1.5 that the character
> may never buy; as **cards** it costs 2 to 4 times what the hand card gains. There is no reading
> of it that is a fair price.

## 4. The node, measured

**3 seeds × 2000 fights.** *One card Spent* is `deck_cut=1, deck_cut_random=True`, which is the
Overloaded rule's own wording — a card of your deck is out of this fight and you did not choose it.

| arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 98.2% | 87.3% | 61.0% | 28.9% |
| the price alone: 1 card Spent | 97.0% (−1.2) | 83.6% (−3.7) | 56.3% (−4.7) | 26.5% (−2.4) |
| **hand 6, 1 card Spent** | **98.1% (−0.2)** | **88.1% (+0.8)** | **60.7% (−0.3)** | **31.4% (+2.5)** |
| hand 6, 2 cards Spent | 96.8% (−1.5) | 82.9% (−4.4) | 54.5% (−6.6) | 26.7% (−2.2) |
| the reward as a loss: hand 4 | 96.2% (−2.0) | 81.8% (−5.5) | 53.2% (−7.9) | 20.5% (−8.4) |

> **−0.2 / +0.8 / −0.3 / +2.5, against the Duellist's −1.0 / +1.9 / +4.1.** The same shape
> [../rules/specializations.md](../rules/specializations.md) calls what a specialization should
> look like — level where you were winning anyway, ahead where it matters — and slightly flatter,
> which for the stat this document opened by calling the most expensive in the game is the right
> side to err on.
>
> **Two cards Spent is clearly overpriced** at −1.5 / −4.4 / −6.6 / −2.2, so the price is one card
> and not a knob to turn.

## 5. The subtraction catalogue has a hole, and a card of hand fits it

[../rules/leveling.md](../rules/leveling.md) §5a.3 requires every major node to **subtract
something the engine enforces**, and 2026-11-01 found that the obvious currency does not work:
*armour is the wrong currency, by about 10×*. Setting the measurements side by side:

| What a node could take away | 4 mixed |
|---|---|
| the ⚡ Sidestep | +1.0 *(free)* |
| the shield | −0.4 |
| **a card of hand size** | **−8.4** |
| two cards of deck | −8.7 |
| armour | −30 |

> **Between one point and thirty there is nothing, and a card of hand sits exactly in the gap.** A
> major node priced *your hand is one card smaller* would be the best-priced subtraction available,
> cost nothing in balance because it is a price rather than a reward, and read in the register
> §5a.3 calls the most vivid anyone has drafted — Berserker, Street Fighter, *you keep nothing
> back*. It needs no cap, because it moves the wrong way to need one.
>
> This is the cheaper half of the idea and probably the better one.

## What this does not test

- **The cap.** Nothing here measures a character at hand 6 holding *Quick* and the *Crown of the
  Quiet Mind* as well. Base 5 plus both is **7** today with nothing stopping it, and that
  combination has never been measured; §1's table puts hand 7 at +41.9% on the Deadly encounter,
  12.8 points over the default.
- **A party.** One hero, as every run in this directory has been. Hand size feeds reactions, and
  reactions are the one thing a second character changes most.
- **A human.** The simulator picks the best-scoring pair in hand. Finding 2 says the sixth card is
  bought for selection, and a player selects differently from a greedy scorer — probably better,
  which would make the card worth **more** than measured here rather than less.
- **Whether a hand-size node belongs on Way of Shade.** Finding 2 is an argument about what the
  stat does, not a measurement of a Way. Shade prunes where Strength adds, and selection is the
  reward that suits a refined deck; that is reasoning, and it is not in this table.
