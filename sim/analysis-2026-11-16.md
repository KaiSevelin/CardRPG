# What the second round of a condition is worth — 2026-11-16

**The decision this measures.** *Set*, the Trapmaker's Signature, carried `intensity: 2` on
**Slowed** — a field that did nothing, because Slowed is not a `valued` condition and an intensity
on it is read by nothing. The number was almost certainly meant to be `duration: 2`, so the card
printed *Slowed (1)* and always had. Changing it is a tuning decision rather than a typo: the prose
says nothing about how long, and two rounds of Slowed from a trap is a different card from one.

So the question is not *what does Slowed cost* but **what does the second round of it add**.

`conditions_sim.py` §5, 2000 fights × 5 seeds, the Deadly encounter the other price sections use
(4 goblins, 2 spears + 2 bows).

---

## What it measures, and what it does not

**It prices the mechanism, on the hero's side of it.** The harness applies the configured condition
to the **hero**, from a goblin's attack, because that is where `halves_move` meets a real Move
budget.

**Slowed on a goblin cannot be measured here at all**, and that is worth stating rather than
working around. A goblin in this model has no Move to halve: `step_toward` returns one free
neighbour that gets strictly closer, one square per turn. Halving one square floors to nought, so a
naive extension would report that **Slowed stops a goblin dead** — enormously strong, and not the
game, where a Move is three to five squares. Any number produced that way would be confidently
wrong, which is worse than a careful proxy.

So what follows is the price of a round of Slowed where Slowed means something, and the Trapmaker's
card inflicts the same mechanism in the other direction.

## The measurement

| | win rate | cost against no condition |
|---|---|---|
| no condition (a plain Strike) | **34.6%** ± 1.3 | — |
| Slowed (1) | 27.5% ± 1.0 | **7.1** |
| Slowed (2) | 23.3% ± 1.2 | **11.4** |
| Slowed (3) | 19.9% ± 0.5 | **14.7** |

**The first round costs 7.1 points. The second adds 4.3. The third adds 3.3.** Monotonic,
diminishing, and every spread is small enough that the steps are real rather than noise.

## What it says about the change

**The second round is not a rounding error.** 4.3 points is on the order of what this project has
measured for a point or two of Health, and well above anything it calls flavour. A Signature that
costs **a turn spent early** — a turn that does nothing while it happens — is paying a lot, and one
round of one enemy slowed was thin against that. Two rounds is proportionate rather than generous.

**And the diminishing curve is the reassuring part.** If the second round had been worth as much as
the first, a duration would be a dial nobody should touch. It is worth about three fifths, so the
card is better and not transformed.

**[OPEN] What this cannot say** is what *Set* is worth, because that needs Slowed on an enemy and
this model has nowhere to put it. Giving goblins a Move budget rather than a one-square step is the
change that would allow it — a real extension to the oldest and most-exercised part of the
simulator, and the kind of thing to do deliberately rather than as a side effect of pricing one
card.
