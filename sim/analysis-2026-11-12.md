# Stamina as a stat on the actor — 2026-11-12

**The rule, as ruled.** Stamina is a stat on the actor: **how many cards you may play in a fight**,
unrelated to deck size. Every card you play costs 1 — primary, secondary or reaction, discarded or
Spent. Nothing you did not play costs any. An empty draw pile with stamina left is shuffled back
from the discard, so the deck cycles for free. At 0 the rest of the draw pile is discarded. **Catch
Breath** moves 2 cards back *and* regains 2 stamina.

[analysis-2026-11-01.md](analysis-2026-11-01.md) §2 measured a **different** rule under the same
name: stamina capped cards *drawn*, and only `min(stamina, collection)` came to the fight at all.
`cfg.stamina` has been rewritten rather than given a second dial, so that document's §2 table is no
longer reproducible from this code. Deliberate: one dial for one concept.

All runs: `combat_sim.py`, 3 seeds, **2000 fights** in §1–§3 and **1500** in §4–§6. Encounters are
the four published ones. The hard wall throughout except where §6 says otherwise.

Standard deviation across seeds is 0.1 to 1.8 points.

### Finding 1: the default is 14, and it is not 16

**Stamina 14 reproduces today's difficulty within 3.7 points on every encounter**
(+0.2 / +1.6 / +3.7 / −2.3). Nothing else is close: 13 is 7 points light on the Deadly fight and 16
is 13 points heavy on the Hard one.

> **Fourteen, against a deck of sixteen cards.** The number is not the deck's length, because
> today's hero does not stop at sixteen — a breath tops the pile back up, and the measured hero
> plays about **17** cards in a five-spear fight. Fourteen plus two per breath is where that lands,
> which is why the stat that reproduces a sixteen-card deck is fourteen and not sixteen.

This is the check §2 of 2026-11-01 demanded and failed to get: *if the intent is to keep the current
difficulty while decoupling the deck, the stat starts where the deck is now.* It does not start
where the deck is. It starts two below.

### Finding 2: stamina is the only stat measured here that does not flatten

Marginal value of **2 points**:

| 8 to 10 | +5.1 | +12.5 | +9.8 | +5.6 |
| 10 to 12 | +2.7 | +10.6 | +13.4 | +6.8 |
| 12 to 14 | +2.0 | +5.7 | +10.2 | +8.3 |
| **14 to 16** | **+0.7** | **+3.9** | **+9.6** | **+8.8** |
| 16 to 18 | +0.1 | +2.1 | +7.0 | +8.5 |
| 18 to 20 | +0.2 | +1.1 | +3.1 | +8.6 |

> **On the Deadly encounter it accelerates.** 5.6, 6.8, 8.3, 8.8, 8.5, 8.6, 11.4 — a stat that is
> worth *more* per point at 24 than at 10. Hand size halves every step
> ([analysis-2026-11-11.md](analysis-2026-11-11.md) §1) and Health is close to linear. This one
> climbs, because the hardest fight is the longest one and the longest fight is the one a budget
> decides.
>
> **So there is no safe upper end to this stat.** Every other dial in this project could be left
> uncapped on the grounds that it runs out of road. This one does not have any.

## 2. Wall or interval: your rule makes the game breathe less

Breaths and Exhausted turns per fight:

| arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **old rule** | 0.98b 0.03e | 2.01b 0.09e | 2.87b 0.18e | 2.14b 0.17e |
| **stamina 14** | **0.72b 0.01e** | **1.50b 0.05e** | **2.20b 0.10e** | **1.53b 0.07e** |
| stamina 20 | 0.08b 0.00e | 0.32b 0.01e | 0.81b 0.02e | 0.73b 0.02e |
| stamina 24 | 0.01b 0.00e | 0.08b 0.00e | 0.29b 0.00e | 0.33b 0.01e |

> **At the calibrated default the hero breathes a quarter less than it does today**, and is
> Exhausted about half as often, while winning the same fights. The free cycle gives back the turns
> that breathing was eating: you no longer spend a turn shuffling cards you were going to see
> anyway.
>
> And **Catch Breath nearly disappears at the top of the range** — 0.01 to 0.33 breaths a fight at
> stamina 24. [../rules/design-direction.md](../rules/design-direction.md) calls the breath *"the
> biggest single lever found so far"* at 22 points of win rate in the hardest fight. Under this
> rule that lever is softened at the default and effectively gone for a high-stamina character,
> which is a design decision this table makes rather than reports.

The wall is an **interval**, as 2026-11-01's open question predicted: a breath costs no stamina and
grants 2, so past the wall a hero alternates one empty turn with one full one for ever. Nothing in
the ruling caps how many times it may be done.

## 3. What a point of stamina is worth, in the two currencies already measured

Per point, read across stamina 12 to 16:

| | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **a point of stamina** | **+0.7** | **+2.4** | **+5.0** | **+4.3** |
| a point of Health | +0.25 | +1.17 | +2.63 | +3.17 |
| a card of hand size | +0.6 | +3.6 | +6.2 | +6.9 |

> **One point of stamina is about two points of Health, and about three quarters of a card of hand
> size.** The ratios against Health are 2.7 / 2.1 / 1.9 / 1.3 and against the hand card
> 1.1 / 0.67 / 0.80 / 0.62.
>
> Health is weight **2** on [../rules/leveling.md](../rules/leveling.md) §5b's rung table. A single
> point of stamina is therefore a weight-4 reward on a table whose top rung is 3 — which puts it
> alongside the hand card rather than alongside Health, and means **it can never be granted two at
> a time**. A career should move this stat by about two points in total.

## 4. Deck size inverts, and it is monotonic

| arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **old rule, 16 cards** | 98.2% | 87.0% | 61.2% | 28.9% |
| old rule, 12 pruned well | −8.6 | −24.7 | −26.3 | −3.5 |
| old rule, 20 cards | +1.0 | +6.6 | +14.7 | −1.0 |
| **stamina 14, 16 cards** | 98.6% | 88.8% | 65.0% | 26.6% |
| stamina 14, **12 pruned well** | −0.2 | −1.6 | −2.4 | **+20.0** |
| stamina 14, 12 pruned at random | −0.1 | +0.0 | +0.3 | +1.8 |
| stamina 14, 20 cards | −1.1 | −2.7 | −7.7 | **−12.4** |
| stamina 14, 24 cards | −1.6 | −6.8 | −12.3 | **−18.3** |

Bigger decks are built the way [../rules/leveling.md](../rules/leveling.md) §4 built them: the extra
slots filled with the deck's best card.

### Finding 3: pruning well is the largest single gain measured in this directory

**+20.0 points on the Deadly encounter**, where the same cut under the old rule was −3.5 and on the
Hard encounter was −26.3. *More cards means more stamina before exhaustion, and your best cards come
up less often* ([../rules/core-rules.md](../rules/core-rules.md) §8) was a trade; the first half of
it is now false, and what is left is one-directional.

> **Why it lands only on the Deadly fight.** Pruning is worth nothing on the three short encounters
> and everything on the long one, because **a short deck only pays once you cycle it.** A hero
> plays about 16 cards in the mixed fight: a 12-card deck comes round and hands back its best
> cards, a 16-card deck does not come round at all.

### Finding 4: and the cap is not a reward any more, it is a drawback

**Twenty cards costs −7.7 and −12.4 on the two hard encounters**, against +6.6 and +14.7 under the
old rule. `deckMax` is granted in two places — *The March* ([nodes.mjs](../foundry/cardrpg/module/content/nodes.mjs)
`carry: 5, deckMax: 2`) and an item ([gear.mjs](../foundry/cardrpg/module/content/gear.mjs)) — and
in both it now takes something away.

> **The extra slots were filled with the deck's *best* card and it still lost twelve points.** The
> mechanism is not dilution, it is coverage: with the budget fixed you see a **smaller fraction** of
> a bigger deck, so the Shield Up or the Second Wind you needed is likelier to be one of the cards
> you never drew. A bigger deck is not less versatile in principle — it is less versatile *in your
> hand*, which is the only place versatility exists.

### Finding 5: the trade-off has no teeth, and this is the problem the rule creates

**Cutting four cards at random costs nothing: −0.1 / +0.0 / +0.3 / +1.8.**

> This is the brake coming off. [../rules/design-direction.md](../rules/design-direction.md) keeps
> the starter deck at sixteen for one stated reason — *"not because small decks are weak… because
> pruning badly collapses it (45%), and a new character can't know what they'll face"*. Under this
> rule pruning badly does not collapse anything. It is still **better than not pruning**.
>
> So deck size is now a **monotonic penalty**: 12 beats 16 beats 20 beats 24, however well or
> badly the cut is made. Every character will play at exactly the deck minimum, and the minimum is
> the only rule left holding the deck together — a number that today is justified by a measurement
> this table has just overturned.
>
> **The versatility the trade-off needs is real but it is not in this table**, and it cannot be: a
> simulator that fights one encounter at a time cannot price a deck that has to answer the next
> fight too. *Pruned at random* is this file's only proxy for *pruned wrong*, and it is free. If the
> trade is to have teeth, something has to give them to it.

## 5. Overloaded has evaporated

| arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| old rule, Overloaded | −2.3 | −6.5 | −11.2 | −8.5 |
| **stamina 14, Overloaded** | **−0.1** | **−0.2** | **+0.8** | **−0.5** |

[../rules/core-rules.md](../rules/core-rules.md) §8 says Overloaded *"costs you stamina, which in
this game means deck"* — two cards Spent before the fight starts.

> **Those two cards were never played, so under this rule they cost nothing**, and the measurement
> is zero on all four encounters. The sentence in §8 is now false in both halves: stamina does not
> mean deck, and Overloaded does not cost stamina. **[OPEN]** A drawback that costs 2 stamina
> directly would restore it to roughly its measured price, and would be the plainer reading of
> *costs you stamina* anyway.

The same arithmetic applies to every card that leaves the deck without being played. **A Wound is
free now** — it clogs a hand slot for a turn and costs no budget. So are Taint, Fear and the
cards an injury carries.

## 6. The two readings of the wall, priced

*"When spent cards equal your stamina the rest of the deck is discarded"* can mean the deck stops or
the player stops. The cards in your hand are not the deck, so under the literal reading you play
them out.

| | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| soft wall, against the hard one | +0.4 | +2.4 | +5.7 | **+9.5** |

> **The soft wall is worth up to 9.5 points**, so this is a real ruling and not a detail. It is also
> the reading under which **a bigger hand buys a second helping of stamina** — the overrun is a
> hand's worth of cards — which would compound with the hand-size node priced on 2026-11-11 and
> make that stat dearer again. The hard wall is recommended on both counts.
>
> > **[2026-11-12] Both halves of that sentence came true.** The wall was ruled hard, and §7
> > re-measured the hand-size node under the ruling: the stat did get dearer, by enough that §8
> > prices the reward and caps it at 7.

## 7. Hand size stopped saturating, and that is this ruling's loudest side effect

2026-11-11 priced a card of hand size under *your action deck is your stamina*, and that rule is
gone, so the number went with it. Under the old reading a bigger hand carried a hidden drawback:
every card held was a card out of a finite draw pile, so a big hand bought earlier exhaustion. Under
§8 drawing is **free**. Hand size is pure selection with no counterweight at all.

3 seeds × 2000 fights, stamina 14:

| arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hand 5 | 98.5% | 88.9% | 64.7% | 26.6% |
| hand 6 | 99.2% | 93.0% | 72.1% | 36.2% |
| hand 7 | 99.4% | 95.4% | 78.9% | 48.1% |
| hand 8 | 99.6% | 97.2% | 82.4% | 59.1% |

| step | old rule | **under stamina** |
|---|---|---|
| 5 → 6 | +7.2 | **+9.6** |
| 6 → 7 | +5.9 | **+11.9** |
| 7 → 8 | +4.3 | **+11.1** |

**The curve stopped saturating and started accelerating.** Hand 8 wins 59.1% of the Deadly
encounter against hand 5's 26.6% — a 32.5-point spread where the old rule's was 17.1.

**And it is not buying defence.** Reactions stay flat at 4.60 / 4.67 / 4.66 / 4.48 and cards played
is flat too, because the budget decides that now. What a bigger hand buys is *which* cards you
spend the same budget on, which is why the gain never runs out: there is no pool to drain.

> **The reproduction check passed first.** The old rule's 5 → 6 measures +0.5 / +3.7 / +6.7 / +7.2
> here against 2026-11-11's +0.6 / +3.6 / +6.2 / +6.9, so the simulator agrees with itself across
> the rewrite and the difference above is the rule rather than the code. §4's two deck arms
> re-confirmed against the breath clamp at the same time: **+20.1** for twelve pruned well and
> **−12.7** at twenty cards, against the +20.0 and −12.4 measured before the clamp existed.

**This is what made a cap urgent rather than tidy.** Base 5 plus *Quick* plus the *Crown of the
Quiet Mind* is **7**, live today with nothing stopping it, and that is now **+21.5** on the Deadly
encounter.

## 8. The price of a card of hand size, and why the obvious price is a trap

Everything against hand 5 / stamina 14, 3 seeds × 2000 fights:

| arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the reward naked (hand 6) | +0.8 | +4.0 | +7.4 | **+9.6** |
| **hand 6, −1 stamina** | **+0.3** | **+2.1** | **+2.6** | **+4.5** |
| hand 6, −2 stamina | −0.7 | −1.4 | −4.4 | −0.9 |
| the price alone, −1 stamina | −0.7 | −2.3 | −4.9 | −4.7 |
| +1 stamina alone | +0.5 | +2.1 | +5.4 | +4.8 |
| +2 Health, the rung's yardstick | +0.6 | +3.2 | +6.9 | +5.9 |
| live today: hand 7 | +0.9 | +6.5 | +14.2 | +21.5 |
| hand 8, −1 stamina | +1.0 | +6.5 | +12.2 | +24.8 |

**The two halves simply add.** Reward plus price predicts +0.1 / +1.7 / +2.5 / +4.9 and the arm
measures +0.3 / +2.1 / +2.6 / +4.5. There is no interaction worth naming, which means the price
does what it says and nothing else.

**Stamina is locally linear at 14.** +1 is +0.5 / +2.1 / +5.4 / +4.8 and −1 is
−0.7 / −2.3 / −4.9 / −4.7: the same number with the sign flipped, so the default is not sitting on
a cliff in either direction. That is the first good news about 14 that §1 could not give.

**At −1 the node is a real choice. At −2 it is a trap.** One point of stamina puts the reward below
both of its rung-2 neighbours on three encounters and level with them on the fourth — a row a player
can turn down, which is the only test a reward has to pass. Two points make it negative or flat on
all four. That is the finding that mattered most here, because −2 was the intuitive price: a card
for a card, doubled for a stat that matters.

**The price is paid in tempo, not in defence.** Reactions barely move (4.60 → 4.62 → 4.57); breaths
do (1.53 → 1.81 → 1.95 on the Deadly encounter). So the node reads as *a better hand, bought with
about a third of a turn spent standing still*.

> **And the cap is 7.** With the price attached, 7 → 8 is **+0.1 / +0.0 / −2.0 / +3.3** — the paid
> curve flattens everywhere but the Deadly fight, where the free curve accelerated. Seven is also
> what the content already builds to, so a cap of 6 would have cancelled a trait or a 1400-value
> wonder, and the tray's hand wraps, so the legibility half of the argument does not need 6 either.
>
> The one thing against it: capping at 7 blesses that +21.5 rather than fixing it. The honest
> alternative is a cap of 7 **and** a re-costed *Crown of the Quiet Mind*, which is its own job.

## 9. Injuries do not compound, which is what the cap of one was guarding against

ideas.md's worry about this list was a **death spiral**, and core-rules §8 answered it with one
injury per category before anything had measured a stack. One *Bad Leg* was on record at &minus;8.1;
two injuries together were not, in any combination.

**Six of the fourteen are measurable here** and the limit is the simulator rather than the rules:
Bad Leg, Shattered Nerves, Night Fears, Hexed Blood, Compelled Step and Rotted. The others are a
lost hand's slot, a lost eye's range on a hero carrying no bow, a caster's slot on a hero who casts
nothing, a scarred face in a conversation, and the two intruders with no dial &mdash; **Rage** and
**Doubt**. Doubt is a *skill-deck* intruder and this simulator has no skill deck, so *Hollow Eyes*
has no arm and Corruption &times;2 could not be measured at all.

3 seeds &times; 2000 fights, stamina 14, against an unhurt hero:

| arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bad Leg (maiming) | &minus;1.0 | &minus;4.2 | &minus;7.0 | **&minus;16.5** |
| Shattered Nerves (breaking) | &minus;0.0 | &minus;0.1 | &minus;0.5 | &minus;1.2 |
| Night Fears (breaking) | &minus;0.3 | +0.2 | +0.2 | &minus;4.9 |
| Hexed Blood (curse) | &minus;1.4 | &minus;4.4 | &minus;6.0 | &minus;3.6 |
| Compelled Step (curse) | &minus;0.8 | &minus;1.4 | &minus;3.8 | &minus;2.3 |
| Rotted (corruption) | &minus;1.1 | &minus;3.7 | &minus;7.1 | &minus;6.7 |
| BREAKING &times;2 | &minus;0.4 | &minus;0.6 | &minus;0.4 | &minus;5.5 |
| CURSE &times;2 | &minus;1.9 | &minus;6.0 | &minus;8.1 | &minus;5.3 |
| worst today, one per category | &minus;6.0 | &minus;17.8 | &minus;24.2 | &minus;21.1 |
| worst at cap 2, plus the Debt | &minus;8.2 | &minus;21.8 | &minus;26.0 | &minus;23.5 |

**They add, and they do not multiply.** Each pair lands within noise of the sum of its parts:
Breaking &times;2 measures &minus;0.4 / &minus;0.6 / &minus;0.4 / &minus;5.5 against
&minus;0.3 / +0.1 / &minus;0.2 / &minus;6.1 apart, and Curse &times;2 is the same story.

**The full stack is sub-additive, and the reason is grim rather than reassuring.** The worst legal
character today measures **&minus;21.1** on the Deadly encounter where the parts sum to
&minus;30.3 &mdash; nine points *less* bad than additive. The mechanism says why: an injured hero
breathes less and the fight is shorter (1.53 breaths to 0.95, 8.5 rounds to 7.0). You lose faster,
so there are fewer rounds left in which to suffer. That is a floor and not a spiral, which is the
finding the cap was set blind against.

> **So two of a kind costs about two points.** Worst-at-2 against worst-at-1 is
> &minus;2.2 / &minus;3.9 / &minus;1.8 / &minus;2.3, roughly one point of Health's worth of extra
> downside for letting a one-eyed character also fear the dark. **[RULED 2026-11-12]**

**Two findings that were not the question.** *Bad Leg* is the most lopsided injury measured:
&minus;1.0 on the easy fight and &minus;16.5 on the Deadly one, which is mobility again. And
**Shattered Nerves measures almost nothing** &mdash; *no reactions in the first round* is a penalty
on the round where least has happened, and it is the one injury on the list that is close to free.

## 10. A day, rather than a fight &mdash; and a Spent card is thirteen points

`day_sim.py`. Every other run in this directory is one fight from a standing start, which cannot ask
two of this project's questions at all. **What carries** is taken from `afterCombat` and `rest`
rather than invented: Health, Spent cards, Wounds and potions carry; stamina comes back in full,
because it measures one fight. So a day is not a stamina test &mdash; what a day costs you is
Health, Spent cards, and dead cards piling up in the deck.

**Two days of comparable weight and different kind**, so the thing measured is the *shape* of a
fight and not its difficulty. An earlier draft put the Deadly encounter in the day and every arm
cleared about 10%: a day containing one fight nobody wins measures that fight and nothing else.

- **melee day** &mdash; 3 spears, 4 spears, 3 spears
- **mixed day** &mdash; 2 bows, 2 spears + 1 bow, 1 spear + 1 bow

### 10.1 Mispruning is catastrophic in exactly one direction

The day is held fixed and only **what the deck was pruned for** changes, so the day's own difficulty
is out of the comparison. 3 seeds &times; 800 days:

| day | pruned right | pruned wrong | being wrong costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| melee | 77.7% | 90.2% | **+12.5** |
| mixed | 71.7% | 6.7% | **&minus;65.0** |

**A melee-tuned deck cannot answer archers: 6.7% of days cleared, against 71.6% for the deck tuned
the other way.** That is the versatility price, and it is the largest number this directory holds.

**But it is not symmetric, and that is the real finding.** A deck pruned for archers is *better* on
the melee day too (+12.5), so pruning for the harder shape dominates on both. Against the pristine
sixteen: **+12.0** on the melee day and **+41.5** on the mixed one. The axis is not
*versatile against effective* &mdash; it is that pruning against a ranged board keeps mobility, and
this project already knows mobility is the most valuable thing in a starter deck.

> **A caveat the number needs.** These are the **simulator's** pruning policy (`prune_value`), not a
> human's. What is measured is *a deck optimised for the wrong shape*, which is the right question;
> whether a player at a table would prune that way is not something this can say.

**And a wide deck is a real drawback over a day**: twenty cards is **&minus;8.2** on the melee day
and **&minus;19.2** on the mixed one, against sixteen. **Blind pruning is still about free**
(&minus;0.9 and +4.4), which reproduces §4's +1.8 from the single-fight runs: a quarter of the
starter deck is chaff whichever four cards go.

### 10.2 The intruder brake does not bite, and a Spent card does

3 spears over and over until the hero falls, 3 seeds &times; 800 days:

| deck | fights cleared of 10 | cleared all 10 | HP left | Wounds held at the end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16, pristine | 7.41 | 46.4% | 3.1 | 0.45 |
| 12, pruned | 8.08 | 59.7% | 4.7 | 0.38 |
| 6, to the bone | 8.41 | 62.6% | 4.5 | 0.39 |
| 12, pruned, one card printed Spent | 7.43 | 46.6% | 3.3 | 0.46 |

**The brake does not appear.** The worry was that a Wound is 8% of a twelve-card deck and 17% of a
six-card one, so dilution should punish a small deck over a long day. It does not show up here: the
six-card deck clears **more** fights than the sixteen, and the wounds held at the end are the same
to within noise (0.39 against 0.45). The reason is the rate &mdash; about **0.4 wounds after eight
fights**, which is too few to move a total.

> **[CORRECTED 2026-11-13] This first read *the brake is not real*, and that is not what was
> measured.** This table compares whole decks at the wound rate the day happens to deal; it never
> asks what a Wound costs **each** deck, and those are two questions. The brake is real and it is
> proportional, which [11](#11-a-bad-card-costs-what-it-takes-up) measures directly: a Wound costs
> the six-card deck &minus;6.2 points on the mixed board against the sixteen's &minus;1.6. What it
> is not is **big enough to matter at this frequency** &mdash; 0.4 wounds against a head start of
> ten points. A hypothesis about a frequency nobody had checked was right about the mechanism and
> wrong about the size.

**A Spent card costs 13 points over a day, and nothing at all in a fight.** One card printed Spent
takes the pruned twelve from **59.7%** to **46.6%** of days cleared, and fights cleared from 8.08 to
7.43. Under the stamina rule a Spent card measures about **zero** in the fight that spends it &mdash;
§5 priced Overloaded's two set-aside cards at nothing, *because a card nobody played costs nothing*.
That was true and it was only true of one fight. **Spent is a price paid in days.**

> **This re-prices every Spent-printed card in the game**, and it is the clearest thing the day
> runner has produced: a cost that reads as free in the unit everything else here is measured in.

## 11. A bad card costs what it takes up

`dilution_sim.py`. The player's question, and a better one than the one 10 answered: *a small deck
will be more damaged by adding bad cards to the deck, right?*

Right. **10 could not have told you**, because it compared whole decks at whatever rate the day
dealt Wounds, and "which deck wins" is not "what does a Wound cost this deck". A deck can be more
brittle per bad card and still win, if it starts far enough ahead and bad cards are rare enough,
and that is exactly what was happening.

So: force **k** dead cards into the starting deck, hold the board fixed, and read the slope.
3 seeds &times; 1500 fights, stamina 14.

**3 spears** &mdash; the control, a fight nobody loses

| deck | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | per Wound | per equal share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16, pristine | 98.4% | 98.0% | 98.2% | 97.4% | &minus;0.3 | &minus;0.3 |
| 12, pruned | 98.1% | 98.0% | 97.8% | 97.4% | &minus;0.2 | &minus;0.2 |
| 6, to the bone | 99.3% | 98.2% | 97.5% | 97.0% | **&minus;0.8** | &minus;0.3 |

**4 spears**

| deck | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | per Wound | per equal share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16, pristine | 89.3% | 88.1% | 86.2% | 84.8% | &minus;1.6 | &minus;1.6 |
| 12, pruned | 87.5% | 86.0% | 85.2% | 83.3% | &minus;1.3 | &minus;1.0 |
| 6, to the bone | 91.0% | 85.2% | 82.3% | 79.5% | **&minus;3.7** | &minus;1.4 |

**2 spears + 1 bow**

| deck | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | per Wound | per equal share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16, pristine | 78.7% | 77.5% | 76.2% | 73.6% | &minus;1.6 | &minus;1.6 |
| 12, pruned | 55.9% | 53.1% | 50.8% | 49.1% | &minus;2.3 | &minus;1.7 |
| 6, to the bone | 89.3% | 82.3% | 76.5% | 70.7% | **&minus;6.2** | &minus;2.3 |

### 11.1 It is the fraction, and almost nothing but the fraction

**A Wound costs the six-card deck two to four times what it costs the sixteen** &mdash; &minus;6.2
against &minus;1.6 on the mixed board, &minus;3.7 against &minus;1.6 on four spears. That is the
answer to the question as asked, and it is not a small effect.

**And the mechanism is the obvious one.** `per equal share` scales each slope by the fraction of
its own deck a Wound occupies, which asks whether anything is happening beyond dilution. The
columns collapse: &minus;0.3 / &minus;0.2 / &minus;0.3, then &minus;1.6 / &minus;1.0 / &minus;1.4,
then &minus;1.6 / &minus;1.7 / &minus;2.3. **A bad card costs what it takes up.** One card in six
hurts about as much as two and a half cards in sixteen, because that is what it is.

> **The one place it is worse than proportional** is the hardest board at the smallest size
> (&minus;2.3 against &minus;1.6), and the reading is that a deck cut to the bone has no slack.
> Every card in it is load-bearing, so a card that cannot be played is not diluting a pile with
> spare answers in it &mdash; it is taking one of six answers away. The effect is small and it
> points the right way.

### 11.2 Which is why section 10 saw nothing

At **0.4 Wounds after eight fights**, a slope of &minus;6.2 is worth about two and a half points.
The six-card deck's head start on the same day is ten. The brake is real, it is proportional, and
it is an order of magnitude too weak to overturn the thing it is braking &mdash; which is why
*the brake is not real* was the wrong sentence and
[10.2](#102-the-intruder-brake-does-not-bite-and-a-spent-card-does) now says so.

### 11.3 What it changes

**An intruder's price is a fraction, not a number.** Nothing in the content prices one that way.
*Hollow Eyes* deals **two** Doubts and *The Eager Hand* two Rages; against a deck cut to six those
are a third of it, and against a pristine sixteen they are an eighth. The same card is worth two or
three times as much depending on a choice the player made at level-up, which is a real interaction
and an unpriced one. **[OPEN]** Whether the injuries that deal cards should scale, cap, or simply
be accepted as sharper against a sharp deck.

**And the floor on the action deck is doing work.** `actionDeck.min` reads like a tidiness rule and
is not: pruning to the bone buys a straight +10 on the mixed day (10.1) and sells the brittleness
measured here. A floor is the only thing standing between a player and a deck where one dead card
is a sixth of everything they have.

**The Spent card compounds with this.** A card printed Spent does not only cost the 13 points of
10.2 &mdash; it makes the deck *smaller* for the rest of the day, which raises what every later
Wound costs. Two effects of the same sign from one card, and the day runner measures their sum
without separating them.

## What this does not test

- ~~**The engine does not implement stamina.**~~ **Built the same day**: `spendStamina` in
  `module/decks/play.mjs` charges every card played through one gate, the deck cycles for free while
  stamina lasts, and the tray shows the clock. The dials remain in the simulator (`cfg.stamina`,
  0 = the old rule, and `cfg.stamina_hard`).
- ~~**Versatility**~~ **answered in §10**, by `day_sim.py`: a day of three fights with one deck,
  pruned once. The price of pruning for the wrong shape is **&minus;65.0**.
- ~~**The intruder brake**~~ **answered in §11**, by `dilution_sim.py`: a bad card costs what it
  takes up, so a six-card deck pays two to four times what a sixteen does for the same Wound. What
  is still untested is the **rate** at anything but the starter's: 0.4 Wounds in eight fights is
  what today's content deals, and a campaign that dealt cards faster would be a different game.
- **A party.** One hero, as every run in this directory has been, the day runner included. Stamina
  pays for reactions and reactions are what a second character changes most.
- **Whether a point is a sensible levelling grant.** §3 prices one point; it does not test a
  character who has bought two against an encounter table written for none.
- **The interval.** §2 says the wall is an interval and nobody has measured a fight long enough for
  the treadmill to decide it. The published encounters all end first.
