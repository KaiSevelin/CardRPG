# Stamina as a stat on the actor — 2026-11-12

**The rule, as ruled.** Stamina is a stat on the actor: **how many cards you may play in a fight**,
unrelated to deck size. Every card you play costs 1 — primary, secondary or reaction, discarded or
Spent. Nothing you did not play costs any. An empty draw pile with stamina left is shuffled back
from the discard, so the deck cycles for free. At 0 the rest of the draw pile is discarded. **Catch
Breath** moves 2 cards back *and* regains 2 stamina.

[analysis-2026-11-01.md](analysis-2026-11-01.md) §2 measured a **different** rule under the same
name: stamina capped cards *drawn*, and only `min(stamina, collection)` came to the fight at all.
`cfg.stamina` has been rewritten rather than given a second dial, so that document's §2 table is no
longer reproducible from this code. Deliberate: one dial for one concept.

All runs: `combat_sim.py`, 3 seeds, **2000 fights** in §1–§3 and **1500** in §4–§6. Encounters are
the four published ones. The hard wall throughout except where §6 says otherwise.

Standard deviation across seeds is 0.1 to 1.8 points.

### Finding 1: the default is 14, and it is not 16

**Stamina 14 reproduces today's difficulty within 3.7 points on every encounter**
(+0.2 / +1.6 / +3.7 / −2.3). Nothing else is close: 13 is 7 points light on the Deadly fight and 16
is 13 points heavy on the Hard one.

> **Fourteen, against a deck of sixteen cards.** The number is not the deck's length, because
> today's hero does not stop at sixteen — a breath tops the pile back up, and the measured hero
> plays about **17** cards in a five-spear fight. Fourteen plus two per breath is where that lands,
> which is why the stat that reproduces a sixteen-card deck is fourteen and not sixteen.

This is the check §2 of 2026-11-01 demanded and failed to get: *if the intent is to keep the current
difficulty while decoupling the deck, the stat starts where the deck is now.* It does not start
where the deck is. It starts two below.

### Finding 2: stamina is the only stat measured here that does not flatten

Marginal value of **2 points**:

| step | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 to 10 | +5.1 | +12.5 | +9.8 | +5.6 |
| 10 to 12 | +2.7 | +10.6 | +13.4 | +6.8 |
| 12 to 14 | +2.0 | +5.7 | +10.2 | +8.3 |
| **14 to 16** | **+0.7** | **+3.9** | **+9.6** | **+8.8** |
| 16 to 18 | +0.1 | +2.1 | +7.0 | +8.5 |
| 18 to 20 | +0.2 | +1.1 | +3.1 | +8.6 |
| 20 to 24 | +0.0 | +0.6 | +3.7 | +11.4 |

> **On the Deadly encounter it accelerates.** 5.6, 6.8, 8.3, 8.8, 8.5, 8.6, 11.4 — a stat that is
> worth *more* per point at 24 than at 10. Hand size halves every step
> ([analysis-2026-11-11.md](analysis-2026-11-11.md) §1) and Health is close to linear. This one
> climbs, because the hardest fight is the longest one and the longest fight is the one a budget
> decides.
>
> **So there is no safe upper end to this stat.** Every other dial in this project could be left
> uncapped on the grounds that it runs out of road. This one does not have any.

## 2. Wall or interval: your rule makes the game breathe less

Breaths and Exhausted turns per fight:

| arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **old rule** | 0.98b 0.03e | 2.01b 0.09e | 2.87b 0.18e | 2.14b 0.17e |
| **stamina 14** | **0.72b 0.01e** | **1.50b 0.05e** | **2.20b 0.10e** | **1.53b 0.07e** |
| stamina 20 | 0.08b 0.00e | 0.32b 0.01e | 0.81b 0.02e | 0.73b 0.02e |
| stamina 24 | 0.01b 0.00e | 0.08b 0.00e | 0.29b 0.00e | 0.33b 0.01e |

> **At the calibrated default the hero breathes a quarter less than it does today**, and is
> Exhausted about half as often, while winning the same fights. The free cycle gives back the turns
> that breathing was eating: you no longer spend a turn shuffling cards you were going to see
> anyway.
>
> And **Catch Breath nearly disappears at the top of the range** — 0.01 to 0.33 breaths a fight at
> stamina 24. [../rules/design-direction.md](../rules/design-direction.md) calls the breath *"the
> biggest single lever found so far"* at 22 points of win rate in the hardest fight. Under this
> rule that lever is softened at the default and effectively gone for a high-stamina character,
> which is a design decision this table makes rather than reports.

The wall is an **interval**, as 2026-11-01's open question predicted: a breath costs no stamina and
grants 2, so past the wall a hero alternates one empty turn with one full one for ever. Nothing in
the ruling caps how many times it may be done.

## 3. What a point of stamina is worth, in the two currencies already measured

Per point, read across stamina 12 to 16:

| | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **a point of stamina** | **+0.7** | **+2.4** | **+5.0** | **+4.3** |
| a point of Health | +0.25 | +1.17 | +2.63 | +3.17 |
| a card of hand size | +0.6 | +3.6 | +6.2 | +6.9 |

> **One point of stamina is about two points of Health, and about three quarters of a card of hand
> size.** The ratios against Health are 2.7 / 2.1 / 1.9 / 1.3 and against the hand card
> 1.1 / 0.67 / 0.80 / 0.62.
>
> Health is weight **2** on [../rules/leveling.md](../rules/leveling.md) §5b's rung table. A single
> point of stamina is therefore a weight-4 reward on a table whose top rung is 3 — which puts it
> alongside the hand card rather than alongside Health, and means **it can never be granted two at
> a time**. A career should move this stat by about two points in total.

## 4. Deck size inverts, and it is monotonic

| arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **old rule, 16 cards** | 98.2% | 87.0% | 61.2% | 28.9% |
| old rule, 12 pruned well | −8.6 | −24.7 | −26.3 | −3.5 |
| old rule, 20 cards | +1.0 | +6.6 | +14.7 | −1.0 |
| **stamina 14, 16 cards** | 98.6% | 88.8% | 65.0% | 26.6% |
| stamina 14, **12 pruned well** | −0.2 | −1.6 | −2.4 | **+20.0** |
| stamina 14, 12 pruned at random | −0.1 | +0.0 | +0.3 | +1.8 |
| stamina 14, 20 cards | −1.1 | −2.7 | −7.7 | **−12.4** |
| stamina 14, 24 cards | −1.6 | −6.8 | −12.3 | **−18.3** |

Bigger decks are built the way [../rules/leveling.md](../rules/leveling.md) §4 built them: the extra
slots filled with the deck's best card.

### Finding 3: pruning well is the largest single gain measured in this directory

**+20.0 points on the Deadly encounter**, where the same cut under the old rule was −3.5 and on the
Hard encounter was −26.3. *More cards means more stamina before exhaustion, and your best cards come
up less often* ([../rules/core-rules.md](../rules/core-rules.md) §8) was a trade; the first half of
it is now false, and what is left is one-directional.

> **Why it lands only on the Deadly fight.** Pruning is worth nothing on the three short encounters
> and everything on the long one, because **a short deck only pays once you cycle it.** A hero
> plays about 16 cards in the mixed fight: a 12-card deck comes round and hands back its best
> cards, a 16-card deck does not come round at all.

### Finding 4: and the cap is not a reward any more, it is a drawback

**Twenty cards costs −7.7 and −12.4 on the two hard encounters**, against +6.6 and +14.7 under the
old rule. `deckMax` is granted in two places — *The March* ([nodes.mjs](../foundry/cardrpg/module/content/nodes.mjs)
`carry: 5, deckMax: 2`) and an item ([gear.mjs](../foundry/cardrpg/module/content/gear.mjs)) — and
in both it now takes something away.

> **The extra slots were filled with the deck's *best* card and it still lost twelve points.** The
> mechanism is not dilution, it is coverage: with the budget fixed you see a **smaller fraction** of
> a bigger deck, so the Shield Up or the Second Wind you needed is likelier to be one of the cards
> you never drew. A bigger deck is not less versatile in principle — it is less versatile *in your
> hand*, which is the only place versatility exists.

### Finding 5: the trade-off has no teeth, and this is the problem the rule creates

**Cutting four cards at random costs nothing: −0.1 / +0.0 / +0.3 / +1.8.**

> This is the brake coming off. [../rules/design-direction.md](../rules/design-direction.md) keeps
> the starter deck at sixteen for one stated reason — *"not because small decks are weak… because
> pruning badly collapses it (45%), and a new character can't know what they'll face"*. Under this
> rule pruning badly does not collapse anything. It is still **better than not pruning**.
>
> So deck size is now a **monotonic penalty**: 12 beats 16 beats 20 beats 24, however well or
> badly the cut is made. Every character will play at exactly the deck minimum, and the minimum is
> the only rule left holding the deck together — a number that today is justified by a measurement
> this table has just overturned.
>
> **The versatility the trade-off needs is real but it is not in this table**, and it cannot be: a
> simulator that fights one encounter at a time cannot price a deck that has to answer the next
> fight too. *Pruned at random* is this file's only proxy for *pruned wrong*, and it is free. If the
> trade is to have teeth, something has to give them to it.

## 5. Overloaded has evaporated

| arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| old rule, Overloaded | −2.3 | −6.5 | −11.2 | −8.5 |
| **stamina 14, Overloaded** | **−0.1** | **−0.2** | **+0.8** | **−0.5** |

[../rules/core-rules.md](../rules/core-rules.md) §8 says Overloaded *"costs you stamina, which in
this game means deck"* — two cards Spent before the fight starts.

> **Those two cards were never played, so under this rule they cost nothing**, and the measurement
> is zero on all four encounters. The sentence in §8 is now false in both halves: stamina does not
> mean deck, and Overloaded does not cost stamina. **[OPEN]** A drawback that costs 2 stamina
> directly would restore it to roughly its measured price, and would be the plainer reading of
> *costs you stamina* anyway.

The same arithmetic applies to every card that leaves the deck without being played. **A Wound is
free now** — it clogs a hand slot for a turn and costs no budget. So are Taint, Fear and the
cards an injury carries.

## 6. The two readings of the wall, priced

*"When spent cards equal your stamina the rest of the deck is discarded"* can mean the deck stops or
the player stops. The cards in your hand are not the deck, so under the literal reading you play
them out.

| | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| soft wall, against the hard one | +0.4 | +2.4 | +5.7 | **+9.5** |

> **The soft wall is worth up to 9.5 points**, so this is a real ruling and not a detail. It is also
> the reading under which **a bigger hand buys a second helping of stamina** — the overrun is a
> hand's worth of cards — which would compound with the hand-size node priced on 2026-11-11 and
> make that stat dearer again. The hard wall is recommended on both counts.

## What this does not test

- **The engine does not implement stamina.** Simulator only, behind `cfg.stamina` (0 = the old
  rule) and `cfg.stamina_hard`.
- **Versatility**, which is §4's whole open question. One encounter at a time cannot price a deck
  that has to answer an unknown fight.
- **A party.** One hero, as every run in this directory has been. Stamina pays for reactions and
  reactions are what a second character changes most.
- **Whether a point is a sensible levelling grant.** §3 prices one point; it does not test a
  character who has bought two against an encounter table written for none.
- **The interval.** §2 says the wall is an interval and nobody has measured a fight long enough for
  the treadmill to decide it. The published encounters all end first.
