# Conditions, combat and dispel — 2026-10-30

First simulation of anything in [../rules/conditions.md](../rules/conditions.md). Everything in that
document and in the condition work of the last several passes was reasoned from the value bands and
**none of it had been measured**, which this fixes for the parts a simulator can reach.

Run with `python conditions_sim.py`. The condition engine itself lives in `combat_sim.py`, which
previously knew only about Dazed, hardcoded; it now carries a `CONDITIONS` table mirroring
`module/config.mjs` and the five removal routes.

**4000 fights per scenario, one seed.** At a win rate near 47% the standard error is **0.8 points**,
so differences under about **1.6 points are noise** and are called noise below.

**The refactor is behaviour-neutral**, checked against the committed version on the baseline
scenario: 98.8% before, 98.9% after, which is inside the 0.2-point error at that win rate.

> **A discrepancy that predates this work:** [starter-cards.md](../rules/starter-cards.md) and
> [analysis-2026-10-27.md](analysis-2026-10-27.md) both print **99.4%** for the 3-spear baseline, and
> the current code gives **98.8%** at the same 3000 fights — before and after this change alike, so
> it is not the refactor. Something else moved between 27 October and now and the older number was
> never re-run. Not chased here; flagged because the 99.4% is quoted in the rules as a tuning
> yardstick.

## 1. The rules against themselves — 26 assertions, all pass

No combat; these exercise the condition rules directly and either hold or the rules are not what they
say.

| What | Result |
|---|---|
| **The clock** — a condition ticks at the start of its **creator's** turn, not anyone else's | ✔ |
| A **(2)** survives one tick and is gone after two | ✔ |
| A creator who leaves combat hands the clock to the victim | ✔ |
| **Wet onto Burning** — neither lands, the Burning goes out | ✔ |
| **Burning onto Wet** — neither lands, the Wet steams off | ✔ |
| **Refresh** takes the longer duration *and* the fiercer value, independently | ✔ |
| **Poisoned 0** costs the secondary and does no damage | ✔ |
| **Move**: halved by Slowed, then reduced by Frozen, floored at 0 | ✔ |
| **Shake Off** spends duration and ends at 0 | ✔ |
| **Shake Off cannot touch an opening**, and cannot touch Cursed | ✔ |
| **Lift** removes Cursed, and cannot touch a timed condition | ✔ |
| A **no-duration** condition survives ten ticks | ✔ |
| Every modelled condition has exactly one removal route | ✔ |

The two cancel assertions are the ones worth having: *water and fire annihilate* is the only
interaction in the whole system, and it is the one place the rules could have been asymmetric by
accident. They are not.

## 2. What Dazed is worth — 5.8 points, about two thirds of a damage rung

> **[SUPERSEDED by §9]** Every figure in this section is a **single seed**. The five-seed means are in §9, and three of the numbers below moved by more than the noise they were inside.

Four goblins, two spears and two bows — the **Deadly** encounter, so there is room to move and the
numbers are not floored. *Dirty Trick* is the vehicle: it applies whatever the scenario says instead
of Dazed, so every row differs by one thing.

| Dirty Trick applies | Win rate | Costs the hero |
|---|---|---|
| nothing (it is just a Strike) | **47.0%** | — |
| **Dazed (1)** | 41.2% | **5.8** |
| **Burning 1 (1)** | 38.3% | 8.7 |
| **Burning 2 (1)** | 29.6% | 17.4 |
| **Burning 3 (1)** | 24.9% | 22.5 |

**A damage rung is worth about 8.7 points** and the ladder is close to linear up to 2, then flattens —
by Burning 3 the hero is dying to the burn before the fight can turn, so more intensity buys less.

**Dazed is 0.67 of a rung.** That is the number every claim about Poisoned rests on.

## 3. Poisoned N is Burning N plus **two thirds** of a rung, not a whole one

> **[SUPERSEDED by §9]** Every figure in this section is a **single seed**. The five-seed means are in §9, and three of the numbers below moved by more than the noise they were inside.

> **[CORRECTION]** [conditions.md](../rules/conditions.md) §2.1 asserted *"Poisoned costs one rung
> more than Burning at the same number"* and priced the ladder accordingly: Poisoned 1 at strength 2,
> Poisoned 2 at strength 3. **That overprices it by about half a rung.**

| | Win rate | Against the Burning of the same number |
|---|---|---|
| **Poisoned 0 (1)** | 41.2% | — (Burning has no 0) |
| **Burning 1 (1)** | 38.3% | |
| **Poisoned 1 (1)** | 34.7% | **+3.6** worse for the hero |
| **Burning 2 (1)** | 29.6% | |
| **Poisoned 2 (1)** | 27.5% | **+2.1** worse for the hero |

So Poisoned N sits **between** Burning N and Burning N+1, nearer the former: +3.6 against a rung of
8.7 is **0.4 of a rung** at N=1, and +2.1 is **0.24** at N=2.

**And the gap shrinks as N rises**, which makes sense once it is measured: the Dazed is worth less
when you are dying faster, because losing a secondary matters most in a fight you could still win.

**The corrected band**, with the shift at the bottom where it is largest:

| Poisoned | Costs about | Belongs to |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | 0.67 of a rung — **less than a Burning 1** | a card, or strength 1 |
| **1** | a Burning 1 and a bit | strength **2** |
| **2** | a Burning 2 and a bit | strength **3** |
| **3+** | monsters only | |

Which is, awkwardly, nearly the table that was already written — the *rungs* were right and the
*reason* was wrong. Poisoned 1 at strength 2 is correct not because it equals a Burning 2 but because
it exceeds a Burning 1, and a strength-2 formula is what a Burning-1-and-a-bit costs. The claim to
retire is **"one rung more"**; the right phrasing is **"two thirds of a rung more, and less than that
as the number rises."**

## 4. A dispel on an action card is not worth its slot

*Shrug It Off* — a Strike primary with **Shake Off 1** on its secondary — bought by cutting one copy
of *Strike*, so the deck stays at sixteen.

| | Win rate | Delta | Shake played |
|---|---|---|---|
| **vs nothing**, plain | 47.0% | | |
| **vs nothing**, *Shrug It Off* | 44.9% | **−2.1** | 0.00/fight |
| **vs Dazed (2)**, plain | 37.0% | | |
| **vs Dazed (2)**, *Shrug It Off* | 35.9% | −1.1 *(noise)* | **0.00**/fight |
| **vs Burning 2 (2)**, plain | 20.7% | | |
| **vs Burning 2 (2)**, *Shrug It Off* | 20.1% | −0.6 *(noise)* | 0.22/fight |
| **vs Poisoned 2 (2)**, plain | 13.8% | | |
| **vs Poisoned 2 (2)**, *Shrug It Off* | 14.2% | +0.4 *(noise)* | **0.00**/fight |

**It is never played.** Two reasons, and both are general:

**A card is in your hand 31% of turns.** One copy in a sixteen-card deck reaches a five-card hand
`1 − C(15,5)/C(16,5)` of the time, and it has to be there on a turn the condition is *also* on you.
Against Burning 2 (2) — the only row where the dispel can legally fire — that coincidence plus the
competition from an ordinary secondary comes to **0.22 plays a fight**, which cannot repay a card.

**And against the conditions that cost you a secondary, it cannot fire at all.** Dazed and Poisoned
both block the secondary, so a Shake Off *on* a secondary is unreachable exactly when it is wanted:
**0.00 plays**. The simulator rediscovered core-rules §7's own rule — *a dispel must not sit on the
half the condition takes* — from the other end, without being told it.

> **[CORRECTION]** [way-cards.md](../rules/way-cards.md) §1.6 priced a Shake Off 1 at *"a secondary —
> free, and the only rung a secondary may buy"*. The half is indeed cheap; **the card is not**, and
> the band ignored it. A dispel wants a surface that is there when the condition is:
>
> | Surface | Available | Costs |
> |---|---|---|
> | an action card's secondary | 31% of turns | a card **and** a half — measured at −2.1 for the slot |
> | a **reaction** line | 31% of turns | the card only, no half |
> | a **prepared spell** | **always** | a slot, not a draw |
> | a **feature** | **always** | a node |
>
> The content already got this right where it matters: Ward's *Shake It Off* is a **reaction spell**,
> so it is prepared rather than drawn and costs no half. It is the band table that overreached.

## 5. What this does not test

- **Party play.** Every number here is one hero, and the two things conditions are *for* — openings
  read by somebody else's card, and a priest lifting an ally's curse — need a party the simulator
  does not have. This is the same limit [analysis-2026-10-27.md](analysis-2026-10-27.md) names.
- **Corrode.** Armor loss needs an equipment model; the sim has a flat `armor` number.
- **Tainting.** Taint is cards in a deck across fights, and the sim runs one fight at a time.
- **The openings themselves.** A combo is two cards in two hands; see party play.
- **Frozen in anger.** The Move arithmetic is asserted in §1, but what *losing* Move is worth
  depends on a map the sim treats as open ground.

## 6. Positioning — the most valuable thing in a starter deck

> **[RE-VERIFIED]** The headline 29.5 reproduces exactly as a five-seed mean (§9); the 4-bows
> row is 16.3 rather than 15.1. Big effects were the safe ones.

Added after the condition work, when the question turned to movement. **core-rules §11 is thirty
lines**, and it governs this.

`positioning_sim.py`. The experiment is blunt: replace **Advance** — the only card in the sixteen
whose primary is pure movement — with a plain attacker. Deck size, card count and Ready count all
stay fixed; only mobility changes.

### Mobility's worth is set by the enemy's reach, not by the hero

| Encounter | With Advance | Without | Mobility is worth |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 spears (all melee) | 98.2% | 97.7% | **0.5** — noise |
| 5 spears (all melee) | 64.0% | 60.5% | **3.5** |
| 4 goblins, 2 spears + 2 bows | 41.2% | 11.8% | **29.5** |
| 4 bows (all ranged) | 17.6% | 2.5% | 15.1 |

**29.5 points is the largest effect anything in this project has measured** — against a damage rung at
8.7, Dazed at 5.8, and a dispel card at −2.1. Mobility is worth **three and a half damage rungs** in
the fight where it counts, and **nothing at all** against pure melee, where the fight comes to you.

And the other direction agrees: swapping a third copy of *Strike* for a third *Advance* is worth
**+11.9 points**. The starter deck is under-equipped for movement, not over.

> **This explains a result from three days earlier.** [analysis-2026-10-27.md](analysis-2026-10-27.md)
> found that *"a mixed group is a tier harder than a uniform one of the same size"* and described the
> mechanism as spears pinning while bows shoot. The real mechanism is narrower: **a mixed group is
> harder because it punishes immobility.** The spears are not there to deal damage — they are there
> to stop you reaching the bows. Four bows alone are *less* dangerous than two bows and two spears.

### It is not a panic button — it is played every turn

| Card | Primary | Secondary | Copies |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Advance** | 1.04 | 1.16 | 2 |
| **Heavy Swing** | 1.06 | 1.18 | 2 |
| Strike | 0.70 | 0.91 | **3** |
| Lunge | 1.01 | 0.11 | 1 |
| Shield Up | 0.54 | 0.02 | 2 |

*Advance* ties *Heavy Swing* for most-played, both off two copies, and both beat **three** copies of
*Strike* on either half. Mobility is not something the hero reaches for when cornered; it is something
they spend half of nearly every turn on.

### What follows for the rules

**Do not add a flanking bonus.** Positioning already swings 29 points where it matters; a bonus for
standing correctly would make it the only axis worth playing. Note that the two positional rules that
*do* exist — **Pack Tactics** and **Shield Wall** — are both **NPC tactics** (§9), so the monsters
have positional rewards and the players have none. After these numbers that looks like the right way
round rather than an oversight.

**Define the ground instead.** Terrain adds decisions without adding power, and three kinds of square
were being used by spells before any was defined: **difficult ground** (four formulas),
**shadowed** (a whole school, six spells) and **high ground** (a resource a specialization pays in).
All three are now in core-rules §11, and high ground deliberately *removes a penalty* rather than
granting a bonus.

**Encounter composition is the positioning dial.** The rules already half-say this — *"build hard
encounters by mixing ranges, not by adding bodies"* — and these numbers say why: range mixing is how a
GM decides whether movement matters in a given fight.

### Still not measured

- **Move is never resolved by the engine.** Figures are pushed by hand, so the most valuable action in
  the game has no implementation beyond the number printed on the card face. That is now the largest
  single engine gap.
- **Terrain.** The simulator's map is open ground with cover from figures only. Difficult ground, high
  ground and shadow are all unmeasured, and shadow in particular carries a school.
- **Party positioning**, which is where Blast against Aimed lives, and the reason §11 says not to make
  Aimed common.

## 7. Shove, trip and grapple — control priced against damage

> **[SUPERSEDED by §9]** Every figure in this section is a **single seed**. The five-seed means are in §9, and three of the numbers below moved by more than the noise they were inside.

core-rules §4 now makes all three the same thing: **a Strike that spends its damage.** So the
question is only whether the condition is worth more than the damage forgone. Measured from the
receiving end, because the simulator has conditions on the hero and not on the goblins — *Dirty
Trick* applies each one instead of Dazed.

| Condition | Costs its victim | Against a damage rung (8.7) |
|---|---|---|
| **Off-Balance (1)** | **0.0** | nothing at all |
| **Dazed (1)** | 5.8 | 0.67 |
| **Prone (1)** | 8.5 | **0.98** |
| **Grappled (1)** | 10.0 | **1.15** |
| **Grappled (2)** | 18.3 | 2.1 |
| *Burning 1 (1)* | *8.7* | *1.00, the reference* |

**Off-Balance costs nothing, and that is the opening design holding under measurement.** It was
written to do nothing at all until a card reads it (core-rules §7), and it measures at exactly 0.0.
Nothing else on this list would.

**A Trip roughly breaks even and a Grapple wins.** Prone costs about one rung, so spending a Strike's
damage on it is close to a wash; Grappled costs *more* than a rung, so the Grapple beats the Strike —
because it blocks 👣 Move, and §6 above measured Move as the most valuable thing a figure does.

> **Position-denial is worth exactly what the target's position was worth.** These numbers come from
> a mixed group, where mobility is worth 29.5 points. Against a brute that wanted to stand next to
> you anyway, a Grapple is worth nothing and the Strike was better. **Grapple the archer; hit the
> ogre** — and that is the decision the three exist to create, which is why they are options on a
> Strike rather than cards of their own.

**One card needed flagging.** *Overbear* (way-cards §3.2) applies **Grappled (2)** off a single
primary — 18.3 points, two damage rungs. The Normal 5 save is the whole thing holding it in band, and
against Off-Balance it rises to Hard 7, which is a two-card play earning a two-rung effect. That is
the strongest line in the minor-node pools and it should be the first card simulated when the sim can
put conditions on the enemy.

### What this does not test

**The direction is backwards from the interesting one.** These are prices for conditions landing on a
*player*, measured because that is where the simulator keeps conditions. A player shoving a *goblin*
is not symmetric: a goblin has 5 Health and dies to two Strikes, so denying its movement for a round
is worth less than denying the hero's. Putting conditions on the enemy side is the next thing the
simulator needs, and it is the same gap that blocks the 48 tactics (§6) and the party combos.

## 8. Items — the economy nobody had tested, and a 23-point error it uncovered

### Ready was inert in every number this project has published

Four of the Warrior's sixteen cards carry ⇄ **Ready** — two *Draw Steel*, *Quick Hands*, *Weapon
Swap* — and the simulator's comment said it plainly: *"ready / taunt: no effect in this test
(everything is equipped, solo hero)."* There was one weapon to hold, so **a quarter of the deck did
nothing in every run ever made**, and the whole item economy — slots, the **back**, swapping
mid-fight — had never been measured at all. [starter-cards.md](../rules/starter-cards.md) admitted as
much in a closing note.

So the simulator now carries two weapon profiles, a `sling` for the back slot, hero ranged attacks
with the ranged-in-melee penalty, and a Ready that swaps.

| Encounter | No sling | Shortbow slung | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 goblins with spears | 99.0% | 98.6% | −0.4 |
| 5 goblins with spears | 64.6% | 57.7% | **−6.9** |
| 4 goblins, 2 spears + 2 bows | 41.5% | 56.7% | **+15.2** |
| 4 bows (all ranged) | 17.7% | 36.5% | +18.8 |

**The kit you bring is a real decision, and that is the finding.** A slung bow is worth about two
damage rungs against a mixed group and **costs** nearly one against five spears, because against
melee you spend halves swapping to a weapon you did not need. Part of that −6.9 is the simulated
player swapping when it should not — which is itself an honest cost of carrying options, though it
means the figure is a floor rather than a measurement of perfect play.

**This is what justifies the back slot.** core-rules §4 says of it: *"what you sling before the fight
is a free swap waiting for you in the middle of one."* That was an assertion until now.

### And it uncovered a stale table in the rules

Checking the new numbers against the published encounter table showed the **Deadly** encounter at
**41.5%** where [starter-cards.md](../rules/starter-cards.md) printed **64.6%** — a 23-point gap, and
not seed variance (41.4, 42.2, 40.6, 41.4 across four seeds).

Bisected across the sim's history:

| Commit | Deadly encounter |
|---|---|
| `58d7ba6` harsher Catch Breath, encumbrance | 59.7% |
| `997dcca` fix the card valuation | **64.6%** ← the number the rules print |
| `c419043` *What a fight costs you is what you chose to spend* | **41.6%** |
| today | 41.5% |

**The flat-2 Catch Breath change of 29 October made every encounter harder and the table was never
re-run.** It was not the condition or item work; `c419043` did it, and the number has been wrong in
the rules for a day. Every row is corrected now, and
[analysis-2026-10-27.md](analysis-2026-10-27.md) carries a superseded notice.

> **The lesson is about the yardstick, not the number.** *Deadly 64.6%* is quoted in the rules as a
> tuning reference, so everything pitched against it was pitched against a hero 23 points stronger
> than the one the rules describe. A simulated figure printed in a rules document needs re-running
> whenever the thing under it moves, and nothing was watching for that.

### State of items otherwise

- **The schema is complete** — slot, fits, hands, tags, weight, value, four verb lines, armor,
  corrosion, consumption, bonus groups, drain and recharge. Nothing is missing from the model.
- **Nine player items, two pieces of NPC gear.** Eight **tools** are documented in
  [starter-cards.md](../rules/starter-cards.md) and exist in **no part of the system** — which matters
  because they carry the only examples of the Tool / Station / Power **bonus groups**, so core-rules
  §5's bonus-group rule has no item exercising it anywhere.
- **Eight items the written rules already asked for** are now specified, each one named somewhere
  before it existed: **Heavy Armor** (core-rules §10's own blocked-icon example), an **Arquebus** (the
  Musketeer *begins loaded*), an **Antidote**, **Flask of Ink**, **Flask of Oil**, a **Repair Kit**
  (for Corrode), a **Cleansing Draught** (for Taint) and **The Kit** (which was a Signature and not an
  item).

## 9. [CORRECTION] Everything above was a single seed, and some of it was noise

Found while aligning this file's seed with the encounter table's. **Every figure in §2, §3, §6 and
§7 was one run of 4000 fights at one seed**, and at a win rate near 45% that carries a standard
deviation of about **0.8 points** — so **±1.6 at two sigma**, and I had been reporting differences
inside that band as findings.

Re-measured at **4000 fights × 5 seeds**, reporting the mean:

| | Published | **Five-seed mean** | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A damage rung** (Burning 1) | 8.7 | **7.4** | the reference everything else is quoted against |
| Off-Balance (1) | 0.0 | **0.0** | structural, not statistical — it holds exactly |
| Dazed (1) | 5.8 | **4.0** | |
| Prone (1) | 8.5 | **6.0** | |
| Grappled (1) | 10.0 | **9.1** | |
| Grappled (2) | 18.3 | **18.3** | holds |
| Mobility, mixed group | 29.5 | **29.5** | holds exactly — a big effect is a safe one |
| Mobility, 4 bows | 15.1 | **16.3** | |
| Slung bow, mixed group | +15.2 | **+16.2** | |
| Slung bow, 5 spears | −6.9 | **−5.4** | |

**What survives.** The big effects are unmoved: mobility at 29.5 is exactly reproducible, Grappled (2)
at 18.3 likewise, and **Off-Balance at 0.0** is not a measurement at all but a structural fact — an
opening does nothing, so there is nothing for variance to act on.

**What changes.**

- **A damage rung is 7.4, not 8.7.** Every ratio quoted against it was therefore about 15% too
  generous to damage.
- **Dazed is 0.54 of a rung, not two thirds.** About half.
- **Prone costs 6.0 against a rung of 7.4** — **0.81**. So a Trip does *not* break even against
  Striking as §7 claimed; it loses about a fifth of a rung. **Grappled at 9.1 still beats it**, at
  1.23 rungs, and that claim comes out stronger.
- **And one claim was a story told about noise.** §3 reported that the Poisoned-minus-Burning gap
  *shrinks* as the number rises (3.6 at N=1, 2.1 at N=2) and explained it — *"losing a secondary
  matters less in a fight you are losing faster."* Across five seeds the gap is **3.9 and 4.4**: it
  does not shrink, and the explanation was invented for a difference that was not there.

  The better answer is simpler. **Poisoned N costs about 4 points more than Burning N, flat** — which
  is just the price of Dazed, the same at every value, because Poisoned N *is* Burning N plus Dazed.
  My first answer was "one rung more", my second was "two thirds, shrinking", and the measurement is
  **half a rung, flat**.

**The structural fix**, so this does not recur: both simulation scripts now carry a `SEEDS` tuple and
a `rate()` helper that returns the mean and the spread across seeds. A figure that cannot be resolved
now looks like one.

## 10. [RULE] Sidestep was a word on a card, and one sentence has two readings

`rules/equipment.md` §12 carried an `[OPEN]` saying nothing on the equipment page was simulated, and
named the armour ladder first. Pricing the ladder needed a rule implemented before it could start,
and that rule was missing in a way nothing had noticed.

> **Heavy Armor's drawback is the loss of ⚡ Sidestep reactions** (core-rules §10). **Sidestep had no
> behaviour anywhere in the simulator.** It was a name printed on *Advance* — two copies of sixteen
> cards — and `take_hit` quietly treated it as a generic Brace worth 1.

So the top two rungs of the armour ladder could not be charged for, because their price was a
reaction that never fired. It is implemented now as core-rules §3 and §11 write it: it **neither
redirects nor reduces**, so you leave the square the Strike was aimed at and the hit finds nobody,
and it resolves at its own trigger rather than inside the one-reaction-per-trigger order.

### 10.1 The first implementation was wrong, and wrong in the generous direction

Worth recording, because the error is the kind that reads as a finding. My first version fired
whenever an enemy *finished a move adjacent* — including when it had not moved at all. A goblin
already in your face playing *Stab & Scurry* breaks out of its move loop without taking a step, and
the hero was answering that: **a free reposition nearly every turn**, which measured as +5.7 points
against five spears. The corrected trigger requires a step *and* a square that was not adjacent
before.

### 10.2 Which leaves a real ambiguity, and it is worth about two points

Core-rules §11 says *"an enemy that **ends its movement next to you** hands you a Sidestep or a Set
Spear"*, under the heading *"leaving is free, **arriving** is answered"*. Those are two different
rules, and the same wording governs *Set Spear* ([way-cards.md](../rules/way-cards.md) §2):

- **arrival** — it took a step *and* was not adjacent before. What "arriving" plainly means.
- **any** — it ended a move adjacent, shuffling in place included. What the sentence literally says.

**4000 fights × 5 seeds a row.** `sidestep=False` reproduces the previously published table exactly,
which is how the rest of the extension is known to be default-neutral.

| Encounter | Inert (as published) | **arrival** | any | arrival's gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 spears | 98.9 ±0.1 | 98.9 ±0.2 | 99.2 ±0.1 | +0.0 — *noise* |
| 4 spears | 89.1 ±0.3 | **90.6 ±0.3** | 91.6 ±0.2 | **+1.5** |
| 5 spears | 63.9 ±1.1 | **67.5 ±0.6** | 69.5 ±0.5 | **+3.6** |
| 2 spears + 2 bows | 41.5 ±0.7 | 40.6 ±0.7 | 39.8 ±0.9 | −0.9 — *noise* |

**The looser reading is worth 1.0 to 2.0 points**, and it buys them by letting the hero step away from
an enemy that is merely standing there — 0.77 sidesteps a fight against 0.45. That is a free
disengage rather than an answer to an approach, so **the rules should say `arrival`**, and §11 should
say it in words rather than leaving it to be inferred from a heading.

### 10.3 What the card is actually worth

**+3.6 points against five spears, +1.5 against four, and nothing measurable against archers.** Small,
and the shape is the interesting part, because §6 measured *mobility* as worth **29.5 points against a
mixed group and nothing at all against pure melee** — and this is the same card:

> **Advance's two halves are valuable in opposite fights.** Its primary (Move 3) is worth 29.5 points
> against archers and nothing against spears; its reaction is worth 3.6 against spears and nothing
> against archers. One card, never dead, and never good at both ends at once.

That is the positional grammar working as core-rules §11 claims, and it is the first time the
**answer** half has been measured at all. Only **0.45 sidesteps a fight** against five spears, which
is the hand economy doing its job: two copies of *Advance*, and spending one defensively spends the
card you wanted for closing.

## 11. The armour ladder stops being content at rung 3

`equipment.md` §1.2 runs armour 1 to 4 and calls the ladder "four rungs and no more". Against every
published encounter, **1200 fights × 5 seeds**:

| | armor 0 | armor 1 | armor 2 | armor 3 | armor 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 spears | 95.0 ±0.7 | 98.7 ±0.4 | 100.0 ±0.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 |
| 4 spears | 72.2 ±1.9 | 90.1 ±0.5 | 98.3 ±0.1 | 100.0 ±0.1 | 100.0 |
| 5 spears | 40.1 ±1.3 | 67.2 ±1.5 | 90.7 ±0.5 | 99.5 ±0.2 | 100.0 |
| 2 spears + 2 bows | 9.8 ±1.1 | 40.4 ±1.5 | 88.9 ±1.0 | 99.9 ±0.1 | 100.0 |

**Two findings, and the second is a content problem rather than a balance one.**

**1. Armour is the strongest single axis this project has measured.** One point — Leather to Mail,
nine kilograms — is worth **+48.5 points** against the mixed group, against mobility's 29.5 and a
damage rung's 7.4 (§9). Nothing else moves a win rate that far.

**2. Rungs 3 and 4 buy nothing that exists.** Every published encounter is at or above **99.5% by
Brigandine**, so armor 3 is where goblins stop being a fight and Plate is indistinguishable from it.
The arithmetic says why, and it states the cliff precisely:

> **The cliff is where armour equals the enemy's Strike.** A goblin strikes for 3. At armor 3 the only
> thing that lands is a **+2** or a **Critical** (which doubles: 6 − 3 = 3) — two cards in twenty.

So **Brigandine and Plate are not upgrades, they are a different enemy roster**, priced for monsters
the game has not written. That is a fair thing for a catalogue to do, but §1.2 should say so, because
a GM reading the ladder cannot tell that half of it has no opponent. The corollary
`analysis-2026-10-26.md` found at armor 2 has not weakened: *keep armour scarce, and when heroes
reach it, retire the trash mob rather than fielding more of it.*

## 12. The cliff edge: confirmed in damage prevented, confounded in win rate

§1.2's mechanism claim is that armour "is subtracted from *every* hit, so a point of it is worth far
more against four goblins striking for 3 than against one ogre striking for 8." Nothing had run the
ogre. These three groups hold the damage at **12 a round** and the Health at **20**, and vary only how
many bodies it arrives in — which is the claim itself rather than a monster invented to suit it.

| | armor 0 | armor 1 | armor 2 | armor 3 | armor 4 | 0 → 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 × 3 damage | 72.2 ±1.9 | 90.1 ±0.5 | 98.3 ±0.1 | 100.0 | 100.0 | +27.8 |
| 2 × 6 damage | 46.5 ±1.4 | 63.5 ±1.2 | 77.8 ±1.3 | 89.2 ±1.2 | 96.6 ±0.3 | **+50.1** |
| 1 × 12 damage | 13.8 ±1.1 | 16.4 ±0.2 | 22.4 ±0.9 | 30.4 ±0.7 | 41.3 ±1.3 | +27.5 |

**The mechanism is confirmed and the framing is wrong.** Damage prevented per fight at armor 4 is
**16.5 against four small hits and 9.7 against one big one** — seventy per cent more, exactly as the
per-hit argument predicts. But the **win rate** gain peaks in the *middle* row, and for a dull reason:
the four-goblin fight starts at 72% and cannot gain more than 28 points, because it was already
nearly won. A ceiling is not a finding, and the two outer rows gaining the same amount (+27.8 and
+27.5) is a coincidence of where they start, not a result.

**What is unambiguous is the thing §1.2 does not say.** Against one enemy striking for 12, Plate takes
the hero from 13.8% to **41.3%** — it helps a great deal, and it never approaches safety:

> **Armour is attrition insurance, and a big hit is not attrition.** Four off a 12 is still 8, and a
> 12-Health hero dies in two. So the honest claim is not that armour is worth *less* against a big
> hitter — it is worth 27.5 points there — but that **no amount of it makes that fight safe**, where
> against small hits rung 3 makes the fight stop existing.

## 13. What Plate costs: the two prices, and the most expensive state in the game

`equipment.md` §1.2 charges Plate twice — twenty of a forty-kilogram allowance, and Heavy Armor's
Sidestep block. Both are measurable now, and **one of them is free**.

**The Sidestep block costs nothing.** Against the mixed group, Plate wins 100.0% ±0.0, and Plate with
the reaction handed back also wins 100.0% ±0.0.

> **A drawback that bites only when the item is weak is not a price.** At armor 4 nothing can hurt
> you, so you never need the dodge, so losing it costs nothing. Heavy Armor's drawback is
> self-cancelling: it costs **Brigandine** something and **Plate** nothing, which is backwards for a
> price meant to rise with the rung.

**The kilograms are the entire cost, and they are the largest number in this file.** Burdened is
**Slowed** with no duration (core-rules §4), and the control is to burden a *lighter* rung to the
same total:

| | Load | Win rate | Cost of the weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mail Shirt, nothing else | 14 kg | 88.9 ±1.0 | — |
| Mail Shirt + 29 kg of loot | 43 kg | **7.2 ±0.5** | **−81.7** |
| Plate, nothing else | 25 kg | 100.0 ±0.0 | — |
| Plate + 18 kg of loot | 43 kg | **31.4 ±1.8** | **−68.6** |
| Plate + 38 kg of loot | 63 kg (Overloaded) | 34.4 ±0.8 | — |

**Burdened is the most expensive state measured in this project**, by a factor of two over anything in
§§1–9. The reason is arithmetic rather than subtle: Slowed **halves** a printed Move, the starter
deck's Moves are 1, 2 and 3, and halving turns every **Move 1 into a Move 0** — a burdened hero
cannot close on an archer at all.

Three things follow, and the third is the one that matters for the catalogue:

- **"Burdened is one bad decision away" is confirmed, and the decision costs 69 points.**
- **Plate cushions the fall it causes.** Burdened at Mail is 7.2%, at Plate 31.4% — heavy armour is
  what keeps you alive once its own weight has slowed you, which is why Plate is a decision and not
  a trap.
- **Overloaded charges nothing on top.** 31.4% against 34.4%, spreads nearly touching: two random
  cards Spent out of sixteen is close to free next to being Slowed. **The whole price of going over
  your limit is the first rung**, and the second is decoration.

> **So the real price of Plate is eleven kilograms of carrying capacity** — not the coat and not the
> reaction. Mail leaves 26 kg before Burdened; Plate leaves 15. Which ties this ladder straight to
> §10.2 of the equipment page: **a party that means to carry treasure out cannot wear Plate in.**

## 14. Reload, and the one weapon on the page that is dominated

The second number `equipment.md` §12 asked for. A reload weapon fires every other turn, so it halves
its damage and buys `ignores N armor` with the half. This is the fate deck's own distribution rather
than a simulation, so it is exact — **expected damage a turn**:

| Weapon | Range | ar 0 | ar 1 | ar 2 | ar 3 | ar 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shortbow | 6 | 2.00 | 1.10 | 0.40 | 0.10 | 0.00 |
| Longbow | 10 | **4.00** | **3.05** | **2.10** | **1.20** | 0.50 |
| Crossbow | 8 | 2.00 | 2.00 | 1.53 | 1.05 | 0.60 |
| Heavy Crossbow | 10 | 3.00 | 3.00 | 2.53 | 2.05 | 1.58 |
| Arquebus | 8 | 3.00 | 3.00 | 3.00 | 2.53 | 2.05 |
| Matchlock Musket | 12 | 3.50 | 3.50 | 3.50 | 3.03 | **2.55** |
| Longsword | 1 | 4.00 | 3.05 | 2.10 | 1.20 | 0.50 |

**§3's claim is true, and conditional on a feature it never mentions.** The page says *"against five
goblins the bow wins; against one armoured knight the crossbow does."* For the starter Warrior the
Crossbow does not overtake the Longbow until **armor 4**, and then by 0.10. Taking ✊ Strong away
moves the crossover forward to **armor 2**:

| at armor 2 / armor 4 | With Strong | Without Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Longbow | 2.10 / 0.50 | 1.15 / 0.15 |
| Crossbow | 1.53 / 0.60 | 1.53 / 0.60 |

**But an arithmetic crossover is not a playable difference.** Against five enemies in Brigandine:

| vs 5 guards (armor 3) | With Strong | Without Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Longbow | 2.1 ±0.5 | **0.0 ±0.0** |
| Crossbow | 2.4 ±0.5 | 2.5 ±0.4 |
| Heavy Crossbow | **34.6 ±1.3** | **17.9 ±1.1** |

A character without Strong holding a longbow against armoured infantry wins **no fights at all**, and
with a crossbow wins 2.5% — so the crossover is real and it crosses between *hopeless* and *nearly
hopeless*. **The weapon that answers armour is the Heavy Crossbow, and the answer is a firearm**
(§15). The plain Crossbow's single point of penetration never pays for the turn it spends loading,
at any armour value, for anybody.

> **The Crossbow is a weapon for a character who is not Strong**, and that is why it looks bad here.
> `Heavy` does two unrelated jobs: it marks the weight class that blocks ⚡ Sidestep, **and** it gates
> Strong's +1. A longbow is Heavy for its draw weight and collects a melee feature's bonus by
> accident; a crossbow — the weapon a weak person can actually use, which was historically the point
> of one — is not Heavy and collects nothing.

**And the Longbow is a Longsword with range 10.** Both are Heavy Strike 3, so they do identical damage
at every armour value, and the bow gives up only the shield. Against five spears that trade is worth
**+22.9 points** (90.1 ±0.8 against 67.2 ±1.5) — and the control says the cause is **reach, not the
tag**, because taking Strong away *widens* the gap to **+24.9** (74.8 against 49.9). Separating Heavy
into two tags is tidiness; **the range band is the balance problem**, and it is §6's finding from the
other side: reach decides fights, and this project keeps measuring it as the strongest thing on the
board.

## 15. Firearms do answer armour, and the engine was not doing it

`equipment.md` §1.3 is titled *"Firearms exist to answer armour"*, and it is the best-confirmed claim
on the page. Five enemies identical in every respect but what they wear — a goblin spear with
Brigandine or Plate on:

| Weapon | 5 spears | 5 guards (armor 3) | 5 plated (armor 4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Longsword + shield | 67.2 ±1.5 | **0.8 ±0.2** | 0.0 |
| Shortbow | 30.0 ±1.1 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| Longbow | **90.1 ±0.8** | 2.1 ±0.5 | 0.0 |
| Crossbow | 34.6 ±1.3 | 2.4 ±0.5 | 0.0 |
| Heavy Crossbow | 50.4 ±1.8 | 34.6 ±1.3 | 18.2 ±0.7 |
| Arquebus | 50.3 ±1.9 | **46.5 ±1.8** | **34.6 ±1.3** |

**An arquebus wins 46.5% of the fights a longsword wins 0.8% of.** Armour at 3 switches off every
melee weapon and every bow in the catalogue, and the two things still functioning are the Heavy
Crossbow and the firearm. Note what the arquebus barely notices: flat from armor 0 to 2, and four
points of win rate from unarmoured spears to armoured ones. That is what `ignores 2` means in play.

**The crossbow does not answer armour so much as survive it** — 34.6% to 2.4% is a collapse.

> **[FIXED] None of this worked in the actual game.** `resolve.mjs` subtracted the whole of a
> target's armour and read nothing from the verb, so **`ignores N armor` was prose on eight items and
> did nothing** — which settles `equipment.md` §12's open question about whether it should be a tag.
> As text it is not a rule, it is a promise. It is a field now, taken off **armour and never off
> Block** (a bolt finds a gap in plate; it does not get past a raised shield), and the validator
> holds the field and the printed text to each other — a card that says one thing and resolves
> another is worse than the gap was.

## 16. What this round still does not test

- **The ogre does not exist.** §12's big hitter is a probe built to hold damage-per-round constant,
  not a stat block. The game has goblins and nothing else, which is exactly why rungs 3 and 4 of the
  armour ladder have no opponent.
- **The hero's AI wants to close even holding a bow.** `score` penalises distance to the nearest
  enemy, so every ranged figure in §§14–15 is a **lower bound**; a kiting AI would read higher, and
  the Longbow's +22.9 is therefore the small version of that finding.
- **Nothing here is a party.** One starter Warrior, as everywhere in this file. Sidestep in
  particular should be worth less in a party, where somebody else is already between you and the
  enemy that is arriving.
- **Reload is measured on a weapon the hero starts holding.** The interesting case — fire the
  arquebus, drop it, draw the pistol — needs the sling and a second reload weapon, and was not run.
- **Armour above 4 and Strike above 6** are both off the end of the catalogue, so the far side of the
  cliff is unmeasured.
