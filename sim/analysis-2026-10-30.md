# Conditions, combat and dispel — 2026-10-30

First simulation of anything in [../rules/conditions.md](../rules/conditions.md). Everything in that
document and in the condition work of the last several passes was reasoned from the value bands and
**none of it had been measured**, which this fixes for the parts a simulator can reach.

Run with `python conditions_sim.py`. The condition engine itself lives in `combat_sim.py`, which
previously knew only about Dazed, hardcoded; it now carries a `CONDITIONS` table mirroring
`module/config.mjs` and the five removal routes.

**4000 fights per scenario, one seed.** At a win rate near 47% the standard error is **0.8 points**,
so differences under about **1.6 points are noise** and are called noise below.

**The refactor is behaviour-neutral**, checked against the committed version on the baseline
scenario: 98.8% before, 98.9% after, which is inside the 0.2-point error at that win rate.

> **A discrepancy that predates this work:** [starter-cards.md](../rules/starter-cards.md) and
> [analysis-2026-10-27.md](analysis-2026-10-27.md) both print **99.4%** for the 3-spear baseline, and
> the current code gives **98.8%** at the same 3000 fights — before and after this change alike, so
> it is not the refactor. Something else moved between 27 October and now and the older number was
> never re-run. Not chased here; flagged because the 99.4% is quoted in the rules as a tuning
> yardstick.

## 1. The rules against themselves — 26 assertions, all pass

No combat; these exercise the condition rules directly and either hold or the rules are not what they
say.

| What | Result |
|---|---|
| **The clock** — a condition ticks at the start of its **creator's** turn, not anyone else's | ✔ |
| A **(2)** survives one tick and is gone after two | ✔ |
| A creator who leaves combat hands the clock to the victim | ✔ |
| **Wet onto Burning** — neither lands, the Burning goes out | ✔ |
| **Burning onto Wet** — neither lands, the Wet steams off | ✔ |
| **Refresh** takes the longer duration *and* the fiercer value, independently | ✔ |
| **Poisoned 0** costs the secondary and does no damage | ✔ |
| **Move**: halved by Slowed, then reduced by Frozen, floored at 0 | ✔ |
| **Shake Off** spends duration and ends at 0 | ✔ |
| **Shake Off cannot touch an opening**, and cannot touch Cursed | ✔ |
| **Lift** removes Cursed, and cannot touch a timed condition | ✔ |
| A **no-duration** condition survives ten ticks | ✔ |
| Every modelled condition has exactly one removal route | ✔ |

The two cancel assertions are the ones worth having: *water and fire annihilate* is the only
interaction in the whole system, and it is the one place the rules could have been asymmetric by
accident. They are not.

## 2. What Dazed is worth — 5.8 points, about two thirds of a damage rung

Four goblins, two spears and two bows — the **Deadly** encounter, so there is room to move and the
numbers are not floored. *Dirty Trick* is the vehicle: it applies whatever the scenario says instead
of Dazed, so every row differs by one thing.

| Dirty Trick applies | Win rate | Costs the hero |
|---|---|---|
| nothing (it is just a Strike) | **47.0%** | — |
| **Dazed (1)** | 41.2% | **5.8** |
| **Burning 1 (1)** | 38.3% | 8.7 |
| **Burning 2 (1)** | 29.6% | 17.4 |
| **Burning 3 (1)** | 24.9% | 22.5 |

**A damage rung is worth about 8.7 points** and the ladder is close to linear up to 2, then flattens —
by Burning 3 the hero is dying to the burn before the fight can turn, so more intensity buys less.

**Dazed is 0.67 of a rung.** That is the number every claim about Poisoned rests on.

## 3. Poisoned N is Burning N plus **two thirds** of a rung, not a whole one

> **[CORRECTION]** [conditions.md](../rules/conditions.md) §2.1 asserted *"Poisoned costs one rung
> more than Burning at the same number"* and priced the ladder accordingly: Poisoned 1 at strength 2,
> Poisoned 2 at strength 3. **That overprices it by about half a rung.**

| | Win rate | Against the Burning of the same number |
|---|---|---|
| **Poisoned 0 (1)** | 41.2% | — (Burning has no 0) |
| **Burning 1 (1)** | 38.3% | |
| **Poisoned 1 (1)** | 34.7% | **+3.6** worse for the hero |
| **Burning 2 (1)** | 29.6% | |
| **Poisoned 2 (1)** | 27.5% | **+2.1** worse for the hero |

So Poisoned N sits **between** Burning N and Burning N+1, nearer the former: +3.6 against a rung of
8.7 is **0.4 of a rung** at N=1, and +2.1 is **0.24** at N=2.

**And the gap shrinks as N rises**, which makes sense once it is measured: the Dazed is worth less
when you are dying faster, because losing a secondary matters most in a fight you could still win.

**The corrected band**, with the shift at the bottom where it is largest:

| Poisoned | Costs about | Belongs to |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | 0.67 of a rung — **less than a Burning 1** | a card, or strength 1 |
| **1** | a Burning 1 and a bit | strength **2** |
| **2** | a Burning 2 and a bit | strength **3** |
| **3+** | monsters only | |

Which is, awkwardly, nearly the table that was already written — the *rungs* were right and the
*reason* was wrong. Poisoned 1 at strength 2 is correct not because it equals a Burning 2 but because
it exceeds a Burning 1, and a strength-2 formula is what a Burning-1-and-a-bit costs. The claim to
retire is **"one rung more"**; the right phrasing is **"two thirds of a rung more, and less than that
as the number rises."**

## 4. A dispel on an action card is not worth its slot

*Shrug It Off* — a Strike primary with **Shake Off 1** on its secondary — bought by cutting one copy
of *Strike*, so the deck stays at sixteen.

| | Win rate | Delta | Shake played |
|---|---|---|---|
| **vs nothing**, plain | 47.0% | | |
| **vs nothing**, *Shrug It Off* | 44.9% | **−2.1** | 0.00/fight |
| **vs Dazed (2)**, plain | 37.0% | | |
| **vs Dazed (2)**, *Shrug It Off* | 35.9% | −1.1 *(noise)* | **0.00**/fight |
| **vs Burning 2 (2)**, plain | 20.7% | | |
| **vs Burning 2 (2)**, *Shrug It Off* | 20.1% | −0.6 *(noise)* | 0.22/fight |
| **vs Poisoned 2 (2)**, plain | 13.8% | | |
| **vs Poisoned 2 (2)**, *Shrug It Off* | 14.2% | +0.4 *(noise)* | **0.00**/fight |

**It is never played.** Two reasons, and both are general:

**A card is in your hand 31% of turns.** One copy in a sixteen-card deck reaches a five-card hand
`1 − C(15,5)/C(16,5)` of the time, and it has to be there on a turn the condition is *also* on you.
Against Burning 2 (2) — the only row where the dispel can legally fire — that coincidence plus the
competition from an ordinary secondary comes to **0.22 plays a fight**, which cannot repay a card.

**And against the conditions that cost you a secondary, it cannot fire at all.** Dazed and Poisoned
both block the secondary, so a Shake Off *on* a secondary is unreachable exactly when it is wanted:
**0.00 plays**. The simulator rediscovered core-rules §7's own rule — *a dispel must not sit on the
half the condition takes* — from the other end, without being told it.

> **[CORRECTION]** [way-cards.md](../rules/way-cards.md) §1.6 priced a Shake Off 1 at *"a secondary —
> free, and the only rung a secondary may buy"*. The half is indeed cheap; **the card is not**, and
> the band ignored it. A dispel wants a surface that is there when the condition is:
>
> | Surface | Available | Costs |
> |---|---|---|
> | an action card's secondary | 31% of turns | a card **and** a half — measured at −2.1 for the slot |
> | a **reaction** line | 31% of turns | the card only, no half |
> | a **prepared spell** | **always** | a slot, not a draw |
> | a **feature** | **always** | a node |
>
> The content already got this right where it matters: Ward's *Shake It Off* is a **reaction spell**,
> so it is prepared rather than drawn and costs no half. It is the band table that overreached.

## 5. What this does not test

- **Party play.** Every number here is one hero, and the two things conditions are *for* — openings
  read by somebody else's card, and a priest lifting an ally's curse — need a party the simulator
  does not have. This is the same limit [analysis-2026-10-27.md](analysis-2026-10-27.md) names.
- **Corrode.** Armor loss needs an equipment model; the sim has a flat `armor` number.
- **Tainting.** Taint is cards in a deck across fights, and the sim runs one fight at a time.
- **The openings themselves.** A combo is two cards in two hands; see party play.
- **Frozen in anger.** The Move arithmetic is asserted in §1, but what *losing* Move is worth
  depends on a map the sim treats as open ground.
