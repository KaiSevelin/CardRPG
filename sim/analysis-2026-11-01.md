# Stamina as a stat, and the Duellist's price — 2026-11-01

Two measurements. The first prices a specialization's *"one thing you give up"* for the first time
and finds the proposed price wrong by a factor of ten. The second reruns the whole difficulty curve
under a rule change: **stamina as a fixed stat rather than your deck size.**

Both arrive through `combat_sim.py` config dials, so every figure published before today is
reproduced with the dials off — and in the stamina case, reproduced **exactly**, which is the
correctness check rather than a finding.

---

## 1. The Duellist: armour is the wrong currency, by about 10×

The proposal was ⚔ **Strike on the secondary half, paid for with armour** — the highest-leverage
device in [../rules/specializations.md](../rules/specializations.md) §4, *"one thing you give up"*.

**5 seeds × 4000 fights.** A double-strike card *replaces* a plain Strike, so the deck stays at
sixteen; otherwise the experiment measures a bigger deck instead of a different one.

| | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brigandine, plain deck | 100.0% | 100.0% | 99.5% | 100.0% |
| Leather, plain deck | 98.7% | 89.9% | 67.0% | 40.4% |
| **No armour**, plain deck | 95.1% | 72.1% | 40.6% | **10.2%** |
| No armour, **1** double-strike | 95.5% | 74.0% | 43.2% | 12.4% |
| No armour, **2** double-strikes | 95.5% | 75.0% | 46.3% | 12.9% |
| No armour, **3** double-strikes | 94.5% | 73.8% | 45.0% | 12.7% |

**Armour is worth about 30 points of win rate and a double-strike card buys back 2 to 6.** Nothing
built on an armour restriction can balance against that: the Deadly encounter goes from 40.4% to
10.2% for taking the coat off, and three double-strikes return 2.5 of the 30.

**The curve peaks at two and falls.** Each double-strike eats a plain Strike and a −1 secondary is a
weak half, so the third costs more than it brings. Worth knowing before anyone writes *"more of
them"* — "more" is not a direction this card scales in.

### What the right currency is

**3 seeds × 3000 fights**, against the Leather / shield 2 / hand 5 baseline.

| What the node gives up | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **a card of hand size** | −6.1 | −9.9 | −8.2 |
| **the shield** | −1.8 | −0.8 | −0.4 |
| the ⚡ Sidestep | −1.2 | −1.7 | +1.0 |

And what one card buys back on top of each:

| Arm | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 90.3% | 66.4% | 40.1% |
| **no shield, 1 double-strike** | **89.3%** | **68.3%** | **44.2%** |
| no shield, 2 double-strikes | 89.8% | 68.8% | 45.3% |
| hand of four, 1 double-strike | 84.8% | 59.6% | 32.7% |

> **The shield is the right price and one card is the right amount.** *No shield, one double-strike*
> is **−1.0 / +1.9 / +4.1** against baseline: level in the easy fights, ahead where it matters, which
> is the shape a specialization should have. Two is too many. It also reads correctly — a duellist
> fights with a blade and a free hand — and it gives Way of Shade a resource the Way lacked, since
> its cell paid in *halves*, which Shade itself already pays in.

**Two findings that were not the question.** **Hand size is the most expensive stat measured anywhere
in this project**, 6–10 points a card — remember that before anything offers one. And **a shield is
worth almost nothing**, 0.4–1.8, which against a Buckler at 6 gold and a Longsword at 15 is a
catalogue problem rather than a node problem ([../rules/equipment.md](../rules/equipment.md) §12).

## 2. Stamina as a stat: the whole curve, rerun

**The rule.** Stamina is how many cards you may **draw** in a fight, as a stat that levels like
Health. A fight starts with `min(stamina, collection)` random cards in the deck; the discard tops the
deck back up while the budget lasts; you are Exhausted when the budget is gone. **Catch Breath
recovers 2 stamina *and* 2 random cards** — under this rule a breath that moved only cards would buy
nothing, because the deck already feeds itself from the discard.

**Why:** today the deck *is* the stamina, so acquiring any card makes you last longer and pruning a
bad one makes you last less. A card's contents and a character's endurance are the same number, which
means pruning is a punishment and the Cutpurse's *Lift* grants endurance nobody designed.

**3 seeds × 3000 fights.** `win% (sd) / rounds / breaths per fight`:

| Arm | 3 spears | 4 spears | 5 spears | 4 mixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **old rule** (deck is stamina) | 98.4% 6.9r 0.8b | 90.3% 9.0r 1.9b | 66.4% 10.1r 2.8b | 40.1% 10.2r 2.8b |
| stamina 6 | 62.5% 9.6r **4.8b** | 28.7% 8.8r 4.5b | 10.6% 7.6r 3.9b | 4.5% 7.1r 3.7b |
| stamina 8 | 76.9% 9.4r 4.1b | 42.2% 9.2r 4.2b | 17.2% 8.1r 3.7b | 7.8% 7.8r 3.6b |
| stamina 10 | 86.8% 8.9r 3.2b | 58.2% 9.6r 3.9b | 28.4% 9.1r 3.7b | 13.7% 8.7r 3.6b |
| stamina 12 | 94.0% 8.2r 2.3b | 72.6% 9.9r 3.5b | 41.5% 9.7r 3.6b | 21.0% 9.4r 3.5b |
| stamina 14 | 97.2% 7.4r 1.4b | 82.9% 9.6r 2.8b | 54.3% 10.2r 3.3b | 30.0% 10.0r 3.3b |
| **stamina 16** | **98.4%** 6.9r 0.8b | **90.3%** 9.0r 1.9b | **66.4%** 10.1r 2.8b | **40.1%** 10.2r 2.8b |
| stamina 20 | 99.7% 6.4r 0.2b | 95.6% 8.1r 0.7b | 82.7% 9.5r 1.5b | 56.0% 10.0r 1.8b |

**Stamina 16 reproduces the old rule to the last decimal on all four encounters.** The starter deck
is sixteen cards drawn to exhaustion, so that is exactly what it should do, and it is the check that
the implementation is the rule rather than something near it.

### Three findings

**1. Stamina is the steepest dial in the game.** Two points of it is worth **7 to 11 points of win
rate** in the hard fights — steeper than a damage rung, steeper than armour per point, and second
only to hand size per unit. It is therefore a **powerful thing to level**, and a dangerous thing to
hand out on an item.

**2. The default has to be about 16, not 10.** A stamina of 10 is not a small change of flavour: the
Deadly encounter falls from **40.1% to 13.7%** and the Hard one from 66.4% to 28.4%. Every published
encounter rating would need rewriting. If the intent is to keep the current difficulty while
decoupling the deck, the stat starts where the deck is now.

**3. A breath buying stamina back makes low stamina a treadmill, not a wall.** At stamina 6 the hero
breathes **4.8 times in a 9.6-round fight** — half the fight is spent breathing. That is coherent,
and it is a different game from the one the exhaustion rules describe: stamina stops being *when you
stop* and becomes *how many of your turns you lose*. Note the rounds column barely moves across the
whole sweep (6.9 to 10.2) while breaths move by 6×. The fight does not get shorter; it gets emptier.

> **An open question the numbers raise, not the rule.** If Catch Breath restores stamina, stamina is
> not a cap at all — it is the interval between breaths. Capping how many times it may be done per
> fight would turn it back into a wall; leaving it uncapped makes a low-stamina character slow rather
> than fragile. Both are playable and they are different games.

## What this does not test

- **The engine does not implement stamina.** This is the simulator only, behind `cfg.stamina`
  (0 = the old rule).
- **The relationship between stamina and hand size.** They are separate dials here. Hand size is
  measured at 6–10 points a card and stamina needs to be about 16, so they cannot be the same
  *number*; whether one advancement should raise both is a design question and an expensive one —
  such a level-up would be worth 12 to 15 points of win rate.
- **A party.** Still one hero, as every run in this directory has been.
- **The minimum deck size** (half stamina, floor 2). Not exercised: the starter collection is
  sixteen, which satisfies it at every stamina measured.
