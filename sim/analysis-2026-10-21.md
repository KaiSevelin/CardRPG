# Simulation analysis – Warrior vs goblins (rules draft 0.1)

3,000 simulated fights per scenario, run with `sim/combat_sim.py`.

## Setup and assumptions

The rules don't define these yet, so the simulator assumes:
- **Warrior:** 12 HP, Leather Armor (armor 1), Longsword (Strike 3), Wooden Shield (Defend 2), 1 Healing Potion, Strong feature.
- **Deck:** the 16-card starter deck, hand size 5.
- **Goblins:** 5 HP, Spear (Strike 2) or Shortbow, using the 6-card goblin deck.
- **Map:** open 10×8 grid with no obstacles. Goblins start 7 squares away.
- **AI:** the warrior picks the best-scoring card pair each turn. Goblins follow their cards literally.
- **Solo hero:** there's no party, so Taunt and Ready do nothing in these fights.

## Findings

### 1. Goblins are far too weak
- Against 3 goblins, the warrior wins every fight and loses only about 2 of 12 HP.
- **Armor 1 against 2-damage attacks halves all incoming damage.** In the baseline, armor stopped 4.7 damage per fight, while the warrior took only 2.4.
- Flat armor is very strong against many small hits. This will matter for the whole game, not just goblins.
- Goblin Strike 3 alone isn't enough (99% wins). With 4 goblins it's still 92%. Adding **archers** is what makes a real threat: 2 spears + 2 bows gives a 62% win rate with 4.4 HP left. The warrior has to chase the bows, and the spears hit it while it does.

**Suggestion:** set goblin Strike to 3. Use 3 goblins as an easy fight and "4 goblins with 2 bows" as a hard one. As a general rule, keep weak enemies' damage at least 2 above the heroes' armor.

### 2. Bug: short conditions expire before they do anything
- When players act first, Daze (1) from Dirty Trick **never had an effect**: the warrior had 0 dazed turns in every players-first scenario.
- The cause is timing. The goblins apply Daze after the warrior's turn, and conditions tick down at the end of the round, so it expires before the warrior acts again.
- The same thing will happen to every condition with duration 1, including Invisible (1) from the Smoke Bomb.

**Suggested fix:** a condition ticks down at the **end of the affected character's own turn**, not at the end of the round. Then Dazed (1) always costs the victim exactly one turn.

### 3. Exhaustion hardly matters in small fights
- The deck runs out around round 6, and fights last 7–10 rounds.
- The warrior catches its breath 0.5–1 times per fight and is almost never Exhausted.
- Changing when the warrior catches its breath (scenarios I and J) made almost no difference.
- That's acceptable: exhaustion should matter in long or big fights, not every skirmish. If you want it to matter sooner, try a 12-card starter deck.

### 4. The heavy-hits rule never triggers against goblins
- It triggered 0.01 times per fight. After armor, goblins almost never deal 5 or more damage.
- The rule only matters against big monsters. Keep it for later tests with an ogre-type enemy.

### 5. Weapon Swap is the strongest card
- Its secondary half is a Strike, so Strike + Weapon Swap gives **two attacks in one turn**.
- Changing that half to Move 1 (scenario H) made fights take a whole extra round (8.1 rounds instead of 7.0).
- **Decide:** is attacking twice a turn allowed? If yes, make it rare and give it a cost. If no, add a rule: "Strike only on a primary," or "at most one Strike per turn."

### 6. Dead card halves in the starter deck
Card halves played as filler (counts per fight):

| Card | Primary | Secondary | Issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taunt | 0.02 | 0.92 | The Taunt half is never used (solo test). |
| Quick Hands | 0.00 | 0.90 | Use and Ready are both rarely useful. |
| Weapon Swap | 0.06 | 1.06 | The Ready half is never used; the card is only played for its Strike. |
| Draw Steel | 1.49 | 0.63 | The Ready half is used as a "do nothing" secondary. |
| Shield Up | 0.53 | 0.03 | Played as a primary, it prevents only 0.3 damage per fight. |

- A sword-and-shield warrior never needs to swap weapons, so its 4 Ready halves are dead weight. Ready cards belong in decks for characters who do swap, such as an archer who switches to a dagger.
- **Shield Up as a primary is weak:** Block 2 is used up by a single hit and competes with attacking. As a reaction (Block 3), it's one of the most used cards. Consider making the Defend primary give "Block 2 against **every** attack until your next turn."

### 7. Smaller observations
- **Who goes first** barely matters (A vs B), so the GM can decide freely.
- **Strong (+1 damage)** shortens fights by about 1.4 rounds. It turns Strike 3 into Strike 4, and a Heavy Swing then kills a 5-HP goblin 70% of the time instead of rarely. A +1 is worth most when it pushes damage up to an enemy's HP total, so check that when you design features.
- **Brace** works as an emergency valve: rarely used in easy fights, used about twice per fight in the hard archer fight.
- **Disadvantage from cover** comes up often once there are archers: about 4.6 goblin attacks per fight in scenario Q.

## Limitations

- The AI plays reasonably but isn't optimal. A human would probably do a little better.
- The room has no obstacles, so cover only comes from figures.
- These are solo fights. Party play (Taunt, healing allies) isn't tested.
- Warrior HP and equipment are guesses. Once you settle them, rerun the simulator.

## How to rerun

```
python sim/combat_sim.py                # all scenarios
python sim/combat_sim.py --only AOQ     # selected scenarios
python sim/combat_sim.py --log 1        # one narrated fight
```

## Update: condition timing fix and goblin tactics

Changes since the first run:
- Conditions now tick at the **start of the creator's turn**.
- Goblins can now have **Pack Tactics** (advantage on melee Strike when another goblin is next to the target) and **Cowardly** (below half Health they flee and escape at the map edge).

| Scenario | Win % | Rounds | HP left (wins) | Goblin attacks with advantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: baseline, 3 goblins (timing fix) | 99.9 | 7.2 | 10.1 | – |
| O: Strike 3, 4 goblins, no tactics | 92.1 | 8.6 | 7.7 | – |
| R: Pack, Strike 2, 3 goblins | 99.8 | 7.1 | 9.8 | 2.0 |
| S: Pack, Strike 2, 4 goblins | 97.7 | 8.9 | 8.4 | 4.2 |
| T: Pack, Strike 3, 3 goblins | 96.4 | 6.9 | 8.7 | 1.9 |
| U: Pack, Strike 3, 4 goblins | 79.5 | 8.0 | 7.2 | 3.9 |
| V: Pack + Cowardly, Strike 3, 4 goblins | 96.8 | 4.4 | 9.6 | 1.5 |
| W: Pack, Strike 3, 2 spear + 2 bow | 54.9 | 8.1 | 4.2 | 0.9 |

### Findings
- **The timing fix works.** With players first, Daze now costs the warrior a turn (0.55 dazed turns per fight, up from 0).
- **Pack Tactics grows with numbers.** With 3 goblins it gives about 2 advantaged attacks per fight; with 4 goblins, about 4. With Strike 3, four pack goblins win 20% of fights, up from 8% without the tactic.
- **Pack Tactics can't beat armor on its own.** At Strike 2, advantage mostly turns 1 damage into 1–2. Tactics multiply a threat but can't create one from nothing. The base numbers still need to get past the heroes' armor.
- **Cowardly is a large weakness.** About 2.4 of 4 goblins fled, and fights ended in 4.4 rounds. (This is exaggerated by the test map, where goblins start one square from the edge.) That's the intended effect: the tactic gives players a weakness to exploit.
- **Mixed groups are the hardest.** Spears that pin the warrior, with archers shooting from behind, is still the deadliest setup. Tactical variety within a group matters more than any single keyword.
