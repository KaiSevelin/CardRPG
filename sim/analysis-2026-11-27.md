# What a caster's chassis is worth, and what a spell engine still cannot do — 2026-11-27

**The decision this measures.** The **Caster** starter deck shipped on 2026-11-27 with an `[OPEN]`
against it. Sixteen cards built to the Warrior's budget field by field, three fields differing, and
a note saying in as many words that *the budget equality is a design argument, not a measurement* —
because `sim/careers_sim.py` modelled eight verbs and **cast was not one of them**. The note was
specific about why running it anyway would be worse than not: the simulator would return not a
silence but a **number**, computed with nine sixteenths of the deck inert, sitting beside the Shade
deck's figure looking comparable.

So the work was the engine. This is what it bought, and what it still cannot see.

`sim/combat_sim.py` and `sim/careers_sim.py`, seed 1. Fight counts are stated per table.

## What the engine does now

**✦ Cast has two sources** (core-rules §4, *Two kinds of casting*) and both are resolved:

- **an implement's Cast line** — repeatable, as often as a Cast half is drawn, and it spends no
  preparation. `IMPLEMENTS` holds the two the game sells, and the validator pins both that it holds
  exactly those two and that their numbers are gear.mjs's.
- **a prepared formula** — cast **once**, then gone until a long rest. The slots are state on the
  character, the lookahead keeps its own copy of what is spent, and two Cast halves in one turn
  therefore cost two preparations rather than casting the same formula twice.

A cast carries damage, a heal, a Shake Off, a Block, an **area**, armour it goes through, and its
own Health price. A card half's printed modifier **adds** to the formula's own and adds nothing at
all to a formula that deals no damage; a printed range is a **floor** and never a ceiling. An empty
hand is `strike=None` — *no line for the verb*, which is a rule rather than a zero — so a caster
holding a wand cannot swing a sword they have not got.

**Strain is paid** (core-rules §8): N other cards out of the hand, cheapest first, never out of a
Wound, and *if you can't pay it in full you can't play the half* — so the option is withdrawn from
the menu rather than discounted.

## Six things that were wrong in the first build of it

Each was found by measuring, by crashing or by a live probe refusing to pass — none by reading —
and the first three flattered the thing being measured.

1. **A Blast did not catch the caster.** core-rules §11 is explicit — *Blast: everyone in the area,
   enemies, allies and you, if you're in it* — and a Fire Wand is a Blast 1. The first Caster
   chassis figure was **+7.1** against the Warrior's deck; charging it cost **1.3 points** on its
   own, and the aim point became a trade rather than a maximum: three goblins and yourself is
   often worse than two and not. (The final +4.7 is that fix and the three below it together.)
2. **Strain was free**, so *The Long Word* — the game's first Strain card, and the Caster deck's
   one above-budget thing — printed ✦ Cast +2 for nothing.
3. **`requires: { tag: "arcane" }` was unenforced**, so *The Sharp Word* was castable with empty
   hands.
4. **The AI's score is tie-sensitive to floating-point order.** Folding `val += a; val += b` into
   `val += a + b` while adding the Strain charge moved the starter Warrior by **0.3 to 1.9 points**
   across the published encounters, with no change of rule at all. The two statements are back, and
   there is a comment on them saying why they are two.
5. **A hand of one Strain card has no legal play**, and `warrior_turn` unpacked `None`. *If you
   can't pay it in full, you can't play the half* is the rule, so the turn passes — but nothing
   before today could reach that state, because no deck printed Strain.
6. **And in the engine change of §6, the caster could not be found in their own footprint.**
   `areaTargets` returns `token.actor`, which for an **unlinked** token is the token's own
   synthetic actor and not the one `resolveAttack` was called with — so `who === actor` was false
   and *your own Blast ignores your armour* would have fired only for linked tokens. The live
   probe printed the footprint as *Starter Warrior, Goblin, Goblin* while the identity test on the
   hero still read false, which is as clear as a defect gets. It is `who === myself || who ===
   actor` now, off the caster's own token.

## 1. The three starter chassis

`python sim/careers_sim.py --chassis`, **3000 fights a cell**, the careers ladder, no career and
nothing prepared: sixteen cards and the hands they need.

| | 5 spears | 6 spears | 2 spear 2 bow | 2 brutes | 2 guard 2 spear | MEAN |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warrior deck, sword and shield | 61.8 | 35.6 | 28.5 | 58.2 | 36.2 | **44.0** |
| Shade deck, same gear | 60.9 | 35.3 | 25.2 | 55.9 | 31.6 | **41.8** (−2.3) |
| **Caster deck, a wand and a free hand** | **82.8** | **82.4** | **54.4** | 21.5 | 2.5 | **48.7** (+4.7) |
| the caster against the Warrior's | +21.0 | +46.8 | +25.9 | **−36.7** | **−33.7** | +4.7 |

**+4.7 on the mean, and the mean is the least interesting number in the table.** The spread inside
it is **eighty points**: the Caster deck is the best of the three chassis against numbers by a
margin nothing else in this project has produced, and the worst of the three against armour by
thirty.

- **Against six goblin spears, +46.8.** A Blast 1 covers a three-by-three; six goblins converging on
  one hero stand inside it. A sword reaches one man a turn and the deck reaches the group.
- **Against armour, −33.7, and that one is not a matchup.** A Fire Wand casts for **2**, armour
  comes off every hit, and a Goblin Guard wears Brigandine, which is **3** — so the deck's attack
  verb does *nothing at all* to it, and the deck prints no ⚔ Strike to fall back on. 2.5% over
  3000 fights is a character who cannot hurt what is standing in front of them.
- **Against two brutes, −36.7, for a different reason: nothing to spread.** A brute wears no
  armour at all. It has **10 Health** and there are two of them, so an area catches at most two
  targets and 2 a cast against 20 Health is nine or ten casts' worth of work while they hit for 6
  apiece. The Blast is the whole deck and a Blast wants a crowd.
- **A shield is bit-for-bit identical.** Not approximately: the same figure in every cell, because
  the Caster deck prints no 🛡 Defend primary and no ⚡ Block reaction, so a shield has nothing on
  it to read. That row is a finding rather than a measurement, and it is why the deck's defensive
  primary is a printed Block 2 instead.

**And the morale rule is worth LESS to a caster than to a fighter**, which is the opposite of what
an area attack suggests. Cowardly routs a goblin once it is below half its own Health, so a Blast
that puts six goblins on 3 of 5 routs none of them while a Strike for 3 routs one every time. Turn
the rule off and every deck collapses, but not equally:

| | as written | nobody flees | what morale is worth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warrior deck | 44.0 | 19.0 | −25.0 |
| Shade deck | 41.8 | 17.8 | −24.0 |
| Caster deck | 48.7 | 27.9 | −20.8 |

So the caster's advantage **grows** to **+8.9** once nothing flees. Cowardly is a reward for
focused damage, and a Blast is the opposite of focused.

## 2. A deck and its hands are one object

3000 fights a cell, five goblin spears.

| | win % | what it draws |
|---|---|---|
| Warrior deck, sword and shield | **61.8** | — |
| Warrior deck, empty hands and a wand | **0.0** | nine ⚔ Strike halves and nothing to swing |
| Caster deck, sword and shield | **0.0** | 1.30 dry ✦ Cast halves a fight, and 0.24 blocked for want of an Arcane item |
| Caster deck, a wand and a free hand | **82.8** | 4.53 casts a fight |

**Neither deck can be played with the other's hands, and both go to zero trying.** That is the
sharpest statement of what a chassis is: not sixteen cards, but sixteen cards **and** what they
need to be in your hands. A Warrior deck with nine ⚔ Strike halves and nothing to strike with wins
nothing; a Caster deck with nine ✦ Cast halves and a sword wins nothing. The deck and the loadout
are one object and cannot be measured apart, which is why the chassis table above changes both at
once and says so.

## 3. The two sources of a cast

3000 fights a cell, five goblin spears, the Caster deck throughout.

| what the ✦ Cast halves have behind them | win % | casts a fight | of those, off a formula | Blast on self |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the Fire Wand alone | **82.8** | 4.53 | 0.00 | 0.85 |
| the wand, and *Open the Vein* prepared | **84.2** | 4.47 | 0.45 | 0.56 |
| *Open the Vein* prepared, no implement | **0.0** | 0.99 | 0.99 | — |
| the *Staff of Healing* alone | **0.0** | 2.21 | 0.00 | — |
| neither | **0.0** | 0.00 | — | — |

Three findings, and the third is a content gap rather than a measurement.

**The deck is the wand.** Nine ✦ Cast halves with no implement and nothing prepared wins nothing at
all, and the preparation route does not rescue it: one strength-1 formula is one cast a fight.

**A preparation adds nothing measurable on top of an implement.** *Open the Vein* is strength 1,
3 points at range 2, round a shield, for a point of the caster's own blood — and the AI spends it
about once every other fight, because the wand's Blast against a group is worth more than a bigger
number against one. That is a true statement about **this** formula at **this** level and not about
preparation in general: a strength-4 formula would read differently, and no character who can reach
one exists at level 1.

**And the game sells two implements, of which one makes this deck unplayable.** *Staff of Healing*
is the other, its whole Cast line is *Heal 3*, and a Caster deck holding it has nine attack halves
that heal. The Staff is not a bad item — it is a two-handed healer, and the deck was written for
the one-handed burner, which is the item it is measured with. What is missing is anything in
between, and that is a shopping list rather than a bug.

**That is the real answer, and it is not the one the count gives.** Nineteen of the twenty careers

**Four of the eleven picks are Blood Magic's**, and that is the graph rather than the menu:
*Asceticism*, *Poison* and *Necromancy* all reach the Blood Magic menu because the cheapest route
to those tier-2 titles runs through its node. So the engine's coverage of Way of Mind is still
narrower than thirteen schools, and the condition gap in §4 is why.

## 5. The two invented numbers, and what they are worth

The engine has two figures that are not in the rules, and both are dials with a measured
sensitivity rather than assertions. 500 fights a cell, five goblin spears, the Caster deck.

**`cast_prep_cost` — what a preparation is worth not spending, per point of strength.** Measured
with a wand **and *Open the Vein* prepared**, because with nothing in a slot the dial has nothing
to price.

| | win % | prepared casts a fight |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 — a slot is free to empty | 77.6 | 0.59 |
| **0.8 — as written** | **78.0** | **0.51** |
| 2.0 — a slot is dear | **80.6** | 0.41 |

**[CORRECTED] This read *it barely matters* before the Blast rule, and it does matter now.** Under
the old reading the range was 1.2 points and the chosen value sat in the middle of it. With the
caster paying full price for their own Blast the dial is worth **3.0 points**, it is monotonic, and
**0.8 is the wrong end of it**: an AI that hoards its formula harder plays 2.6 points better.

It is left at 0.8 rather than retuned, for one reason and with one consequence. The reason is that
**the chassis figures in §1 to §3 prepare no formulas at all**, so none of them moves. The
consequence is that **every table-C and table-D figure for a career with a castable formula is a
floor**, by up to about that much, because the AI spends its one preparation sooner than it should.

**`BLOOD_HURT`/`BLOOD_WHOLE` — what a point of the hero's own Health costs them.** Measured with
the wand alone, so the only blood being priced is the Blast's own.

| | win % | Blast on self a fight |
|---|---|---|
| 1.2 / 0.3 — exactly what healing is worth | **82.6** | 1.28 |
| **2.0 / 1.2 — as written** | **77.6** | **0.97** |
| 4.0 / 3.0 — a careful caster | **58.2** | 0.10 |

**This one matters, and what it says is about the rule rather than the model.** A caster who
*refuses* to stand in their own Blast loses **19.4 points of win rate** — it was 8 before the
armour rule — because the extra goblin the footprint catches is worth more than the two points it
costs them.

The value as written sits on the flat part of that curve, with the careless setting inside noise of
it and the careful setting measurably worse. So the choice is defensible, and the thing worth
remembering is the shape of the curve and not the number.

## 6. Balancing the Blast, and one rule that was never applied at all

Two things were wrong with ✸ Blast, and only one of them is about balance.

**The engine resolved no footprint.** `resolveAttack` took the targets the player had clicked and
read `area` for exactly one thing — the disadvantage a ranged or area attack takes with an enemy
adjacent. Nothing measured the squares an area covers, so nothing ever put an ally or the caster
inside one: *everyone in the area — enemies, allies and you, if you're in it* cost nothing unless
a GM enforced it by eye. **An Area resolves against everything standing in its footprint now**
(`areaTargets`, core-rules §11), measured from the square the player aimed at, with one fate draw
for the whole action as §6 already required. A live probe drives it: a Blast 1 centred on the
creature beside the caster catches the caster, one centred a square further over does not, and an
Aimed area of the same size passes over their own side.

**And armour made your own Blast free.** This is the balance half, and the number is what found it:

| the caster's coat | what their own Blast 1 cost them, before |
|---|---|
| Leather (armour 1) | 1 point |
| **Plate (armour 4)** | **nothing at all** |

A Fire Wand casts for **2**, armour comes off every hit, and Plate is four points — so the
best-armoured caster in the game stood in their own fire for nothing, which is exactly backwards.
**A Blast you set off is not reduced by your armour** (core-rules §11, ruled today): you are inside
it rather than facing it. It is deliberately narrow — an ally caught in it keeps their coat and
nothing an area does to an **enemy** changes — so it does not become a free armour bypass
competing with the eight items that buy *ignores N armor* and pay for it.

What it costs, 500 fights a cell against five goblin spears, with the caster's coat varied and the
rule on:

| the caster's coat | win % | their own Blast, per fight |
|---|---|---|
| none | 66.0 | 0.76 |
| Leather (1) | 77.6 | 0.97 |
| Mail (2) | 88.0 | 1.30 |
| Brigandine (3) | 88.6 | 1.74 |
| Plate (4) | 95.6 | 2.17 |

Armour is still overwhelmingly worth wearing — which is the right outcome, and consistent with the
**+35.8** this project has measured for gear against **+4.0** for a deck. What changed is that the
coat no longer makes the Blast free: at Plate the caster now takes **2.17** points of their own fire
a fight where they took almost none.

On the chassis, over 3000 fights a cell, it is worth **3.3 points**: the Caster deck read **+4.7**
against the Warrior's with the coat applying and **+1.4** without, which puts the third chassis
level with the first instead of ahead of it.

> **And it is a price rather than a deterrent, which is worth admitting.** The AI did not become
> careful: self-blasts per fight went **up**, from 0.84 to 0.97, because a caster who survives
> longer casts more. Catching one more enemy is still worth more than the two points it costs, and
> §5's figures now say that louder than before — a caster tuned to *avoid* their own Blast loses
> **19.4 points** rather than the 8 it cost under the old reading. So the rule made the Blast cost
> what it should cost. It did not change where anybody aims it.

**What is still not priced is Aimed**, and it cannot be priced here. The reason to buy a
Discriminating Wand is your party, and this simulator has one hero in it. That is the next thing a
party model would answer, and it is the same gap §4b runs into from the other side.

## What this cannot say

**Nothing here is about allies.** A Blast hits them (core-rules §11), several formulas target
`alliesInRange`, Vax's two miracles are *adjacent allies* in their entirety, and the simulator is
one hero against goblins. Every number above is the solo reading, and for a Way whose miracles are
mostly written about other people that is a floor and not an estimate.

**`ignores_block` is recorded and never read**, because a goblin has armour and no Block. *Open the
Vein* and *Blood Price* both buy something here that cannot be charged for.

**An area is aimed at a creature, not at a square.** core-rules §11 says an area hits *the target
square and all squares within X of it*, and a player may aim at empty ground to catch two goblins
and spare themselves. The simulator picks among the goblins' own squares, which is a restriction on
the hero — so the wand is understated, which is the safe direction.

**Preparation is a greedy loadout.** Cheapest castable formula first, until the slots run out. With
at most four known formulas against five or six slots, nothing in the graph makes that a hard
choice yet, and when something does this is the line to revisit.

**And a level-1 caster has two slots and no formulas at all.** *Scholar* and *Sworn*, the tier-0
titles of the two magical Ways, grant `spellSlots: 2` and zero spells; the first school node is
tier 1 at level 5. So at the level the starter decks are for, **a caster's ✦ Cast halves have
exactly one source**, and the Caster chassis figure is a measurement of the Fire Wand as much as of
the deck. That is not a defect in the measurement. It is what the content says, and it is worth
knowing before anybody tunes the deck.
