# Simulation analysis – draft 0.3 (Warrior vs goblins)

3,000 simulated fights per scenario, run with `sim/combat_sim.py`.
Supersedes [analysis-2026-10-26.md](analysis-2026-10-26.md), whose numbers were invalidated by the
bug below. [analysis-2026-10-21.md](analysis-2026-10-21.md) recorded draft 0.1.

## What changed since the last run

**A simulator bug, and it mattered.** Routing was detected by comparing op lists — and the goblin
deck's *Cower* card (`Move 3 away`) has ops **identical** to a rout. Playing Cower was therefore
indistinguishable from breaking, so **healthy, full-Health goblins permanently left the battle
whenever Cower was drawn** and reached a map edge. Every previous run with Cowardly on was inflated.
Fixed: a Cower goblin retreats and comes back; only a routed one leaves for good. Flee counts fell
from 1.15 to 0.48 in the baseline and from 1.88 to 0.34 in the archer fight.

**The round structure changed** (core-rules §3):

| Change | Effect on the model |
|---|---|
| Group turns replaced by **individual initiative** | Every goblin draws its own slot, so goblins act scattered around the hero's turn instead of as a block |
| **Intent cards**: one card per NPC *type* per round, drawn at the start of the round | Unchanged in substance — the sim already drew one card for the whole type — but now the draw and the acting are separate steps |
| Initiative drawn per fight, **ties redrawn** | `first='initiative'` is now the default; `players`/`goblins` force the old block order for comparison |
| A condition ticks on its **creator's** turn, or the victim's if the creator is gone | Daze now remembers which goblin applied it |

Together these make fights **longer** (4 goblins: 6.5 → 8.0 rounds) because fewer enemies run away
and the hero no longer gets a clean turn ahead of the whole enemy side.

## Setup and assumptions

- **Warrior:** 12 HP, Leather Armor (armor 1), Longsword (Strike 3), Wooden Shield (Defend 2), 1 Healing Potion, Strong feature, 16-card starter deck, hand size 5.
- **Goblins:** 5 HP, armor 0, Goblin Spear or Goblin Shortbow (Strike 3), 6-card goblin deck, Pack Tactics + Cowardly.
- **Map:** open 10×8 grid, goblins start 7 squares away. A routed NPC escapes at the map edge once 5+ squares from the hero.
- **Solo hero:** no party, so Taunt, Ready and Foresee still do nothing.

## Findings

### 1. The encounter ladder is real now

With the flee bug fixed, the goblin numbers finally produce a difficulty curve instead of a flat
line of near-certain wins:

| Encounter | Win % | HP left |
|---|---|---|
| 3 spears | 99.3 | 10.1 |
| 4 spears | 92.6 | 8.6 |
| 5 spears | 77.6 | 7.4 |
| 4 goblins, 2 spear + 2 bow | 67.1 | 4.8 |

**Mixed spear-and-bow groups are twice as dangerous as their numbers suggest.** Four goblins with
two bows beat a solo starter Warrior a third of the time, while four spears beat it one time in
thirteen. The spears pin the hero in melee, the bows shoot from behind cover, and the hero spends
rounds crossing the map under fire. Dazed lands 2.08 times per fight here, against 0.65 with spears
alone, because *Dirty Trick* goblins get free hits while the hero chases archers.

**Design rule:** a mixed group is a tier harder than a uniform one of the same size. Build hard
encounters by mixing ranges, not by adding bodies.

### 2. Individual initiative made exhaustion a real cost

This was an accident of the turn-structure change and it's a good one.

| | Draft 0.2 (group turns) | Draft 0.3 (individual) |
|---|---|---|
| Rounds, 4 goblins | 6.5 | **8.0** |
| Deck runs dry | 64% of fights | **89%** |
| Catch Breath per fight | 0.51 | **0.75** |

The last two analyses both concluded "exhaustion hardly matters in small fights". That is no longer
true: the deck runs dry in roughly 9 of 10 four-goblin fights, and the hero stops to Catch Breath in
most of them. Longer fights did what a smaller deck was supposed to do.

**So don't shrink the starter deck.** Scenario S is the warning: a 12-card deck in the archer fight
wins **38.2%** against the 16-card deck's 67.1%, catching breath 1.62 times per fight. In the easy
fights it barely registers (R: 89.5 vs 92.6). A 12-card deck doesn't make exhaustion matter — it
makes hard fights unwinnable while leaving easy ones untouched, which is the wrong shape. Keep 16.

**When** to Catch Breath now matters slightly, where before it was noise: 91.4% waiting until
exhausted, 93.1% going early. Still small enough to leave to the player.

### 3. Persistent Block is worth more than I predicted, not less

I expected a standing block to be worth *less* under individual initiative, on the reasoning that it
would only cover the goblins acting after the hero's turn. That was wrong — it's worth more, and
most in the fight that matters:

| | One-shot block (draft 0.1) | Blocks every attack (current) | Gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 spears | 99.2% | 99.3% | noise |
| 4 spears | 91.9% | 92.6% | +0.7 |
| **2 spear + 2 bow** | **63.8%** | **67.1%** | **+3.3** |
| Damage prevented (archers) | 0.6 | **1.5** | 2.5× |

The mechanism: fights are longer and the hero is under fire from more directions, so a block that
persists through a round absorbs from several attackers instead of one. It scales with how
threatened you are, which is exactly what a defensive stance should do.

Shield Up is still a *modest* primary — 0.65 plays and 0.7 damage prevented in the 4-spear fight —
but in the hard fight it earns its slot. The earlier recommendation to buff shield Defend values is
**withdrawn**; the duration fix was sufficient. Revisit only if it stays weak in party play, where a
Defend primary protecting allies would be worth far more.

### 4. Cowardly: group morale is better, with one clear exception

Yesterday's recommendation was built on inflated flee numbers, so here it is again on clean data.

| 4 goblins, Strike 3 | Win % |
|---|---|
| No tactics (K) | 91.0 |
| **Pack Tactics only (L)** | **79.7** |
| Pack + Cowardly as written, per goblin (C) | 92.6 |
| Pack + group morale (V) | 88.5 |

The core problem survives the bug fix: **Cowardly as written still hands back more than Pack Tactics
takes.** Two tactics together (92.6%) make goblins *easier* than no tactics at all (91.0%). Group
morale fixes the relationship — 88.5% is harder than no tactics, as a tactic should be — and it
scales properly with numbers:

| | Per-goblin Cowardly | Group morale |
|---|---|---|
| 4 spears | 92.6 | **88.5** |
| 5 spears | 77.6 | **62.7** |
| 2 spear + 2 bow | **67.1** | 84.1 |

**The exception is ranged enemies, and it's instructive.** Group morale makes the archer fight much
*easier* (84.1% vs 67.1%), because the morale break triggers on group casualties while the archers
are still healthy — so the most dangerous enemies on the board walk away intact. Per-goblin Cowardly
keeps wounded-but-lethal archers shooting.

That points at a cleaner conclusion than "pick a morale rule":

> **Don't put Cowardly on ranged enemies at all.** A *Sniper* already retreats when something closes
> on it. Stacking a flee tactic on top of a kiting tactic is redundant, and it defuses the best fight
> in the set.

**Recommendation:** adopt group morale, and restrict Cowardly to melee NPC types.

> **Cowardly** — When half or more of its group is down, the survivors break and flee.

Even then it's a net discount (88.5 vs 79.7 for Pack alone), so price a Cowardly monster below its
stat line, or make the escape cost something: the ones that got away warn the next camp, carry off
the loot, or come back with friends.

### 5. Goblin Strike 3 was being masked, and now shows its true value

| | Strike 2 | Strike 3 |
|---|---|---|
| 3 goblins | 100.0% (F) | 99.3% (A) |
| 4 goblins | 99.7% (G) | **92.6%** (C) |

Worth 7.1 points at four goblins, against 2.8 in the buggy run. The raise was the right call and the
"weak enemies at least 2 above hero armor" rule holds.

**Armor matters more now too**, since there are more hits to absorb:

| Warrior armor | Win % (3 goblins) | Damage taken |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 95.5 | 4.9 |
| 1 | 99.3 | 2.8 |
| 2 | 99.9 | 1.8 |

One point of armor is worth 3.8 points of win rate against Strike 3. The corollary stands: don't let
hero armor climb to 2 while Strike 3 trash mobs are still the content.

### 6. Initiative position is noise

3 spears, warrior first 99.3% · goblins first 99.4% · drawn 99.3%. Identical, as in both previous
runs. Who wins initiative does not decide fights, so the draw is pure flavour and the GM can hand it
out freely. Worth knowing before building initiative automation: it needs to be *correct*, not
*fair*.

### 7. Smaller observations

- **Strong (+1 damage)** is worth 1.0 round and 1.2 points of win rate (A vs M).
- **Double strike** is worth 0.7 rounds (A vs N), and remains the best card in the deck. The
  leveling-reward decision stands.
- **The heavy-hits rule still never fires** — 0.16 discards per fight with four goblins. Keep it for
  big monsters; it costs nothing.
- **Dead card halves persist.** Taunt primary 0.01, Quick Hands primary 0.02, Weapon Swap primary
  0.05 — the Ready and Taunt halves are inert in a solo fight, as expected. This can only be
  resolved by party tests.
- **Brace** rose from an emergency valve to a staple: reactions prevent 6.0 damage per fight at four
  goblins and 7.8 against archers, against 3.7 in the baseline.

## Limitations

- **Solo fights only.** No party, so Taunt, Ready, Stabilize, Bodyguard, Commander and **Foresee**
  are all untested. Foresee especially: its whole value is informational, and a solo AI that already
  sees the board state can't model what hidden intent costs a real table.
- **Wounds and Taint aren't modelled.** The sim discards a card on a heavy hit rather than shuffling
  in a Wound that clogs the hand. Damage carried between fights is untested.
- **Nothing out of combat.** The skill rules, challenges and the twist deck have no simulator, and
  their numbers are still first guesses.
- **Empty room.** No obstacles, so cover comes only from figures, and Sniper and Shield Wall can't be
  tested.
- **The AI is decent, not optimal**, and it does not use Foresee or play around intent cards.

## How to rerun

```
python sim/combat_sim.py                # all scenarios
python sim/combat_sim.py --only ACDVX   # selected scenarios, by leading letter
python sim/combat_sim.py --log 1        # one narrated fight, with initiative and intent
```
