# Simulation analysis – draft 0.4 (Warrior vs goblins)

3,000 simulated fights per scenario, run with `sim/combat_sim.py`.
Earlier records: [analysis-2026-10-26.md](analysis-2026-10-26.md) (superseded, had a bug),
[analysis-2026-10-21.md](analysis-2026-10-21.md) (draft 0.1).

## What changed in the rules since the last run

| Change | Where |
|---|---|
| **Catch Breath recovers only half the discard pile**, at random, and 1 of the cards left behind is Spent | core-rules §8 |
| The Spent card is **random**, not chosen | core-rules §8 |
| **Carrying and encumbrance**: 40 kg limit, Burdened over it, **Overloaded** over half again — 2 random cards Spent | core-rules §4 |
| Bonus groups cap numeric stacking | core-rules §5 |
| Each NPC **type** has its own fate deck | core-rules §6 |

The Catch Breath change is the one that moves numbers, because it fires in nearly every fight.

## Findings

### 1. Harsh Catch Breath costs 7 points, and only in long fights

| | Old Catch Breath | Half-recovery |
|---|---|---|
| 4 spears | 92.6% | 92.9% |
| **2 spear + 2 bow** | 67.1% | **59.7%** |
| 5 spears | 77.6% | 76.5% |

Exactly the right shape. In fights the hero wins before the deck matters, nothing changes. In the
long fights — where Catch Breath fires 1.2 to 1.3 times per fight — it takes 7 points. Each recovery
returning less than the last is now the thing that loses hard fights.

### 2. Overloaded: 2 random cards is the right cost

Measured directly, with **random** removal (which is what the rule says):

| Cards Spent | Deck | 4 spears | 2 spear + 2 bow |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 16 | 92.9% | 59.7% |
| 1 | 15 | 92.9% | 57.2% |
| **2** | **14** | **90.7%** | **52.4%** |
| 3 | 13 | 88.6% | 47.2% |
| 4 | 12 | 84.6% | 40.7% |

Two cards costs **7.3 points** in a hard fight and **2.2** in a moderate one. One card is invisible
in the moderate fight; three starts taking the fight away. So two it is, and the difficulty-scaling
is the point: hauling loot through a quiet dungeon is nearly free, hauling it into a real fight is a
coin flip (52.4%).

### 3. Correction: the persistent-Block finding does not survive

The previous analysis reported persistent Block worth +3.3 points in the hard fight. That is **no
longer supported**:

| | One-shot block | Persistent |
|---|---|---|
| 4 spears | 92.3% | 92.9% |
| 2 spear + 2 bow | **61.7%** | 59.7% |

Persistent Block now measures slightly *worse* in the hard fight and slightly better in the moderate
one — i.e. noise, with no clear effect either way.

Part of this is the simulator's own fault: the AI values a standing block by how many enemies can
reach it, so it plays Shield Up as a primary more often under the persistent rule, giving up attacks.
That valuation was tuned when Catch Breath was generous; spending primaries on defence costs more now.

**The rule should stay** — a defensive stance holding for a turn is what players expect, and it is
coherent. But do not claim a measured benefit for it. Shield Up remains a marginal primary.

### 4. A model limitation worth recording

The simulator can remove deck cards two ways: the **weakest** (modelling a player pruning) or at
**random** (modelling Overloaded). At 3–4 cards the weakest-removal is *worse* than random:

| Cards removed | Weakest | Random |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 41.6% | 47.2% |
| 4 | 24.7% | 40.7% |

That is backwards, and the cause is the AI's static card values: the "weakest" cards include both
copies of **Advance** (Move 3), and in the archer fight mobility is what lets the hero reach the bows.
A real player pruning for that fight would keep them.

So **the weakest-removal numbers understate a pruned deck**, and the keep-values need a pass before
they can model deck-building decisions. The random-removal numbers, used for Overloaded above, are
unaffected.

### 5. Keep the 16-card deck, now emphatically

| | 16-card | 12-card |
|---|---|---|
| 4 spears | 92.9% | 85.5% |
| 2 spear + 2 bow | 59.7% | **24.7%** |

A 12-card deck catches breath **2.4 times per fight** and wins the hard fight one time in four. With
half-recovery the small deck is no longer a trade-off, it is a trap.

### 6. The encounter ladder, restated

| Encounter | Win % | HP left |
|---|---|---|
| 3 spears | 99.3 | 10.0 |
| 4 spears | 92.9 | 8.7 |
| 5 spears | 76.5 | 7.5 |
| 4 goblins, 2 spear + 2 bow | 59.7 | 4.8 |

Mixed groups remain a tier harder than uniform ones of the same size, and the gap widened: the
archer fight is now a near coin-flip for a solo starter Warrior, against 93% for four spears.

### 7. Cowardly: the earlier conclusions hold

| 4 goblins | Win % |
|---|---|
| No tactics (K) | 90.3 |
| **Pack only (L)** | **76.8** |
| Pack + Cowardly as written (C) | 92.9 |
| Pack + group morale (V) | 88.8 |

Unchanged in direction: Cowardly as written still makes goblins *easier* than having no tactics at
all, and group morale fixes the relationship. Group morale still makes the archer fight much easier
(82.3% against 59.7%) by letting healthy archers walk away, so the recommendation stands — **adopt
group morale, and keep Cowardly off ranged types.**

### 8. Smaller observations

- **Initiative position is still noise.** 99.3 / 99.4 / 99.3 across warrior-first, goblins-first and
  drawn. Four analyses in a row now.
- **Strong** is worth 1.1 rounds and 0.9 points; **double strike** 1.4 rounds and 0.4 points.
- **Armor** matters more than ever: armor 0 is 95.9%, armor 1 is 99.3%, armor 2 is 100.0% against
  three spears.
- **The heavy-hits rule still never fires.**
- **Catch Breath timing still barely matters** (92.4 waiting, 93.0 early) — but see the limitation
  below; the sim cannot really test this any more.

## Limitations

- **The sim can't test Catch Breath timing properly.** Its AI only breathes when the draw pile is
  empty, so it always breathes with a full discard. The new rule's incentive — that breathing early
  recovers half of *little* — is arithmetic, not a measured result.
- **Keep-values need recalibrating** before weakest-removal models anything (finding 4), and the
  AI's valuation of a standing block is tuned for the old Catch Breath (finding 3).
- **Solo fights only.** No party, so Taunt, Ready, Stabilize, Bodyguard, Commander and Foresee are
  untested — Foresee especially, since its value is informational and the AI already sees the board.
- **No carrying in the model.** Overloaded is simulated as a smaller deck, which is its mechanical
  effect, but nothing tests the decision to pick loot up.
- **Wounds and Taint aren't modelled**, and nothing out of combat is: the skill rules, challenges and
  the twist deck have no simulator.
- **Empty room.** No obstacles, so cover comes only from figures.

## How to rerun

```
python sim/combat_sim.py                # all scenarios
python sim/combat_sim.py --only ACDYZ   # selected scenarios, by leading letter
python sim/combat_sim.py --log 1        # one narrated fight
```
