# Simulation analysis – draft 0.4 (Warrior vs goblins)

> Re-run after fixing the AI's card valuation (§0). Every number below is post-fix.

3,000 simulated fights per scenario, run with `sim/combat_sim.py`.
Earlier records: [analysis-2026-10-26.md](analysis-2026-10-26.md) (superseded, had a bug),
[analysis-2026-10-21.md](analysis-2026-10-21.md) (draft 0.1).

## What changed in the rules since the last run

| Change | Where |
|---|---|
| **Catch Breath recovers only half the discard pile**, at random, and 1 of the cards left behind is Spent | core-rules §8 |
| The Spent card is **random**, not chosen | core-rules §8 |
| **Carrying and encumbrance**: 40 kg limit, Burdened over it, **Overloaded** over half again — 2 random cards Spent | core-rules §4 |
| Bonus groups cap numeric stacking | core-rules §5 |
| Each NPC **type** has its own fate deck | core-rules §6 |

The Catch Breath change is the one rules change that moves numbers, because it fires in nearly every
fight. But the **simulator fix** below moved more.

## 0. The AI fix, and why it invalidated the previous run

Card worth was a **static number per card** — `Advance: 2.5`, `Heavy Swing: 5.0` — so a card's value
never changed with the board. That one number drove three decisions: which cards to keep in hand,
which to burn on a reaction, and which to prune.

It was wrong in any fight that was not a melee brawl. Worth is now computed from a card's ops against
the current board: adjacent enemies, distance to the nearest, whether any of them kite, whether you
are hurt.

| Card | 3 adjacent spears | 2 archers at range |
|---|---|---|
| **Advance** | 0.15 (last) | **6.15 (first)** |
| Strike | 4.90 | 2.20 |
| Shield Up | 6.00 | 4.40 |

**The hard fight gained 4.9 points from this alone** — 59.7% to 64.6%, with no rules change. The old
model was losing that to its own bad play: it treated the Move-3 cards as junk and then failed to
reach the archers. Pruning follows the same valuation, averaged over three moments of a fight
(closing, engaged, pressed) so that situational cards are judged for the moment they are *for*.

Two residual limitations, both of the same kind — the heuristic rates cards **independently** and
cannot see set dependencies:

- It pruned both *Quick Hands* and *Second Wind*, the only two cards that can drink a potion, leaving
  the potion as dead weight. There is now an explicit guard against cutting the last Use card.
- Others may lurk. Treat any pruning result as suspect until the pruned list is eyeballed.

## Findings

### 1. Harsh Catch Breath costs 7 points, and only in long fights

| | Old Catch Breath | Half-recovery |
|---|---|---|
| 4 spears | 92.6% | 93.2% |
| **2 spear + 2 bow** | ~72% | **64.6%** |
| 5 spears | 77.6% | 75.9% |

(The hard-fight "old" figure is adjusted for the AI fix, which is worth about +5 on its own.)

Exactly the right shape. In fights the hero wins before the deck matters, nothing changes. In the
long fights — where Catch Breath now fires 1.2 to 1.3 times per fight — it takes about 7 points.
Each recovery returning less than the last is what loses hard fights.

### 2. Overloaded: 2 random cards confirmed

| Cards Spent | Deck | 4 spears | 2 spear + 2 bow |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 16 | 93.2% | 64.6% |
| 1 | 15 | 91.7% | 61.0% |
| **2** | **14** | **90.7%** | **57.1%** |
| 3 | 13 | 88.2% | 51.5% |
| 4 | 12 | 85.3% | 45.0% |

Two random cards costs **7.5 points** in a hard fight and **2.5** in a moderate one — within a
rounding error of the pre-fix measurement, so the decision stands. One card is nearly invisible;
three starts taking the fight away. The difficulty-scaling is the point: hauling loot through a quiet
dungeon is cheap, hauling it into a real fight is a coin flip.

### 3. Correction: the 12-card deck is not a trap — deck *fit* beats deck *size*

The previous analysis concluded that a small deck "doesn't make exhaustion matter, it makes hard
fights unwinnable (25% vs 60%)". **That was an artifact of the broken valuation**, which pruned both
copies of *Advance* and then couldn't reach the archers.

Measured properly:

| 12-card deck, hard fight | Win % |
|---|---|
| Pruned **well** for the fight | **63.1%** |
| Pruned at **random** | 45.0% |
| Full 16-card deck | 64.6% |

A well-pruned 12-card deck is level with a 16-card one. A badly pruned one collapses. So the real
trade is **fit, not size** — and in the melee fight pruning still costs (85.5% against 93.2%),
because there is less to cut that is genuinely dead.

**The starter deck should still be 16**, but for a different reason than I gave: a new player cannot
prune well, and pruning for the wrong fight is punishing. Deck size is training wheels, not a
balance lever.

### 4. The persistent-Block claim is still not worth making

| | One-shot block | Persistent |
|---|---|---|
| 3 spears | 99.1% | 99.4% |
| 4 spears | 91.7% | **93.2%** |
| 2 spear + 2 bow | 64.7% | 64.6% |

Better than the previous run, where the sign was against it: now +1.5 points in the moderate fight
and nil in the hard one. **Keep the rule** — a stance that holds for a turn is coherent and what
players expect — but claim at most a point, not the +3.3 the earlier analysis reported.

### 5. The encounter ladder

| Encounter | Win % | HP left |
|---|---|---|
| 3 spears | 99.4 | 9.9 |
| 4 spears | 93.2 | 8.5 |
| 5 spears | 75.9 | 7.3 |
| 4 goblins, 2 spear + 2 bow | 64.6 | 4.8 |

Mixed groups remain a tier harder than uniform ones of the same size: four goblins with two bows
beat the hero a third of the time, four with spears one time in fifteen.

### 6. Cowardly: conclusions unchanged across four runs

| 4 goblins | Win % |
|---|---|
| No tactics (K) | 91.3 |
| **Pack only (L)** | **77.4** |
| Pack + Cowardly as written (C) | 93.2 |
| Pack + group morale (V) | 88.0 |

Cowardly as written still makes goblins *easier* than having no tactics at all; group morale fixes
the relationship. Group morale still defuses the archer fight (81.0% against 64.6%) by letting
healthy archers walk away, so the recommendation stands — **adopt group morale, keep Cowardly off
ranged types.**

### 7. Smaller observations

- **Initiative position is noise**, for the fourth run in a row: 99.4 / 99.1 / 99.4.
- **Strong** is worth 1.7 points and 1.1 rounds; **double strike** 0.2 points and 0.6 rounds. The
  double-strike edge keeps shrinking as the AI plays better — it is no longer the standout card.
- **Armor**: 95.6% at armor 0, 99.4% at 1, 100.0% at 2 against three spears.
- **The heavy-hits rule still never fires.**
- **Catch Breath timing is still noise** (92.4 waiting, 93.2 early), and the sim can't really test it
  — see Limitations.

## Limitations

- **The sim can't test Catch Breath timing properly.** Its AI only breathes when the draw pile is
  empty, so it always breathes with a full discard. The new rule's incentive — that breathing early
  recovers half of *little* — is arithmetic, not a measured result.
- **The card valuation rates cards independently** and cannot see set dependencies (§0). The potion
  case is guarded; others may lurk, so eyeball the pruned list before trusting a pruning result.
- **Solo fights only.** No party, so Taunt, Ready, Stabilize, Bodyguard, Commander and Foresee are
  untested — Foresee especially, since its value is informational and the AI already sees the board.
- **No carrying in the model.** Overloaded is simulated as a smaller deck, which is its mechanical
  effect, but nothing tests the decision to pick loot up.
- **Wounds and Taint aren't modelled**, and nothing out of combat is: the skill rules, challenges and
  the twist deck have no simulator.
- **Empty room.** No obstacles, so cover comes only from figures.
