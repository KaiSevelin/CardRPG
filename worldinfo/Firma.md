# History

Humans slowly took more and more of the continent of Firma. The elves left for the Second Side, a
magical parallel world, while the demons went to the Third Side and made it their own. Monsters and
nature spirits were banished to remote places, and trolls, minor gods and dragons began their long
sleep. The old reckoning begins at 0, when the warrior-king Athor's empire reached the coast in every
direction. After 100 years the empire cracked, and the various pretenders and other powerful figures
with armies divided the enormous realm into small states and independent cities. The following 500
years were shaped by constant wars, large and small, over where the borders ran. Isolated regions grew
more and more unlike one another. The assassin state in the oasis of the Hyena Desert was founded, the
blood mages were established on the island of Hyrlock, and the Strigoi princes organised themselves to
increase their undead power. The new reckoning begins in year 674, the new year 0.

Strange airships from outside appeared in the sky in year 0, and the invasion began. Nobody knows
whether the creatures came from another world or from an unknown land across the sea. The Horde was
insect-like, but with a shared intelligence and, like ants, specialised into different roles.
Everyone knew what everyone was doing and they worked together, while humans were divided and
isolated. A thousand-year war began, and at an unhurried pace city after city fell to the Horde —
even though humans won several battles, the direction of the war was plain. The Strigoi princes made
a pact with the Horde and kept their neutrality. In year 87, when things looked darkest, a pact was
made with the blood mages of Hyrlock. Six great territories, **the districts**, were protected by a
ritual powered by thousands of human sacrifices. Isolated enclaves were given stationed blood mages
who each year reinforced smaller protective barriers with sacrifices. The Horde had taken over the
world, but isolated parts were kept safe. The war turned into a war of attrition: the Horde attacked
communication between the enclaves, the caravans and armies that left the protective barriers to
reach other enclaves and districts, while the humans waged guerrilla war against the **hives**, the
nests the Horde established on the continent.

Tunnels began to be built so that other enclaves and districts could be reached. Over the years the
network grew and parts of it fell into obscurity. Monsters found safety in the tunnels and occupied
sections, landslides let the Horde into others, while human dissidents formed independent communities
elsewhere. Ancient underground elven ruins were struck here and there.

The annual human sacrifices were not popular, and in 879 an enraged mob beat the blood mages of the
Fifth District to death. A month later the Horde broke in and the district fell.

The year is 1034. The Horde has continued to develop, and rumour says it is close to being able to
penetrate the districts. Humanity is still divided; enclave stands against enclave and district
against district, and internal schisms over religion, power and politics are under way everywhere.

# Districts

## The First District

The First District lies inland on the fertile moist plain encircled by the **Spear Mountains**. The district
protects Geistburg, the ancient capital of Athor's empire, five satellite cities and the land
between them. After the empire's collapse the realm went by the name Denaria. Geistburg is a melting
pot with complicated politics and a long lineage tied to a dozen corrupt noble houses beneath a
powerless ruler who is still titled emperor. The centre of the mages' academy and the cathedral of the Church
of the Eternal God lies on the plain and can be regarded as small towns on their own, centered around magic and religion. Hypocrisy and religious persecution go hand in hand. The capital is built on a huge mesa, the rich and noble live inside Geistburg, the rest of the mesa is covered by slums, and ordinary people occupy the satellite cities. Cable
cars connect the mesa with the underlying plain.

## The Second District

The Second District is the second largest and protects the westernmost of the three large fiords in the north coast, where the sea cuts deep inland and a few islands lie close enough in to be part of the same district; six enormous lighthouses mark where the borders run. The capital Black Harbour
shares the region with a few fishing villages and a fortified northern town. The area was previously the seat
of power for an association of fishing barons, smugglers and pirates known as the Salt Barons. The
old faith in The Five is strong here, and the Church of the Eternal God is seen as blasphemy. The
district is the largest producer of fish, and a broad tunnel, the **West Road**, with a
couple of underground cities, ties the district to the First District.

## The Third District

The rolling hills of the south-east form the Third District. Historically a region of pleasant
temperature where the rich built their winter palaces, known for its wines and fruit orchards. The
coast supplies the district with fish and its farmland with meat, vegetables and grain, but wine is
the best-known export. The **South Road**  ties the district to the First. The
underground city of Sol is a retreat for the rich of the First and Third District who want to enjoy themselves;
they say everything is permitted there. Religion and politics are fairly relaxed in the Third
District, which is governed by a council voted in by the various districts. Three smaller cities lie
along the coast, and although none of them dominates, the council sits in the city at the centre,
Tanto.

## The Fourth District

The Fourth District covers a roughly square northern half of the Hyena Desert and the mineral-rich
Mouse Mountains north of it. The city of Sammel in the north oasis is the only large city,
and nearly the size of Geistburg. Villages and a few mining camps are scattered through the Mouse
Mountains, but the rest of the Hyena Desert is desolate apart from a couple of caravan camps and a holy, sparsely populated town at the western oasis where there is water. The Grey and his siblings are worshipped in the district, which is ruled by emirs who
have divided Sammel into separate, isolated **Rote**. The former assassin state now rules the central
Rote around the region's tallest building, the Ziggurat. Embassies that offer services under the
table can be found in every district except the Sixth, and in a couple of enclaves. The city is linked
to the First District by the **East Road**, a less safe tunnel system, since the whole
region's mine workings give rise to countless break-ins by monsters and the Horde. A freight and
passenger train, the **Night Express**, runs regularly through the tunnels, which
are sooted by the coal that drives the engine.

## The Fifth District

The north-eastern Fifth District was linked to the First by the **North-East Road**, to the Fourth
by a rail line, the Mine Line, through the **Mine Road**, and to the Sixth by the
**River Road**, an underground river route which could handle timber vessels. The tunnels have been unsafe for the
past hundred years and the train no longer runs. The Fifth District was the centre of smithing and
manufacture, and had an alchemical academy and a centre of magical research, the Rune Temple, in the
labyrinthine city of Weder, once the third largest city on the continent. Technology and magic
mattered more than religion here, and it was the Fifth District that built the trains of the Fourth,
the sea defences off the Second and the cable cars between the heights of the First. The once moist pine forests of the Fifth district turned in to a barren bog land after the Horde's invasion and the city of Weder is but ruins. Refugees from the
catastrophe of 879 have formed engineering guilds in every district and have kept their language,
Valgar, and their lack of religion — which causes trouble in the First District now and then.

## The Sixth District

The Sixth District is the most easterly and covers the second largest area of the districts. It is a
region of coastal wilderness that was never entirely civilised. Dragons turn up from time to time, and
trolls are found in both forests and mountains. Dense, damp conifer forest covers most of the
territory, with lakes, bogs and mountains here and there. The largest export was timber, before the
Fifth District collapsed and the export routes were cut. Two smaller coastal towns, a few villages and an inland
capital, Karmat, which is now in decline since the forest barons' export income was cut off.
Mercenaries have become the only export, and a squadron of hardened soldiers makes its way each year
through the wilderness and the Horde to the nearest district, the Fourth.

The forest barons' grip on the district is broken but remains a force, and every town and village is
independent, with regular bloody internal struggles over the mayor's seat. Religiously the district is
a motley mosaic; the war god Vax has the most followers, but tolerance and freedom of religion are
taken for granted.

# Enclaves

Some twenty isolated enclaves hold out over smaller territories entirely surrounded by the Horde.
These are the most important:

## Hyrlock

The island where the feared — or heroic, depending on who you ask — blood mages live. Hyrlock is a
largish island a week's sailing from the abandoned port towns west of the First District. The enclave
of Kelp Bay is the only one left on that coast and protected from the Horde, and on
rare occasions a ship makes it across from Hyrlock; otherwise the island is isolated. Hyrlock is a
divided society centred on the city of Mander Cliff. The island has a caste system
with the mage caste at the top, followed by administrators and merchants. The religion followed is
that of the blood goddess Parfebe. The worker caste has it hard, but better at least than the thralls,
who are bred to be used in blood sacrifice. The Red Tower stands fifty metres high at the top of
Mander Cliff, and all power in the enclave radiates from the blood mages within it. The other
districts and enclaves pay an annual tax in goods and thralls to Hyrlock to maintain the protective
enchantments — commonly called the **blood tax**.

It is the only source of income, but it is a large one, and it forces monthly, dangerous boat freight
from Kelp Bay to Hyrlock.

## Kelp Bay

Kelp Bay was an insignificant port town before year 0, but now that it is the only surviving port on
the west coast its importance has grown. As the sole way in to Hyrlock and the important blood tax, it
has drawn adventurers willing to take the risk of signing onto a ship, for the rich payment that
brings if you survive. Three large shipping companies rule the town and run the voyages in an unstable
cartel. The nearness of death and wealth has marked the town with high crime and low morals. Fog rolls
constantly through the cobbled alleys with their blue lanterns for bars, red for brothels and green
for shady business. The smell of rotting bodies, spilt beer and cheap perfume works its way now and
then through the all-pervading stench of fermenting kelp.

## Solemn

The white limestone island south-east of the Third District is the centre of the fabled witches, a
sisterhood with a magic of their own that they keep to themselves. Religiously they follow the
Primal Mother, a fertility goddess. Solemn is one of the few enclaves that does not rely on blood
magic for protection; the witches have their own protective enchantment. How it works is unknown, but
it has been speculated that the island's thin borders to the Second and Third Sides may have something
to do with it. A walled city at the top of the mountainous island, full of towers and spires, Sonebra,
is the enclave's centre.

## Regensburg

The capital of the militaristic western realm situated in a valley of the Spear Mountains in the north-east of the First District is the most important
inland enclave. Regensburg has preserved its military background and could certainly survive a couple
of years without enchantments. The three concentric city walls are ten metres high and divide the city
into three rings. Within each ring are further walls dividing the rings into sections, all to resist
invasion as long as possible. The city has a military government with a strict hierarchy and a Marshal
of the Realm at the top. Companies of its cavalry are hired out to other districts in exchange for
deliveries of the supplies the enclave depends on. The enclave is linked to the First District by an
unsafe tunnel through cave-riddled limestone, the **Cracked Road**. A large
underground lake midway along it has given rise to two sketchy harbour villages named From and To.

## Wolf Peak

A religious centre of those who profess the faith of the Fire, at the top of a lone dormant volcano
north of the Sixth District, has held out undefeated against the Horde. Wolf Peak has
roads to the summit that are easy to defend, and its ingenious use of the surfaces outside and inside
the mountain, together with careful husbandry of resources, makes it self-sufficient. The enclave's
advanced technology in gunpowder and firearms helps. The materials for powder are inside the mountain:
never-failing guano fields from bats and large sulphur deposits from the volcanism, while charcoal is
harvested from fast-growing trees that thrive in the fertile volcanic soil. The priesthood of
Wolf Peak's temple and monastery have a language of their own, Perga, and a unique style of dress, and are
always covered in tattoos. They are rarely met outside the enclave.

## Echterhof

Echterhof is ruled by Strigoi princes and is a neutral large city in the middle of Horde territory. The farm land around the city has been left untouched by the horde.
Strigoi live off humans, so there is a large population of humans in the city caught in an unpleasant
bind between the Strigoi and the Horde. To keep its citizens in order a human mayor has been appointed
and a treaty signed. The mayor selects food for the vampires, usually criminals, but when they run
short, lots, internal enmity or caprice have chosen the victims. The city welcomes guests — the more
people, the less risk that you are the one chosen. The nobility of the city are exempt from sacrifice
unless they have broken the laws. A complicated law, the **Guest Law**, both ensures that guests are
not killed and that they are killed if they break any of its many clauses. The Guest Law also contains
incentives to draw people to the city, such as grants of land and the chance of a noble title in time.
The Strigoi worship the First Vampire.

## Velderon

Velderon is the elves' embassy on Firma. It is an ancient ruin from the elves' time, a small part of
which has been restored to its former glory. The elves are neutral and have removed themselves to the
Second Side; their own magic protects them from the Horde here, and the embassy's main purpose is to
watch the Horde on the ground and make sure it does not begin to threaten the Second Side. Velderon is
a closed place that you do not enter without invitation, located on the coast east of the Second
District.

## Saag et Haar

Saag et Haar is the former centre of the realm of Seetch, south of the Hyena Desert. Seetch was a
realm ruled by necromancer kings, and the enclave of Saag et Haar is still ruled by the greatest
necromancer king, Fahlas, who has lived 400 years and become a minor god. These days the city depends
on blood mages for protection, but raising the dead means the number of blood sacrifices needed is
lower than elsewhere. The city's form of government is tyrannical and its religion is entirely devoted
to the death goddess Aecht. Their own language is Veche. There is a trade route from the city to
Sammel that runs via the Third Side: portals in Sammel and Saag et Haar lead to a road on the Third
Side that the demons allow the necromancers to use in exchange for human sacrifices. Saag et Haar buys
slaves in Sammel and pays with silver, gold and gemstones from the mines outside the city.

## The Triple Enclave

The Triple Enclave is three frontier enclaves north of the Fourth District on the western slopes of the Spear Mountains. The
towns of Farsight, Highfall and Marke are walled small towns, each around a central castle ruled by
a duke. They all lie in mountainous country. All three have enchantments from a shared group of blood mages. The enclaves have a common
safe tunnel connecting them, the **Steel Road**.

The towns were important outposts for Denaria before year 0, and they still recognise the emperor in
Denaria as their ruler, even if exchange between them is sporadic these days. In Farsight the Church
of the Eternal God holds total power and the duchess also carries the title of cardinal; religion
stands behind every law. Marke has the high bogs of its valley as the source of its export, **dreamwort**, a
flower whose seeds are dried into a powerful drug. The duke of the town is powerless
and real power lies with the **Dream Guild**, a criminal organisation with branches in every district.
They are even said to have a treaty with the Horde for safe passage for their caravans. Highfall tries
to operate as in the old days and keep morale high and its citizens safe; to manage it they help the
Dream Guild with troops and receive money and blood-sacrifice slaves from the Fourth District in
return. The town is set into a mountain with a waterfall that falls over its walls.

## Desert Stream
Desert Stream is the third oasis in the Hyena Desert, outside the Fourth District's protective ward, but with a small ward of its own just covering the oasis. People here have gone weird from the isolation and have a bad reputation, but the oasis provides food for everyone.

# Hives

The hives mostly look like gigantic anthills built of earth and organic material. Inside they hold
winding tunnels and chambers for larvae and for the various Horde roles such as workers and soldiers.
Twelve hives are known.

## Xtchaargh

Xtchaargh is the only hive that receives human envoys for negotiation. It is said that this is where a
group from the Fifth District went shortly before the blood mages were killed, and that the Horde was
behind it. The hive has a human quarter with ordinary houses and squares in the middle of the mound's
chaotic tunnels and chambers, the **Summer Village**, where human subjects of the Horde live and train
spies to destabilise enclaves and districts.

## Chguuth

Chguuth is the main hive, located in the south-east of Firma. It is a hive that reaches twice the
height of the others and is reinforced with chitin from dead critters. The enormous flying Skaah that
were mistaken for airships during the invasion all set out from here, and the Queen has her seat here.

## The Underworld
The underworld, tunnels and caves are not unoccupied. Beings such as demons, minor gods and powerful monsters pushed from the overground has set up strongholds here. The minor goddess, the queen of the dark as well as the Troll King has always been here and do not like intruders. Other parts have become infested with the Horde.

# Religion

## The Church of the Eternal God

"The Eternal God is the only true god, and the emperor his only true representative on Firma."

### Tenets

The Church of the Eternal God has three tenets:

* Purity of soul and body. Avoid emotion and poisons, and keep body and mind trained. All other gods
  are impure.
* Obedience. What the god, the emperor and his mouthpieces — high priests, priests and deans — say is
  the law. To question is to blaspheme.
* Solidarity. Help your brothers and sisters in faith, and ask no coin for your help.

### Miracles

Ascetic

### Symbolism

The symbols of the religion are chiefly a white circle enclosing a black one. An asterisk is common in
older religious writings.

### Saints

The chief saint is Athor, who spread the religion across all of Firma. Lesser saints such as the
dragonslayer Vesper, the healer Selma and the martyr Bullius are worshipped alongside local saints and
every emperor in history.

### Groupings

* **The Black Circle** makes sure the flock follows the faith, and is a religious secret police.
* **The Holy Shield** is an order of knights and the faith's military spearhead.
* **The White Brothers** is the largest order of monks in the church.
* **The Black Sisters** is the largest order of nuns.
* **The Flagellants** are the most committed and extreme group. Often violent.

## The Church of the Eternal Spirit

A sister church to the Church of the Eternal God, which holds that the Eternal God's spirit drifts
through all the air on Firma. The two churches are rivals, but accept one another tolerably, even if
accusations of blasphemy surface now and then.

### Tenets

The same as the Church of the Eternal God.

### Miracles

Air

### Symbolism

The symbols of the religion are chiefly a black circle enclosing a white one. A triangle is common in
older religious writings.

### Saints

Has the same saints as the Church of the Eternal God, but also worships the founder Vegnar, Athor's
brother, who founded the church.

### Groupings

* **God's Spirit** makes sure the flock follows the faith, and is a religious secret police.
* **The Holy Sword** is an order of knights and the faith's military spearhead.
* **The Devoted** is an order of monks and nuns.
* **The Unseen** is an order of assassins that walks the church's path.

## The Five

The Five were the largest religion before Athor took over the land. Historical writings and
inscriptions show that the religion was known in isolated areas all along the coasts of Firma.

### Gods

**Sessyfyn** (winged fish) is goddess of fishing and seafaring. She is said to be wise to the edge of
madness.

**Valtor** (two-pointed spear) is her brother and husband, and god of strength and battle. His beard
grew a metre a day.

**Fagar** (mask of fish scales) is the trickster god of the Five, child of Valtor and Sessyfyn, who
gets the elves' help to trick them both into sleeping with him so that he can father gods of his own.

**Vulpet** (shark) is the daughter of Fagar and Sessyfyn, and was so ugly that she was driven out by
everyone and became ruler of the land of the dead.

**Brendan** (conch horn) is the son of Fagar and Valtor, and symbolises the beauty that can come out of
barren ground.

### Tenets

* Religious inspiration matters more than any law. Follow your soul and use the gods' power through
  sacrifice to reach your goal.
* Selfishness is holy. Each is alone and must look after themselves.
* The gods are not infallible. Anyone who claims otherwise blasphemes.

### Groupings

* **Mummers** are religious groups dressed in animal masks who walk about, sing and encourage drinking.
* **Berserkers** fill themselves with religious inspiration through drums and dance before battle.

### Miracles

Sessyfyn: Water
Valtor: Strength
Fagar: Glamour
Vulpet: Necromancy
Brendan: Earth

## The Grey

The Grey and his siblings have their origin in the assassin state in the Hyena Desert and its
obsession with murder.

### Gods

**The Grey** (Möbius ring) is the one who embodies assassination. In the tales he or she is cunning and
treacherous before the killings happen. A couple of assassinated emperors are attributed to the Grey.

**The Purple** (alembic) is the Grey's sibling, and the one who excels at brewing poisons. Like the
Grey, sex and appearance are never specified.

**The Yellow** (yellow smoke) is the other sibling, who stands for lies, deceit and betrayal.

### Tenets

The Grey and his siblings have no tenets. Their knowledge is, however, held in three holy works:

* **The Grey Book**, on avoiding discovery and killing silently.
* **The Purple Book**, on potent poisons and their recipes.
* **The Yellow Book**, on misinformation and techniques of disguise.

### Groupings

* **The Grey** are the traditional assassin sect.
* **The Purple** are the poison experts.
* **The Yellow** are spies and spreaders of rumour.

### Miracles

The Grey: Shadow
The Purple: Poison
The Yellow: Glamour

## Minor gods

Unlike the other gods, the minor gods demonstrably exist. The minor gods are a hundred or so powerful
nature spirits and beings who were forced into isolated places and underground by Athor's conquest of
Firma. Minor gods grant no miracles, but they are the basis of the Spiritualism school.

### Gods

Some of the known gods are:

* The Winter Lady
* Queen of the Dark
* The Troll King
* Baal Fagor
* The Land Jelly
* The Cat God
* Fahlas
* Mother Longhand

### Groupings

Esoteric societies and cults tend to follow one of the minor gods in order to gain power.

* **The Silent** are a cult that follows the Winter Lady.
* Essentially all trolls follow the Troll King.
* **The Soot Club** is an esoteric society that tries to summon Baal Fagor.
* **The Dancers** are a cult that uses drugs to communicate with the Land Jelly.

# Specializations — Way of Mind

## Ritual magic

Ritual magic takes time and is not something used directly in a fight. All ritual formulae follow a
recipe, and one particular ritual may have many different recipes that give the same result. Simple
rituals have simple procedures that rarely fail; complicated rituals can cause great trouble if you do
something wrong in one of the ritual's steps. Anyone who can read can perform a ritual. All summoning
of demons and the like is done through rituals.

## Direct magic

Direct magic can be used in a fight and is divided into different schools. **It always costs something
to learn a formula**, and the cost depends on the school the formula belongs to and on its strength.
**Formulae come in strengths from 1 to 5.** Priests belong to a school according to their religion, but
are more limited in the number of formulae. Priests always pay with vows to their god, or by carrying
out a task such as a pilgrimage, whatever their school.

### Blood magic

Blood magic uses blood to gain power. Learning a formula means taking damage equal to the formula's
strength. Strength 4 and 5 formulae make that damage permanent. Many of its formulae also require you
to harm yourself or someone else in order to cast them.

### Necromancy

Necromancy concerns magic to do with the dead or undead. Every formula learned requires you to forget
something else.

### Elemental magic

Elemental magic is specialised in the five different elements:

* Air
* Water
* Earth
* Fire
* Ether (that which fills space and flows through the three Sides)

Learning elemental formulae costs money and materials.

### Poison

Poison brewers make poisons and have a few formulae of their own as well. Preparing poison formulae
requires time and dedication, and setting aside as many skill cards as the formula's strength.

### Glamour

Glamour is the main part of the elves' magic. It deals with charming and misleading an observer's
senses. Glamour magic is learned from elves, and they do not teach it for free.

### Strength

Strength magic is about tattoos that give yourself strength. In most cultures it counts as cheating in
a fight. Every formula learned gives the GM a secret to hold against the player in social conflicts.

### Asceticism

Ascetics sacrifice body parts to learn magic. The little toes usually go first. Ascetic magic is
chiefly oriented towards being able to endure things.

### Shadow

Shadow mages draw their power from the contrast between light and dark. They gain a permanent Wild
card in the deck for every formula learned. Shadow mages can use shadows for instantaneous travel,
among other things.

### Spiritualism

Spiritualists, such as the witches of Solemn, make a pact with the minor gods for every formula
learned. The formulae vary depending on which minor god is negotiated with. Shape-changing is common.

### Household magic

Household magic is a collection of formulae that affect local households. Typical formulae are making a
cow's milk dry up, or making land unusable or give a double harvest. Household magic is connected to
the beings of the elf group that once lived among humans and used magic as reward and punishment.
Learning formulae means negotiating with these beings.

## Priest magic

Priests have a limited formula list based on their school. Each individual religion also has a couple
of unique powers.

### Smiths

### Orators

# The Sides

The Sides are parallel worlds that exist across the same surfaces. To begin with they were rather alike,
but human influence on the First Side, elven influence on the Second and demonic influence on the Third
have made them grow apart.

## The First Side

The First Side is Firma, the normal world.

## The Second Side

The Second Side lies parallel over the First. The mountains and lakes that exist on the First Side also
exist on the Second. Vegetation and buildings are different, and because the elves went there, large
parts of the world are smeared with glamour that makes everything look more beautiful.

## The Third Side

The demons took over the Third Side when Athor's armies defeated them. Demons like to destroy things, so
the Third Side is in considerably worse condition than the others. The demons have dug up lava flows in
the ground and built jagged black mountains in other places, so the geography varies more than between
the First and Second.

## The Fourth Side

The Fourth Side is the land of the dead, where spirits go when someone dies. The notes that exist about
this Side are so unclear that it is impossible to say what it looks like. Some describe it as a
paradise, others as a hell or a shadow world. Only the dead are let in.

## The Fifth Side

It has long been rumoured that there is a Fifth Side, and perhaps a Sixth and Seventh. So far nobody has
been able to prove they exist.

# Monsters

## The elf group

### Elves

Elves are the ones who ruled Firma before humans began to spread out. Elves kept company with the little
folk and nature spirits, and a few of them became minor gods themselves. The generic elf is beautiful
(glamour), proud and considers itself better than humans, but like humanity the elven kind holds every
sort of personality. Elven magic is innate and based on glamour.

Elves are rarely outright evil, but they like to mess with humans when they get the chance, and are a
little gleeful that the Horde has pushed humanity out of the top predator spot — while at the same time
being afraid of an invasion by the Horde onto the Second Side.

### Tomtar (house spirits)

Tomtar are created by nature spirits. Once they attached themselves to humans; these days most follow
the elves on the Second Side. Tomtar are well-meaning, with a very particular interpretation of logic.
They practise household magic.

### Goblins

The elves' sometimes malicious slaves.

### Little folk

The little folk are cousins to the elves, and small. They live under stones, among the roots of great
oaks, or in stands of bracken. Many dabble in household magic.

### Nature spirits

A nature spirit is the protector of something in nature — it may be a pond, a tree, a mountain or a
waterfall. What they protect determines the form they can take when not in spirit form. They cause
little trouble as long as what they protect is preserved; when it is destroyed or threatened with
destruction, they take revenge. Many nature spirits remain on Firma to take revenge or protect their
areas, but most have gone to the Second Side, drawn by the elves.

Examples of spirits:

* **Underfolk** — tied to cattle herds
* **Wood-wife** — forest
* **Hill-wife** — mountain
* **Lake-wife** — lake
* **Nuno** — anthills
* **Nixie** — rivers
* **Church Grim** — graveyards
* **Sirens** — reefs
* **Xana** — waterfalls
* **Will-o'-the-wisp** — bogs

Some nature spirits have become minor gods.

## Minor gods

Minor gods are a motley group of unique individuals who have become powerful enough to be seen as gods.
What marks out a minor god is that they have mastered ageing, that they exist on more than one Side and
can recreate themselves as long as they do not die on all Sides, and that they can grant powers to
others (Spiritualists).

They can come from different origins:

* Elves, such as the Winter Lady
* Animals, such as the Cat God
* Nature spirits, such as Mother Longhand
* Demons, such as Baal Fagor
* Humans, such as Fahlas
* Monsters, such as the Troll King
* Ancient entities that existed before the elves, such as the Land Jelly
